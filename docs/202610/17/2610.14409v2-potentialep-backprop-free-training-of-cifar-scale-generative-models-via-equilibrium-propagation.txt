# ABSTRACT 

Generating an image with a diffusion or flow-matching model typically involves integrating a velocity field for hundreds of sequential steps. That computation suits a GPU poorly and an analog neuromorphic substrate well, where one fast, low-power physical relaxation could replace the whole sequence. Two things are needed: a generative algorithm whose computation is a relaxation, and a way to train it without backpropagation, whose global gradient transport is hard to re-alise in analog. Energy Matching supplies the first, sampling by gradient-descent relaxation on a time-independent potential — but obtaining the gradient that de-scent follows is itself a backward pass, once per integration step. We prove that a predictive coding network with a single linear injection term added to its energy relaxes to a state whose visible layer carries that gradient inherently, with an error that vanishes for small injection strengths. An Energy Matching model trained by backpropagation can therefore be loaded into this network unchanged and sampled by relaxation alone, matching the original model’s FID to within 0.04 at CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-32 scale. The network can also be trained without backpropaga-tion: Equilibrium Propagation reproduces backpropagation’s parameter updates to a cosine similarity of 0.9998 , and training end to end with those updates reaches an FID of 3.64 on unconditional CIFAR-10 against 3.54 for backpropagation. To our knowledge this is the first CIFAR-10 generative model trained with Equilib-rium Propagation, and the first at competitive quality by any backpropagation-free rule. This quality does not require group normalisation or attention: an architec-ture of only convolutions and pointwise nonlinearities, both native to an analog crossbar, reaches an FID of 3.51 when trained by backpropagation. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Modern generative models are expensive to train (Strubell et al., 2019) and expensive to sam-ple (Luccioni et al., 2024): diffusion, flow matching and related methods typically produce one image by integrating a velocity field over hundreds of sequential steps, each a full pass through a large network. Sequential work of this kind suits a GPU poorly and a physical system well. Asubstrate that relaxes to the minimum of an energy performs in one continuous settling what a GPU performs as hundreds of discrete evaluations, and analog neuromorphic devices promise large gains in speed and energy on that basis. Two things stand in the way: finding a generative algorithm whose computation is a relaxation, and removing the backpropagation that training relies on. Energy Matching (Balcerak et al., 2025) is one such algorithm. It generates by gradient descent on a potential Vθ (x), the scalar output of a feedforward network, whose minima are where the data lies. The potential does not change with time, so every step descends the same fixed landscape, which is what a physical system does when it relaxes. But the velocity it follows is −∇ xVθ , and in its original formulation obtaining that gradient is a backward pass — once per integration step during sampling, on top of the usual ∇θ L backward pass per training step. The remaining obstacle is therefore backpropagation, whose global gradient transport is difficult to realise in analog. Mixed-signal crossbar designs convert between analog and digital at every layer, at

The correspondence. We show that the physical dynamics of a predictive coding network can compute −∇ xVθ inherently. Build a PCN from the same layers, one prediction error per node, and add a single linear term of strength γ on the scalar output node, giving an energy EPCN . The states of this PotentialPCN relax to an equilibrium at which −∇ xEPCN = γ (−∇ xVθ ) + O(γ2), so dividing by γ recovers the velocity, exactly in the limit of small γ (Section 3.1). 

Generation without a backward pass. An Energy Matching model trained conventionally by backpropagation can therefore be loaded into this network unchanged and sampled by relaxation, so that a substrate hosting it needs no backward pass anywhere in the generation path. At CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-32 scale the velocities agree with autograd’s to a cosine similarity of 1 to nine decimal places, and the FID of the generated samples matches to within 0.04 (Section 4.1). No retraining is required, so the energy and latency of generation — most of a deployed model’s lifetime cost — could be cut on analog hardware without any change to how the model is trained. 

Training without a backward pass. Training can move on-chip as well. The PCN relaxes on an explicit energy, so it can be trained by Equilibrium Propagation (Scellier & Bengio, 2017), which reads parameter updates −∇ θ L from local measurements at equilibrium and so needs none of the global transport that makes backpropagation awkward in analog. We use the GradEP variant (Gower, 2026), which trains gradients of energy landscapes — here the velocities Energy Matching generates with. We call the resulting procedure PotentialEP . Its parameter updates reproduce backpropaga-tion’s to a cosine similarity of 0.9998 at our operating point (Section 4.2), and a model trained end to end with those updates reaches an FID of 3.64 on unconditional CIFAR-10, against 3.54 for back-propagation (Section 5). To our knowledge this is the first CIFAR-10 generative model trained with Equilibrium Propagation, and the first at competitive quality by any backpropagation-free rule. 

The architecture. A backpropagation-free algorithm is of little use if the architecture it trains is itself hard to realise in analog, and two components of the one we inherit from Energy Matching are: group normalisation, which couples every channel through a shared statistic, and attention, whose pairwise products are not a fixed weighted sum. We find that removing both costs nothing measurable at this scale — a network of only convolutions, pointwise nonlinearities and UNet skips, every operation of the kind an analog crossbar performs natively, reaches a CIFAR-10 FID of 3.51 

(Section 5.1). 

# 2 BACKGROUND 

2.1 ENERGY MATCHING 

Energy Matching (Balcerak et al., 2025) generates images by relaxation on a learned scalar potential. A feedforward network with a scalar output defines 

Vθ (x) = α F θ (x), (1) where Fθ (x) is the network output and α a fixed output scale set by the architecture, and samples are produced by integrating ˙x = −∇ xVθ (x) from noise. Unlike diffusion or flow matching, the velocity field is the gradient of a time-independent potential: the same landscape is descended at every step, and the model’s minima are where the data lies. Training has two phases. Section 3.1 shows that the PCN’s relaxation reproduces the generation velocity −∇ xVθ for any weights θ, whether or not those weights were themselves trained on the PCN. Training them on the PCN requires two more things: that the correspondence hold for the loss’s parameter gradient and not only for the velocity, and a way to measure that parameter gradient without a backward pass.

To our knowledge this is the first CIFAR-10 generation result for a model trained by Equilibrium Propagation. 5.1 HARDWARE -PLAUSIBLE ARCHITECTURES 

PotentialEP is neuromorphically compatible as an algorithm, but the energy it trains is only as neuro-morphically implementable as the architecture defining it. Two components we inherit from Energy Matching are not easily realised on an analog substrate: group normalisation, which couples every channel at a layer through a shared statistic, and attention, whose pairwise products are not a fixed weighted sum. We therefore ask how much generation quality is lost when both are removed. Replacing them with weight standardisation, which normalises each filter’s weights rather than its activations, residual scaling by 1/√depth, and a flat linear head in place of the ViT (App. I) leaves an architecture with no operation beyond fixed weighted sums and pointwise nonlinearities — both native to analog crossbar hardware, the sums through Ohm’s and Kirchhoff’s laws (Ielmini & Wong, 2018; Sebastian et al., 2020). We train this arm by backpropagation, our compute budget not ex-tending to a second full EP run, and it reaches an FID of 3.51 against the full architecture’s 3.54 :the two are indistinguishable at the resolution of the measurement. Generation competitive with the diffusion and flow-matching baselines of Table 2 therefore does not require either component. This architecture is wider ( 78 M against 50 M) and used a different schedule and a longer first phase (App. I), so we take it as an existence proof and leave a cheaper recipe to future work. 

# 6 RELATED WORK 

Predictive coding. Generative predictive coding typically places the image at the bottom of a hierarchy of predictions: Monte Carlo predictive coding samples it by adding Langevin noise to inference, at MNIST scale (Oliviers et al., 2024), and Langevin predictive coding samples a latent code the same way and decodes it, reaching CIFAR-10 at FID 113 (Zahid et al., 2023). In both, the image is the predicted layer of a prediction-error likelihood. In ours it is the input of a network whose scalar output is an explicit potential, and the force on it is that potential’s gradient. 9Preprint. Under review. 

Generation by energy descent. Energy Matching (Balcerak et al., 2025), which we build on, generates by descent on an explicit scalar potential. Equilibrium Matching (Wang & Du, 2025) learns a time-invariant gradient field, in its best-performing form without an explicit energy, and Rodriguez et al. (2026) train it on an Energy Transformer, whose blocks relax latent tokens on a Hopfield-type energy, reaching class-conditional FID 28 .56 on CIFAR-10. There the image-space update is a learned function of the relaxed tokens rather than the gradient of any energy in x; in ours it is the gradient of γ−1EPCN = Vθ + O(γ). Each of these works trains by backpropagation. 

Physical and neuromorphic generation. A parallel line generates with physical substrates. Sim-ulated Kuramoto oscillators (Unconventional AI, 2026) and continuous spins descending a learned quadratic energy (Sun et al., 2026) evolve a latent state from a random start and render it through a small convolutional decoder, reaching class-conditional CIFAR-10 FID 8.76 and clean-FID 9.71 

respectively, the former also at ImageNet 64 × 64 . Optical systems have implemented a diffusion model’s denoiser (Oguz et al., 2024) and a single-pass generator (Chen et al., 2025), demonstrated in hardware at MNIST scale, and spiking diffusion models target event-driven neuromorphic chips (Cao et al., 2024). All of these are trained by backpropagation. B¨ osch et al. (2026) generate 28 × 28 

MNIST zeros and ones with a local rule, on an energy with no hidden units. Ours relaxes in image space with no decoder, and uses Equilibrium Propagation to obtain local updates for an energy with hidden layers. 

# 7 DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS 

Simulation cost. On a GPU, an EP training step costs about sixteen times a backpropagation step, and an implicit-solve step about five, with the relaxation budgets dominating. A substrate that settles physically would not pay that cost, but its speed, energy and settling accuracy remain unmeasured: everything here is simulated. 

Locality and hardware realisation. The relaxation is local in the sense EP requires: every quan-tity a weight update needs is present at its two ends. The nodes of our training runs are, however, architecture blocks rather than single weight matrices, chosen to reduce simulation cost, so a literal implementation would need circuitry to carry the error through each block’s internal layers. One node per weight matrix removes this, and left the training gradient unchanged in the one checkpoint we measured (App. D). Analog EP also obtains the backward drive J⊤e from a feedback pathway programmed to match the forward one; any drift between the two leaves dynamics that descend no energy at all (Kendall et al., 2020). 

Scope of the evidence. App. K collects the scope of each claim. Each arm is a single run, so train-ing variance is unknown, and the small FID differences do not establish a ranking. We demonstrate EP training on CIFAR-10 only; at ImageNet-32 we verify the inference correspondence on the au-thors’ released checkpoints, but did not train. The hardware-plausible architecture was trained and sampled only as a feedforward network, so it has not been demonstrated together with EP. The equi-libria were computed by noise-free digital relaxation in error coordinates; the local state dynamics share their fixed points (App. E) but were not run to equilibrium at this scale. γ and β. Consequences. The error parameterisation is therefore not just a convenience but what makes the relaxation of this network practical. Three further properties follow from its near-identity Hessian, each measured elsewhere in the paper. The relaxation reaches equilibrium in a single deadbeat sweep from feedforward initialisation (App. F). The implicit-gradient solve of Section 3.2 inverts a matrix already close to the identity, so one conjugate-gradient step is as accurate as fifty (App. H). Finally, float32 suffices: at the operating γ the velocity’s deviation from autograd’s sits below the arithmetic’s own floor (Section 4.1). Part of the reason is that the error parameterisation never forms an error as the difference of two nearly equal states, which in state coordinates costs significant digits of precision in every error.

# I THE HARDWARE -PLAUSIBLE ARCHITECTURE 

This appendix specifies the norm- and attention-free architecture of Section 5.1 in enough detail to reimplement, together with the training recipe it needed. It shows only that competitive generation is possible on an architecture an analog substrate could run, not that this particular architecture is the optimal one. We give our reasons for the individual choices where we have them, but which of them are necessary, and at what cost, we leave to future work. 

What was removed and what replaced it. The architecture is built from the Energy Match-ing UNet but with three changes. Firstly, all 51 group-normalisation layers are replaced by the identity. Secondly, all 6 attention blocks are replaced by the identity, which must be done on the built module tree rather than by configuration: the UNet’s middle block hardcodes ResBlock– Attention–ResBlock, so setting the attention resolutions alone cannot produce an attention-free net-work. Thirdly, the ViT head is replaced by a flat one, which flattens the UNet’s 3 × 32 × 32 output and applies Linear(3072 , 512) , SiLU, Linear(512 , 1) . We re-initialise the UNet’s final convolution from N (0 , 0.02) rather than leaving it at the flow-matching codebase’s zero initialisation, after an earlier variant of the head failed to train from zero. We did not test whether the flat head trains without this. Two further changes are made to the remaining convolutions and residual blocks. Firstly, weight standardisation (Qiao et al., 2020) replaces each convolution’s weights by

Module census. The built model contains 42 standardised convolutions, 23 plain convolutions, 22 

ResBlocks, 26 linear layers, 69 SiLU activations, and zero group-normalisation, attention or layer-normalisation modules, against 51 , 14 and 16 of those respectively in the architecture it replaces. It carries 78 .4M parameters against 50 .0M, from the flat head’s 3072 × 512 first linear layer and a base channel width of 192 against 128 , which widens every stage through the same multipliers. 

How we arrived at this configuration. We reached it by trial rather than by systematic search. Attention and the ViT head could each be removed without much cost, but removing normalisation made training diverge, and weight standardisation was what made the fully stripped network train-able. We did not establish which ingredients are necessary, at what width, or under what schedule; that investigation is left to future work. 

Training recipe. This arm is trained by backpropagation, with the settings of Table 6. # J EXPERIMENTAL IMPLEMENTATION 

This appendix gives the settings needed to reproduce the runs of Section 5, and the implementation choices that the PCN formulation requires but a feedforward one does not. Code is available at 

https://github.com/alexgower/EnergyMatchingEqProp .

Shared recipe. Table 6 gives the settings shared by all arms, and the relaxation settings of the two PCN arms. The three arms of Section 5 share the architecture, optimiser, learning rate, schedule, effective batch size, EMA decay, step budget and data pipeline; the norm- and attention-free arm differs as App. I describes. What they do not share is the random realisation: there is no global seed in the trainer, so each arm has its own initialisation, its own noise samples for the optimal-transport coupling and the contrastive negatives, and its own dropout masks. With one run per arm we have no estimate of run-to-run variance, and the differences in Table 2 should be read with that in mind. The implicit-solve arm holds x fixed at xt and differentiates the flow loss on vPCN (xt) with respect to θ, using the implicit function theorem for the dependence of h∗ on θ, so it uses no tether; the tethered objective LPCN approaches this fixed-x one as γ/λ → 0 (App. B). 

Asynchronous relaxation. Nodes are updated in even/odd sweeps (Kerjan et al., 2026), the second half-step seeing the first’s updated values, so one sweep costs two energy evaluations. On a chain this is exactly Gauss–Seidel, since same-parity nodes share no energy term; on the DAG of App. D a skip may connect two same-parity nodes, so the decoupling is only approximate. We adopt it because Kerjan et al. (2026) find synchronous updates substantially worse at the same step size, and sometimes divergent; we did not repeat that comparison here. The fixed point is the same either way. 

Frozen dropout. A PCN training step evaluates the network many times: Tfree sweeps during the free phase and Tnudge in each nudged phase, each with Kh inner sweeps, where a feedforward step evaluates it once. Standard dropout would draw a fresh mask at every evaluation, so each sweep would relax a different energy and there would be no single equilibrium for the free phase to reach or for the finite difference to compare. We therefore use one mask per sample, held fixed across every sweep and phase of that sample’s training step. It is drawn as standard dropout draws it, so only the redrawing frequency differs, and for the feedforward arms — a single evaluation per step — the two coincide. 

FID protocol. All FIDs are computed from 50k samples against the full training set, with EMA weights, and Euler–Heun integration at dt = 0 .01 . The stopping time τs is chosen per model by sampling over a ladder of values and taking the minimum: phase-1 checkpoints are best at the noiseless endpoint τs = 1 , and after the contrastive phase the optimum moves well past it (App. I). The τs = 3 .25 chosen as optimal for the backpropagation arm is also near-optimal for both PCN arms, which a paired sweep of τs confirms. For the ImageNet-32 checkpoints we use the authors’ 

τs = 2 .5.

# K SCOPE OF THE CLAIMS 

This appendix states, in one place, what the paper does and does not claim, on four points where a reader might reasonably read more into the results than the evidence supports. 

The solver is not the substrate. Every equilibrium in this paper was found by gradient descent in error coordinates (App. E), and a single deadbeat sweep of that solver is algebraically a backward pass (Goemaere et al., 2026, Thm. C.9). That is a choice of simulation method: the claim of Sec-tion 3.1 is a property of the equilibrium, not of the route to it. The local state dynamics equation 36 share that equilibrium exactly and converge towards it (App. E), but too slowly under explicit dis-cretisation to run at this scale. What the paper establishes is that a state whose local errors carry 

−∇ xV exists, that the parameter updates EP reads from it match backpropagation’s, and that train-ing on those updates works at CIFAR-10 scale. What is new. The paper builds on known ingredients: that a PCN’s equilibrium errors implement the backward pass (Whittington & Bogacz, 2017; Millidge et al., 2022), the spring-clamped free phase of GradEP (Gower, 2026), and the linearised nudge of Dual Propagation (Høier et al., 2023). Its contributions are the injection-term construction that turns a scalar-output network into one whose visible layer carries −∇ xV (Proposition 1); the joint γ–β window and its derivation (App. C, Ta-ble 3); and the demonstration that these compose into competitive generation on CIFAR-10, where prior backpropagation-free generative models remain at MNIST scale. 

Where a backward pass remains in training. The EP arm’s parameter updates in both phases come from measurements at equilibrium. Its phase-2 negatives, however, were sampled by a feedfor-ward twin of the network using autograd velocities, since relaxing 200 Langevin steps per negative was beyond our budget. No parameter gradient passes through the twin, and the two velocity fields agree to a cosine similarity of 1 to nine decimal places (Section 4.1), so we expect replacing it with relaxation changes the negatives by an amount below the sampler’s own noise, though we did not test this. “Without backpropagation” in Section 5 refers to how the weights were updated, not to every computation the training loop performed. 

The hardware-plausible architecture is an existence proof. The norm- and attention-free net-work of Section 5.1 is wider than the reference, used a different schedule, and was trained and sampled feedforward only. Its FID of 3.51 shows that neither component is needed for competitive generation; it does not show that they can be removed at matched cost, nor that the resulting network trains under EP. Both are left to future work, as is the run-to-run variance of every arm in Table 2, each of which is a single run.
