## Abstract 

Generative inbetweening aims to synthesize temporally co-herent intermediate frames between a start frame and a tar-get frame. Although pretrained image-to-video diffusion models provide strong priors for large-motion inbetween-ing, they are not explicitly designed to reach a specified tar-get frame. They may therefore generate realistic individ-ual frames while drifting from the target or producing un-stable motion under large displacements and non-rigid dy-namics. We propose DSG-GI, a framework that adapts pre-trained latent video diffusion models through Destination-Conditioned Adaptation and Semantic-Geometric Align-ment. Destination-Conditioned Adaptation treats the target frame as the destination of the sequence and provides tem-porally progressive guidance along the generated trajec-tory. Semantic-Geometric Alignment aligns the token-wise spatio-temporal relational geometry of backbone features with that of a frozen video foundation encoder and applies a flow-matching-aware latent flow constraint to samples near the data endpoint of the probability path. Together, these components strengthen destination guidance and pre-serve coherent semantic-geometric motion structure. Exten-sive experiments on standard and large-motion inbetween-ing benchmarks demonstrate improvements in temporal sta-bility, target-frame faithfulness, and perceptual quality. Our code will be made publicly available. 

## 1. Introduction 

Generative inbetweening is a fundamental task in video understanding and generation. Given a start frame and a target frame, it synthesizes intermediate frames that form a smooth, visually plausible transition. Applications in-clude slow-motion video synthesis, frame-rate conversion, animation inbetweening, video editing, and content cre-ation. The target frame defines the destination of the sequence and should influence temporal positions with different strengths. As illustrated in Figure 1(a), early frames should remain strongly an-chored to the start-frame context, while target-frame guid-ance should progressively increase toward later positions to steer the trajectory toward the destination. The second chal-lenge is semantic-geometric consistency. As illustrated in Figure 2(b)–(c), individually plausible intermediate frames may form a sequence with discontinuities between sampled trajectories or motion that deviates from the underlying tra-jectory. Generative inbetweening therefore requires realis-tic frames and coherent spatio-temporal relations across the sequence. The work most closely related to ours is EF-VI [5], which strengthens destination conditioning through EF-Net. EF-Net estimates correlation weights between the termi-nal latent and preceding latent positions to determine the contribution of target-frame information at each position. However, Figure 1(b) shows that EF-Net assigns most of the weight to the terminal latent and suppresses the weights of most preceding latents toward zero. Constraints derived solely from the terminal latent therefore provide limited destination guidance at earlier temporal positions. To address these challenges, we propose DSG-GI, a gen-erative inbetweening framework that adapts pretrained la-tent video diffusion models through two complementary components. Destination-Conditioned Adaptation (DCA) encodes the target frame and expands its features along the latent temporal axis, providing sequence-level conditioning that progressively guides generation toward the destination. Semantic-Geometric Alignment (SGA) aligns the token-wise spatio-temporal relational geometry of backbone fea-tures with that of a frozen video foundation model and ap-plies a flow-matching-aware latent flow constraint to sam-ples near the data endpoint of the probability path. To-gether, DCA and SGA encourage the sequence to satisfy the target-frame constraint while preserving coherent semantic-geometric motion structure, without changing the architec-ture of the pretrained backbone. Our contributions are as follows. We propose DSG-GI, a framework that adapts pretrained video diffusion mod-els to generation constrained by a target frame, bridging open-ended generation and coherent inbetweening. We in-troduce two complementary components: DCA provides temporally progressive target-frame guidance, while SGA transfers relation-level video semantics and flow-matching-aware local geometric constraints from frozen auxiliary models to stabilize intermediate motion. Extensive exper-iments and ablation studies demonstrate improvements in target-frame faithfulness, temporal stability, and perceptual quality on standard and large-motion inbetweening bench-marks. 22. Related Work 

Video Frame Interpolation. Video frame interpolation has been widely studied in classical and generative settings. Traditional methods synthesize intermediate frames by es-timating optical flow, motion kernels, or local correspon-dences [3, 13, 16, 18, 19, 21, 28, 29, 32, 34]. These meth-ods generally perform well under small or moderate mo-tion, but may struggle with large displacements, occlusions, non-rigid deformations, and complex scene changes that make low-level correspondence ambiguous. Recent gener-ative inbetweening methods use pretrained image-to-video diffusion models conditioned on the start and target frames, sometimes combined with bidirectional sampling or auxil-iary controls [5, 7, 8, 14, 25, 26, 38, 41, 52]. Despite pro-ducing visually plausible frames, these methods still face challenges in maintaining temporal consistency and satis-fying the target-frame constraint. These limitations moti-vate the destination-conditioned adaptation and semantic-geometric alignment in DSG-GI. 

Controllable Video Generation. Controllable video gen-eration methods incorporate depth, pose, segmentation, tra-jectories, or optical flow into pretrained diffusion mod-els [9, 10, 20, 27, 31, 42, 46, 49, 50]. Representation alignment similarly transfers knowledge from pretrained vi-sual encoders by aligning intermediate features [48]. These strategies improve motion fidelity and structural consis-tency, but may require dense frame-wise supervision or ad-ditional data. Building on these ideas, DSG-GI treats the target frame as a sequence-level destination condition and combines relation-level representation alignment with flow-matching-aware local geometric consistency. This design regularizes intermediate motion without requiring dense control annotations during inference.

3.2. Destination-Conditioned Adaptation 

Inspired by EF-VI [5], we design Destination-Conditioned Adaptation (DCA) as a lightweight mechanism for injecting target-frame features. The pretrained backbone integrates these features, while SGA regularizes the learned represen-tations. This separation preserves the representation and integration capabilities of the backbone without adding a feature-fusion module. AlignVid [24] further shows that pretrained models exhibit coarse foreground–background separation, with most foreground-sensitive blocks concen-trated in the earlier half of the network. This distribu-tion motivates injecting target-frame features across mul-tiple stages instead of a single fixed layer. DCA therefore converts the target frame I1 into a sequence-shaped control signal and injects it into different stages of the DiT back-bone. Let the pretrained DiT backbone contain N transformer blocks [30, 37]. DCA contains K lightweight target-frame control blocks. I2V models rely primarily on low-level appearance objec-tives, which encourage realistic individual frames without explicitly preserving sequence-level semantic relations or motion structure. Semantic-Geometric Alignment (SGA) addresses this limitation by aligning the spatio-temporal re-lational geometry of backbone representations with that of a frozen video foundation encoder. It also provides local ge-ometric supervision through a latent flow constraint when the estimated data latent is sufficiently reliable. 

Relation-Level Semantic Geometry. Inspired by Vide-oREPA [48], we use intermediate DiT features as student representations for semantic alignment. However, the TRD loss in VideoREPA targets higher-level constraints, such as physical regularities of motion in T2V generation, and may be less suited to generative inbetweening. Here, the start and target frames constrain the semantic evolution of the intermediate sequence, so the generative model and video understanding model are expected to capture similar seman-tic changes over time. We therefore use interpolation in-stead of convolution to spatially downsample the intermedi-ate DiT features to the teacher feature resolution and apply a margin-relaxed L1 loss to their global relation matrices to preserve the spatio-temporal relational structure. We transform Aθ with a lightweight MLP and interpolate the feature volume to the spatio-temporal resolution of the teacher features. For sim-plicity, Aθ also denotes the transformed features below. ## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Experimental Setting 

Datasets. For training, we collect 20k real-world video clips from Videvo 1, a publicly available stock-media plat-form. The clips cover human actions, animal motion, vehi-cle motion, and natural phenomena, providing diverse mo-tion patterns for adapting the pretrained video generation model. We resize each clip to 1024 × 576 and use its first 25 frames for training. Qwen3-VL [2] generates a text de-scription from the start and target frames of each training clip, which serves as the text condition. For evaluation, we follow recent generative inbetweening studies and use the TGI-Bench and FCVG test sets [6, 52]. These benchmarks cover standard interpolation and large-motion generative in-betweening. 

Baselines. We compare DSG-GI with seven VFI meth-ods. The three non-generative methods are FILM [32], AMT [19], and SPEED [51]; the four generative methods are GI [41], FCVG [52], MPD [15], and EF-VI [5]. We evaluate all baselines using their official default settings and recommended configurations. 

Metrics. We evaluate DSG-GI with four complementary metrics: LPIPS, FID, FVD, and FVMD [11, 22, 36, 47]. LPIPS measures perceptual similarity between generated and ground-truth frames. FID compares feature distribu-tions of generated and real frames to assess visual quality. FVD assesses video quality through differences in spatio-temporal feature distributions, while FVMD evaluates mo-tion quality through differences in motion feature distribu-tions.

## 5. Limitations 

DSG-GI has several limitations. First, SGA depends on a frozen video representation model and a frozen optical-flow estimator during training. Neither is required during inference, but inaccurate teacher features or flow estimates under severe occlusion, rapid non-rigid motion, or non-photorealistic content may weaken the learned constraints. Second, the soft destination prior in DSG-GI does not pro-vide explicit physical reasoning. The model may therefore struggle with highly stochastic events, topological changes, or multiple equally plausible motion paths. 

## 6. Conclusion 

DSG-GI formulates generative inbetweening as destination-constrained flow matching. DCA provides temporally progressive target-frame guidance, while SGA regular-izes intermediate motion through relation-level semantic alignment and flow-matching-aware local geometry. Quan-titative and qualitative results demonstrate improvements in perceptual quality, target-frame faithfulness, and motion coherence. ## 7. Text Conditioning 

Following the protocol of EF-VI for using a vision-language model in video inbetweening [5], we use Qwen3-VL-8B [2] to generate text prompts solely from the start and end frames. We provide each start-end-frame pair together with the instruction: “This is the first and last frame of a video clip. Describe this video in one short motion caption.” The resulting caption serves as the text condition during training and inference. Figure 7 presents examples of the generated text prompts. 

## 8. More Visual Comparisons 

Figure 9 provides additional intermediate-frame compar-isons, and Figure 10 presents generated sequence examples. The examples cover human actions, animal motion, and moving objects, illustrating how DSG-GI preserves object structure and scene appearance while generating coherent transitions between the start and end frames. The MP4 ex-amples in video demo.zip provide more detailed com-parisons of motion and temporal consistency. 

## 9. Failed Cases 

Figure 8 presents failure cases of DSG-GI. In these exam-ples, the model fails to preserve object structure or consis-tent subject appearance in scenes with substantial viewpoint changes, multiple moving subjects, and complex dynamics. Simultaneous camera and object motion requires coordina-tion of global viewpoint changes with local movement, de-formation, and occlusion. Overlapping or changing motion patterns may exceed the learned motion-modeling capabili-ties of the pretrained backbone. These failures are consistent with limitations of the adap-tation and alignment mechanisms. DCA provides tempo-rally progressive target-frame guidance, but its soft desti-nation prior does not specify intermediate camera or object trajectories. SGA encourages global semantic relations and local flow consistency during training, but does not explic-itly disentangle sources of motion. These constraints may therefore be insufficient to resolve ambiguous intermedi-ate dynamics from the endpoints, particularly when camera
