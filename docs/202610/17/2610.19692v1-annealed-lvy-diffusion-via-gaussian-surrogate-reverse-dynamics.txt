## ABSTRACT 

Gaussian diffusion models [Ho et al., 2020] offer a practical framework for generative modeling. Denoising Lévy Probabilistic Models (DLPM) [Shariatian et al., 2025] introduce heavy-tailed per-turbations with a fixed tail index throughout denoising. We introduce Annealed Lévy Diffusion (ALD) . ALD varies the stability index α(t) to combine heavy-tailed exploration with Gaussian re-finement. Our marginal-first formulation represents stable noise as a scalar Gaussian scale mixture. This formulation enables standard Gaussian noise-prediction training with one additional scale in-put. We study two sampling processes. ALD-renoise estimates the clean sample and injects fresh marginal noise. ALD-posterior preserves dependence between successive states through an accu-mulated conditional variance and a Gaussian bridge update. Both samplers reuse the same trained predictor with different scalar inputs. We evaluate ALD on four 2D benchmarks and an imbalanced nine-mode mixture. We also examine training efficiency and the effects of prediction perturba-tions on clipped Holtsmark data. Additional experiments evaluate MNIST and CelebA64 genera-tion and CIFAR-10LT augmentation. ALD preserves local geometry on the evaluated light-tailed and manifold-structured targets. 

## 1 Introduction 

Diffusion models [Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015, Song and Ermon, 2020, Ho et al., 2020] have established themselves as the dominant paradigm for high-fidelity generative modeling, with applications spanning image synthesis [Dhariwal and Nichol, 2021, Rombach et al., 2022] and scientific problems [Pandey et al., 2025, Watson et al., 2023, Zeni et al., 2025]. Their core mechanism is to corrupt data x0 ∼ pdata via a fixed stochastic process and learn to reverse it: the forward process produces xt = mtx0 + σtn with n ∼ N (0, I), and generation proceeds via the estimated reverse kernel pθ (xt−1 | xt). The common choice of Gaussian noise is motivated by analytical tractability: it admits closed-form forward kernels, a stable ϵ-prediction objective, and a reverse-time SDE representation under suitable regularity conditions via Anderson’s time-reversal formula [Anderson, 1982]. However, for target distributions with polynomial tails, the Gaussian terminal prior and light-tailed perturbation kernels provide exponentially weaker relative coverage of extreme regions than the data law. With finite samples and training budgets, the network therefore struggles to estimate the score accurately in the tail regions due to the scarcity of training samples there. This score estimation difficulty is consequential for a broad and scientifically important class of problems. The Holtsmark distribution describes Coulomb force statistics from random point charges and is a 3/2-stable distribution (α = 3 /2, with PDF decaying as |x|−5/2) [Holtsmark, 1919, Samorodnitsky and Taqqu, 1994, Nolan, 2020]; financial return distributions can exhibit power-law tails, motivating heavy-tailed alternatives to Gaussian models [Gopikrish-arXiv:2610.19692v1 [cs.LG] 14 Oct 2026 Annealed Lévy Diffusion AR XIV PREPRINT 

nan et al., 1999, Nolan, 2020, Larralde and Mota Navarro, 2025]; heavy-tailed statistics also arise in fluid dynamics and related transport phenomena, as illustrated by studies of anomalous transport and turbulence spreading [Solomon et al., 1993, Buonocore et al., 2020, Milovanov et al., 2025]. For such distributions, Gaussian diffusion can suffer from tail blindness : the terminal prior is light-tailed and the training distribution gives sparse effective coverage to rare extremes, so learned scores in tail regions are poorly constrained and may collapse toward bulk-dominated behav-ior. This bias is not merely a representational limitation: increasing capacity alone does not repair the sparse far-tail coverage induced by Gaussian corruption [Song and Ermon, 2019, Yu and Yu, 2026]; see also Experiment 5.3. The natural remedy is to replace Gaussian noise with α-stable Lévy noise [Applebaum, 2009], whose tail probability decays polynomially as Pr( |z| > x ) ∼ Cαx−α for α ∈ (0 , 2) ; at the boundary α = 2 the distribution reduces to the Gaussian, whose tails decay exponentially and the polynomial formula no longer applies. This provides a para-metric family that can be matched to infinite-variance polynomial-tail targets with tail exponent in (0 , 2) by choosing 

α appropriately. However, prior attempts encounter one of two fundamental obstructions. Continuous Lévy–Itô SDE approaches such as LIM [Yoon et al., 2023] require a reverse-time formulation adapted to jump-driven dynamics. Although Lévy processes are semimartingales and admit Itô calculus, the Brownian reverse-SDE formula based on the ordinary score ∇x log pt(x) cannot be applied directly. For isotropic α-stable noise with α < 2, the generator is non-local and involves the fractional Laplacian −(−∆) α/ 2. LIM derives the corresponding reverse-time formulation using a nonlocal fractional score and learns it through fractional denoising score matching, introducing additional analytical complexity relative to Gaussian diffusion. Discrete Markov chain models such as DLPM [Shariatian et al., 2025] avoid this fractional-score formulation by representing stable noise as a scalar Gaussian scale mixture and deriving approxi-mate reverse updates through conditional Gaussian posterior calculations. Their explicit stable marginal laws rely on convolution closure among independent α-stable increments with a common stability index. DLPM therefore keeps 

α fixed throughout the chain; varying the index would generally take the accumulated noise outside the stable family used in its derivation. Although the noise amplitude can decrease during denoising, this fixed-index construction does not support independently annealing the tail index toward the Gaussian boundary α = 2 during final refinement. This leaves no tail-index schedule for assigning different tail behavior to exploration and geometry refinement. Recent theoretical and empirical work also identifies a trade-off between improved initialization and the statistical difficulty of reverse estimation under heavy-tailed noising [Cherkaoui et al., 2026]. These paradigms expose complementary modeling constraints: jump-driven formulations require nonlocal reverse operators, while standard fixed-index stable chains do not provide a tail-index schedule. This motivates constructions that keep conditional Gaussian calculations tractable without requiring stable closure across time. We introduce Annealed Lévy Diffusion (ALD) to address the fixed-index restriction through a marginal-first construction that specifies α(t)-stable perturbation kernels directly at each time. By retaining a scalar Gaussian scale-mixture representation, ALD allows α(t) to vary while preserving tractable conditional Gaussian calculations and standard Gaussian noise-prediction training with one additional scale input. The resulting schedule combines heavy-tailed exploration with Gaussian refinement. We adopt a marginal-first formulation related to diffusion models with location-scale noise [Jolicoeur-Martineau et al., 2023]. Instead of deriving the forward process from discrete convolution or a Lévy SDE, we define the perturbed state directly via the sub-Gaussian representation of an α(t)-stable vector: 

xt = mtx0 + σt

pSt n, St ∼ A α(t)/2, n ∼ N (0, I),

where Aα(t)/2 is a positive (α(t)/2) -stable variance multiplier [Samorodnitsky and Taqqu, 1994, Sec. 2.5, Prop. 2.5.2]. Because xt is not derived from convolution of historical increments, α(t) can be any prescribed function of t, enabling a two-phase annealing schedule that transitions from heavy-tailed exploration ( t ≥ τ ) to pure Gaussian refinement (t < τ , St ≡ 1). The reverse process is a discrete ancestral sampler: conditioned on St, the forward likelihood and denoising score target are Gaussian, so training reduces to standard Gaussian score matching with a single additional scalar input log St. The practical samplers reconstruct a clean-data estimate with a Gaussian noise predictor. We study direct marginal re-noising (ALD-renoise) and a variance-controlled adjacent transition (ALD-posterior) that retains the current-state contribution and tracks the accumulated conditional noise variance. 

Contributions. Our central contribution is a marginal-first construction that uses time-dependent α(t)-stable pertur-bation kernels and conditional Gaussian noise prediction. This construction permits a two-phase schedule from heavy-tailed exploration to Gaussian refinement and can reuse standard diffusion backbones with a scalar scale-conditioning input. We provide two practical reverse updates, ALD-renoise and ALD-posterior, which share a trained predictor but differ in adjacent-state coupling. Through experiments on synthetic and image datasets, we analyze tail recovery, geometric fidelity, and sensitivity to prediction errors, and evaluate the effectiveness of ALD for downstream data augmentation. 2Annealed Lévy Diffusion AR XIV PREPRINT 

## 2 Related Work 

Score-based generative models Song and Ermon [2020], Ho et al. [2020] learn to reverse diffusion via score estimation; the score SDE framework Song et al. [2021a] unifies discrete and continuous approaches, identifying the probability flow ODE as a deterministic counterpart to the reverse SDE. Flow Matching Lipman et al. [2023], Liu et al. [2023] regresses directly on conditional velocity fields, while Stochastic Interpolants Albergo et al. [2025], Ma et al. [2024] subsume both perspectives by deriving smooth marginal velocity fields from algebraic interpolation. Among heavy-tailed generative models, DLPM Shariatian et al. [2025] replaces Gaussian noise with fixed-α stable noise in a discrete Markov chain via convolution closure; LIM Yoon et al. [2023] embeds α-stable Lévy processes into a continuous SDE and derives a reverse process via fractional denoising score matching; t-EDM and t-Flow Pandey et al. [2025] use Student-t distributions with a fixed Student-t tail parameter under the EDM framework. DLPM and LIM use a fixed stable index, while t-EDM and t-Flow use a fixed Student-t tail parameter. ALD prescribes a time-dependent stable index through a conditional Gaussian scale-mixture formulation. Cold Diffusion Bansal et al. [2023] studies more general image degradations, including deterministic transforms such as blur and masking, rather than requiring stable-noise perturbations. The sub-Gaussian representation z d

= √S n is classical Samorodnitsky and Taqqu [1994], Feller [1971] and has been exploited in robust statistics and neural network weight analysis Hodgkinson and Mahoney [2021]; we use it as the structural foundation of our marginal-first forward process. ALD’s two-phase annealing draws philosophical inspiration from Annealed Importance Sampling Neal [2001] and parallel tempering Swendsen and Wang [1986], but performs annealing within a single continuous-time reverse trajectory via the dynamic α(t)

parameter.

## 6 Discussion & Conclusion 

Training budget and prediction accuracy. ALD-renoise and ALD-posterior share the same noise-prediction train-ing formulation. In the Holtsmark experiments, ALD-renoise performs better at smaller training budgets, whereas ALD-posterior becomes more effective after more training updates. These results suggest that the preferred sampler depends on prediction accuracy. The controlled prediction-output perturbation experiment further supports this trade-off: ALD-renoise is less sensitive to perturbation added to the network’s predictions, while ALD-posterior achieves better generation quality without this added noise, as observed in the Holtsmark experiment (Appendix A.3). 

Mathematical interpretation of ALD-posterior. The forward perturbation in Eq. (6) specifies the distribution at each time but leaves the dependence between times unspecified. Whether the process is Markovian cannot be deter-mined from these marginal distributions alone. ALD-posterior introduces an explicit Markov forward process through Eq. (11), with accumulated conditional variance Vt given by Eq. (12). This construction replaces the original condi-tional noise variance σ2 

> t

St by Vt and generally changes the intermediate marginal distributions. It nevertheless retains the same initial data distribution, since m0 = 1 and V0 = 0 .For this new forward process, Gaussian conditioning gives an exact adjacent posterior when the clean sample x0

and the relevant variance variables are known. During generation, x0 is replaced by the denoiser ˆxP0 . The resulting Gaussian approximation omits the contribution of clean-data posterior uncertainty to the reverse covariance. ALD-posterior is thus a variance-controlled reverse sampler for an alternative forward process, whose intermediate marginals differ from those used by ALD-renoise. 13 Annealed Lévy Diffusion AR XIV PREPRINT

ALD-renoise makes 74 network calls because the endpoint t = 1 

uses the empirical data mean, whereas ALD-posterior makes 75. Without perturbation, ALD-posterior has the lower W2 and MMD 2. Small perturbations leave ALD-renoise nearly unchanged but already increase the posterior W2; by σnoise = 0 .25 , ALD-renoise has the lower W2, and the gap widens thereafter. The improvement of ALD-renoise at intermediate noise levels should not be interpreted as monotone robustness: the added prediction noise expands its initially concentrated samples, while its W2 and MMD 2 worsen again at σnoise = 1 . Validation-only ALD sampler selection. Before formal evaluation, we registered nine ALD settings from η ∈{0, 0.5, 1} and time-grid power p ∈ { 1, 1.5, 2}. Each setting generated 5,000 images at 1,000 NFE and was evaluated against the independent validation split using all six metrics. We formed the Pareto frontier by minimizing FID and KID and maximizing precision, recall, density, and coverage; from its six nondominated settings, we froze the one with lowest validation FID: (η, p ) = (0 .5, 1.5) (validation FID 4.5266). The other validation results and tradeoffs are retained in Table 9. No formal train-reference result was used to change this configuration. 

Training and evaluation settings. We use the CelebA64 split with 162,770 training and 19,867 validation images. Following the released LIM preprocessing [Yoon et al., 2023], the aligned images are cropped to 128 × 128 at top 57 and left 25, resized to 64 × 64 by bilinear interpolation, and normalized to [−1, 1] ; random horizontal flipping is used during training. All three models follow the released LIM CelebA U-Net configuration [Yoon et al., 2023]: base width 128, channel multipliers (1 , 2, 2, 2, 4) , two residual blocks per downsampling level, attention at resolution 16 × 16 and in the bottleneck, and dropout 0.1. ALD additionally receives the noise-scale input. Training uses one seed, 600,000 optimizer updates, global batch size 256 (153.6 million image presentations), and an EMA decay of 0.9999. The optimizer is AdamW with zero weight decay, initial learning rate 10 −4, linear learning-rate decay without warm-up, and gradient-norm clipping at one. We sample from each final EMA checkpoint with sampling seed 0, generating 50,000 images at 1,000 network evaluations (NFE) per image. The architecture, image count, training budget, EMA, generated count, NFE, and real-image reference protocol are matched, while each method retains its own training objective and reverse sampler. 

Evaluation protocol. We extract the same 2,048-dimensional InceptionV3 features for generated and real images us-ing pytorch-fid . FID [Heusel et al., 2017] is the mean and sample standard deviation across five fixed 50,000-image draws from the 162,770-image training pool, compared with the same 50,000 generated images. KID [Bi´ nkowski et al., 2018] uses the unbiased MMD-squared estimator with the degree-three polynomial kernel and is reported as the mean and sample standard deviation across 100 fixed 1,000/1,000 subsets. Precision and recall [Kynkäänniemi et al., 2019] use k = 3 ; density and coverage [Naeem et al., 2020] use k = 5 , all computed by our implementation on the same fixed 10,000/10,000 real and generated feature subsets.
