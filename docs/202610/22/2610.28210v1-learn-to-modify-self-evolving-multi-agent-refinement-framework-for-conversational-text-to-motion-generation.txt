Abstract 

Despite rapid progress in text-to-motion generation, existing methods are commonly designed for single-shot generation. When users are dissatisfied with specific motion details, they are often forced to regenerate the entire sequence from scratch, which is computationally inefficient and potentially destroys previously established kinematic context. A practical gener-ation system should instead support iterative user feedback and refine only the affected spatio-temporal regions while preserving the remaining motion. Therefore, we introduce a conversational motion generation paradigm that requires the system to coordinate instruction understanding, motion generation, localized editing, and quality assessment. Recent advances in LLM-driven multi-agent systems provide a promis-ing foundation for such role-specialized coordination. To tackle these challenges, a Self-evolving Multi-Agent Refinemen T

Framework (SMART) is proposed for conversational motion generation. Within SMART, to preserve semantic consistency, we design a semantic-guided masked motion editing mech-anism, which precisely grounds natural language feedback into spatiotemporal motion regions to enable localized refine-ments without altering unedited regions. To reduce redundant exploration, we propose a memory-based self-evolving strat-egy that dynamically accumulates motion editing knowledge from interaction history and reuses past successful experience. Extensive experiments demonstrate that SMART effectively supports multi-turn conversational motion generation and en-ables efficient motion refinement. 

# Introduction 

Text-to-Motion (T2M) generation (Zhu et al. 2023; Chen et al. 2024; Yu et al. 2024), which aims to synthesize realistic human motions from natural language descriptions, has emerged as a fundamental task with broad applications in gaming, virtual reality, and robotics (Guo et al. 2025; Tashakori et al. 2025; Zitkovich et al. 2023). Despite recent progress, T2M generation remains challenging because it requires both accurate language understanding and faithful translation of textual semantics into temporally coherent motion sequences. Existing T2M methods have explored diverse generation paradigms. Autoencoder-based methods (Zhang et al. 2023b; Zhong et al. 2023; Geng et al. 2024) learn compact latent mo-tion representations for synthesis. GAN-based methods (Ahn et al. 2018; Xu et al. 2023; Ahuja and Morency 2019) improve motion realism through adversarial training. Autoregressive

Recently, driven by rapid advancements in large language models (LLMs) (Achiam et al. 2023; Zhao et al. 2023; Li et al. 2025), LLM-based motion generation approaches (Jiang et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2024b; Wu et al. 2025) have made significant strides in generating high-fidelity motions that align with complex textual instructions. However, most existing approaches are still designed for single-shot generation. When users are dissatisfied with spe-cific motion details, they usually have to regenerate the entire sequence from scratch. Such a paradigm is computationally inefficient and may discard previously established kinematic context, causing unnecessary changes to motion regions that are already semantically correct. This limitation is particularly problematic in real-world creative workflows, where users often refine their intent progressively through feedback rather than specifying all desired details in a single instruction. These observations motivate a more user-centric setting, arXiv:2610.28210v1 [cs.CV] 20 Oct 2026 namely conversational motion generation, where motions are iteratively refined through multi-turn natural language inter-action. Unlike single-shot generation, this setting requires the system to coordinate instruction understanding, motion synthesis, localized editing, quality assessment, and long-term interaction memory. Recent advances in multi-agent systems (Cardoso and Ferrando 2021; Calegari et al. 2021) provide a promising foundation for such role-specialized co-ordination. Nevertheless, instantiating conversational motion generation with multi-agent refinement remains challenging. First, personalized edits usually target only specific body parts or temporal segments, but naive refinement may unintention-ally alter already correct motion regions and compromise global semantic consistency. Second, without an explicit memory mechanism, agents may repeatedly explore similar editing strategies across interaction turns, leading to redun-dant reasoning and inefficient refinement. To address these challenges, we propose SMART , a Self-evolving Multi-Agent Refinemen T framework for conversa-tional motion generation. SMART organizes multiple role-specialized agents into a closed-loop workflow for instruction rewriting, motion generation, video-text checking, and lo-calized editing. A semantic-guided masked motion editing mechanism is designed to use a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to ground natural-language feedback into a mask ma-trix, enabling precise refinement while preserving unaffected motion regions. To reduce redundant exploration, SMART further incorporates a memory-based self-evolving strategy that accumulates and updates generation knowledge from interaction history, allowing the system to optimize genera-tion motion over multiple turns. We further implement an interactive conversational platform to evaluate SMART under realistic multi-turn refinement scenarios. Extensive exper-iments demonstrate that SMART effectively supports con-versational motion refinement, preserves unaffected motion regions, and improves refinement efficiency across interaction rounds. • We design a semantic-guided masked motion editing mechanism that converts structured feedback into spatio-temporal editing masks, enabling localized refinement while preserving unaffected motion regions. 

• We develop a memory-based self-evolving strategy that accumulates successful failure–repair experiences and reuses them to reduce redundant exploration during multi-turn refinement. 

# Related Work 

Text-to-Motion Generation 

Existing text-to-motion (T2M) methods can be broadly cate-gorized into autoencoder-based, GAN-based, autoregressive, and diffusion-based approaches. Autoencoder-based methods (Zhang et al. 2023b; Zhong et al. 2023; Geng et al. 2024) learn compact latent representations to capture motion structures, while GAN-based methods (Ahn et al. 2018; Xu et al. 2023; Ahuja and Morency 2019) employ adversarial learning to im-prove motion realism and diversity. Autoregressive methods generate discrete motion tokens sequentially. For example, MoMask (Guo et al. 2024) adopts masked token prediction with residual refinement to improve generation efficiency, whereas MotionChain (Jiang et al. 2024) and Motion-Agent (Wu et al. 2025) incorporate language-based planning to sup-port more complex motion tasks. Diffusion-based methods iteratively denoise motion sequences conditioned on textual descriptions. MotionDiffuse (Zhang et al. 2024) introduces text-conditioned diffusion with temporal modeling, MDM (Tevet et al. 2022) directly predicts clean motion samples with geometric losses, and ReMoDiffuse (Zhang et al. 2023c) enhances text-motion alignment through retrieval-augmented generation. 

LLM-based motion generation 

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong capabilities in language understanding, task decomposition, tool invocation, and self-correction (Chang et al. 2024; Ruan et al. 2023; Wu et al. 2023; Li et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2025b, 2024a). These capabilities have motivated agent-based gener-ative systems that coordinate specialized tools for complex generation and editing tasks (Durante et al. 2024; Masterman et al. 2024). For instance, GenArtist (Wang et al. 2024c) employs a multimodal agent to select and coordinate visual tools, demonstrating the potential of agents to unify genera-tion and editing. In motion generation, MotionGPT (Jiang et al. 2023) represents motions as discrete tokens and uni-fies text and motion modeling within a language-modeling framework. MotionGPT-2 (Wang et al. 2024b) further extends this paradigm to motion generation, understanding, and edit-ing. # Self-evolving Multi-agent Refinement Framework 

An overview of SMART is illustrated in Fig. 2. SMART formulates conversational motion generation as a multi-agent self-evolving refinement process. The framework operates at two levels. At the outer level, the user progressively refines an initially generated motion through a sequence of natural-language instructions, as shown in Fig. 2(a). At the inner level, each user instruction is resolved through agent-driven refinement cycles within SMART. It adopts a role-specialized directed workflow with explicit motion states and verification feedback. Moreover, SMART introduces a memory-based self-evolving mechanism that records successful refinement experiences and reuses them in later interaction rounds. In this way, the framework not only supports closed-loop motion refinement within each turn but also improves refinement efficiency across turns through accumulated historical experi-ence.

# Experiments 

Experimental Setup 

To comprehensively evaluate the performance and generaliz-ability of our interactive motion generation framework, We conduct experiments on two benchmark datasets: Hu-manML3D (Guo et al. 2022a) and KIT-ML (Plappert, Man-dery, and Asfour 2016). These datasets cover diverse motion types, annotation styles, and levels of semantic complex-ity, enabling a comprehensive evaluation across different scenarios. Note that, for the comparison experiments, we instantiate SMART with SALAD as the motion generator on both datasets. For ablation studies, we use an MDM-based generator on HumanML3D unless otherwise specified.

Ablation Study 

Effect of Multi-agent Collaboration We ablated SMART by individually removing the Chatter, Checker, or Editor. As shown in Table 3, each ablation degraded semantic alignment and motion quality, demonstrating the complementary con-tribution of each agent. Removing the Checker and Editor caused the performance drop, underscoring the importance of semantic feedback for accurate motion refinement. 

Effect of Core Modules We evaluated the memory-based self-evolving module and spatio-temporal (ST) editing through ablation studies. As shown in Fig. 3, full SMART achieved the highest R-Prec@1 and diversity. Compared with full editing, ST editing improved R-Prec@1 from 0.531 to 0.535 and diversity from 9.561 to 9.662, with a slightly higher FID of 0.503 versus 0.491. Removing memory reduced R-Prec@1 and diversity while substantially increasing FID, demonstrating the benefit of experience reuse for motion refinement.

# Conclusion 

In this paper, we introduced the Self-evolving Multi-Agent 

Refinemen T Framework (SMART) for conversational text-to-motion generation. By integrating the Chatter, Generator, Editor, and Checker agents, SMART demonstrates consistent improvements in motion quality and semantic consistency. Our ablation studies highlight the crucial contributions of the four agents, with the full SMART configuration yielding superior performance over individual agents.
