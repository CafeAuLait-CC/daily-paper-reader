ABSTRACT
We present PixGR, an efficient framework that integrates lightweight pixel-space
generative modeling into feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstruction to model scene
uncertainty. Context observations are encoded once into a scene latent representa-
tion, which conditions generative decoding from noisy target views to produce a
shared 3D Gaussian representation. We formulate this process using pixel-space
flow matching, enabling end-to-end optimization of 3D reconstruction with direct
pixel-space generative supervision through differentiable rendering. This avoids
VAE-based latent compression and error propagation inherent in previous two-
stage pipelines. To preserve feed-forward efficiency, PixGR caches the scene
representation and performs iterative inference using a lightweight DiT decoder.
Experiments demonstrate that PixGR outperforms regression-based approaches
in challenging sparse-view settings, particularly with widely spaced observations
and view extrapolation. Meanwhile, it maintains sub-second inference and re-
mains competitive with substantially heavier pretrained generative models. Our
project page is available at https://ranrhuang.github.io/pixgr/.

INTRODUCTION
Reconstructing 3D scenes from a few images is a long-standing and fundamental problem in com-
puter vision. Recent feed-forward generalizable methods have made remarkable progress by directly
regressing 3D scene representations from input observations (Charatan et al., 2024; Chen et al.,
2024a; Huang & Mikolajczyk, 2025a; Xu et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025; 2026), as shown in Fig. 1a.
These regression-based methods offer efficient single-pass inference and achieve impressive recon-
struction quality in well-observed regions. However, under sparse-view observations, the underlying
geometry and appearance of a 3D scene are weakly constrained, admitting multiple possible 3D ex-
planations and introducing substantial reconstruction uncertainty. A deterministic regressor cannot
explicitly model such ambiguity and instead commits to an averaged estimate, often resulting in
blurry reconstructions or failures in occluded and unseen regions.
To address the high ambiguity of sparse-view reconstruction, generative modeling is introduced
due to its ability to model a distribution over plausible scene explanations. Previous optimization-
based approaches progressively densify sparse observations with generated novel views and dis-
till them back into 3D (Wu et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2025b). To
avoid their costly per-scene iterative optimization, feed-forward generative methods mainly follow
two paradigms: (i) generation then reconstruction (Fig. 1b): it first generates novel views and
subsequently uses a feed-forward network to regress a 3D Gaussian splatting (3DGS) representa-
tion (Kerbl et al., 2023) from them (Szymanowicz et al., 2025); (ii) reconstruction then genera-
tion (Fig. 1c): it first reconstructs 3DGS and then renders them at target viewpoints to condition a
generative model for target-view refinement or synthesis (Chen et al., 2024b; Wewer et al., 2024;
Stracquadanio et al., 2026).
However, these feed-forward generative approaches still face several limitations. First, they decou-
ple generation and reconstruction, preventing the two stages from being jointly optimized within
a unified 3D representation. For generation then reconstruction, inconsistencies across generated
views can introduce artifacts into the subsequent 3D reconstruction. For reconstruction then gen-
eration, the errors in the initial reconstruction are propagated into the generative stage, while the
refined target views cannot directly revise the feed-forward 3D reconstruction. Moreover, compared

lightweight pixel-space generative modeling directly into feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstruction.
PixGR consists of a deterministic scene encoder and a generative decoder. Shown in Fig. 1d, in-
stead of encoding context observations into an explicit 3D reconstruction that may already contain
errors, the scene encoder produces a 3D-aware latent, deferring uncertain 3D decisions to generative
decoding. Conditioned on this latent and noisy target views, the generative decoder predicts at each
sampling step a global 3D Gaussian representation that supports both the observed context views
and evolving target views, directly shaping a cross-view-consistent 3D scene. To avoid the rep-
resentation bottleneck of latent-space generation, PixGR performs flow matching directly in pixel
space (Li & He, 2026), where renderings of the predicted 3D Gaussians serve as denoised target
predictions, allowing the generative objective to directly supervise 3D reconstruction through dif-
ferentiable rendering. For efficient generative inference, PixGR adopts a lightweight DiT (Peebles
& Xie, 2023) decoder, while the input scene is encoded only once and cached throughout sampling.
Experiments show that this lightweight probabilistic formulation substantially improves reconstruc-
tion quality under high uncertainty while retaining sub-second inference.
PixGR outperforms
regression-based approaches in challenging settings, including widely spaced input views and view
extrapolation, and is even competitive with methods that rely on much heavier pretrained generative
models. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• We introduce PixGR, a highly efficient and end-to-end trainable framework that formu-
lates generalizable feed-forward 3D reconstruction in a probabilistic manner by integrating
lightweight generative modeling directly into 3D Gaussian reconstruction.
• We develop pixel-space flow formulation conditioned on a 3D-aware scene latent, avoid-
ing compressed latent-space bottlenecks and enabling direct pixel-space supervision of 3D
reconstruction through differentiable rendering.
• Extensive experiments show that PixGR consistently improves over regression-based re-
construction in challenging sparse-view settings, while remaining competitive with much
heavier generative approaches and achieving sub-second inference.

RELATED WORK
2.1
FEED-FORWARD 3D REGRESSION
Recent advancements in feed-forward 3D regression aim to directly regress 3D representations in a
single forward pass (Fig. 1a) (Charatan et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024a; Wang et al., 2024; Huang &
Mikolajczyk, 2025a; Wang et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025; Ye et al., 2025; 2026), significantly improv-
ing efficiency compared to per-scene optimization pipelines (Schonberger & Frahm, 2016; Kerbl
et al., 2023). Among these approaches, methods based on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) (Kerbl
et al., 2023) are particularly relevant to our work due to their strong rendering efficiency and ability
to produce high-quality novel view synthesis. Within this paradigm, prior work explores a variety
of strategies, including incorporating geometric inductive biases (Charatan et al., 2024; Chen et al.,
2024a), leveraging pretrained depth priors (Xu et al., 2025), enabling pose-free inputs (Huang &
Mikolajczyk, 2025a;b; Ye et al., 2025; 2026), and adopting self-supervised training schemes (Zhao
et al., 2026; Huang et al., 2026). Despite these advances, feed-forward regression-based methods
lack the ability to model uncertainty, often producing blurry reconstructions or failures in occluded
and unseen regions.
2.2
3D GENERATION
A line of work learns generative models directly in native 3D representations, including point
clouds (Nichol et al., 2022), voxel grids (M¨uller et al., 2023), triplanes (Shue et al., 2023), and
3D Gaussians (Zhang et al., 2024a), or in structured 3D latent spaces (Lin et al., 2025a; Xiang
et al., 2025). These methods model 3D distributions directly, but typically rely on large-scale 3D
supervision and are mostly developed for bounded object-centric domains.
To exploit 2D generative priors, many methods follow generation then reconstruction (Fig. 1b): they
first synthesize novel views using image or video diffusion (Chan et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023a; Gao
et al., 2024; Hollein et al., 2024; Long et al., 2024; Sargent et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024; Wu et al.,
2024; He et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2025b;a; Liu et al., 2026), and then recover 3D
through per-scene optimization (Mildenhall et al., 2021; Kerbl et al., 2023) or feed-forward recon-
struction (Hong et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024a; Zhang et al., 2024b; Szymanowicz
et al., 2025). Although effective, per-scene optimization is computationally expensive, while feed-
forward variants remain sensitive to inconsistencies and artifacts across generated views, which can
propagate into the reconstructed geometry. Another paradigm follows reconstruction then gener-
ation (Fig. 1c): an initial 3D representation is first reconstructed and then rendered to condition a
generative model (Chen et al., 2024b; Wewer et al., 2024; Stracquadanio et al., 2026). Such designs
generate novel views conditioned on a potentially imperfect 3D reconstruction and cannot directly
revise errors in the initial geometry.
Some approaches instead embed differentiable rendering into the generative process (Anciukevicius
et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2024b; Anciukevicius et al., 2024; Cai et al., 2025), but focus mainly on
synthetic object-centric scenarios and repeatedly process context observations during generation.
In contrast, PixGR targets real-world scene reconstruction and performs pixel-space flow matching
with a lightweight decoder conditioned on a cached 3D-aware scene latent, providing consistent
scene guidance across sampling steps while enabling efficient iterative 3D reconstruction.
2.3
DIFFUSION AND FLOW MATCHING
Diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021a) and flow matching (Albergo & Vanden-
Eijnden, 2023; Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023b) have emerged as fundamental frameworks in
modern generative modeling, both learning probability flows from a simple initial distribution to a
target data distribution. While latent-space methods now dominate high-resolution image genera-
tion (Rombach et al., 2022b; Peebles & Xie, 2023; Ma et al., 2024), early diffusion models operated
in pixel space with computationally expensive U-Net backbones (Ronneberger et al., 2015; Ho et al.,
2020; Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021; Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021; Song et al., 2021b). Recent works re-
visit pixel-space modeling with Vision Transformers (ViT) (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021; Chen et al.,
2025; 2026; Li & He, 2026; Yu et al., 2026; Lu et al., 2026); we are particularly inspired by JiT (Li
& He, 2026), which predicts denoised images that are hypothesized to lie on a low-dimensional
manifold (Zhu, 2005).

PixGR builds upon the flow-based paradigm (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2023; Lipman et al., 2023;
Liu et al., 2023b) and uses 3D Gaussians as the scene representation. We therefore briefly review the
necessary background on pixel-space flow matching, its multi-view extension, and the 3D Gaussian
representation before presenting our full method.

Generative Gaussian Decoder.
The decoder D maps observed context views and noisy target
views at timestep t to pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians. Following (Xu et al., 2024b; Zhang et al., 2024b),
each camera P j is encoded as a Pl¨ucker ray map. The corresponding image is concatenated channel-
wise with its Pl¨ucker ray map, resulting in a 9-channel posed image that provides per-pixel camera
conditioning. After patchification and linear projection, each target view is represented as a sequence
of patch tokens. The full set of target tokens is stacked as T ∈RVtgt×P ×d.
The scene latent S and target tokens T are concatenated along the view dimension to form a uni-
fied token sequence F ∈RV ×P ×d, where V = Vcxt + Vtgt. This sequence is processed by a
series of DiT blocks Peebles & Xie (2023) with AdaLN-Zero conditioning on timestep t. Follow-
ing Yu et al. (2026), we replace LayerNorm with RMSNorm Zhang & Sennrich (2019) and apply
2D RoPE Su et al. (2024) in all attention layers. Self-attention is performed globally over the entire
token sequence F , facilitating cross-view information aggregation and enforcing multi-view consis-
tency. The refined tokens F are projected to Gaussian parameters via a linear layer and unpatchified
to produce pixel-aligned Gaussians G. Specifically, we project the tokens to per-view Gaussian pa-
rameters and depth maps, with the depth maps lifted to 3D Gaussian centers using the corresponding
context and target camera parameters.
Overall, the architecture conditions lightweight global generative decoding with 3D-aware scene
features, and is trained end-to-end with Eq. (5) to produce cross-view-consistent 3D Gaussians while
denoising target views across sampling steps.

EXPERIMENTS
5.1
EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
Implementation Details. PixGR is implemented in PyTorch and trained on A100 GPUs at 256×256
resolution, using AdamW with linear warmup and cosine decay. The logit-normal noise schedule is
set to µt = −0.8, σt = 0.8, biasing training toward higher noise levels. The generative decoder is
12 DiT blocks with hidden dimension 768 and is trained from scratch. At inference, we use an Euler
sampler. Further details are provided in Sec. B.
Metrics. We employ standard novel view synthesis (NVS) metrics: PSNR, SSIM (Wang et al.,
2004), LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018), and FID (Heusel et al., 2017), capturing pixel-level accuracy,
structural similarity, perceptual quality, and distributional alignment, respectively.
Datasets.
We train and evaluate on RealEstate10K (RE10K) (Zhou et al., 2018) and outdoor
DL3DV (Ling et al., 2024), and further conduct out-of-domain evaluation on the outdoor ACID
dataset (Liu et al., 2021) in Sec. C.
Baselines. We compare against two categories of methods. (1) Feed-forward regression-based
3DGS methods, including the pose-required MVSplat (Chen et al., 2024a), DepthSplat (Xu et al.,
2025), and YoNoSplat (Ye et al., 2026), as well as the pose-free AnySplat (Jiang et al., 2025).
(2) Generative methods, including generation-then-reconstruction method Bolt3D (Szymanowicz
et al., 2025), and reconstruction-then-generation methods MVSplat360 (Chen et al., 2024b) and
latentSplat (Wewer et al., 2024), ReconSplat (Stracquadanio et al., 2026). Additional baseline com-
parisons are provided in Sec. C, while Sec. B summarizes the geometric and generative priors used
by each method and reports their parameter counts.
Target-view selection. Unless otherwise specified, PixGR takes three target keyframe viewpoints
as queries and generates a shared 3D Gaussian representation conditioned on the scene latent. We
render the resulting representation at all evaluation viewpoints for metric computation.

5.2.2
VIEW EXTRAPOLATION
We further evaluate 2-view RE10K and 4-view DL3DV on view extrapolation, where novel cameras
move beyond the observed range and the model must infer geometry and appearance that are weakly
or not at all constrained by the context views. For RE10K, we use its three evaluation poses as
targets. For DL3DV, we sample three target poses evenly across the extrapolation range and render
the resulting 3DGS at all 42 evaluation poses. As shown in Tab. 2, PixGR achieves large gains over
regression-based methods across all metrics, highlighting the difficulty of deterministic regression
in weakly observed regions. This is also reflected qualitatively in Fig. 4, where regression baselines
exhibit blurring and severe artifacts in unseen or under-constrained areas.
Although previous generative methods improve FID over regression baselines on RE10K, they still
struggle to synthesize high-quality extrapolated views. The decoupling between generation and re-
construction can introduce geometry errors and cross-view misalignment, while compressed latent-
space generation may lose fine details and cause hue shifts. As shown in Fig. 4, these methods
exhibit geometric errors and detail loss. In contrast, PixGR performs pixel-space generation directly
within the 3D reconstruction process, producing sharper, coherent novel views with finer details.

5.2.4
ABLATION STUDY
Encoder Design.
As shown in Tab. 3, removing VGGT initialization or replacing the VGGT-
based encoder with a randomly initialized ViT-L degrades all metrics, indicating that the generative
decoder benefits from a 3D-aware scene latent. Nevertheless, both variants still outperform most
baselines in Tabs. 1 and 2, demonstrating that the decoder remains effective across different encoder
choices and that our paradigm does not rely entirely on pretrained 3D priors.
Generative vs. Deterministic Decoding.
In Tab. 3, replacing the DiT decoder with a 12-block
deterministic regressor of comparable model size leads to a consistent performance drop under in-
terpolation and a substantially larger drop under extrapolation, where scene uncertainty is higher.
This widening gap under extrapolation supports our motivation that deterministic regression is lim-
ited when geometry and appearance are weakly constrained by the observations. Fig. 6 further
demonstrates that the generative decoder recovers sharper appearance and more coherent geometry
when context views have limited or no overlap.
Keyframe Selection.
We further study the number of target keyframes used to construct the shared
3D Gaussian representation. As shown in Fig. 7, using more keyframes consistently improves per-
formance, with larger gains under extrapolation where scene ambiguity is higher. Jointly denoising
target views at additional camera poses encourages the shared representation to cover a broader
viewing range and strengthens cross-view consistency in weakly observed regions. Most of the
improvement is obtained with two keyframes, while a third provides a further quality gain at an
additional latency of only 0.09 s. We therefore use three target keyframes by default.
6
CONCLUSION
We present PixGR, a novel framework for generative 3D reconstruction that combines a 3D-aware
scene encoder with a lightweight generative decoder. Despite using a lightweight decoder trained
without pretrained generative priors, PixGR achieves strong performance in both interpolation and
extrapolation, outperforming regression-based methods and remaining competitive with heavier
generative approaches. These results show that lightweight pixel-space generation provides an ef-
fective and scalable approach to modeling uncertainty in sparse-view 3D reconstruction.

MORE IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
Implementation Details.
PixGR is trained for 100,000 steps and an initial learning rate of 1e−4.
During training, target views are sampled from an extrapolated range of the context views, so that
the target views may lie outside or inside the context range. All cameras in one scene are expressed
relative to the first context view, and the camera poses are normalized to a fixed scale across all
scenes. For interpolation, we adopt the evaluation splits from Charatan et al. (2024); Xu et al. (2025)
for 2-view RE10K and ACID (Tab. 5), and the 6-view DL3DV benchmark. For extrapolation, we
adopt the extrapolated evaluation splits from latentSplat for RE10K, and remove the first and last
two views from the 6-view DL3DV benchmark to form a 4-view extrapolation setting. We use three
target keyframes for both interpolation and extrapolation and 10 sampling steps unless otherwise
specified. We sample 4 novel views for auxiliary supervision.
Architecture.
VGGT operates on images with patch size 14, while our decoder tokenizer pro-
cesses 256×256 images with patch size 8. To align the token counts between encoder and decoder,
we resize the conditioning images to 448× 448, so that VGGT outputs 1024 (32× 32) tokens per
view, matching the number of target tokens. The per-view tokens are then concatenated and passed
to the decoder to produce per-view pixel-aligned Gaussians. We adopt a lightweight DiT architec-
ture with a hidden dimension of 768, 12 attention heads, and 12 transformer blocks, comprising only
131M parameters, as reported in Tab. 4.

LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK
While PixGR achieves strong performance in both interpolation and extrapolation, it has several lim-
itations. First, PixGR employs a lightweight generative decoder trained from scratch, which is less
expressive than large pretrained generative models. This limits its ability to synthesize high-fidelity
18


Preprint
novel views in highly ambiguous scenarios, such as long-sequence generation from a single view.
Future work could explore incorporating pretrained generative priors while preserving efficient in-
ference. Second, the current 3D Gaussian representation is limited to static scenes and cannot model
dynamic changes such as motion or deformation. Extending this paradigm to dynamic scenes with
4D representations is a promising direction for future work.
