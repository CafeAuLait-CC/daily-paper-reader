Equal contribution. Project website: https://ripl.github.io/StackGen3D-web/ I. I NTRODUCTION 

Creative assembly often begins from an incomplete specifi-cation rather than a fully defined 3D target: a user may provide a sketch, silhouette, or other abstract description of what the final structure should look like [1], [2]. We study a concrete instance of this problem: given a fixed set of primitive blocks and three orthographic silhouettes, generate a physically stable 3D structure that matches the specified views. This setting presents two coupled challenges. First, silhouette-to-3D generation is inherently underdetermined. Many block-pose configurations can produce nearly identical projections, while only a subset avoid interpenetration and remain stable. The goal is therefore to model a multimodal distribution over physically feasible structures consistent with the visual specification. Second, learning such a distribution requires a diverse dataset of stable 3D structures paired with silhouettes. Obtaining this data is difficult: physical feasibility must be evaluated for each candidate structure, while repeat-edly searching and validating configurations with a physics simulator is expensive and difficult to scale. Simple procedural stacking heuristics provide stable examples, but cover only a limited portion of the space of possible structures. We propose StackGen3D to address these challenges. First, we construct a scalable dataset of physically stable structures. We train a physics-informed graph neural network (PI-GNN) over block support relations as a fast surrogate for stability evaluation, and use it to curate diverse stable structures that are paired with multiview silhouettes. Sec-ond, using this dataset, we train a conditional flow-matching model to capture the multimodal distribution of block poses conditioned on the block inventory and target silhouettes. At inference time, lightweight AABB-based Best-of-N selection or Remove-then-Rerun refinement reduces residual inter-block collisions without physics simulation. Our preliminary results support both stages of the approach. First, our data-generation pipeline provides a strong basis for scalable curation of physically stable structures. A PI-GNN trained only on simple structures transfers substan-tially better to complex structures than a parameter-matched MLP (92.66% vs. 46.86% accuracy), indicating that support-relation reasoning generalizes across structural complexity and can serve as an effective stability model. StackGen3D follows a two-stage structure: we first con-struct a large-scale dataset of physically stable structures, and then learn a conditional generative model from this dataset. 

A. Relational Stability and Dataset Curation 

Physical stability is inherently relational: whether a block remains stable depends on how it is supported by neighboring blocks and on the load transmitted through those supports. We therefore represent each structure as a directed support graph and use a PI-GNN [3], [4] to predict block-level stability and displacement. Nodes encode block geometry, pose, mass, and accumulated supported mass, while edges encode geometric support relations such as contact area and relative displacement. Message passing allows these local support interactions to propagate through the structure. The larger challenge is obtaining diverse stable structures for generative-model training. As shown in Fig. 1, we first train an initial PI-GNN Gsimple on balanced simple stacks generated by a COM-based heuristic. We then construct more complex candidates by randomly removing blocks from dense Filler3D structures and use MuJoCo [5] to obtain stability supervision. Combining these examples with the simple-stack data yields a predictor Gcurated , which efficiently filters candidate structures during dataset construction. We next ask whether a generative model can learn the distribution of stable 3D structure compatible with a silhouette specification. Because the mapping is inherently one-to-many, we use conditional flow matching rather than predicting a single deterministic pose configuration. A. PI-GNN Stability Prediction 

We first evaluate whether relational stability reasoning can support our data-generation pipeline. We compare Gsimple 

with a parameter-matched M LP simple trained on the same simple structures, and with Gcomplex , which uses the same PI-GNN architecture but is trained directly on complex struc-tures. The PI-GNN and MLP contain 261,576 and 261,383 parameters, respectively. Because 85.1% of blocks in the complex data are stable, we additionally report balanced accu-racy, BAcc = 12 (TPR + TNR) . Despite similar performance on simple structures, Gsimple transfers substantially better to complex structures than the parameter-matched MLP (92.66% vs. 46.86% accuracy), supporting the importance of explicitly modeling support relations. On the same complex test set, 

Gsimple approaches Gcomplex (92.66% vs. 97.58%), although the balanced-accuracy gap shows that direct complex-domain training remains beneficial for identifying unstable blocks. B. Structure Generation without Online Simulation 

We next evaluate whether the conditional flow model trained on the curated stable-structure dataset can generate structures that satisfy the target silhouettes while retaining useful physical regularities. We evaluate 10 folds with 100 samples per fold. Inference uses only AABB overlap, while PyBullet [8] is used afterward to evaluate physical stability. As shown qualitatively in Fig. 3, the generated structures closely follow the input silhouettes, while post-hoc simulation reveals the remaining physical failure modes. Quantitatively, a single sample already achieves 0.967 silhouette IoU (Tab. I), indicat-ing that the learned conditional distribution strongly captures the geometric specification. Best-of-10 improves stability by 

27 .1% over a single sample while leaving silhouette IoU essentially unchanged, showing that the learned distribution contains substantially more physically viable modes than are exposed by one sample. Remove-then-Rerun similarly im-proves stability with only a modest reduction in IoU. Beyond
