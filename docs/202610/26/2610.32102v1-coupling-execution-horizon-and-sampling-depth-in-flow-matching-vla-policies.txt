# Coupling Execution Horizon and Sampling Depth in Flow-Matching VLA Policies 

Jiabing Yang 1,2,5, Yuan Xu 1,2, Yixiang Chen 1,2, Qisen Ma 1,2, Peiyan Li 1,2, Kai Wang 1,2,Chenhang Cui 3, Tao Yu 1,2, Zichen Wen 4, Wen Ye 1,2, Bowen Fang 1,2, Ziheng He 1,2,Xiangnan Wu 1,2, Yingda Li 1,2, Yan Huang 1,2,5,†, Liang Wang 1,2

Abstract — Flow-matching Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models generate expressive continuous trajectories for robotic manipulation, yet balancing real-time responsiveness with execution stability remains a persistent challenge. Standard deployment workflows typically treat numerical solver depth and execution horizon as decoupled choices. The horizon amortizes inference latency, while solver depth is kept high to preserve integration fidelity. In this paper, we reveal a fundamental compute–execution coupling in flow-matching VLAs governed by a systematic sign reversal. While coarse numerical integration degrades precision under rapid replanning, it markedly improves task performance under extended open-loop commitment. Geometrically, numerical truncation contracts lateral action dispersion around an invariant trajectory centroid, serving as an implicit regularizer that suppresses compounding open-loop drift. Across LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0, and real-world Franka manipulation on representative VLA backbones including π0, π0.5, and GR00T, exploiting this joint coupling consistently achieves superior task success while drastically lowering inference latency, entirely training-free and without architectural modifications. These findings establish numerical sampling depth as an operational variance regulator, shifting its role from a static precision budget to an active deployment knob in robot learning. I. I NTRODUCTION 

Flow-matching Vision-Language-Action (VLA) poli-cies [1]–[5] generate continuous action trajectories [6], [7] by numerically integrating learned velocity fields from prior Gaussian noise [8]. Deployment typically fixes two operational parameters, namely the execution horizon s, the open-loop action prefix executed before re-querying the policy, and the sampling depth N , the numerical integration steps allocated per chunk (equaling the number of function evaluations under forward Euler). For example, standard benchmark protocols for π0 on LIBERO conventionally default to (s, N ) = (5 , 10) .Prevailing deployment practices treat s and N as indepen-dent choices. Receding-horizon frameworks [4], [9] sweep execution horizons to amortize perception latency while holding solver depth fixed. Conversely, policy acceleration methods [5], [10], [11] distill or truncate numerical ODE solvers primarily under standard, short horizons. However,

In this work, we systematically investigate this interaction across frozen VLA checkpoints. On LIBERO-Long with π0,the paired performance contrast between shallow ( N =2 ) and deep ( N =10 ) sampling exhibits a systematic sign reversal (Fig. 1). The reversal is consistent. While coarse solvers degrade task success under rapid replanning ( s=5 ), they outperform deep solvers under extended open-loop execution (s=20 ). Multi-seed matched evaluations show that the con-trast crosses zero between s=5 and s=10 , shifting toward shallow sampling as the horizon lengthens. Experiments on RoboTwin 2.0 replicate this behavior on π0.5 across bimanual tasks, while also highlighting boundary conditions governed by predictive horizons and baseline task capacity. To understand this mechanism, we examine generated action distributions under matched initial noise. Forward Euler truncation contracts trajectory dispersion around an invariant centroid. Under rapid replanning, wider dispersion aids task progress as feedback quickly catches deviations; under extended open-loop execution, however, that dispersion compounds into drift. Here, the contracted distribution of a shallow solver acts as an effective implicit regularizer. The mechanism is geometric. Diagnostic interventions corroborate this account. Scalar noise rescaling fails to recover the effect and distorts mean trajectories, while freezing sampling noise across replans eliminates the deep solver’s short-horizon advantage, which isolates redraw stochasticity as the primary driver. This coupling provides practical and conceptual impli-cations for robot learning. Practically, co-adjusting solver arXiv:2610.32102v1 [cs.RO] 23 Oct 2026 depth with execution horizon yields training-free Pareto improvements, improving task success while amortizing inference latency. Conceptually, it reframes numerical solver depth as an operational variance regulator that directly governs trajectory dispersion across the commitment window, offering a grounded perspective for compute–execution co-design (Figure 2). Action Chunking and Horizon Scheduling. Chunked visuomotor policies trade feedback frequency for tempo-ral smoothness [6], [7], underpinning representative flow-matching VLAs such as π0 [1], π0.5 [2], and GR00T [3]. To preserve reactivity, recent efforts introduce dynamic replan-ning mechanisms. Bidirectional decoding resamples candidate trajectories [9], reactive diffusion pairs slow planners with fast correction loops [12], and VLA-Corrector triggers replanning from latent visual cues [13]. Other frameworks score trajectory prefixes via evaluation models [14], monitor chunk staleness with world models [15], train across variable horizons [16], or overlap inference with asynchronous execution [4]. Yet across these strategies, numerical sampling depth is held fixed at the backbone’s default setting. 

Accelerated Sampling for Visuomotor Generation. Iterative numerical integration forms the primary latency bottleneck in diffusion and flow-matching policies [8], [17]–[20]. To accelerate inference, prevailing methods distill multi-step integration into few-step generators [5], [10], [11], [21], stream partially denoised latents [22], straighten velocity fields via MeanFlow [23], or adaptively truncate solver steps via error heuristics [24]. While these methods primarily compress sampling budgets to lower inference latency, our work studies how sampling depth dynamically interacts with the execution horizon. We find that numerical solver truncation acts as an effective implicit regularizer under extended open-loop execution. 

Joint Computational and Execution Scheduling. Execution horizon and solver depth have traditionally been optimized in isolation. Reactive controllers tune replanning rates assuming fixed latency [4], [12], while accelerated samplers compress per-query iterations under fixed commitments [5], [10]. Recent work begins to couple compute and control dynamically: EQRL [25] trains an RL meta-controller to adapt denoising steps, DVAC [26] monitors sampling variance to trigger early execution, and FASTER [27] distributes compute non-uniformly across action tokens. While these frameworks design learned meta-controllers or variance monitors to allocate computational resources, our work demonstrates on frozen, unmodified backbones that numerical truncation naturally regularizes trajectory variance, offering a training-free foundation for compute–execution co-design. C. Coupling Mechanism: Dispersion Contraction vs. Open-Loop Drift 

As schematized in Figure 2, standard deployment work-flows typically decouple N and s as independent engineering choices. In physical reality, however, they are intrinsically coupled: N governs how much prior stochastic dispersion survives into the generated chunk, while s determines how long the robot commits open loop to that realization before closed-loop feedback intervenes. During reverse probability flow integration from τ =1 

to τ =0 , the learned velocity field initially contracts prior noise toward the conditional mean trajectory ( τ ≈ 1). Distinctions among plausible trajectory modes are resolved late in the flow ( τ → 0), where probability density re-expands along feasible branches. A truncated coarse solver concentrates its evaluations in the initial contraction phase, capturing the conditional centroid while truncating late-stage lateral dispersion. Contraction in isolation neither helps nor hinders task completion; the execution horizon s determines its opera-tional value. Under frequent replanning ( s=5 ), every query redraws the initial noise, and sensory feedback arrives before deviations compound. Here, diverse trajectory samples across redraws allow visual feedback to steer the robot around obstacles, while deep integration ( N =10 ) supplies this dispersion. Under extended open-loop execution ( s=20 ), the robot follows one draw for twenty steps without guidance. Here, wide dispersion compounds into physical drift, and coarse integration ( N =2 ) stabilizes rollouts by removing excess variance while preserving the central path. This produces the sign reversal; reducing N penalizes frequent replanning yet improves extended open-loop execution. The trade-off is direct.

Task Geometry. Task geometry strongly governs operational horizon sensitivity across both LIBERO and RoboTwin benchmarks. We categorize manipulation tasks along acontinuum of physical spatial tolerance. Coarse transport tasks (e.g., placing both moka pots on the stove, possessing an 8 cm basin of attraction) exhibit wide geometric tolerance. In these tasks, extended open-loop commitment eliminates boundary chattering and suppresses high-frequency overshoot, surging success from 18% to 82% under (s=20 , N =2) . Conversely, narrow-funnel insertion tasks with millimeter-scale clearances (e.g., peg insertion or micro-switch activation) require frequent sensory relocalization to ensure alignment against unexpected physical contact. In such contact-rich regimes, extended open-loop rollout allows slight angular misalignments to accumulate beyond the insertion funnel threshold, requiring rapid replanning ( s ≤ 5) with deep integration to recover from collisions. This geometric taxonomy reveals that the advantage of shallow long-horizon commitment scales inversely with task constraint, providing practitioners with a clear heuristic to allocate extended open-loop horizons to gross transport phases while reserving reactive high-frequency replanning for tight contact interactions. 

C. Real-World Evaluation 

Real-World Setup. We evaluate real-robot manipulation on a 7-DoF Franka Research 3 manipulator with a parallel-jaw gripper, controlled at 25 Hz via Cartesian impedance targets. Observations comprise an overhead ZED 2i RGB camera and joint proprioception. We evaluate five diverse tasks (Figure 5): Put Corn on Pink Plate , Press Yellow Button, then Blue , Cover Blue Block with Box , Lift Corn onto Upper Rack , and Move Tomato from Turntable to Plate . For each task, we collect 50 SpaceMouse teleoperation demonstrations (250 demonstrations, 102 ,416 frames total; longest episode 

22 s) under randomized object layouts. Policies are fine-tuned under standardized recipes ( π0 and π0.5 for 30k steps, GR00T-N1.7 for 10k steps). We test four factorial configurations ((s=10 , N 0), (s=10 , 2) , (s=H, N 0), (s=H, 2) ) across 40 matched physical trials per cell, totaling 2,400 hardware rollouts. 

Results. Figure 5 summarizes physical manipulation out-comes across all three foundation models. In the four quasi-static tasks, the empirical sign reversal replicates unanimously. Under rapid replanning ( s=10 ), shallow sampling ( N =2 )incurs a modest drop across π0, π0.5, and GR00T-N1.7 due to reduced reactive diversity. Under full-chunk com-mitment ( s=H), however, shallow sampling systematically outperforms deep reference solvers, achieving absolute gains of +10 .0% on π0 (90 .0% on Cover Block ), +6 .9% on 

π0.5 (97 .5% on Lift Corn ), and +11 .3% on GR00T-N1.7. On physical hardware, early Euler truncation suppresses compound lateral drift, preventing end-effector excursions outside the grasp envelope. Conversely, the dynamic turntable task (5 RPM, v ≈ 8 cm/s) marks the operational boundary. on π0 reduces compute burden from 1.0 to 0.04 NFE per step (25 × reduction), eliminating edge GPU thermal throttling. V. C ONCLUSION 

We investigate the coupled deployment dynamics between numerical sampling depth and receding-horizon execution in flow-matching Vision-Language-Action policies. While standard workflows treat solver acceleration and action chunking as independent choices, early Euler truncation operates as an intrinsic variance regularizer: it contracts stochastic dispersion along the probability flow while pre-serving trajectory centroids. Consequently, solver truncation reverses across execution regimes. Shallow sampling penalizes high-frequency reactive control by discarding exploratory diversity, yet enhances extended open-loop execution by suppressing compounding lateral drift. Across 55 simulation tasks and 2,400 physical trials, co-designing shallow sampling with extended commitment yields up to 5.6× inference speedups while improving manipulation success.
