# iGPC: Generative Motion Priors for Object-Aware Humanoid Interaction 

Anujith Muraleedharan 1, Abdul Ahad Butt 1, Nolan Fey 2, Yash Prabhu 2,Anamika J H 1, Sandor Felber 3, Maurice Rahme 3, Ivan Laptev 1

Abstract — Humanoid robots operating in unstructured en-vironments must combine robust whole-body control with the ability to perceive and physically interact with surrounding ob-jects. While large-scale human motion data provides powerful priors for natural and versatile humanoid control, effectively transferring such priors to perception-driven object interaction remains challenging. To address this bottleneck, we propose a framework that extends the recently proposed Generative Pretrained Controller (GPC) from general human motion to full-body humanoid–environment interaction. First, we adapt GPC into interaction experts conditioned on scene affordance cues and privileged state information. These experts leverage the pretrained human motion prior while learning task-specific contact behaviors, including reaching toward objects, grasping environmental supports for stabilization, and pushing movable objects. Second, we introduce a perception-driven student that retains the pretrained GPC policy and distills interaction skills from the experts using onboard sensory observations. To bridge the gap between privileged expert observations and sensory inputs, we propose two complementary training objectives that enable effective adaptation of the pretrained motion prior during distillation. Notably, our experiments across multiple whole-body interaction tasks demonstrate that large-scale gen-erative human motion priors provide an effective foundation for learning deployable policies for humanoid interactions in contact-rich real-world environments. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Recent advances in humanoid control have demonstrated that large-scale human motion data can provide powerful priors for learning natural and versatile whole-body behav-iors. Motion-tracking approaches such as ASAP and Beyond-Mimic transfer agile human motions to humanoid robots [1], [2], while generalist controllers such as GMT and SONIC scale this paradigm to increasingly diverse motion repertoires and command modalities [3], [4]. More recently, generative approaches have gone beyond motion replay by learning reusable distributions over motor behaviors. In particular, GPC [5] learns an autoregressive generative motor prior from large-scale motion data and adapts this prior to downstream control tasks. These results suggest an appealing alternative to learning individual humanoid skills from scratch: pretrain a general motor prior once and adapt it to increasingly complex downstream behaviors. A key challenge, however, is extending such motion priors from free-space whole-body control to physical interaction with the environment. Reaching for an object, grasping a

Human motion priors provide useful knowledge about coordinated whole-body movement, but do not by themselves tell the robot where and how to interact with a particular scene .Recent approaches address perceptive humanoid con-trol through terrain-conditioned motion tracking [6], [7], reference-conditioned object interaction [8], [9], or perception-based loco-manipulation [10], [11], [12]. Nev-ertheless, it remains unclear how a generative motor prior learned from large-scale human motion can be systematically adapted to object-aware interaction and subsequently retained when the controller must operate from onboard sensing rather than privileged state information. We address this problem with iGPC , a framework that extends GPC from generative motion control to object-aware, perception-driven humanoid interaction . First, we develop an improved interaction expert that conditions the GPC motion prior on scene affordances describing object geometry, contact, and robot stability. We initialize adap-tation using human interaction motions aligned with scene geometry and subsequently optimize task-specific behaviors with reinforcement learning. The resulting experts exploit large-scale human motion priors while learning task-specific interactions, including reaching, grasping environmental sup-ports for stabilization, and pushing movable objects, without requiring reference motions at execution time. To enable real-world deployment, we propose a perceptive student that retains the same GPC motion prior rather than replacing it with a separately learned action policy. The student uses onboard depth, LiDAR, and proprioception arXiv:2610.08120v1 [cs.RO] 6 Oct 2026 to estimate the interaction and body-state context required by GPC. We introduce distillation objectives that supervise both autoregressive motor-token decisions and resulting joint targets, together with prior-input calibration and task-adapter refinement. Consequently, the large-scale human motion prior benefits both the experts and perceptive controllers. Experiments demonstrate strong improvements at both the expert and student levels. For object-aware reaching, iGPC achieves 99.32% contact success, compared with 31.30% for GPC, while the sensor-based student retains 95.95% success and substantially reduces body collisions. The approach also improves hand-supported recovery from strong disturbances and sustained box pushing, where both the expert and student substantially outperform VisualMimic [13]. Together, these results show that generative human motion priors provide an effective foundation for both privileged interaction learning and perception-driven humanoid control. In summary, our main contributions are threefold:  

> •

Object-aware interaction experts. We extend GPC with scene affordance, contact, and stability condi-tioning, enabling motion-prior-based reaching, support-assisted stabilization, and object manipulation without reference motions at execution time.  

> •

Motion-prior-based perceptive control. We introduce a multimodal distillation framework that transfers priv-ileged experts to onboard depth, LiDAR, and proprio-ception while retaining the pretrained GPC motor prior and decoder.  

> •

Extensive experimental validation. We demonstrate strong performance of iGPC across reaching, stabiliza-tion, and box pushing, with ablations validating our expert and student designs and comparisons showing substantial improvements over the baselines. II. R ELATED WORK 

Reusable motor representations. Motion priors provide coordinated behavior that can be reused for new objec-tives. B. Student Distillation and Sensor Transfer 

The student retains the expert’s motor prior and decoder and learns a sensor-based input interface. Transfer proceeds in three steps. First, observation reconstruction, motor-token matching, and decoded-action matching train only the esti-mator Fϑ through the frozen task-adapted controller. Second, with the estimator fixed, a bounded residual ∆η calibrates the inputs used for token selection without modifying the body-state estimate used for decoding. Third, task reinforcement learning updates the student adapter and a training-only critic while keeping the estimator, calibration map, motor backbone, decoder, and sampling reference fixed.

A. Experimental Setup 

Implementation details. We use a Unitree G1 humanoid with a 29-DoF learned body controller operating at 50 Hz with joint-level PD control. Simulation is performed in Isaac Lab at 200 Hz with a control decimation of four. For rail-grasping experiments, Dex3 hands provide an additional 14 actuated finger joints controlled separately from the learned whole-body policy. In simulation, finger closure is triggered when the selected palm establishes rail contact above 1 N, excluding contact with the rail underside. The finger controller uses simulator-provided palm and finger contact forces, together with hand–rail relative motion, to regulate capture and holding. Excessive contact force causes incremental opening, and episode reset clears the grasp state. Sensor-based students use 96 × 64 depth images, 16 × 180 

LiDAR rasters, 25 frames of proprioceptive history, and onboard state estimates to reconstruct the expert inputs defined in Sec. III. 

Pre-Training. Stage-3 motion–scene adaptation uses 1,024 clips, totaling approximately 2.24 hours, selected from the Unitree G1-retargeted motions in BONES-SEED [22]. It con-tains 512 interaction clips spanning wall support, pushing, vaulting, and ground rising, together with 512 walking clips. Interaction pre-training uses this selected motion collection to adapt the motor prior through frozen-tracker token super-vision. Affordance pre-training pairs each clip with a support scene as described in Sec. IV-A. We use a temporal fall-back when annotations are unavailable. Walking examples

C. Perceptive Student Policy 

We evaluate transfer to onboard sensing while retaining the pretrained GPC motor prior. We compare the privileged expert, a direct-action MLP student, and iGPC variants that ablate observation supervision, token supervision, or prior-input calibration. The MLP and no-calibration students use the same sensor history as full iGPC and also undergo task-level PPO refinement. The no-calibration variant retains full-loss distillation but omits the learned prior-input residual. All students execute autonomously from sensor observations. Here, teacher-target MAE measures the mean absolute difference between student and frozen-expert joint-position targets after action processing, in radians. We average over 29 joints and 64 uniformly spaced samples per trial. The same frozen expert labels states visited by each student; this is an on-policy imitation diagnostic, not an action compar-ison on identical state sequences. As shown in Table III, the full iGPC student achieves 95.95% contact success, retaining most of the expert’s 99.32% performance while outperforming the expert in terms of body collisions. In contrast, distillation of an MLP student that preserves no motion prior achieves only 46.53% success. When ablating the design choices of our iGPC student, we observe that removing both observation and token supervision reduces success to 25.39%, while removing either component in-dividually yields 90.14–93.41%. Prior calibration further improves success from 91.02% to 95.95% and reduces the 90th-percentile endpoint error from 13.53 cm to 5.62 cm.

D. Whole-Body Interaction Experiments 

Reactive stabilization. The humanoid receives two external pushes while a rail is available for hand support. The policy must reactively reach and grasp the rail to maintain balance, without a prescribed reaching motion. We compare the privileged iGPC expert, sensor-based iGPC student, and leg-only GPC baseline using 512 matched trials in each push-induced velocity-change band: 1.5–2.0, 2.0–2.5, and 2.5– 3.0 m/s. A physical fall is defined as knee, pelvis, torso, or head contact with the terrain above 10 N for three consecutive control frames. Table IV(a) reports physical falls as counts and per-centages. At 2.0–2.5 m/s, leg-only GPC falls in 18.36% of trials, compared with 4.69% for the expert and 4.88% for the student. At 2.5–3.0 m/s, the student’s fall rate is 17.19%, compared with 40.43% for leg-only GPC. These results support the benefit of environmental hand support for reactive stabilization. E. Deployment to the Real Robot 

The robot Unitree G1 receives depth images from an Intel RealSense D435i camera at 30 Hz and LiDAR measurements from a Livox MID-360 at 10 Hz. The sensor-reading loop op-erates at 50 Hz. Proprioceptive inputs include joint positions and velocities, estimated motor torques, IMU-derived orien-tation and angular velocity, previous joint-position targets, joint-tracking errors, and their history. Policy inference runs on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU, taking approximately 18 ms per update during hardware execution. The controller operates continuously in closed loop, sustaining a measured wall-clock policy-update rate above 50 Hz. The resulting joint-position targets are sent to the robot’s low-level proportional–derivative controllers. For commanded reaching, the hand-selection condition directs the robot to establish rail contact with the requested hand. Fig. 5 compares example executions of the direct-action MLP and iGPC student. In box pushing, the robot es-tablishes contact, sustains pushing for 10 s, and subsequently releases contact. For reactive stabilization, the robot grasps the rail in response to an external push, while remaining in stable standing when no disturbance is applied. Additionally, the robot walks to a sofa and uses hand support to slow its descent into sitting and to rise back to standing. VI. C ONCLUSION 

We presented iGPC for object-aware humanoid interaction and transfer to onboard sensing, retaining the pretrained motor backbone and decoder. Real world deployment results support iGPC across the evaluated reaching, stabilization, and box-pushing tasks. Broader evaluation of generalization and contact safety remains future work.
