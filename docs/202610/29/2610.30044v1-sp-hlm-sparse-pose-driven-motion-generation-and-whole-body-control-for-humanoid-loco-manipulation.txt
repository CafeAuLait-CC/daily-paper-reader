Abstract— Humanoid loco-manipulation requires an effec-
tive interface between high-level task policies and whole-
body control. Existing interfaces typically rely on a single
action representation for both task intent and whole-body
coordination. This may burden the high-level policy or leave
one aspect underspecified. We present SP-HLM, a framework
built on a sparse-pose-driven interface. The interface combines
five task-critical body poses with a learned latent motion
prior to represent spatial intent and whole-body coordination
in complementary forms. SP-VLA, a vision-language-action
model, first predicts task-relevant sparse poses and finger
joint positions from multimodal observations and language
instructions using asynchronous action-chunk inference. Given
these sparse targets, SP-MGM, a motion generation model
trained through two-stage distillation, reconstructs coordinated
full-body motion in real time. A pretrained motion encoder
maps this motion to a compact latent representation. SP-GMT,
a general motion tracker, then integrates the explicit sparse
targets with this implicit motion prior for whole-body control.
Experiments in simulation and on a real humanoid robot
demonstrate improved motion tracking, whole-body coordina-
tion, and loco-manipulation success over existing approaches.
Additional results and videos are available on our project page.
I. INTRODUCTION
The motion interface between high-level task policies
and whole-body control is an important design choice in
humanoid loco-manipulation [1], [2], [3]. Existing interfaces
emphasize different aspects of humanoid motion. Joint-
space actions [4], [5], [6] directly specify joint config-
urations, but can require the high-level policy to model
whole-body coordination alongside task intent. Task-space
actions provide a compact, task-grounded representation, yet
sparse targets alone do not fully specify coordinated whole-
body motion. Token-space actions [7] can encode reusable
coordination patterns, while task-critical spatial targets may
arXiv:2610.30044v1  [cs.RO]  21 Oct 2026


remain implicit. These trade-offs motivate an interface that
combines representations of spatial intent and whole-body
coordination, rather than encoding both in a single form.
We argue that task-critical spatial intent and whole-body
coordination should instead be represented in complemen-
tary forms. Spatial intent remains explicit, specifying where
task-relevant body parts should move, whereas whole-body
coordination is encoded implicitly as a learned motion
prior describing how the body should move. Following this
principle, we expose only five task-critical body poses—
the pelvis, two wrists, and two ankles—as explicit spatial
constraints and complement them with a latent whole-body
motion representation. This interface preserves direct task-
space control while avoiding the need for the high-level
policy to explicitly specify full-body coordination.
Following this formulation, we present SP-HLM, a hu-
manoid loco-manipulation framework built on a sparse-pose-
driven interface. At its core, SP-GMT performs whole-
body control by jointly conditioning on sparse task-space
targets and a learned motion latent. We pretrain a motion
variational autoencoder (VAE) to encode full-body motion
into a compact latent representation. This latent provides
coordination information not fully specified by the sparse
targets while enabling efficient policy training.
To obtain this implicit motion prior from sparse task-
space targets at deployment, we introduce SP-MGM, which
reconstructs coordinated full-body motion from five-point
pose targets in real time. Starting from a pretrained motion
generation model, SP-MGM reduces network depth and de-
noising steps and employs two-stage distillation to improve
sparse-pose adherence and temporal consistency.
The proposed interface also reduces the burden of high-
level action prediction. SP-VLA predicts only task-relevant
sparse body poses and finger joint positions from multi-
modal observations and language instructions, without di-
rectly predicting full-body configurations. We further adopt
asynchronous action-chunk inference for responsive closed-
loop execution. Together, SP-VLA specifies task-space in-
tent, SP-MGM recovers coordinated whole-body motion, and
SP-GMT integrates the explicit sparse targets and implicit
motion prior for whole-body control.
We separately evaluate SP-GMT, SP-MGM, and the
complete SP-HLM system. SP-GMT achieves competitive
motion-tracking performance against representative state-
of-the-art baselines. SP-MGM improves sparse-pose adher-
ence over the motion-generation baseline while achieving
a 1.45× higher generation rate. For loco-manipulation, SP-
HLM achieves a 52.75% overall success rate across eight
simulated tasks, outperforming the token-space and joint-
space baselines. We further validate SP-HLM on four real-
world tasks, demonstrating its feasibility for humanoid loco-
manipulation. Our main contributions are:
• We propose a sparse-pose-driven motion interface
for humanoid loco-manipulation, combining five task-
critical body poses with a learned motion prior to
represent spatial intent and whole-body coordination in
complementary forms.
• We develop SP-GMT, a whole-body tracker condi-
tioned jointly on sparse targets and a pretrained mo-
tion representation, and SP-MGM, a real-time sparse-
conditioned motion generator trained through two-stage
distillation.
• We present SP-HLM, a humanoid loco-manipulation
framework that integrates the above two components
with SP-VLA, which uses asynchronous action-chunk
inference to predict task-relevant sparse poses and fin-
ger joint-position targets from multimodal inputs. The
resulting system achieves higher overall success than
the evaluated baselines across eight simulated tasks and
is further validated on four real-world tasks.
II. RELATED WORK
A. General Humanoid Motion Generation and Control
Recent work increasingly uses motion generation as an
intermediate layer between high-level semantic commands
and low-level humanoid control, typically producing dense
whole-body references for downstream tracking [8], [9],
[10]. Some methods formulate motion synthesis as an in-
betweening problem [11], whereas others employ generative
models as online motion planners [12], [13]. Although these
approaches demonstrate the effectiveness of generative mo-
tion priors for hierarchical control, they largely rely on dense
motion references rather than explicit task-space interfaces
tailored to loco-manipulation.
In parallel, general motion tracking has emerged as a
scalable approach to acquiring versatile whole-body control
from large and diverse motion corpora [7], [14], [15], [16].
Recent studies improve generalization by scaling motion
data and model capacity [17], [18], [19], [20], while others
leverage heterogeneous motion sources or expert specializa-
tion [21], [22], [23]. Related efforts learn reusable behavioral
representations that can be transferred to downstream control
tasks [24], [25]. Together, these advances provide increas-
ingly general and robust motion generation and control
capabilities, while the interface for conveying task-relevant
whole-body intent from high-level loco-manipulation poli-
cies remains comparatively less explored.
B. Motion Interfaces for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation
Recent humanoid loco-manipulation systems increas-
ingly pursue unified whole-body behaviors through vision-
language-action models (VLAs) [1], [5], [4], [26], [27],
world-action models [28], [29], and perceptive reinforcement
policies [2], [30]. Beyond the choice of policy architecture,
an important design consideration is the motion interface
through which task-level intent is conveyed to whole-body
execution. Existing approaches span decoupled upper- and
lower-body control [5], [1] and explicit whole-body kine-
matic representations [31], [4]. While these interfaces pro-
vide direct control over robot motion, they can require the
high-level policy to reason over relatively detailed body
configurations. Other methods adopt more compact learned
motion representations, such as motion tokens [28], [32],


which capture reusable coordination patterns but make task-
relevant spatial constraints less explicit.
Recent work also explores compact task-space interfaces
based on sparse body targets or low-rate task-space trajec-
tories, leaving the remaining whole-body coordination to
downstream controllers [33], [34], [35]. In contrast, SP-HLM
combines explicit task-space targets with a learned whole-
body motion prior, providing a compact interface between
task-level intent and coordinated whole-body execution.
III. METHOD
A. SP-HLM Overview
As illustrated in Fig. 2, SP-HLM connects high-level
loco-manipulation policies to low-level whole-body control
through a sparse-pose-driven interface. The sparse poses
specify task-relevant spatial constraints on five body parts
in the world frame: the pelvis, both wrists, and both ankles.
Each pose comprises a 3-D position and a 6-D rotation.1
In addition, a full-body motion prior generated from these
poses provides whole-body coordination consistent with the
specified task geometry. An explicit–implicit tracking policy
then integrates the sparse-pose constraints with the latent
motion prior to enable humanoid loco-manipulation.
Specifically, at each planning step, SP-VLA predicts a
sequence of five-point target poses together with the fin-
ger joints of both hands. SP-MGM then generates a full-
body motion trajectory conditioned on these targets, and a
pretrained motion encoder maps the trajectory to a latent
command. SP-GMT combines this latent command with
the sparse targets expressed in the current pelvis frame to
produce joint position commands. The finger targets are
passed directly to the hand PD controllers. Formally,

IV. EXPERIMENTS
Through our experiments, we aim to answer the following
three research questions:
• RQ1: Does the proposed explicit–implicit tracking pol-
icy improve motion-tracking performance, and how
does it compare with SOTA general motion trackers?
• RQ2: Can the proposed two-stage distillation strategy
accelerate motion generation while preserving accurate
adherence to sparse end-effector constraints?
• RQ3: Does our sparse-pose-driven interface provide
higher task success than joint-based and token-based
interfaces across diverse loco-manipulation tasks, and


how well does the complete system transfer to real-
world humanoid deployment?
A. Motion Tracking Performance
We evaluate tracking on two 500-motion datasets: Filtered
CLOT-500 [39] and BONES-SEED Diverse-500 [36]. Each
dataset is evaluated over ten seeded runs, where perturbations
are applied to the reference trajectories. Both are unseen
during SP-GMT training, while BONES-SEED Diverse-500
is included in SONIC’s training data.
1) Comparison
with
Motion-Tracking
Baselines:
As
shown in Table II, among the compared methods, SP-GMT
achieves the lowest global MPJPE and global EE error on
both datasets. For local tracking, it ranks second in five
of the six dataset–metric comparisons across local MPJPE,
joint error, and local EE error. Notably, SP-GMT is trained
on a substantially smaller motion corpus with only 192
GPU-hours, compared with 21k GPU-hours for SONIC and
15k GPU-hours for Humanoid-GPT. These results indicate
competitive local motion fidelity and strong global trajectory
tracking with substantially less data and training compute.
2) Ablation Study of SP-GMT: The ablations suggest that
the explicit and implicit references provide complementary
information. Using only the latent reference (w/o Sparse
Reference) leads to substantially larger global tracking er-
rors, while using only the five-point reference (w/o Implicit
Reference) degrades local tracking and joint accuracy. Re-
placing the latent reference with a joint-space reference also
increases global tracking error. Combining sparse spatial
targets with the learned latent reference in SP-GMT yields
the lowest global MPJPE and EE error on both benchmarks
while remaining competitive in local tracking, demonstrating
the benefit of the explicit–implicit representation.
Removing the robot pelvis pose causes only a moder-
ate performance drop compared with the full model. This
suggests that SP-GMT is not strongly dependent on precise
pelvis localization.
B. Motion Generation Quality and Efficiency
We select 600 motions from BONES-SEED [36], covering
diverse motion dynamics and spatial extent. Sparse-pose
adherence is measured by EE error, and motion smoothness
by EE jerk and body jerk. Lower error and jerk values
indicate better motion generation quality, while a higher
generation rate indicates greater efficiency. All results are
summarized in Table III.
1) Comparison with Motion-Generator Baseline: Under
the same 20-step denoising setting, we compare SP-MGM
against the uncompressed Kimodo baseline. Despite modest
increases in EE jerk and body jerk, SP-MGM achieves lower
mean and P95 EE errors. Notably, its generation rate reaches
up to 1.45× that of Kimodo. These results demonstrate that
our two-stage distillation strategy enables more accurate and
efficient motion generation under sparse-pose constraints.
2) Ablation study of SP-MGM: Full SP-MGM achieves
the best performance across all metrics among the compared
SP-MGM variants. With Stage I alone (w/o Stage II), SP-
MGM exhibits larger EE errors and higher body jerk, with
P95 EE error increasing from 3.212 to 12.772 cm. These re-
sults indicate that Stage I establishes basic motion generation
capability, while Stage II substantially improves adherence
to the sparse-pose targets and temporal smoothness. Without
Ltemp, EE accuracy remains close to that of full SP-MGM,
but EE and body jerk P95 increase by 27.8% and 37.0%,
respectively. Temporal regularization thus suppresses high-
frequency jitter while also slightly reducing EE errors.

task using SONIC, whereas SP-HLM employs SP-GMT for

evaluates whether the sparse-pose-driven interface preserves
task intent across different whole-body trackers.

V. CONCLUSIONS
We presented SP-HLM, a humanoid loco-manipulation
framework built on a sparse-pose-driven interface that inte-
grates explicit sparse-pose constraints with an implicit full-
body motion prior. Three components realize this interface:
SP-VLA predicts five-point poses and hand commands, SP-
MGM reconstructs coordinated full-body motion, and SP-
GMT integrates the explicit and implicit commands for


whole-body control. Experiments on motion tracking, mo-
tion generation, and loco-manipulation in both simulation
and the real world demonstrate the effectiveness of the
proposed interface and the complete framework.
However, this study focuses primarily on whole-body
coordination. Hand control is specified solely through finger
joint-position targets, and we do not explore alternative
hand-level interfaces or dexterous manipulation. Extending
the proposed interface to support whole-body dexterous
manipulation is an important direction for future work.

C. SP-VLA Training and Inference Details
The SP-VLA policy is fine-tuned from GR00T N1.7 for
160,000 optimization steps with a global batch size of 32 and
a learning rate of 1×10−4. It predicts 40-step action chunks
using a 59-D representation comprising 14 hand-joint values,
a 9-D root pose, and four 9-D end-effector poses. Prefix-
conditioned RTC is enabled during training, with delays
randomly sampled from 0 to 8 steps. During inference, the
first 34 actions are executed and the final 6 actions are
retained as the clean prefix for the next prediction, forming
a rolling-horizon scheme. Action generation uses four flow-
matching inference steps.
