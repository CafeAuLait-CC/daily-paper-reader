Recent multi-view Transformers (e.g., DUSt3R and VGGT) have advanced 3D reconstruction
by scaling training data and model capacity from two views to many views and long sequences,
subsuming the classical SfM/SLAM pipeline of feature extraction, matching, and pose estimation
in a single feed-forward pass.
Yet this subsumption hides the interfaces between pipeline
stages—correspondences, geometry, and pose are entangled in a single forward pass, leaving
supervised fine-tuning as the major path to improvement and offering no mechanism to
correct predictions from geometric evidence at test time. We propose PosePlaner to close
this gap by reviving the classical coupling between correspondences and pose via flow matching,
treating pose refinement as a conditional generation problem that takes coarse geometric
matches and initial pose as input condition and fuses the resulting pose hypotheses into a single
refined estimate, providing a continuous, data-driven analog of RANSAC voting. Our method
is trained efficiently on two-view data, yet serves as a periodic refiner for streaming n-view
pose estimators with no retraining. Empirically, it corrects failures of feed-forward predictors,
substantially improves correspondence-based solvers, and reduces accumulated drift in long
streaming sequences. Project page: https://robbyant-research.github.io/PosePlaner/.

PosePlaner: Denoising Any Feed-Forward Pose Predictor
1. Introduction
Recovering relative camera pose between views of a scene is a foundational primitive in 3-D vision
and the entry point of structure-from-motion (Schönberger & Frahm, 2016), multi-view stereo (Yao
et al., 2018), SLAM (Mur-Artal et al., 2015), and novel-view synthesis (Mildenhall et al., 2022; Kerbl
et al., 2023). The problem is hard at both ends: in the two-view setting under limited baselines and
outlier-prone correspondences, and across long streaming sequences where errors accumulate frame by
frame. Every pose failure propagates into the downstream pipeline.
Two prominent paradigms in recent work reach this problem from opposite directions. Feed-forward pose
predictors (Wang et al., 2025a; 2024) map an image pair directly to (R, t) via visual priors, but their
prediction is terminal: downstream correspondences cannot refine it. Matcher-plus-solver pipelines (Sarlin
et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2021; Edstedt et al., 2024; Leroy et al., 2024) hand rich correspondences to a
five-point solver (Nistér, 2004) wrapped in RANSAC (Fischler & Bolles, 1981), but the matcher’s priors
collapse into per-pair inlier / outlier decisions and the RANSAC threshold tuning seldom transfers across
scenes. Both paradigms break the match-pose coupling: visual priors and per-match geometric evidence
never reach the same differentiable channel.
In this paper, we bridge the gap between these two paradigms by reintroducing visual correspondences
into feed-forward 3D reconstruction. Analogous to the algebraic solvers that seed the classical SfM
pipeline (Schönberger & Frahm, 2016), we treat feed-forward models as pose initializers and refine their
output as a learned geometric correction step. Our key insight is that 3D surface points paired with
their normals already encode a cross-view geometric constraint: under the true relative pose, matched
patch-normal pairs must satisfy an algebraic warping identity, and departures from it yield differentiable
residuals that grow with pose error. Planar primitives realize this constraint compactly—one 3-D center
and one normal per image patch. We learn to denoise the pose residual via flow matching, conditioned
on the initialized pose and matched patch-normal pairs, yielding a refiner that generalizes from two-view
training to N-view inference without retraining.
We introduce PosePlaner (Fig. 1), a pose refiner that accepts any initial pose Tinit and returns a
refined relative pose given patch-normal matching pairs. Building on Plana3r (Liu et al., 2025a), we use
planar primitives as compact patch-normal representations, where each patch predicts a 3D center and
plane normal in its own camera frame; pairing these patch-level primitives via an external pixel matcher
(e.g., MASt3R (Leroy et al., 2024), LoFTR (Sun et al., 2021)) yields M two-view correspondences
whose warping residuals serve as the conditioning signal for the refiner. For pose refinement in SE(3), we
exploit the asymmetric difficulty of rotation and translation: a flow-matching (Liu et al., 2023; Lipman
et al., 2023) multi-query Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017; Peebles & Xie, 2023) denoises the rotation
residual conditioned on the plane-warping residuals, producing M hypotheses that a learned score head
fuses with the initial pose by softmax-weighted voting; translation is then recovered by a geometric
solver under the refined rotation. Trained once on two-view pairs, PosePlaner extends as a periodic
tail refiner for streaming n-view pose estimators on long sequences with no retraining. Beyond classical
bundle adjustment and pose-graph backends (Schönberger & Frahm, 2016), to the best of our knowledge
this is the first learned, diffusion-based refiner that operates on the pose outputs of streaming n-view
estimators without sequence-specific retraining. Experimentally, our PosePlaner consistently improves
upstream estimators from both paradigms on two benchmarks, and its gains grow with sequence length
in the streaming setting, where drift correction matters most—e.g., on ScanNet at N=2000, rotation
AUC@5◦improves by +20 points for CUT3R (Wang et al., 2025b) and +21 for TTT3R (Chen et al.,
2025).
Our contributions.
• We propose PosePlaner, the first learned, diffusion-based pose refiner that revives the classical
correspondence–pose coupling for feed-forward 3D reconstruction models, treating pose refinement as
a conditional generation problem over plane-warping residuals.
• We design a hybrid SE(3) refinement scheme: a flow-matching multi-query Transformer for rotation
with score-based hypothesis fusion, and a geometric solver for translation, enabling single-step

2. Related Work
Two-view and feed-forward pose estimation.
Classical structure-from-motion (Schönberger &
Frahm, 2016; Mur-Artal et al., 2015) recovers relative pose from sparse correspondences via a closed-
form essential-matrix solver (Nistér, 2004) wrapped in RANSAC (Fischler & Bolles, 1981); modern
matchers (Sarlin et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2021; Edstedt et al., 2024; Leroy et al., 2024) replace hand-
crafted descriptors with learned dense features, but the discrete solver still collapses each match into an
inlier / outlier vote, and a single bad RANSAC threshold can erase the entire inlier set. Differentiable
RANSAC variants (Brachmann et al., 2017; Brachmann & Rother, 2018; 2022) relax this voting into
a soft, end-to-end-trainable selection, yet gradients still flow through aggregate inlier counts rather
than per-correspondence geometric residuals. A complementary line skips the solver and regresses pose
directly via large feed-forward 3-D backbones (Wang et al., 2024; Leroy et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025a)
or their online streaming descendants (Wang et al., 2025b; Chen et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025; Yuan et al.,
2026; Zhuo et al., 2025; Lan et al., 2025; Wang & Agapito, 2025; Chen et al., 2026); both produce
a terminal prediction whose downstream correspondences and accumulated drift never re-enter the
prediction loop. PosePlaner sits downstream of either paradigm, correcting any initial pose using
the geometric constraint encoded in matched plane primitives and restoring the pose–correspondence
coupling that both break.
Planar primitives for 3-D understanding.
Indoor scenes are dominated by piecewise-planar
structure, motivating reconstruction by sparse 3-D plane primitives rather than dense points or meshes (Yu
et al., 2019; Jin et al., 2021; Xie et al., 2022; Tan et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025b;a). Early
feed-forward methods (Liu et al., 2018; 2019; Shi et al., 2023) regress plane masks and per-pixel plane
parameters via instance segmentation, but depend on manually annotated plane labels (ScanNetV2 (Dai
et al., 2017), Matterport3D (Chang et al., 2017)), capping both training scale and cross-dataset
generalization. PlanarSplatting (Tan et al., 2025) breaks this bottleneck with a differentiable
plane-primitive rasterizer whose rendering loss is annotation-free, and Plana3r (Liu et al., 2025a) uses
it to train a feed-forward stereo plane-primitive backbone at scale, predicting per-patch normals and
centers end-to-end. On the pose side, NopeSAC (Tan et al., 2023) matches a few scene-level plane
instances, feeds each pair through a shared MLP to produce one pose hypothesis per correspondence,
and soft-fuses these (with the init) via a learned scoring layer over their warping costs. PosePlaner
retains the soft-fusion idea but operates at the much denser patch level and replaces the per-plane MLP
regressor with a continuous flow-matching denoiser jointly conditioned on the plane-warping residuals of
all matched patch pairs.
Diffusion models for pose and 3-D.
Denoising diffusion (Ho et al., 2020) has been adapted to
pose by PoseDiffusion (Wang et al., 2023) (camera trajectories conditioned on image features),
RayDiffusion (Zhang et al., 2024) (denoising a per-pixel ray bundle), and DiffusionSfM (Zhao et al.,
2025) (jointly denoising per-pixel ray origins and endpoints to recover structure and motion); all three
are visual-prior-driven, with no explicit per-correspondence geometric channel. Flow matching (Lipman
et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023) replaces the noise-prediction objective with a deterministic vector-field
target, enabling single-step inference. PosePlaner adopts this flow-matching parameterization and
conditions on plane-warping residuals rather than pixels, so the denoiser sees the algebraic quantity
a classical solver would minimize—a geometric channel decoupled from any single matcher’s visual
statistics.
3. Method
PosePlaner takes any initial two-view relative pose Tinit of the input image pair (I1, I2) and refines it
into a more accurate estimate. The main idea rests on a classical observation:

Proposition 1 (Plane-Warping Identity). Let (n1, d1) and (n2, d2) be the parameters of a 3D plane
observed in two camera frames related by a relative pose Trel = (R, t). Then
n1 = R n2,
d1 = d2 −(R n2)⊤t.
(1)
Departures from this identity produce the plane-warping residuals
∆n(R) = ∥n1 −R n2∥2 ,
∆d(R, t) = |d1 −d2 + (R n2)⊤t|,
(2)
which vanish at the true pose and grow with pose error.
These residuals provide an estimator-agnostic conditioning signal for any upstream source. Given a
two-view Vision Transformer that reads out patch-level 3D planes in each view’s own camera frame
(Sec. 3.1), we compute the plane-warping residuals between matched pairs and use them as conditioning
for a flow-matching Transformer that denoises the rotation residual; translation is then recovered by a
geometric solver under the refined rotation (Sec. 3.2). See Fig. 2 for an overview.
3.1. Patchwise Plane Primitives and Correspondences
Our method follows the convention of Vision Transformers (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021), patchifying the input
images (I1, I2) into S patch tokens each. A siamese ViT encoder processes both views independently,
and two cross-attention decoders fuse information across views, predicting a 3D planar primitive per
patch, specifically a center depth, two radii, and an orientation quaternion from which the plane normal
n and offset d are recovered.
Plane representation from Siamese Vision Transformers.
Each patch in a view predicts a local
planar primitive parameterized by a 3D center P ∈R3 and a unit surface normal n ∈S2, both expressed
in its own camera frame. The plane offset follows as d = −n⊤P , giving a compact (n, d) per patch.
We adopt the architecture of Plana3r (Liu et al., 2025a) with one key change: whereas Plana3r
expresses all primitives in a shared global frame (view 1), we set each primitive to live in its own source
camera’s frame. The training details of this Siamese Vision Transformer network are in App. B.1.
Two-view plane correspondences.

views, we establish patch-level correspondences from any external pixel matcher (e.g., SuperGlue (Sarlin
et al., 2020), LoFTR (Sun et al., 2021), ROMA (Edstedt et al., 2024), or MASt3R (Leroy et al.,
2024)). Given pixel-level matches, we convert each matched pixel pair to its enclosing patch indices and
accumulate a vote matrix V ∈RS×S counting the number of pixel matches per patch pair. Patch-level
correspondences are then extracted by mutual nearest neighbors: patch i in view 1 is matched to patch
j in view 2 if and only if j = arg maxk Vik and i = arg maxk Vkj. This yields M matched plane pairs

4. Experiment
We evaluate PosePlaner as a plug-in refiner for both two-view and streaming n-view pose estimators
across two benchmarks, ScanNet1500 (Dai et al., 2017) and 7Scenes (Shotton et al., 2013), in Sec. 4.1
and Sec. 4.2; an additional zero-shot two-view evaluation on Matterport3D (Chang et al., 2017)
(SparsePlanes (Jin et al., 2021) test split) is reported in App. C.1 (Tab. 7). Implementation and
training details are in App. B.
4.1. Two-View Camera Pose Refinement
Evaluation protocol.
We evaluate on ScanNet1500 (Dai et al., 2017) (1500 standard test pairs
from (Sarlin et al., 2020)) and 7Scenes (Shotton et al., 2013) (4070 pairs formed at frame gap 50 across
all 46 sequences; zero-shot, not in our training mixture). We attach PosePlaner downstream of
seven upstream pose sources: four correspondence-based solvers (SuperGlue (Sarlin et al., 2020),
LoFTR (Sun et al., 2021), ROMA (Edstedt et al., 2024), MASt3R (Leroy et al., 2024)) and three
diffusion-based pose estimators (PoseDiffusion (Wang et al., 2023), RayDiffusion (Zhang et al.,
2024), DiffusionSfM (Zhao et al., 2025)). Each correspondence-based upstream uses its own native
pose solver followed by a two-view bundle adjustment to produce the init pose. We report pairwise
rotation and translation AUC at 5◦and 10◦(higher is better).
NopeSAC configuration.
We evaluate the frozen NopeSAC (Tan et al., 2023) refinement head
using MASt3R patch matches and our predicted plane pairs. Initial poses are post-BA estimates for
correspondence-based upstreams and independently rerun predictions for diffusion-based upstreams on
the same test pairs. We bypass its PlaneTR frontend, native matching, and pixel camera prediction,
retaining the official plane warp, AIM, 50-slot head, and soft fusion. The released ScanNet checkpoint is
used without retraining on either benchmark. We report its directly predicted rotation and translation;
PosePlaner retains its geometric translation refinement (Eq. (13)). This evaluates the released head
under shared frontend inputs.
Quantitative results.
In Tab. 1, PosePlaner outperforms the NopeSAC head at both reported
rotation and translation thresholds for all four correspondence-based upstreams on both benchmarks.
The visual-conditioning outputs of PoseDiffusion, RayDiffusion, and DiffusionSfM also im-
prove substantially after refinement, indicating that the plane-warping residuals carry information
complementary to visual-only priors.
Comparison with classical refinement.
Both methods start from the same LoFTR- or MASt3R-
derived pre-BA poses and share the same MASt3R matching output. BA uses MASt3R’s point
correspondences, while PosePlaner uses patch-level correspondences. Overall, PosePlaner substan-

with MASt3R initialization on 7Scenes (Tab. 2).

4.3. Ablation Study
We ablate PosePlaner along three axes within Tab. 5: (i) what conditioning the refiner needs (w/o
{gm}, c), (ii) how the M+1 pose hypotheses are aggregated (w/o score head, i.e., the softmax-weighted
score-head fusion is replaced by a uniform average), and (iii) how the pose residual is parameterized
(w/o translation drops the translation channel from z0, leaving a rot-only z0 ∈R6; w/o rot6d replaces
the rot6d rotation latent with a unit quaternion in R4).
Conditioning ablation.
Removing the geometry tokens {gm} collapses performance to the init level:
without per-match evidence the model can only reproduce the prior mean. Removing the pose token
c (equivalently, replacing Tinit with the identity) forces the model to estimate pose from scratch; the
SuperGlue and LoFTR columns become identical since the upstream pose no longer enters the model.
The model still recovers a reasonable pose from warping residuals alone, but consistently below the
accuracy obtained with a real init.

Score-head fusion vs. uniform averaging.
The w/o score head variant uniform-averages the M+1
pose hypotheses instead of fusing them by the score head’s softmax weights. Performance drops: outlier
hypotheses receive the same weight as well-aligned ones, whereas the score head learns to discount them
via plane-warping cost (Eq. (9)).
Pose-residual parameterization matters.
The w/o translation variant drops the translation channel
from the diffusion residual (rot-only z0 ∈R6); rotation accuracy degrades because the bilinear coupling
between R and t in ∆d provides a contextual signal that tightens the rotation prediction. The w/o rot6d
variant replaces the continuous 6-D rotation representation (Zhou et al., 2019) with a unit quaternion
in R4 and reduces rotation accuracy. Quaternions form a double cover of SO(3): q and −q represent
the same rotation. Choosing a unique quaternion for each rotation introduces discontinuities that can
produce abrupt changes in Euclidean flow-matching targets. The continuous 6-D representation avoids
this ambiguity. Appendix A.2 provides the mathematical explanation.
Training objective.
Flow matching with a single Euler step outperforms direct regression and 100-
step DDPM in rotation and translation-direction AUC on ScanNet1500 with LoFTR initialization.

4.4. Limitations
PosePlaner relies on reliable planar evidence, so scenes with limited planar structure or inaccurate plane
predictions can reduce refinement gains. The plane source is interchangeable: Appendix C.5 demonstrates
refinement with MoGe-derived planes (Wang et al., 2025c), suggesting that improved geometry predictors
could further benefit our method. For already-accurate initial poses, plane-prediction noise can outweigh
the remaining opportunity for correction and cause slight regressions, as observed for ROMA and
MASt3R on ScanNet1500 (Tab. 1). Refinement is therefore most useful when the initialization leaves
substantial room for correction; it is not guaranteed to improve an already-near-optimal estimate.
5. Conclusion
We presented PosePlaner, a flow-matching pose refiner that restores correspondence–pose coupling in
feed-forward 3-D reconstruction. It combines single-step rotation refinement conditioned on matched
planar primitives with geometric translation recovery for two-view inputs. Trained on two-view pairs,
the same refiner improves streaming multi-view poses without retraining. Experiments demonstrate
gains across correspondence-based and diffusion-based pose estimators and reduced drift in streaming
reconstruction. Extending the conditioning to other geometric primitives is a promising direction for
future work.

A.2. Rotation as a Continuous 6-D Representation
The rotation part of the pose residual z0 in Eq. (6) is encoded in the 6-D representation of Zhou et al.
(2019), not as a unit quaternion. Under a flow-matching objective this choice is forced, not cosmetic:
quaternions carry a topological discontinuity that interacts destructively with continuous probability
paths.
Formal definition of a continuous representation.
Following Zhou et al. (2019), a representation
of SO(3) with domain X ⊆Rd is a pair of maps
f : SO(3) →X,
g : X →SO(3),
g ◦f = idSO(3),
(16)
and the representation is continuous when both f and g are continuous under the Euclidean topology
of Rd. Neural networks are continuous under this topology, so a discontinuous g forces the network to
reproduce a discontinuity somewhere inside its training support.
The d ≤4 obstruction.
Zhou et al. (2019) prove that no continuous representation of SO(3) exists
for d ≤4. Concretely, the discontinuity always lives in the encoder f, since the polynomial decoders
typically used for these representations are themselves continuous:
• Euler angles (d=3) have a gimbal-lock singularity where f is not unique and g is not one-to-one.
• Axis-angle (d=3) maps SO(3) to a closed ball of radius π, with antipodal points on the boundary
∥r∥= π identified to the same rotation; the encoder f must therefore choose one of the two boundary
representatives, creating a sign-flip discontinuity at ∥r∥= π.
• Unit quaternions (d=4) form a double cover of SO(3): every R maps to an antipodal pair ±q on
S3. The decoder g : R4 →SO(3) is the standard quaternion-to-matrix polynomial, which is continuous
everywhere; the obstruction lies in the encoder f : SO(3)→S3, which must select one of ±q globally
and therefore admits no continuous global section—arbitrarily close rotations either side of the chosen
branch are encoded to far-apart quaternions of opposite sign.
SO(3) is compact and topologically non-trivial, so no homeomorphism to Rd exists for any finite d; a
continuous embedding is the best one can hope for, and Zhou et al. (2019) show d = 5 is the minimum
(d = 6 is the simplest practical construction).

• The latent is 9-dimensional, far lower than image-scale diffusion, so the marginal velocity is an easy

D. Social Impacts
Positive social impact.
PosePlaner corrects relative camera pose at the geometric level and
extends from two-view training to streaming n-view inference without retraining. This makes accurate
pose estimation more accessible to downstream applications such as AR/VR, robotics, and indoor scene
understanding.
Potential negative impact.
As with any technology that improves camera pose estimation, our
method could in principle be used in privacy-sensitive contexts. We recommend deploying it within a
broader pipeline that applies the appropriate consent and access controls.
