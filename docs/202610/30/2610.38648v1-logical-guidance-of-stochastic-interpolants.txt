ABSTRACT 

Guidance and composition of generative models have applications in visual de-sign, chemistry, and biology. Logical guidance composes conditional scores ex-actly for diffusion models. However, Flow Matching (FM) and Stochastic Inter-polants (SI) learn velocity fields. We show that negation and exclusive disjunction admit exact conditional velocity composition on any interpolant, whereas con-junction, and therefore any formula containing one, is exact on Gaussian paths but in general incurs a drift-interaction error, so composed velocities need not sam-ple the logical target. LOGSI solves this by using score-based re-equilibration as our principled mechanism and falling back to the Gaussian-path velocity rule as an approximate transport predictor. In non-Gaussian bridge problems, genera-tion alternates transport steps with score re-equilibration steps. On image trans-fer (EMNIST to CMNIST, CelebA) and QM9 source-to-target molecular editing with logical attribute constraints, L OGSI improves rule conformity at the same network-evaluation budget over composed-velocity baselines, while also improv-ing fidelity on the image tasks. More broadly, L OGSI applies wherever stochastic interpolants need guidance and logical combinations of constraints are natural. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Guidance and composition are central to generative AI, enabling controlled generation across do-mains ranging from visual design to chemistry and biology (Saharia et al., 2022; Hoogeboom et al., 2022; Watson et al., 2023; Skreta et al., 2025; Blohm & Garg, 2026). Guidance steers a model toward desired properties, while composition combines multiple conditions or concepts to produce outputs that satisfy more complex requirements. Most existing methods target diffusion models (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015; Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021; 2020), where one endpoint is a fixed Gaussian, and the reverse dynamics depend only on the score. A natural extension is to lift logical guidance to stochastic interpolants (SI) (Albergo et al., 2025), which unify diffusion and flow match-ing (Lipman et al., 2023) and are not tied to a Gaussian endpoint. Classical guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021; Ho & Salimans, 2022) for SI has been studied (Feng et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2026), but logical composition of atomic conditions in this regime remains open. We focus on domain transfer (Liu et al., 2023): an interpolant transports a source distribution ρ0 to a target ρ1, samples can be initialized from concrete source instances (Albergo et al., 2024), and guidance enforces a Boolean formula φ over target attributes. For example, we may edit a source molecule by adding a geometry (e.g. rings) and an atom-composition constraint (e.g. oxygen). Throughout, we distinguish between unconditional velocity and score fields (or velocities and scores for short), denoted by vt(x) and st(x), and conditional velocity and score fields, denoted by vt(x | c)

and st(x | c), which steer generation toward samples satisfying a constraint or property c.The core challenge is that SIs learn conditional velocities, but it is not clear a priori how these fields should be combined to enforce a Boolean formula φ. For conditional scores, Alesiani et al. (2026) (LogDiff) derive exact recursive composition rules. We introduce L OGSI to address this asymmetry between velocity and score compositionality (Fig-ure 1). On general stochastic interpolants with a non-Gaussian starting distribution, both velocity and score are available at sampling time, but only the score admits exact logical composition for every formula (Alesiani et al., 2026). In L OGSI, composed velocities are exact on Gaussian-path bridges and can be used as a proxy transport field elsewhere. L OGSI therefore separates roles: an approximate transport step along the proxy transport field ˜vt(· | φ) advances mass along the in-terpolant, while exact composed conditional scores re-equilibrate samples toward pt(· | φ) at each timestep. In the limit of sufficient score-correction steps at fixed t, the procedure is consistent with the logical target. Concretely, we realize this split as a transport–re-equilibration sampler: each outer step applies a transport update along ˜vt, followed by K overdamped Langevin steps with the exactly composed conditional score. We evaluate our method on image and molecular transfer with non-Gaussian endpoints, testing general Boolean templates (conjunction, disjunction, negation) and randomly generated expression trees. At matched network-evaluation budget, score-guided re-equilibration with a proxy velocity improves rule conformity over composed-velocity integration. It also yields higher fidelity on the image experiments. Our main contributions are as follows: • Score–velocity asymmetry for logical SI. On Gaussian-path flow matching, LogDiff score composition induces an exact velocity composition rule. For general stochastic interpolants with non-Gaussian endpoints, score composition carries over unchanged, velocity rules fail for conjunction and CI-disjunction, and therefore for general logical statements φ.• Logical guidance by score correction. We sample under Boolean formulas by re-equilibrating with exactly composed conditional scores at each timestep, avoiding any closed-form composed velocity field. • Proxy velocity transport. We reuse the Gaussian-path velocity rule as a transport proxy, cutting the number of correction steps needed and improving conformity over the composed-velocity baseline. 2Under review 

2 RELATED WORK 

Generative methods and bridges. Diffusion models generate data by reversing a fixed noising process (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015; Ho et al., 2020; Song & Ermon, 2020), unified by Song et al. (2021) in the notion of stochastic processes. Song et al. (2021) also propose to sample with a predictor-corrector scheme, where a Langevin corrector uses the score to remove the predictor’s discretization error while both target the same marginal. Our sampler is similar but structurally different: because only the score composes exactly for formulas containing conjunctions or general disjunctions (Remark 1), the score–velocity asymmetry (Section 4.2) forces the transport step to be a deliberate proxy ˜vt̸ = vt(· | φ), while the corrector is the exactness mechanism enforcing the logical constraint, needed beyond discretization error even with infinitesimal transport steps. Flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) learns a velocity field between two distributions, and stochastic interpolants (Albergo et al., 2025) generalize both diffusion and flow matching while dropping the Gaussian-endpoint assumption, which is why we adopt them for our method. Finally, Schr¨ odinger bridges (De Bortoli et al., 2021; Shi et al., 2023) transport between arbitrary distributions under an entropy-optimal-transport objective. Since our logical composition is orthogonal to how the bridge is trained, extending it to such samplers is an appealing direction for future work. 

Guidance and compositional conditioning. Guidance of generative models was first achieved by training a separate classifier to steer diffusion sampling (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021). The noise-aware classifiers we use for the posterior weights in our logical composition play an analogous role. Classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) shortly after removed the need for a sepa-rately trained classifier by jointly modeling conditional and unconditional scores. Both approaches condition on a single condition and do not address how multiple conditions should be combined. Compositional generation, started in products of experts (Hinton, 2002) and revived more recently (Du et al., 2023; Gaudi et al., 2025), targets exactly this problem, and subsequent work developed compositional and logical conditioning for diffusion models (Liu et al., 2022). Closely related, Skreta et al. (2025) introduce SuperDiff, which composes pretrained diffusion models at inference time through logical AND and OR operators. We discuss the connection to our bridge setting in Section A.6. Most relevant to us, Alesiani et al. (2026) derive exact recursive rules for compos-ing conditional scores under Boolean formulas. Guidance has also been extended to flow matching (Feng et al., 2025) and stochastic interpolants (Jiang et al., 2026), but to our knowledge logical composition in these settings remains unexplored. 

3 PRELIMINARIES 

To build our method we rely on the framework of stochastic interpolants by Albergo et al. (2025) and the logical guidance work of Alesiani et al. (2026). 3.1 STOCHASTIC INTERPOLANTS 

In this work we use the framework of stochastic interpolants by Albergo et al. (2025), which unifies diffusion and flow matching models. The idea of this framework is to replace the diffusion or linear interpolation of diffusion and flow matching models between the two distributions ρ0 and ρ1 with an interpolant of the form 

xt = I(t, x 0, x 1) + γ(t)z, t ∈ [0 , 1] , z ∼ N (0 , I ), (1) where (x0, x 1) ∼ π is a coupling with marginals x0 ∼ ρ0 and x1 ∼ ρ1, the interpolation I satisfies the boundary conditions I(0 , x 0, x 1) = x0 and I(1 , x 0, x 1) = x1, and the noise scheduler γ satisfies 

γ(0) = γ(1) = 0 and γ(t) > 0 for all t ∈ (0 , 1) . One can think of this as an interpolation between the distributions ρ0 and ρ1, bridged by intermittently adding Gaussian noise to the process. For diffusion models, exact logical composition was developed by Alesiani et al. (2026). There only the score enters the reverse-time dynamics, so it is natural to work with posterior logical scores rather than with conditional densities directly. Let C = {c1, . . . , c n} be a set of atomic predicates (e.g. atomic conditions), and let φ be a Boolean formula over C built from conjunction, disjunction, and negation. For t ∈ (0 , 1] and x ∈ Rd, define the backward truth probability and the logical score 

pt(φ | x) := P x1 |= φ | xt = x, st(φ | x) := ∇x log pt(φ | x), (6) where x1 |= φ means that the terminal state satisfies φ. By Bayes’ rule, the conditional score, used for generation, satisfies st(x | φ) := ∇x log pt(x | φ) = st(x) + st(φ | x). For an atomic predicate 

ci, the corresponding logical score can be obtained from a conditional and an unconditional score network as st(ci | x) = st(x | ci) − st(x). 5 EXPERIMENTS 

We evaluate L OGSI on three domain-transfer benchmarks that test logical composition under non-Gaussian endpoints. In each setting we train a stochastic interpolant transporting a source distri-bution ρ0 to a target distribution ρ1, then sample from ρ1 subject to a Boolean constraint φ over semantic attributes. Atoms are class-conditional velocity and score networks, plus noise-aware clas-sifiers for the posterior weights in disjunctions and negations (Section B). All results are averaged over 3 training and evaluation runs. 

Baselines and compute matching. We compare our method against two composition-only base-lines without score re-equilibration ( K=0 ): CompVel , deterministic ODE integration of the com-posed proxy velocity in Equation (11) (the analogue of LogDiff-style guidance (Alesiani et al., 2026)), and CompSI , SDE integration that combines that velocity with the exactly composed score. Our sampler is run as LOGSI-ODE and LOGSI-SDE , using the ODE or SDE predictor of Al-gorithm 1 with K> 0 Langevin correctors at fixed t. For the logical AND we additionally report 

Oracle-ODE/SDE : compute-matched CFG on the joint label (guidance weight 1), without compo-sition or score correction. All main-text comparisons are compute-matched in number of function evaluations (NFE): each L OGSI run with N outer steps and K correctors is matched against the corresponding K=0 baseline with enough extra integration steps to equalise the budget (L OGSI-ODE against CompVel, L OGSI-SDE against CompSI), which isolates score-based re-equilibration from simply spending more network calls on transport. 

Metrics. Our primary metric is logical conformity : the fraction of generated samples whose at-tributes satisfy φ. On the image benchmarks attributes are read off by a held-out evaluator classifier, distinct from the noise-aware classifier that drives sampling. On QM9 they are recomputed from the generated structure with RDKit (RDKit contributors), along with chemical validity, and con-formity is measured only over valid molecules. For images we additionally report FID against an expression-conditioned reference set (real images whose ground-truth attributes satisfy the same φ)and LPIPS to the source image. For QM9 we report validity ×conformity, source-to-sample Kabsch root mean square deviation (RMSD), and novelty. Full definitions are in Section B.4.

6 CONCLUSION 

We studied logical composition for stochastic interpolants that transport between non-Gaussian source and target distributions. While conditional scores compose exactly in the LogDiff sense on general interpolants, the analogous velocity rule survives only in part: negation and exclusive disjunction remain exact on any interpolant, whereas conjunction, and hence any formula contain-ing one, is exact only on Gaussian-path bridges. L OGSI therefore separates roles: a proxy com-posed velocity advances samples along the interpolant, and exactly composed conditional scores re-equilibrate them toward pt(· | φ) at each timestep. Across EMNIST-to-CMNIST, CelebA, and QM9 editing benchmarks, this transport–re-equilibration procedure improves logical conformity at matched network-evaluation budget over composed-velocity baselines, for both fixed Boolean templates and randomly generated formulas. It additionally improves fidelity (FID) on the image benchmarks and novelty on QM9. More broadly, the same score-based viewpoint may extend to other bridge learners and to settings where logical combinations of constraints arise together with non-Gaussian transport.

SuperDiff Skreta et al. (2025) introduce a method to track changes in the conditional densities pt(x |

ci) along a sample path without training a noise-aware classifier. Their Theorem 1 gives an Itˆ oestimator for d log pt(x | ci). In principle, this construction could also be applied to stochastic interpolants and bridges as in our setting, although they only develop it for diffusion models. For logical AND , they choose weights κi (with P 

> i

κi = 1 ) by requiring equal infinitesimal den-sity updates, d log pt(x | ci) = d log pt(x | cj ), and transport the state with a κ-weighted linear combination of the atomic SDE drifts, so that in expectation the endpoint is equally likely under each conditional model, p1(x | ci) = p1(x | cj ) for all i, j . Crucially, matching the increments 

d log pt(x | ci) along the trajectory only pins down terminal densities up to the initial gap : integrat-ing from 0 to 1 gives 

log p1(x1 | ci) − log p1(x1 | cj ) = log p0(x0 | ci) − log p0(x0 | cj ), (65) so the construction lands on the AND manifold {x : p1(x | ci) = p1(x | cj ) ∀i, j } only when the initial laws already agree, p0(x | ci) = p0(x | cj ) for all i, j . This is true for standard diffusion models, where generation begins from a shared Gaussian independent of the condition, but fails in our bridge setting: we do not start from noise but from explicit samples x0 ∼ ρ0, and in general 

p0(x | ci)̸ = p0(x | cj ). By equation 65, the endpoint gap then equals the initial gap and the resulting sample is not on the AND manifold. A further practical difference concerns the divergence term in their density update. For VP/OU diffusion, ⟨∇ , f t⟩ in Theorem 1 is available in closed form because the forward drift ft is linear. For general stochastic interpolants one would instead need div of each atomic drift vt(· | ci) +  

> 12

¯γ(t) st(· | ci), which for neural vt, s t must be estimated stochastically at every step (e.g. via Hutchinson’s estimator), adding per-step cost and variance to the density updates rather than being a fundamental obstruction. For these reasons we do not compare against SuperDiff in our experiments. Adapting their density-tracking construction to bridge interpolants, either by re-deriving the AND weights so that equa-tion 65 is closed by the choice of κ, or by initialising from a coupling under which the initial gap vanishes, is a natural future direction.

In the QM9 editing setting this is undesirable, so we keep the schedule-tied step size as the default: it trades a possible conformity gain for edits that remain local to the source.
