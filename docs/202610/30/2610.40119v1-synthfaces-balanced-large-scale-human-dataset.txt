Abstract 

The pursuit of fair and equitable AI in human-centric tasks is critically hindered by the inherent biases and privacy risks of real-world datasets, which offer imbalanced rep-resentations of identity and often contain sensitive personal information. Consequently, state-of-the-art generative models trained on this skewed data exhibit unfair performance, particularly in identity-preserving generation. In this work we propose a pipeline for controllable generation of dataset with diverse faces for generative models training and finetuning. We achieve this by expanding foundational Arc2Face diffusion model with a flow matching generative model in the manifold of identity embeddings. We demon-strate that such approach achieves both goals of generative fidelity and label diversity not present in real human-centric datasets. As a result we collect and further contextual-ize a highly balanced identity-centric dataset of humans named SynthFaces. By design, our released images and identities are synthetic, reducing direct reliance on real personal images; we empirically check identity separation with a nearest-neighbor non-leakage metric. We purpose it for training and tuning of fair models for various downstream tasks, and showcase its usability in finetuning an existing adapter-based method for per-sonalized generation. 

# 1 Introduction 

Human image generation is one of the most common applications of modern generative models. The field originated with unconditional generators such as StyleGAN [4], which allowed to sample novel faces without any control, and was later expanded to text-to-image diffusion models and adapter-based approaches (e.g., PhotoMaker, SDXL-based personal-ization), which allow conditioning on a novel face from a single image [21, 28, 32, 36] and combined with prompt allowed to place human in different context. Unfortunately, most released face-conditional models report averaged quality, which conceals an important as-pect of such methods - do they perform equally for different human subgroups ? Captions for images, describing both humans and surrounding context, collected images are ID-grouped , meaning that there are multiple images with same person, and lastly we aim to obtain HQ image with high resolution. These properties may be missing for certain types of datasets mentioned in table. For example under Raw Images we denote large-scale raw collections obtained via web-scrapping, are large enough to include all types of faces, however they might still require label-balancing strategy, as well image filtering to obtain high-quality images. HQ Captioned datasets are high quality, but they balance in human representation as we show later, as well as typically do not include multiple images of same person. Face Recognition datasets are typically also without captions, low resolution and also skewed in terms of human representation. In comparison our collected dataset satisfies all of these properties, while also using released images and identities that are synthetic and empirically screened for identity separation. # 2 Related Works 

Synthetic face datasets have been studied primarily for face recognition [1, 14, 18]. Ex-isting pipelines combine GAN samples with per-identity adaptation [15, 29], or exploit the conditional space of face foundation models such as Arc2Face [26]. HyperFace [30] ex-plores this hyperspherical space directly, but its generated identities remain demographically imbalanced. Arc2Face and Vec2Face [34] instead perturb real ArcFace embeddings with PCA, requiring real identity embeddings at inference time and offering no explicit sepa-ration from them. ID-Flow learns a controllable prior over the identity manifold, samples without reference embeddings at inference time, and evaluates separation using ID-NonLeak (Table 2).

# 4 Dataset construction 

In order to build a dataset of synthetic released identities and images, we follow a 3 stage design described in Figure 2. Each stage is motivated by a specific problem of existing pipelines - at stage 1 we ensure that dataset is private while also free of skewed label distri-bution, at stage 2 we construct maximum fidelity faces from diverse identity representation, and at stage 3 we achieve high resolution and context coupled with captions.

# 5 Downstream applications 

We evaluate whether SynthFaces data yields fairness benefits in distinct downstream tasks: finetuning personalized generation pipeline, face recognition (FR) verification and demo-graphic attribute classification. Fine-tuning tests usefulness for id-preserving generation, FR tests whether balanced synthetic identities reduce verification disparity, and VQA tests whether balanced human images help demographic attribute recognition.

Training details Finetuning conditional diffusion model comes with a challenge of data distribution drift . It’s a problem that arises because of discrepancy between data that was used for training original checkpoint and data that is being used for finetuning. Main charac-teristic that seemingly may hurt performance of these methods in terms of face preservation is learned face size . Face size in collected data serves as hidden factor that can be learned by diffusion model and directly affect similarity metric. Therefore to mitigate effect of this hidden factor we apply approach from [5] termed shortcut finetuning - specifically we at-tach frozen ControlNet [37] to condition on human pose and face position in source image, making finetuned IP-adapter layers learn only essential features related to face preservation. Details about this approach and hyperparameters for training are given in Appendix A.8. We provide finetuning results for StableDiffusion coupled with IPAdapter-FaceID. We observe a significant mean improvement in fair face reconstruction benchmark, while keep-ing comparable performance of contextualized generation. However, it is also important to inspect the change in quality per label. In Fig. 8 we showcase improvement across different label values per age and race groups - improvement is not isolated and ultimately makes performance more uniform. More detailed finetuning results with another personalized gen-eration method are provided in Appendix A.8.

# 6 Conclusion 

We introduced SynthFaces, a large-scale, identity-grouped and captioned synthetic human dataset designed for demographic balance while reducing reliance on released images of real individuals. Its ID-Flow sampler provides explicit demographic control while main-taining identity diversity and empirical separation from the real identities used for training. Across personalized generation, face recognition, and demographic VQA, models trained with SynthFaces improve subgroup parity while retaining useful task performance. These results show that controlling identity composition during dataset construction is an effective lever for fairer human-centric models. Future work should reduce dependence on poten-tially biased face encoders and foundation generators and investigate hybrid real–synthetic balancing strategies. 14 KARPUKHIN ET AL .: SYNTHFACES 

# 7 Limitations 

While SynthFaces addresses key gaps in existing datasets, several limitations should be ac-knowledged. 

Bias inheritance from foundation models. Arc2Face and Flux despite being state of the art generators for corresponding parts of pipeline carry aesthetic and demographic biases from their respective training corpora. Our pipeline guarantees label-level balance across age, race, and gender, but within-group visual diversity and image style remain bounded by what these upstream models can faithfully generate. Demographic combinations that are underrepresented in Arc2Face’s training data may yield lower-fidelity or less varied outputs. 

Synthetic-to-real distribution gap. Images produced by diffusion models may contain subtle distributional artifacts absent from real photographs. While our VLM-based filter-ing step removes overt defects, residual domain differences could limit transferability when SynthFaces-trained models are evaluated on real-world data. 

Dependency on upstream model capabilities. The expressive range of generated iden-tities is ultimately constrained by Arc2Face’s capacity for identity preservation and Flux’s generation quality. Improvements to either foundation model would directly benefit down-stream dataset quality, but also introduce a moving dependency. 

# 8 Ethics statement 

The creation of SynthFaces is a direct response to the ethical crisis surrounding web-scraped human-centric datasets, many of which have been rightfully retracted due to non-consensual use of personal images, including those of minors. To reduce these privacy and consent risks, SynthFaces releases synthetic identities generated by our pipeline rather than scraped personal images. Because ID-Flow is trained from ArcFace embeddings extracted from real datasets, we do not claim a formal privacy guarantee; instead, we empirically evaluate nearest-neighbor separation with the ID-NonLeak metric and design the dataset to avoid direct re-use of real personal images. Furthermore, we address the issue of demographic bias that plagues both real and syn-thetic datasets. Our pipeline is explicitly designed to generate a balanced representation across age, gender, and ethnicity, creating a resource purposed for training fairer models and mitigating the harmful disparities common in existing technologies. While we acknowledge that no synthetic data is perfect and subtle biases may persist, we believe this work demon-strates a responsible path forward, decoupling progress in human-centric computer vision from the exploitation of personal data.

A.3 Additional data and model details 

Data collection Our training stage is conducted fully in latent space of ArcFace embed-ding. To obtain them, we first processed images from Cosmic-HQ, FFHQ, EasyPortait and also enriched with images of people from the internet. We included only embeddings that were detectable with fixed bounding box size set to 640, since embeddings tend to signif-icantly differ depending on detection size. We implement a deduplication stage, where all collected embeddings are filtered out by a threshold value of 0.4 pairwise cosine similar-ity, ensuring diverse faces are used for training and avoiding identity collapse, where model learns only fixed amount of identities. Then we also annotate all collected images with Fair-Face, providing age/race/gender labels, and remove embeddings that correspond to images that failed labeling stage. As a result, we train on around 50k identity embeddings with la-bels. For evaluation we use hold-out set of Cosmic-HQ images and CelebA, which weren’t used during training, and apply same pipeline to obtain embeddings and labels, without deduplication stage.
