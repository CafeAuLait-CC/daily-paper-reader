## ABSTRACT 

We address the task of long-video, camera-controlled generation from a single im-age. The dominant autoregressive paradigm suffers from two key limitations: (1) error accumulation over time, as distant frames are conditioned on previously gen-erated outputs rather than ground truth, leading to drift; and (2) ambiguity in how to incorporate camera control. Standard solutions to both—such as self-forcing and conditioning on explicit 6-DOF poses—either incur significant computational overhead or produce drifting scene geometries over time. We propose a simple and lightweight framework that combines autoregressive generation (WanAR) with hierarchical long-context denoising (FixAR). To enforce scene geometry remains consistent with input cameras, we draw from feed-forward novel-view synthesis and reformulate video generation as a warping-and-inpainting problem. To miti-gate drift, we introduce a hierarchical denoising strategy in which different stages condition on different temporal histories. Specifically, we first generate frames conditioned on the immediate past, and then refine them using a second denoiser conditioned on a temporally subsampled long-range history to enforce global con-sistency. This fine-to-coarse scheme enables each generated frame to effectively condition on the full temporal context without incurring a prohibitive cost. Ex-periments on RealEstate10k and DL3DV demonstrate that WanAR+FixAR out-performs prior methods for camera-controlled long-video generation, producing results that are both temporally stable and consistent with input camera trajecto-ries. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Recent advances in video diffusion models Blattmann et al. (2023); Yang et al. (2024); Team et al. (2025); HaCohen et al. (2024) have enabled photorealistic video generation with increasing levels of control. In this work, we study the problem of long-video generation from a single image given future camera trajectories. This setting is particularly appealing for applications such as virtual scene exploration and content creation, where controllable viewpoint changes must remain 3D consistent over extended time horizons. However, generating long videos that faithfully follow camera motion while maintaining spatial and temporal coherence remains a significant challenge. 

Why is this hard? Existing approaches predominantly rely on autoregressive generation, where future frames are conditioned on previously generated outputs over a sequence of rollouts. While this is effective for short sequences, such methods accumulate errors over time, leading to drift in long videos. A second challenge lies in how camera motion is incorporated. One widely adopted approach is to condition on explicit 6-DOF pose representations, such as Pl¨ ucker coordinates or ray maps. One subtle challenge is that many flagship benchmarks (like RealEstate10K Zhou et al. (2018)) provide cameras only up-to-scale, making camera-conditioned video generation somewhat undefined - e.g., the world looks different when moving forward 10cm versus 10m.

Our approach. We address these challenges with a simple and lightweight framework that com-bines online reconstruction with hierarchical denoising. First, to respect the fixed but unknown scale of input camera trajectories, we draw inspiration from feed-forward novel-view synthesis and refor-mulate video generation as a warping-and-inpainting problem, aligning intermediate depth predic-tions to the scale of the ground-truth geometry (typically provided in the same scale as camera inputs, often obtained via COLMAP). Second, to mitigate autoregressive drift, we propose a hierarchical denoising strategy in which different stages condition on different temporal histories. Concretely, we generate frames in a fine-to-coarse manner: an initial denoiser conditions on the immediate past to produce high-frequency details, while a second denoiser conditions on a temporally subsampled long-range history to enforce global consistency. This design allows each generated frame to effi-ciently incorporate information from the entire past without incurring the cost of conditioning on an exhaustive set of historical frames. In summary, our contributions are as follows: (1) we identify scale inconsistency as a key failure mode of pose-conditioned video generation and propose a warping-based formulation that enforces scale consistency between the scene and camera trajectory; (2) we introduce a hierarchical denois-ing scheme that reduces long-horizon drift by conditioning on multi-scale temporal context; and (3) we present a simple combination of models, WanAR and FixAR, that achieves state-of-the-art performance on RealEstate10k and DL3DV for camera-controlled long-video generation, producing videos that remain consistent with the scale of input camera trajectories. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Autoregressive long video generation. Modern video diffusion models Ho et al. (2022); Blattmann et al. (2023); HaCohen et al. (2024); Yang et al. (2024); Team et al. (2025); Chen et al. (2025c) can generate photorealistic videos, but are typically limited to short temporal horizons. To extend gen-eration length, autoregressive formulations are widely adopted to roll out longer sequences Chen et al. (2025a); Teng et al. (2025); Deng et al. (2024); Jin et al. (2024); Chen et al. (2024); Yin et al. (2025); Song et al. (2025); Huang et al. (2025); Cui et al. (2025). However, a fundamental chal-2lenge in autoregressive video diffusion is drift , which progressively degrades visual quality over long rollouts. Recent approaches attempt to mitigate this issue by either exposing models to drifted distributions during training Chen et al. (2024); Song et al. (2025); Huang et al. (2025); Zhu et al. (2026); Liu et al. (2025), or by modifying attention mechanisms and introducing memory compres-sion modules to reference earlier, cleaner context during inference Zhang et al. (2025b); Cai et al. (2025); Zhang et al. (2025c); Yang et al. (2025); Li et al. (2026b). While these methods improve long-range consistency, they still suffer from limitations. For example, diffusion-forcing Chen et al. (2024); Song et al. (2025) relies on approximated drift distributions and exhibits a train–test drift distribution mismatch, while self-forcing Huang et al. (2025) incurs increased training cost due to multi-step rollouts at every training iteration. In this work, we propose to decouple autoregressive generation (WanAR) and drift correction (FixAR) via a hierarchical denoising framework, where FixAR is trained on precomputed data pairs with real drift. 

Camera-conditioned video world model. By introducing action controllability into long video generation and making the underlying world behind visual content explorable, video world models have emerged and demonstrated strong performance Bruce et al. (2024); Ball et al. (2025); Valevski et al. (2024); Team et al. (2026a); Hong et al. (2025); Team et al. (2026b). Most video world models primarily used keyboard inputs as a simple form of action control He et al. (2025b); Guo et al. (2025); Etched AI (2025), while many recent works Yu et al. (2025a); Xiao et al. (2025); Kong et al. (2025); Li et al. (2025b); Zong et al. (2020); Wu et al. (2025c); Gao et al. (2025) increasingly emphasizes camera motion as the control signal to enable more precise manipulation and support specific application scenarios. A natural question is how to incorporate camera information into video generative models. Existing approaches can be broadly categorized into three groups: (i) direct camera encoding, (ii) injecting 3D geometry through positional encoding fields Li et al. (2025a); Bai et al. (2025b), and (iii) injecting 3D geometry via rendering in pixel space. Most existing methods Yu et al. (2025a); Xiao et al. (2025); Shen et al. (2026a); Li et al. (2025b); Team et al. (2026b;a); Hong et al. (2025); He et al. (2025b); Hong et al. (2025); Yu et al. (2025a) fall into the first category, where normalized representations (e.g., Pl¨ ucker rays or ray maps) are encoded into latent space to guide the generation process, allowing camera control to be learned from large-scale camera annotations. However, such latent-space conditioning often leads to imprecise control when reflected in pixel-space generation, and tends to generalize poorly to out-of-distribution camera trajectories. The second line of work Shen et al. (2026a); Zhang et al. (2025a) models 3D geometric transformations induced by camera motion through modifications to positional encoding. This approach achieves more accurate camera control, but the modified architecture introduces additional training overhead. In this work, we adopt the third line of work Wu et al. (2025c); Kong et al. (2025), which directly models camera control by estimating 3D geometry using a metric depth estimator and warping it into future frames in an online fashion. This approach enables accurate camera control while naturally reformulating autoregressive generation as an inpainting task. 

Generative novel-view synthesis. Current state-of-the-art novel-view synthesis (NVS) methods are largely based on generative diffusion models Gao et al. (2024); Wu et al. (2025b); Watson et al. (2022); Yu et al. (2025c;b); Chen et al. (2025b); Bai et al. (2025a); He et al. (2025a); Ren et al. (2025); Li et al. (2026a); Bai et al. (2025b); Seo et al. (2024); Zhao et al. (2024). They are closely related to camera-conditioned video world models, but their spatial and temporal horizons for novel-view generation remain limited. Early NVS approaches also widely adopted direct camera encoding in latent space Gao et al. (2024); Wu et al. (2025b), but lacked precise camera control. More recent methods instead employ 3D geometry-based warping-and-inpainting formulations Yu et al. (2025c;b); Chen et al. (2025b); Ren et al. (2025), leading to improved visual quality and more accurate camera control. In addition, there is a line of work in NVS that uses diffusion models to correct rendering artifacts caused by large viewpoint changes Wu et al. (2025a); de Lutio et al. (2026). This perspective is closely related to the motivation behind our FixAR. In our setting, we interpret autoregressive drift in long-horizon video generation as another form of accumulated artifact over time, which can be corrected using video diffusion. 3···

## 3 METHOD 

3.1 PRELIMINARIES : F LOW MATCHING VIDEO DIFFUSION 

We adopt the open-source DiT-based Peebles & Xie (2023) video diffusion model Wan-I2V-14B Team et al. (2025) as our base model. Unlike traditional diffusion models that rely on score matching and stochastic reverse diffusion, Wan is trained with rectified flow matching Esser et al. (2024); Liu & Gong (2023); Lipman et al. (2023), which formulates generation as solving an ordi-nary differential equation (ODE) without stochastic terms. This formulation provides a stable and scalable training objective while preserving a likelihood-based interpretation. Specifically, given a clean video latent x1, a random noise latent x0 ∼ N (0 , I ), a conditioning signal c, and a timestep t ∈ [0 , 1] , flow matching defines the interpolated latent as 

xt = (1 − t)x0 + tx 1.

The model is trained to predict the constant velocity field that transports noise x0 to data x1:

L = ∥fθ (xt, c, t ) − (x1 − x0)∥22 .

3.2 WAN AR: C AMERA CONTROL FOR AUTOREGRESSIVE VIDEO GENERATION 

We consider the problem of camera-conditioned long video generation: given an input image I0 and a target camera trajectory {Ct}Tt=0 , the goal is to generate future observations {It}Tt=1 that follow the specified camera motion, where T can be large. General-purpose video diffusion models such as Wan-I2V-14B cannot be directly applied to this setting, because they (i) do not explicitly support 4camera conditioning, and (ii) are limited to a short generation horizon L, where typically L ≪ T .To address this, we build WanAR on top of Wan. WanAR achieves camera control by propagating explicit 3D geometry (depth and camera), and extends generation length through an autoregressive formulation. Given the initial observation I0, we first estimate its metric depth D0 using the monocular depth estimator MoGe-2 Wang et al. (2025b;c). Since the corresponding camera C0 is known, this gives us sufficient 3D geometry for the first frame. To train FixAR on realistic drift patterns, we construct its training set by following the same WanAR rollout process and the same sparse keyframe sampling strategy used at inference time. Unlike self-forcing approaches Huang et al. (2025), FixAR training does not require multi-step inference during each training iteration; instead, it uses precomputed input-target pairs, which substantially reduces computational overhead. Once trained, FixAR can be applied either globally to an entire generated sequence or autoregressively at a low frequency. FixAR mitigates drift for two reasons. First, due to its hierarchical design, it can directly reference earlier frames with higher visual quality, or even ground-truth frames during training, allowing ap-pearance and style to be propagated over a longer horizon. Recovering the original video framerate. Because of the limited base model horizon and for com-putational efficiency, FixAR operates only on sparse keyframes. To recover the full video sequence at original framerate, we train an additional video diffusion model, InterpolatAR , to interpolate be-tween neighboring corrected keyframes. This is a substantially easier task, since adjacent corrected keyframes are already visually and temporally close, and little hallucination is required. Importantly, InterpolatAR does not introduce additional drift, because it only fills in intermediate content rather than performing autoregressive extrapolation as in WanAR.

Baselines. We compare our method with baselines from both camera-conditioned long video gen-eration and novel-view synthesis. For camera-conditioned video generation, we compare with VMem Li et al. (2025b), DFoT Song et al. (2025), Lingbot-World Team et al. (2026b), and World-Warp Kong et al. (2025). VMem, DFoT, and Lingbot-World use Pl¨ ucker rays or ray maps as camera-control representations, while WorldWarp uses 3D Gaussian rendering as camera guidance. Lingbot-World is an open-source industrial model, and we use its flagship bidirectional model for evaluation. For novel-view synthesis, we adopt video diffusion-based methods that also use warp-and-inpaint-style camera control, including ViewCrafter Yu et al. (2025c), TrajCrafter Yu et al. Since these methods are designed for fixed-length rendering, we evaluate them in an image-to-video autoregressive manner, following the same rollout setting as our method. 

Metrics. We follow standard evaluation protocols used in related work Xiao et al. (2025); Kong et al. (2025); Li et al. (2025b); Shen et al. (2026b), including pairwise reconstruction metrics PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, as well as distribution-level metrics FID and KID. We also report FVD to evaluate video generation quality. In addition, we adopt masked PSNR (mPSNR) Gao et al. (2022), which computes PSNR only over the covisible region from the first frame. This metric better reflects camera-control accuracy without scale ambiguity. Since some baselines Song et al. (2025); Li et al. (2025b) only support a resolution of 256 × 256 

and a temporal frame skip of 10, we crop and resize the outputs of all methods, including ours, to the same spatial and temporal resolution for fair comparison. 

Implementation details. We initialize our models from the official Wan-I2V-14B-480P check-point Team et al. (2025) and fine-tune them using LoRA with rank 32. We use a batch size of 8 and a learning rate of 1 × 10 −4. For each model, including WanAR, FixAR, and InterpolatAR, we fine-tune for 10K steps on each dataset using 8 H100 GPUs at a resolution of 256 × 256 . Training each model takes approximately one day. Effect of hierarchical denoising. We conduct ablation studies on a 50-sequence subset of RealEstate-10K. Fig. 4 and Tab. 3 show the qualitative and quantitative effects of the hierarchical denoiser. Both outcomes are reasonable under the given input. We therefore evaluate Top-K generation metrics by sampling multiple outputs with different random seeds. 

## 5 DISCUSSION 

We present a simple framework for long-video, camera-controlled generation that combines autore-gressive generation with hierarchical denoising. Our results show that reformulating generation as a warping-and-inpainting problem helps maintain consistency with input camera trajectories, par-ticularly in the presence of up-to-scale ambiguity, while hierarchical denoising improves temporal stability by allowing each frame to incorporate both short- and long-range temporal information. Compared to prior approaches that rely on self-forcing or explicit pose conditioning alone, our method achieves stronger long-horizon consistency. These findings suggest that both scale consis-tency and long-range temporal conditioning are important for generating stable, camera-controlled videos over extended sequences. 9LIMITATIONS 

We note that our proposed warp-and-inpaint hierarchical video generation can only be used for static scenes. WanAR relies on the quality of underlying geometry, and errors in depth or reconstruction can propagate through the warping process, leading to artifacts or inconsistencies. WanAR rollouts only warp the last frame of the previous rollout and therefore do not have any memory mechanism baked in. This means that loop closure in long sequences may not be accurate. While hierarchical denoising with FixAR reduces drift, it does not fully eliminate it as our second stage operates over a finite horizon of frames too. Finally, our models trained on one dataset cannot be used for another dataset if the scale distributions between the two datasets are drastically different. We cover a detailed inference cost of our method with respect to other baselines in appendix. BROADER IMPACTS 

Efficient and temporally consistent camera-controlled video synthesis could broaden access to tools for creative media production, 3D exploration in virtual reality applications, and embodied AI re-search, though care should be taken to mitigate risks associated with realistic synthetic video gener-ation.

In this section, we elucidate our claim that camera-controlled video generation is a special case of novel-view synthesis (NVS) and discuss relevant paradigms for evaluation which are inspired by the literature in NVS. CASE I: I MAGE -TO -V IDEO GENERATION WITH POSE / GEOMETRY INFORMATION 

When input observations are accompanied by reconstruction information, such as calibrated camera poses and/or 3D geometry defined in a fixed coordinate system, future camera trajectories should also be specified in that same coordinate system. This corresponds to the standard NeRF-style set-ting, where methods are conditioned on input images together with calibrated camera poses. More generally, this setup may also be instantiated using a single image paired with depth or XYZ infor-mation. For example, given an estimate of scene geometry, future camera motion can be defined relative to the reconstructed scene structure (e.g., moving a fixed distance toward an observed ob-ject).
