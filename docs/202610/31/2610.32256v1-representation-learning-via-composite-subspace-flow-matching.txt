## ABSTRACT 

Distilling semantic meaning from raw image data remains a challenge in machine learning. Current self-supervised paradigms face a trade-off: joint-embedding architectures produce relevant features by aggressively discarding intra-class variance, irreversibly spatial and reconstruction. Conversely, generative models preserve spatial details but struggle to isolate semantics, as their optimization is dominated by pixel variations. To bridge this divide, we propose composite subspace flow matching (CSFM). Rather than corrupting data with unstructured isotropic noise, CSFM formulates representation learning as the variance-minimizing inversion of a deterministic degradation curriculum across spectral, color, and illumination subspaces. By bounding trajectory variance with hard temporal style truncation, a capacity-constrained encoder is driven to ignore transient style artifacts and encode persistent content. Empirical evaluations across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and STL-10 show that CSFM yields competitive discriminative transferability while preserving generative synthesis across both convolutional and vision transformer architectures. By avoiding the collapse pathologies of non-generative methods, CSFM forms a smooth latent space that outperforms standard optimal transport flows on linear probing and few-shot classification, while enabling coherent synthesis and trajectory interpolation. These results confirm that steering the physical dynamics of generative flows offers a scalable paradigm for learning representations that are simultaneously linearly separable and generatively invertible. 

Keywords Representation Learning · Flow Matching · Generative Modeling · Self-Supervised Learning 

## 1 Introduction 

The primary objective of self-supervised representation learning is to extract informative, high-level, and useful features for downstream tasks from unlabeled raw data [1–3]. While self-supervised learning (SSL) has catalyzed advances across computer vision, natural language processing, and world models for autonomous decision-making [4–7], extracting representations from unlabelled visual data remains challenging due to the high noise-to-signal ratio inherent to pixel space [8]. Unlike discrete textual tokens, which represent semantically compressed units, raw pixels are uncompressed physical measurements [8]. Consequently, high-magnitude nuisance variables, such as localized textures, illumination shifts, and background clutter, statistically dominate the input variance, frequently overshadowing the underlying semantic targets [9, 10]. To address these nuisance factors, visual SSL research has largely split into two paradigms with conflicting trade-offs [8]. On the one hand, reconstruction-based architectures, such as masked autoencoders [11] and denoising autoencoders [12, 13], model the data distribution by inverting input-space corruptions. However, because standard L2 pixel recovery aligns with raw variance rather than semantic abstraction, these models remain biased toward unpredictable, high-variance details [7, 10, 14]. Conversely, joint-embedding architectures (JEAs), such as VICReg [15], and LeJEPA [16], circumvent pixel synthesis entirely by enforcing invariance across augmented views in a latent representation space

Although this abstraction shields JEAs from nuisance variations, it leaves them vulnerable to informational and dimensional collapse [19, 20]. More fundamentally, recent theoretical work reveals a structural friction between discriminative extraction and generative reversibility [8, 10, 21]. To maximize linear separability on semantic tasks, JEAs suppress all intra-class sensitivity. While this invariance maximizes classification margins, it permanently deletes high-frequency and spatial information, rendering the learned representations structurally irreversible and incapable of image synthesis [21, 22]. Continuous-time generative models, specifically continuous normalizing flows and diffusion models [23–25], provide fully invertible spatial transformations that preserve geometry [24, 26]. Recognizing their capacity to model complex distributions without spatial loss, a recent paradigm shift repurposes continuous generative trajectories as proxy objectives for representation learning [27–29]. By conditioning a continuous vector field on a bottlenecked latent code, the encoder is driven to distill the sufficient statistics required to resolve trajectory ambiguities [29, 30]. Because this objective relies on continuous trajectory reconstruction rather than explicit embedding-space invariance, it avoids the dimensional collapse common in JEAs while maintaining spatial reversibility [31]. Despite this promise, continuous representation models face a significant limitation: standard frameworks rely almost exclusively on isotropic Gaussian noise [23, 25, 32]. This spatially uncorrelated corruption perturbs all image coordinates independently and identically, without accounting for the underlying structure of visual data. Recent generative works have expanded beyond pure Gaussian noise by designing physical degradation trajectories, such as blurring or masking [33–35]. However, adapting these cumulative multi-operator processes to representation learning proves challenging: sequential noise injections incur an intractable covariance inversion cost and introduce an irreducible target noise floor that disrupts encoder optimization. To resolve these limitations while balancing representation quality with generative reversibility, we propose composite subspace flow matching (CSFM). It replaces uniform corruption with an ordered sequence of deterministic subspace projections spanning the frequency, color, and illumination domains. By pairing this composite drift with a single, decoupled isotropic noise schedule, CSFM yields a simulation-free target vector field that nullifies irreducible target noise with minimal computational overhead. Under this continuous curriculum, content is defined by temporal persistence under physical transport. CSFM isolates content within a capacity-bounded latent code while offloading transient spatial details to the generative vector field, balancing downstream linear separability with a smooth, reversible generative manifold. The core contributions of this work are three-fold: 1. Composite subspace flow matching (CSFM): we introduce a simulation-free flow matching framework that couples an ordered visual degradation path with a decoupled isotropic noise schedule, eliminating target velocity variance with minimal memory overhead 2. Optimal encoder geometry: we show that minimizing trajectory variance induces a block-diagonal precision geometry, prevents features from the style subspace from dominating encoder capacity, and proves that style truncation is necessary for content alignment. 3. Comprehensive empirical validation: across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and STL-10 ( 96 × 96 ), using CNN and vision transformer backbones, CSFM outperforms unguided flows and autoencoders in linear and few-shot probing while preserving an invertible, connected latent space for generative decoding. Section 2 reviews self-supervised representation learning paradigms, continuous normalizing flows, trajectory conditioning, and sketched Gaussian regularization. Section 3 introduces CSFM, including the composite projection curriculum, asymmetric gradient routing, and forward-mode analytical computation. Section 4 provides theoretical proofs for subspace disentanglement, capacity saturation, and the style truncation requirement. Section 5 presents empirical evaluations on representation quality, generative fidelity, and scalability across architectures and resolutions. Section 6 presents our conclusions and outlines directions for future work. 

## 2 Background and Related Work 

2.1 Paradigms of Representation Learning: JEAs vs. Generative Models 

The goal of self-supervised representation learning is to extract from raw sensory data useful features for downstream tasks, while discarding task-irrelevant style and nuisance [15, 16, 36]. Following recent theoretical formulations [8, 22], an observation x ∈ RD is decomposed into complementary orthogonal subspaces RD = Xc ⊕ X s via

Reconstruction-based generative architectures, such as denoising autoencoders (DAE) [12, 13] and masked autoencoders (MAE) [11], learn by inverting input corruptions in pixel space [8]. While this ensures generative reversibility and preserves fine spatial, standard L2 pixel recovery optimizes for total raw variance rather than semantic features [7, 14]. Consequently, the encoder exhausts substantial capacity fitting textures and high-variance background noise [8]. Conversely, JEAs bypass pixel synthesis entirely by training an encoder to match latent representations across independently augmented views [17, 18, 37]. Predicting strictly in latent space protects JEAs from raw pixel [8]. However, by aggressively discarding all variance orthogonal to the hand-crafted invariances, JEAs permanently discard spatial structure, yielding structurally irreversible representations that are insufficient for image reconstruction [21, 22]. 

Representational Collapse and Latent Regularization. A critical failure mode of optimizing latent invariance in JEAs without generative constraints is representational collapse, wherein representations degrade to a trivial constant vector (information collapse) or span a degenerate lower-dimensional subspace (dimensional collapse) [19, 20]. While contrastive objectives [37] and covariance decorrelation penalties (e.g. VICReg [15], Barlow Twins [19]) prevent collapse, second-moment decorrelation incurs quadratic computational complexity and is insufficient to guarantee true Gaussianity, which is provably optimal for minimizing downstream prediction risk [16]. Leveraging the Cramér-Wold theorem, SIGReg matches random 1D projections of the empirical characteristic function against an analytical standard normal with linear complexity, providing a robust, collapse-free bottleneck without variational sampling noise [16].

## 5 Experiments 

5.1 Experimental Setup Datasets and Evaluation Protocols. We validate our framework across three visual benchmarks [59, 60]: CIFAR-10 (32 × 32 , 10 classes) for primary benchmarking and ablations; CIFAR-100 ( 32 × 32 , 100 classes) for granular category scaling; and STL-10 ( 96 × 96 ) for higher spatial resolution. For STL-10, unsupervised pre-training uses all 105k images (5k labeled + 100k unlabeled), while downstream probes evaluate on the standard 5k train and 8k test splits. Frozen representations are evaluated via linear probing, non-parametric k-NN classification ( k = 20 ), and few-shot learning (1% and 10% labels per class) [11, 37]. To measure latent cluster separation independently of classifiers, we report the silhouette score on test embeddings. Finally, secondary generative quality is evaluated in Appendix E via FID 1k [61], numerical ODE integration error, and latent space traversals. 

Architectures and Capacity Scaling. To isolate decoupled transport dynamics from model size, backbones are benchmarked under multiple capacity controls: • Convolutional Models (NCSN++ U-Net): The representation encoder is a WideResNet-28-4 ( Eϕ, 5.98M parameters, latent dimension c = 256 ) [62]. The vector field regressor ( vθ ) is evaluated under three regimes: (i) Baseline (20.81M total): Base filters nf = 64 with channel multipliers (1 , 2, 2, 2) and self-attention at 16 × 16 

(14.83M U-Net parameters), serving as our primary ablation testbed. (ii) High-Capacity (65.08M total): Expanded filters nf = 128 with FIR anti-aliasing (59.10M U-Net parameters). Encoder capacity remains fixed at 5.98M, ensuring generative gains reflect regressor capacity rather than expanded latent bandwidth. (iii) High-Resolution STL-10 (24.89M total): A 18.91M parameter U-Net regressor (paired with the 5.98M encoder) fit for 96 × 96 feature maps under fixed memory limits. • Vision Transformers (ViT/DiT): A custom ViT encoder ( Eϕ, 5.62M parameters; patch size 4 × 4, embedding dimension 256, depth 6, 8 heads, MLP ratio 4.0, class-token pooling) paired with a continuous diffusion transformer regressor (D ITC ONTINUOUS , 58.17M parameters, ≈ 63 .8M total) [32, 63]. The regressor comprises 12 DiT blocks (patch size 2 × 2, hidden dimension 384, 6 heads, MLP ratio 4.0). Continuous latents z ∈ R128 are projected via a 2-layer SiLU MLP and injected additively with timestep embeddings into adaptive layer norm (adaLN-Zero) blocks. 

Implementation and Compute Constraints. All models are trained on a single NVIDIA L4 GPU (22.5GB VRAM) using mixed precision ( bfloat16 ). We optimize CNNs with Adam [64] and vision transformers with AdamW [65], both at a base learning rate of η = 2 × 10 −4. Default hyperparameters are style truncation horizon t1 = 0 .5, asymmetric routing cutoff t0 = 0 .05 , radial Gaussian cutoff rlow = 6 (Eq. (9)), and SIGReg weight λreg = 10 −3. Under single-GPU resource limits, all benchmarks are evaluated across standard fixed evaluation splits using a single consistent random seed. Detailed training schedules and hyperparameters are in Appendix C.5.

To establish generality beyond CIFAR-10, we evaluate CSFM across three scaling dimensions under controlled parameter budgets within each tier (Table 4): (i) Semantic cardinality (CIFAR-100): scaling category complexity tenfold ( 10 → 100 ) while reducing training data to 500 images per class. (ii) Architectural inductive bias (ViT / DiT): replacing convolutional inductive biases with patch tokenization and global multi-head self-attention. (iii) Spatial resolution scaling (STL-10): scaling from 32 × 32 to 96 × 96 pixels on an uncurated 105k image pool. 

Semantic Cardinality: CIFAR-100. Evaluating on CIFAR-100 tests representation robustness under higher label granularity and limited per-class supervision. Standard OT-Flow achieves 42.27% linear accuracy and drops to 10.66% with 1% labeled data (5 samples per class). In contrast, CSFM attains 51.58% linear accuracy ( +9 .31% ) and 17.65% on 1% labels ( +6 .99% ). While uniform OT perturbation forces the regressor to resolve high-frequency noise across all scales, CSFM suppresses style variance along the trajectory, preserving linear separability in fine-grained regimes. 

Architectural Scalability: Vision Transformers (ViT/DiT). Evaluating transformer backbones demonstrates that CSFM is architecture-agnostic. CSFM scales from 53 .30% at 40K steps to 61.49% at 80K steps ( +8 .19% ), while standard OT-Flow yields diminishing returns ( 51 .84% → 53 .27% ). On 1% few-shot labels, CSFM achieves 49.69%, outperforming OT-Flow by +8 .63% . Transformers lack local translational inductive biases and typically require aggressive data augmentation; CSFM’s structured trajectories provide a stabilizing generative prior that guides token representations. 

Spatial Resolution Scaling: STL-10 ( 96 × 96 ). Benchmarking native 96 × 96 images on STL-10 tests scalability to higher pixel dimensions. Due to GPU memory constraints at this resolution, we employ a lighter, lower-capacity U-Net decoder ( ≈ 19M params) and reduce the batch size from 64 to 16; as established in our CIFAR-10 capacity ablations, reduced decoder capacity inherently lowers representation quality. Nevertheless, pre-training on the 105k uncurated images and evaluating frozen features on the 5k labeled split yields consistent gains over OT-flow: CSFM achieves 65.74% linear accuracy ( +1 .84% ), 50.54% on 1% labels ( +2 .20% ), and 57.91% on k-NN ( +2 .29% ). This confirms that physical subspace decomposition scales to higher image resolutions without sacrificing content selectivity. 

## 6 Conclusion 

In this paper, we introduced composite subspace flow matching (CSFM), a generative framework that repurposes continuous normalizing flows for representation learning. By pairing deterministic drift across canonical visual subspaces with a decoupled noise path, CSFM eliminates the irreducible target variance floor of multi-source stochastic diffusion while evaluating in O(D log D) time. We showed that untruncated exponential degradation causes encoders to allocate capacity to high-frequency style details, and proved that a finite style cutoff ( t1 < 1) enforces strict feature allocation toward the content subspace Xc. Empirically, CSFM outperforms unguided flows and pixel-level 12 denoising autoencoders across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and STL-10. Under resource-limited training schedules, it proves competitive with discriminative joint-embedding baselines on linear probes while scaling across convolutional and transformer architectures and retaining generative invertibility.

LeJEPA Projector Architecture. To avoid dimensional collapse, the LeJEPA baseline appends a 3-layer MLP projector to the WideResNet backbone following VICReg [15]. The projector expands the backbone feature dimension (d = 512 ) to a 2048-dimensional hidden layer before bottlenecking to the final representation z ∈ R256 . The first two linear layers are followed by 1D batch normalization and ReLU activations to bound variance, while the final layer is linear. 

Dual-View Data Augmentations. LeJEPA optimizes invariance over asymmetric view pairs (v1, v 2) constructed according to BYOL [17] and DINO [18] specifications adapted for CIFAR-10 ( 32 × 32 ): • Common Transformations: Both views share random resized cropping (scale [0 .2, 1.0] , bicubic interpolation), random horizontal flipping ( p = 0 .5), color jittering ( p = 0 .8, with brightness, contrast, and saturation factors of 0.4,and hue factor of 0.1), and random grayscale conversion ( p = 0 .2). • Asymmetric Blurring: View 1 applies Gaussian blur with probability p = 0 .999 , using a 3 × 3 kernel ( ≈ 10% of spatial resolution) and σ ∈ [0 .1, 2.0] . View 2 applies the identical blur kernel with probability p = 0 .1.
