## Abstract 

Accurate uncertainty quantification is critical for many time series forecasting applications, and recent studies have increasingly adopted generative models to capture predictive uncer-tainty. Existing approaches often combine deterministic forecasting with generative model-ing to improve predictive performance. However, their joint training is typically unstable because the deterministic model is embedded within the generative process, resulting in deep computational graphs and changing the probability transport path. In this work, we propose DV-Flow, a unified flow-matching formulation that incorporates the determinis-tic predictor as a parallel velocity component rather than as the endpoint of the trans-port path. This design enables stable end-to-end optimization while subsuming several existing formulations as special cases, allowing direct comparisons among them. Exten-sive experiments on six real-world datasets demonstrate that DV-Flow consistently im-proves probabilistic forecasting performance over strong baselines. Our code is available at 

https://github.com/tufts-ml/dv-flow-time-series .

## 1 Introduction 

Time series forecasting plays a crucial role in domains such as finance, logistics, and healthcare (Zhang et al., 2024). Many downstream decision-making tasks built on forecasts require probabilistic quantification of future trajectories. From a modeling standpoint, this involves learning the conditional distribution of future time series given the past. Accordingly, many deterministic and probabilistic approaches were developed. Deterministic forecasting models (Lin et al., 2024a; Wu et al., 2021; Zeng et al., 2023; Fan et al., 2022; Box & Jenkins, 1970; Sims, 1980; Liu et al., 2023; Nie et al., 2022; Lim et al., 2021) are typically trained to minimize the squared prediction error and therefore approximate the conditional mean of the data distribution. These models excel at producing accurate point forecasts, but their characterization of predictive uncertainty can only come from a Gaussian assumption derived from the mean squared error. Probabilistic models such as diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020; 2022) and flow-matching frameworks (Lipman et al., 2022; Tong et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2022) have demonstrated strong capabilities in modeling complex, high-dimensional distributions, and have been successfully applied to time series forecasting (Rasul et al., 2021; Kerrigan et al., 2024b; Tamir et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2024; Kollovieh et al., 2024b; Fan et al., 2024). Although these models enable sampling of diverse future trajectories and thus 1arXiv:2610.18621v1 [cs.LG] 13 Oct 2026 Published in Transactions on Machine Learning Research (10/2026) support full-trajectory uncertainty quantification, they are not explicitly designed to recover the conditional mean, which remains a central quantity for forecasting. To leverage the complementary strengths of deterministic and probabilistic models, recent works have ex-plored hybrid formulations that combine both components. Existing approaches typically fall into two categories. Two-stage methods (Gong et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2025) first train a deterministic predictor and then fit a probabilistic model conditioned on its output. Although stable, such approaches forgo the potential benefits of joint optimization. Alternatively, several recent methods (Li et al., 2025; Srivastava et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024) pursue fully end-to-end training by incorporating the deterministic predictor directly into diffusion or flow-matching formulations. In these approaches, the deterministic model either defines a residual variable or serves as a prior state of the generative path. A key consequence of the previous joint training designs is that the generative model takes the output of the deterministic predictor as part of its input when constructing the intermediate states of the flow or diffusion process, forming deep computational graphs and yielding a non-stationary regression task during joint optimization. Similar optimization challenges arising from moving targets have been widely recognized in reinforcement learning and adversarial learning (Mnih et al., 2015; Goodfellow et al., 2014). In addition, many existing end-to-end hybrid approaches optimize only the diffusion (or flow-matching) objective, up-dating both components without explicitly supervising the deterministic predictor, which may not learn the conditional mean and simply increases the network depth. In this work, we propose a novel joint-training flow-matching formulation that absorbs the deterministic forecast into the flow-matching velocity as a global velocity component. Specifically, the deterministic forecast gives a global velocity direction, which depends only on the historical context. The original flow-matching velocity is then retained as the local velocity, which is state- and time-dependent and captures the complex distribution of the target time series. Moreover, we add an auxiliary loss that explicitly supervises the deterministic component to leverage the strength of the deterministic point forecaster, while the local velocity captures higher-order variability and uncertainty. This formulation preserves the strengths of both deterministic and probabilistic models and enables principled, stable end-to-end joint training, leading to improved forecast performance and more reliable uncertainty quantification. We evaluate our model on six real-world benchmark datasets spanning diverse domains, including electric-ity consumption and stock prices, where reliable uncertainty quantification is critical for decision-making. Beyond comparisons with SOTA baselines, we identify a unified framework that captures existing hybrid formulations as special cases and conduct a systematic empirical study in a controlled experimental set-ting. This controlled comparison enables a direct assessment of how different hybrid formulations affect distribution fitting, velocity fitting, and optimization stability. In summary, our work contributes to research on probabilistic forecasting of time series in three aspects: • We introduce a Dual Velocity flow matching framework (DV-Flow) that treats the deterministic predictor as a velocity component and thereby enables stable end-to-end joint training. • We analyze the decomposition of probabilistic forecasting into a center prediction and variance estimation through trajectory sampling. • We evaluate DV-Flow through controlled experiments and on real-world datasets, demonstrating that the proposed DV-Flow method exhibits stable training dynamics and improves forecast proba-bilistic distribution performance. 

## 2 Related work 

Deterministic forecasting models generate single-point predictions of future values from past observations without modeling uncertainty. Classical statistical approaches such as ARIMA (Box & Jenkins, 1970) capture linear dependencies through autoregression, differencing, and moving averages. Recently, Transformer-based models achieve strong results by modeling long-range dependencies with attention, such as Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) (Lim et al., 2021), PatchTST (Nie et al., 2022), and iTransformer (Liu et al., 2023). 2Published in Transactions on Machine Learning Research (10/2026) Probabilistic models forecast distributions rather than point estimates, enabling uncertainty quantification for risk-sensitive applications such as finance, supply chain, and healthcare. Early neural approaches like DeepAR (Salinas et al., 2020) used RNNs to model conditional distributions across related series. More recent work leverages diffusion (Ho et al., 2020; 2022) and flow-matching frameworks for probabilistic forecasting (Gao et al., 2024; Fan et al., 2024). Examples include TimeGrad (Rasul et al., 2021), which introduces diffusion-based autoregression, Diffusion-TS (Yuan & Qiao, 2024), which captures seasonality and trends with Fourier-based objectives, TSDiff (Kollovieh et al., 2024a) which uses a self-guiding diffusion framework, and TSFlow (Kollovieh et al., 2024b), which applies flow matching with Gaussian Process priors. To leverage the strength of both deterministic models and probabilistic models, several works have explored hybrid methods that combine them. D3U (Li et al., 2025) leverages a pretrained point forecasting model to predict the high-certainty part of the data and trains a separate diffusion model to learn the residual distribution. STVD (Srivastava et al., 2024) combines a deterministic downscaling component for a coarser prediction and a diffusion module to learn the residual error to add finer details. DiffCast (Yu et al., 2024) trains a global deterministic motion model and a local residual diffusion. CGFM (Xu et al., 2025) combines a pretrained deterministic model and a flow matching model to learn the residual. TMDM (Li et al., 2024) leverages a deterministic transformer model to learn the expectation of future trajectory based on context, and uses it as the endpoint prior in the diffusion process. NsDiff (Ye et al., 2025) further generalizes this deterministic-as-prior formulation by pretraining conditional mean and variance estimators and using their predictions to define a context-dependent Gaussian endpoint and an uncertainty-aware noise schedule. These estimators are fixed during diffusion training, resulting in a cascaded alternative to joint end-to-end optimization. CasCast (Gong et al., 2024) combines a deterministic component and diffusion in a cascaded manner. It first trains a deterministic model, and then uses its prediction as condition in the downstream diffusion model.

## 4 Method 

In this section, we introduce DV-Flow ( Dual-Velocity Flow), a new hybrid flow-matching framework that combines global and local velocity fields for time series forecasting. In this method, we use the deterministic component as a velocity term rather than as an endpoint of the transport path. We still use Gaussian noise ϵ as the start of the ODE in the flow matching model, and p(y|x) as the target distribution. In particular, we parameterize the velocity as:

Choice of point-wise loss. We use MAE as the point-wise loss term in the objective function. As shown in Bar-inghaus & Franz (2004); Szekely et al. (2005); Gneiting & Raftery (2007), CRPS can be written as the following, where 

Z, Z ′ ∼ F are independent samples and x is the observation: CRPS = EF |Z − x| − 12 EF |Z − Z′|. (10) It shows that MAE is closely aligned with the CRPS eval-uation objective used in probabilistic forecasting. This for-mulation highlights CRPS as a distributional generalization of MAE: the first term corresponds to the expected absolute deviation from the observation, while the second term penal-izes excessive dispersion. In the degenerate case where F collapses to a point mass, CRPS reduces to MAE. Therefore, MAE provides a more natural auxiliary objective when the downstream goal is strong CRPS performance. Under this view, the deterministic model supplies a median-centered baseline forecast, while the flow component focuses on refining the predictive distribution around this baseline. Table 6 shows that training with MAE loss has an overall better CRPS sum than training with MSE loss. Furthermore, MAE supervision preserves skewness more effectively than MSE supervision on skewed data. We demonstrate it with the Electricity dataset, which exhibits large outliers during peak usage periods. As shown in Figure 6, the ℓ2 distance between the prediction mean and median decreases as λ increases under MSE training, while remaining relatively stable under MAE training. 

5.4 Ablation 

To distinguish the contribution of the proposed dual-velocity parameterization from those of SATN and the Gaussian-process prior, we conduct a controlled incremental ablation. Starting from vanilla flow matching, we progressively introduce SATN, the Gaussian-process prior, and the dual-velocity parameterization. All configurations use the same backbone architecture, optimization settings, and training budget. As shown in Table 7, DV-Flow achieves the lowest CRPS sum across all datasets, often by a substantial margin, and the lowest Q90 on four of the five datasets. Most importantly, the comparison between the complete DV-Flow model and vanilla flow matching with both SATN and the Gaussian-process prior differs only in the velocity parameterization. Therefore, the improvement between these configurations directly isolates the contribution of the proposed dual-velocity design, rather than attributing it to SATN or the Gaussian-process prior.

## 7 Conclusion 

We introduce DV-Flow, a hybrid flow-matching framework that incorporates a deterministic model as an additional velocity component, rather than treating it as the endpoint of the transport path. This design avoids deep computational graphs and preserves a fixed probability path, resulting in more stable and effi-cient end-to-end training. Empirically, DV-Flow consistently outperforms baseline methods across multiple benchmark datasets, improving both predictive performance and training stability in fully joint settings. The proposed dual-velocity parameterization may also extend to broader conditional-generation domains, which we leave for future work. 

## Impact Statement 

This work aims to improve the reliability and stability of probabilistic time series forecasting, which may benefit decision-making in applications such as weather, energy, and finance, while sharing the same consid-erations regarding data bias and responsible deployment as other forecasting models.
