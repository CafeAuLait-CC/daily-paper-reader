ABSTRACT 

Multi-teacher on-policy distillation enables rectified-flow models with diffusion-transformer architectures to combine task-specific capabilities within a single stu-dent. However, matching model outputs does not guarantee that their internal representations are aligned, and it remains unclear where and when additional fea-ture supervision is most useful. We analyze the teacher–student feature mismatch across Transformer blocks and denoising timesteps, and find that the mismatch is concentrated in deeper Transformer blocks during early denoising and persists after OPD distillation, indicating ineffective distillation. Motivated by this obser-vation, we introduce FocusOPD, which augments the base OPD objective with token-wise directional feature alignment restricted to deep layers and early de-noising steps. Across two rectified-flow backbones and multiple OPD objectives, FocusOPD improves capability transfer in compositional generation, text render-ing, and preference-based evaluation. On the primary benchmark, it improves over DiffusionOPD from 0.953 to 0.967 on GenEval, from 23.797 to 23.944 on PickScore, and from 6.052 to 6.199 on LAION-Aesthetic. Ablations show that selective deep and early supervision is more effective and efficient than alignment over the full network and trajectory. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

On-policy distillation (OPD) transfers teacher behavior through feedback on student-generated tra-jectories (Agarwal et al., 2024; Lu & Thinking Machines Lab, 2025). For diffusion models, multi-teacher OPD integrates specialized capabilities, including compositional generation, text rendering, and alignment with human preferences, into a single student (Ma et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026; Fang et al., 2026). Recent methods select denoising states for teacher feedback and align representation changes across network blocks (Zhou et al., 2026; Wei et al., 2026). Yet these developments leave open how the value of supervision varies across blocks and denoising stages. We therefore ask: 

what patterns characterize the utility of additional feature supervision across network depth and denoising steps? 

To investigate this question, we begin by examining teacher–student feature mismatch in SD3.5-M Esser et al. (2024) and Z-Image (Z-Image Team et al., 2025). Evaluating teacher and student on the same noisy inputs, we find that feature mismatch is generally larger in deeper blocks and during early, high-noise denoising. Its relative organization remains qualitatively similar before and after output-level OPD, even though its magnitude may change. We call this a persistent depth–temporal structure . The marginal trends locate larger discrepancies; the joint profiles reveal that their organi-zation persists through training. Neither observation alone explains the underlying mechanism. We examine two mechanisms consistent with this structure. Two lemmas characterize how local dis-crepancies propagate across network blocks and how weaker observations preserve prior-dependent

Building on this account, we derive a local criterion for selective feature supervision: under local smoothness and gradient-compatibility conditions, allo-cating a fixed auxiliary weight to sufficiently larger discrepancies can yield greater output-loss re-duction for small auxiliary updates. Two lightweight diagnostics support the proposed mechanisms: upstream differences dominate mismatch at selected deep blocks, and noise-dependent scaling im-proves held-out prediction over a timestep control. Motivated by this analysis, we introduce FocusOPD, a straightforward way to use the persistent depth–temporal structure in multi-teacher OPD (MOPD). It augments the base output objective with tokenwise cosine alignment of conditional image features in deep layers during early denoising. Output and feature queries are specified independently, and inference requires no additional com-ponents. On SD3.5-M with DiffusionOPD, it improves GenEval from 0.953 to 0.967, OCR accu-racy from 0.958 to 0.960, and PickScore from 23.797 to 23.944, and ImageReward from 1.471 to 1.517. We identify persistent structure of teacher-student mismatch across depth and denoising time in the MOPD of SD3.5-M and Z-Image. • Theory & Evidence (Sections 3–4). We improve capability transfer across composition, text rendering, and preference metrics. The ablations show that selective supervision of deep layers during early denoising is effective, while broader temporal or depth coverage adds cost without consistent gains.

This raises the question: What gives rise to this structure, and why does it persist after output-level distillation? Section 3 develops a mechanistic account of these observations.

5 FOCUS OPD: S TRUCTURE -G UIDED FEATURE DISTILLATION

6 EXPERIMENTS 

6.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Models and Evaluation. Following the multi-teacher setting of DiffusionOPD (Li et al., 2026), we evaluate two diffusion backbones, SD3.5-M (Esser et al., 2024) and Z-Image (Z-Image Team et al., 2025), at a resolution of 512 × 512 . For each backbone, the student and three task-specific teachers are initialized from the same base model, with teachers specializing in compositional alignment, text rendering, and human preference optimization. For SD3.5-M, we use the teachers trained in DiffusionOPD, while for Z-Image we train three teachers using the same task-specific RL protocols. All students generate ten-step on-policy trajectories with a deterministic flow solver and only LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) parameters are optimized. All methods are trained for 1000 epochs. We evaluate compositional alignment on GenEval (Ghosh et al., 2023) and report its six category scores and overall score. Text rendering is evaluated on a held-out OCR split using OCR accu-racy. Human preference and visual quality are evaluated on DrawBench (Saharia et al., 2022) with PickScore (Kirstain et al., 2023), HPSv2.1 (Wu et al., 2023), LAION-Aesthetic (Schuhmann, 2022), and ImageReward (Xu et al., 2023). We use the same evaluation prompts, resolution, sampler, guid-ance settings, random seeds, and evaluator versions for all methods within each backbone. 

Baselines. We compare the base model and single-task teachers with multi-teacher OPD methods. DiffusionOPD (Li et al., 2026) and DanceOPD (Zhou et al., 2026) represent output-level distillation, while STEP-OPD (Wei et al., 2026) additionally supervises internal representation dynamics. Our method builds on each OPD objective while preserving its output target and teacher-routing strategy. 6.2 MAIN RESULTS

Main-result analysis. We compare output-only, uniform KD, and our selective method on a fixed replay bank, pairing depth/denoising mismatch profiles with generation scores. This analy-sis tests whether better alignment in the supervised regions accompanies better generation quality, 7and whether the method with the lowest global feature error necessarily has the best task scores. Results confirm that selective supervision in the diagnosed regions (deep layers, early denoising) improves generation more effectively than uniform supervision across all positions. 

Cross-baseline and cross-backbone consistency. Our method consistently improves different OPD baselines, as shown in Table 2. DiffusionOPD, DanceOPD, and STEP-OPD each use different output objectives and query strategies, yet all benefit from the same Last-12 Front-40% feature supervision on both SD3.5-M and Z-Image backbones. This confirms that the proposed feature objective is complementary to various output-distillation protocols. The same table also shows that the diagnostic-guided selection strategy generalizes from SD3.5-M to Z-Image. While both backbones use the same Last-q principle (deep-layer, early-phase supervision), the specific q and r values are calibrated independently for each architecture through backbone-specific diagnostics and ablations. 6.3 ABLATION STUDIES

Depth selection. Table 4 examines which layers benefit most from feature supervision, holding temporal coverage fixed at Front-40%. Shallow supervision (First-6, blocks 0–5) yields minimal improvement over output-only (GenEval 0.948 vs. 0.951), suggesting that early-layer features are 8already well-aligned through output distillation. Middle layers (Mid-6, Mid-12) show moderate gains but remain below deep-layer configurations. Deep-layer supervision consistently outperforms shallow supervision: Last-6 (blocks 18–23) reaches GenEval 0.962 and OCR 0.962, the highest OCR among all depth windows. Expanding to Last-12 (blocks 12–23) achieves a LAION-Aesthetic score of 6.214 while maintaining strong com-positional performance (GenEval 0.960). Further expansion to Last-18 or All-24 increases GenEval slightly (0.962, 0.965) but at diminishing returns: All-24 requires double the feature computation of Last-12 with only +0.5% GenEval gain. Non-contiguous selection (First-6+Last-6) does not out-perform Last-12, confirming that a contiguous deep window suffices. These results support the hypothesis that mismatch concentrated in deeper blocks benefits most from explicit alignment, as these representations are more task-specific and further from the shared backbone prior. 

Temporal selection. Table 5 varies the denoising-step coverage with Last-12 depth fixed. Supervis-ing the full trajectory (steps 0–9) provides no gain over output-only (GenEval 0.951 for both), despite supervising 2.5 × more layer-step pairs than Front-40%. Middle 40% (steps 3–6) and Last 40% (steps 6–9) similarly underperform, with Last-40% yielding the lowest LAION-Aesthetic (6.092) and HPSv2.1 (0.335) among all temporal configurations. In contrast, early-phase supervision drives consistent improvements. Front-20% (steps 0–1) already lifts GenEval from 0.951 to 0.959. Extending to Front-40% (steps 0–3) reaches GenEval 0.960 and LAION-Aesthetic 6.214, the best aesthetic score across all temporal windows. Front-50% matches this GenEval and LAION-Aesthetic, with marginal HPSv2.1 gain (+0.001), but extending further to Front-60% or Full trajectory degrades performance. The sharp drop from Front-50% (GenEval 0.960) to Full (0.951) indicates that late denoising steps add no value to feature supervision and may even dilute the training signal from high-noise steps. This occurs because noise levels are low and model priors converge at late steps. This empirically confirms the prediction that feature mismatch matters most when noise modulates sensitivity to prior differences, precisely the regime our theory identifies. 7 RELATED WORK 

We provide a detailed discussion of related work in Appendix B. On-policy distillation (OPD) trains students on their own trajectories, reducing the distribution mismatch between training and inference (Agarwal et al., 2024). Recent studies (Li et al., 2026; Fang et al., 2026; Zhou et al., 2026; Jiang et al., 2026) extend OPD to multi-teacher capability integration in diffusion models, but primarily rely on output-level supervision. Feature distillation Romero et al. (2015); Lee et al. (2024); Yu et al. (2025); Wei et al. (2026) adds intermediate representation supervision, while selective knowledge distillation (Liang et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024) adapts the supervised content or location according to teacher relevance. However, how to select feature supervision across network depth and denoising stages in multi-teacher diffusion OPD remains underexplored. Building on output-level OPD, our method supervises conditional image-token representations in selected deep blocks during early denoising to study their depth- and stage-dependent benefits. 98 CONCLUSION 

In this work, we investigate where and when intermediate supervision is most effective for multi-teacher on-policy distillation in diffusion models. We identify a persistent depth–temporal structure in teacher–student mismatch and provide theoretical and empirical evidence linking it to selective supervision. Building on these findings, we introduce FocusOPD, which complements output-level distillation with directional feature alignment in deep layers during early denoising. Experiments across two diffusion backbones and multiple OPD baselines show consistent gains, demonstrating the effectiveness of structure-guided feature supervision for multi-teacher capability integration.

B FULL RELATED WORK .

On-policy distillation for capability integration. On-policy distillation (OPD) (Agarwal et al., 2024) transfers teacher knowledge on student-generated trajectories, addressing the distribution mis-match between training and inference. Recent work (Ma et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026; Fang et al., 2026) extends this paradigm to capability integration from multiple teachers in language and diffu-sion models. DiffusionOPD Li et al. (2026) derives transition-level supervision for diffusion pro-cesses, while Flow-OPD Fang et al. (2026) combines task-specialized teachers with a task-agnostic anchor for multi-task alignment. DanceOPD Zhou et al. (2026) formulates capability transfer as gen-erative field distillation, using sample-wise routing and a single low-noise velocity query. D-OPSD Jiang et al. (2026) applies on-policy self-distillation to continual adaptation of few-step models. Building on output-level OPD, our work investigates intermediate teacher supervision for multi-task capability transfer. 

Feature alignment in diffusion models. Feature distillation supplements output matching with intermediate teacher representations (Romero et al., 2015). In diffusion models, KOALA Lee et al. (2024) studies self-attention distillation for compact text-to-image generators, while REPA Yu et al. (2025) aligns denoising features with pretrained visual representations to improve diffusion-transformer training. STEP-OPD (Wei et al., 2026) introduces representation-change alignment alongside output extrapolation, matching the direction and magnitude of inter-block updates. Our method directly aligns conditional image-token representations within selected deep blocks and an early denoising window, with feature supervision scheduled independently of output queries. 

Selective and adaptive knowledge distillation. Selective distillation accounts for differences in the relevance and transferability of teacher knowledge. TED Liang et al. (2023) learns task-aware fil-ters for intermediate representations in language-model compression. DFD Liu et al. (2024) assigns different distillation constraints to spatial regions according to teacher–student feature disparity in object detection. SAKD learns a routing policy to adapt distillation locations to individual samples and training iterations. These methods select feature content, spatial constraints, or distillation lo-cations in discriminative tasks.
