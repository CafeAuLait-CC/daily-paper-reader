ABSTRACT
Generating human-object interactions (HOIs) with multiple objects is challenging
because the resulting motions arise from different underlying causes. Human
motion reflects semantic intent, intentionally manipulated objects must remain
coordinated with the human, while surrounding objects move primarily as physical
consequences of contact and collision. We present BUMP, a text-driven framework
for multi-object HOI generation based on a simple principle: learn intentional
motion and simulate physical consequences. Our method first generates full-body
human motion from text and scene context, then predicts trajectories for a small
number of intentionally manipulated driver objects conditioned on the human mo-
tion. We then obtain responder object motions through physics simulation, allowing
the framework to handle varying numbers and configurations of surrounding ob-
jects without expanding the learned trajectory output space. To address the scarcity
of paired full-body multi-object HOI data, we combine heterogeneous supervision
from human-only motion, full-body HOI, and hand-centric manipulation data aug-
mented with plausible body motion. We further introduce outcome-conditioned
interaction editing, which infers a local modification of the causal driver object
trajectory from a desired responder object behavior without requiring an explicit
target driver object trajectory. Experiments demonstrate strong performance on
both single-object and multi-object HOI benchmarks. Qualitative results showcase
diverse scenarios involving inter-object relationships and edits, highlighting the
advantage of our unified model formulation.

INTRODUCTION
Human activities in the real world rarely involve a single object in isolation. Everyday behaviors such
as preparing food or playing sports often involve intentionally manipulating one or two objects while
interacting with several surrounding objects. While most HOI generation methods focus on a single
manipulated object (Li et al., 2023; 2024; Xu et al., 2023; Peng et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2024; Wu
et al., 2025), recent work has begun to address multi-object interactions (Lv et al., 2024; Lu et al.,
2025; Di et al., 2026; Pratikaki et al., 2026). However, explicitly generating every interacting object
couples human motion, multiple object trajectories, contacts, and inter-object relationships within the
learned generation problem.
We argue that these sources of complexity need not be modeled symmetrically. The semantic structure
of an interaction is often expressed primarily by human motion and intentionally manipulated
objects. For example, swinging a baseball bat is defined by the human and bat motions, while the
subsequent motion of a struck ball, or other objects displaced by that ball, is largely determined by
contact dynamics. This motivates a simple principle: learn intentional motion and simulate physical
consequences.
Based on this principle, we propose BUMP, a text-conditioned framework that decomposes an
interaction into the human, intentionally manipulated driver objects, and physically induced responder
objects. We first generate full-body human motion from text and scene context, then generate a small
number of driver object trajectories conditioned on the human. Responder object trajectories are
obtained through physics simulation from contacts with the human, driver objects, or other responder

To address limited full-body multi-object HOI supervision, we combine human-only motion, captured

to realize the requested outcome.
In summary, our main contributions are:
• We propose BUMP, a factorized multi-object HOI framework that learns human and driver
object motion and simulates responder object motion, decoupling the learned trajectory space
from the number of responder objects.
• We introduce a heterogeneous full-body interaction data construction strategy that lifts fine-
grained hand-centric and partial-body manipulation data to a common full-body representation,
providing additional dexterous and multi-object supervision.
• We introduce outcome-conditioned interaction editing that locally modifies driver object motion
to achieve a desired responder object behavior without an explicit target driver object trajectory.

RELATED WORK
Human-Object Interaction Modeling. Prior work has modeled human-object affordances, contacts,
and geometric relationships from 3D observations (Deng et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2023; 2024;
Tripathi et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024), providing useful priors for interaction locations. Full-body
HOI generation methods such as OMOMO (Li et al., 2023), CHOIS (Li et al., 2024), InterDiff (Xu
et al., 2023), HOIDiff (Peng et al., 2025), AffordMotion (Wang et al., 2024), TesMo (Yi et al., 2024),
and InterDreamer (Xu et al., 2024) further synthesize full-body human motion for object and scene
interactions. Recent approaches improve generalization or physical plausibility, e.g., DAViD (Kim
et al., 2025a) adapts a pretrained human-motion prior to HOI, while HOIfHLI (Wu et al., 2025)
combines kinematic generation with physics-based tracking. Methods that jointly generate human
and object motion primarily focus on a single manipulated object.
Multi-Object HOI Generation. Recent work extends HOI synthesis to multiple objects. HIMO (Lv
et al., 2024) generates full-body two- and three-object interactions with a dual-branch diffusion model,
while Dex2HOI (Pratikaki et al., 2026) coordinates multiple object-motion streams for dexterous
one- and two-object manipulation. PAMotion (Di et al., 2026) further introduces physics-aware
2


objectives for joint generation of human and multi-object trajectories. In these approaches, interacting
object motions remain explicit outputs of learned generative models. Our work instead separates
intentionally manipulated driver objects from physically induced responder objects, learning only
human and driver object motion while obtaining responder object trajectories through simulation.
This allows additional objects without expanding the learned trajectory output space.
HOI Data and Heterogeneous Supervision. Full-body multi-object HOI data remains limited.
HIMO (Lv et al., 2024), HUMOTO (Lu et al., 2025), and ParaHome (Kim et al., 2025b) capture
multi-object interactions, while GRAB (Taheri et al., 2020), InterCap (Huang et al., 2024), BE-
HAVE (Bhatnagar et al., 2022), ARCTIC (Fan et al., 2023), and InterAct (Xu et al., 2025) provide
complementary HOI supervision. We additionally exploit broader human-motion data and fine-
grained manipulation datasets such as TACO (Liu et al., 2024b) and OakInk2 (Zhan et al., 2024),
lifting heterogeneous sources into a common full-body representation.
Physics-Based Interaction and Editing. Existing physics-aware HOI methods use physics to
regularize, refine, or track motions produced by learned models (Di et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2025). In
contrast, our simulator directly produces responder object trajectories, making physics part of the
generative process itself. We further exploit this causal structure for outcome-conditioned editing
by modifying the causal driver object motion from a desired responder object outcome rather than
requiring a prescribed object trajectory.

driver object motion and simulating responder object motion. Driver objects are those intentionally
manipulated by the human, whereas responder objects move as physical consequences of contact
and collision involving the human, driver objects, or other responder objects. Formally, given a text

3.2
STAGE 1: HUMAN MOTION GENERATION
We first generate the human trajectory H, which serves as a fixed condition for the subsequent stages
(Eq. 2). GH is trained to recover the clean sequence H from its noised version, conditioned on the
text ℓ, the initial pose h1, and the scene O0. Each input object oi is embedded as a separate token,
giving N ≤4 scene tokens.
Optional human guidance. For qualitative visualization, we optionally guide reverse diffusion
toward contact and force-closure grasp targets while penalizing penetration and temporal artifacts;
this guidance is not used for quantitative evaluation.

3.4
STAGE 3: RESPONDER OBJECTS BY PHYSICS SIMULATION
We obtain responder object trajectories R by kinematically replaying human and driver object motion
in a rigid-body physics simulator and letting responder objects react to contacts (Eq. 4). We build a
MuJoCo (Todorov et al., 2012) scene from the object meshes and their initial poses. Each mesh is
decomposed into multiple convex collision parts with CoACD (Wei et al., 2022). Responder objects
are dynamic rigid bodies with a shared uniform density and the same MJCF contact parameters, so
no per-object mass or inertia measurements are required. The generated human motion drives capsule
and sphere collision proxies attached to the body, with human-driver object collisions disabled. The
simulator never modifies the prescribed human and driver object motion.
The human and driver object trajectories are defined at 30 fps and replayed at 60 fps using spline
interpolation, with 36 simulation substeps per playback frame.
3.5
OUTCOME-CONDITIONED INTERACTION EDITING
Given a desired responder object outcome gR, we locally edit the causal driver object trajectory,
evaluate the response through simulation, and retarget the human motion to the edited driver object.
Goals specify either a motion direction and response strength, optionally with a rotation direction, or
a spatial relation to preserve.
For motion goals, we map the direction category to a unit vector u ∈R3 at the interaction event.
Horizontal directions are defined relative to the human yaw, while vertical directions follow the
world vertical axis. Response strength specifies a directional speed interval [smin, smax], and α is the
allowed angular deviation from u.

3.6
HETEROGENEOUS INTERACTION DATA CONSTRUCTION
Data collection and unification. We combine human-only motion, captured full-body HOI, and
fine-grained manipulation data into a common full-body representation. Human-only motion comes
from HumanML3D, Motion-X++, AIST++, BEAT2, OpenBiomechanicsProject, and LAFAN1 (Guo
et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2025; Li et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2024a; Wasserberger et al., 2022; Harvey
et al., 2020). Full-body interactions are obtained from OMOMO, HuMoTo, GRAB, and InterAct (Li
et al., 2023; Lu et al., 2025; Taheri et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2025), while TACO and OakInk2 provide
detailed hand-object manipulation (Liu et al., 2024b; Zhan et al., 2024). We map all human motions
to a common neutral-body model, resample all sequences to 30 fps, and segment them into 120-frame
windows.
Object role assignment. We assign object roles automatically per training window using object
motion and hand proximity. Moving objects whose sampled surfaces come within 2 cm of a hand
joint in any frame are driver object candidates; others are responder objects. We retain at most two
driver objects, selecting the highest-scoring candidates when necessary.
Full-body fitting from incomplete observations. For hand-only TACO sequences, we preserve ob-
served finger articulation and fit a neutral SMPL-X body to wrist trajectories, regularizing unobserved
regions toward a neutral standing posture and encouraging temporal smoothness. For OakInk2, we
retarget the observed upper body and hands, then complete the lower body with grounded, stationary
feet while limiting changes to the observed upper body.
Canonicalization and supervision. For each window, human and object trajectories share a canon-
ical coordinate frame defined by the initial human heading and horizontal root position. Hand-
object contact labels use the same 2 cm surface-proximity criterion as driver object identification.
Dataset-specific supervision masks account for missing or reconstructed body channels; observed
manipulation signals are treated as more reliable than synthetically completed body motion.

4.1
DATASETS
Benchmark evaluation. For single-object interaction, we use InterAct, while for multi-object
interaction we use the two- and three-object subsets of HIMO as separate evaluation settings. Within
each setting, BUMP and all compared methods are trained using the same benchmark-specific training
data and evaluated under the same split and evaluation protocol. The heterogeneous corpus introduced
in Sec. 3.6 is not used for these apples-to-apples benchmark comparisons.
Heterogeneous-supervision evaluation. We separately evaluate the effect of the heterogeneous
training corpus from Sec. 3.6, which combines human-only motion, captured full-body HOI, and
lifted manipulation data. For these ablations, we keep the evaluation set fixed to the held-out
HUMOTO split and vary only the available training supervision. The complete corpus contains
57,434 human-only training windows and 65,237 HOI training windows, including 13,545 lifted
TACO and OakInk2 windows.
4.2
EVALUATION METRICS
We evaluate both the quality of the generated motion distribution and the consistency of the resulting
human-object interaction. All metrics are computed independently for each benchmark setting;
therefore, scores are compared only among methods evaluated on the same dataset and object-count.
8


Generation quality and text alignment. Following the standard text-to-motion evaluation proto-
col (Guo et al., 2022), we report Fr´echet Inception Distance (FID), R-Precision (R@1/2/3), Mul-
timodal Distance (MM-Dist), and Diversity. FID measures the discrepancy between the feature
distributions of generated and ground-truth motions, with lower values indicating better distributional
agreement. R-Precision evaluates text-motion retrieval accuracy, while MM-Dist measures the dis-
tance between matched text and motion embeddings. Diversity measures variation among generated
motions. Because larger diversity is not necessarily better, we additionally report the ground-truth
diversity in parentheses and interpret generated values by their proximity to this reference.
Interaction quality. We evaluate hand-object interaction consistency using pairwise Contact F1.
For each frame, contact is determined separately for every hand-object pair using a 2 cm proximity
threshold. Contact F1 is computed over these hand-object-frame assignments, such that a prediction
is correct only when both the contacting hand and the contacted object match the ground truth. We
report Contact F1 as a percentage.
4.3
RESULTS
Quantitative comparisons. Table 1 compares BUMP with prior methods across single- and multi-
object interaction settings. BUMP achieves competitive generation quality on InterAct while showing
improved hand-object contact consistency. The gains become more evident in the multi-object HIMO
settings, where BUMP maintains strong generation quality while substantially improving Contact F1
over existing methods. These results support our factorized formulation for coordinated multi-object
interaction generation.
Qualitative comparisons. Figure 4 shows representative generations across the single-, two-, and
three-object settings. BUMP produces coordinated full-body and object motion while maintaining
the hand-object relationships required by each interaction. In multi-object examples, the generated
motions additionally preserve the coordination among simultaneously manipulated objects.
4.4
ABLATION STUDY
Lifted manipulation data. We first study the effect of augmenting full-body HOI training with
the lifted TACO and OakInk2 sequences. As shown in Table 1, removing the lifted data degrades
both generation quality and Contact F1 on the held-out HUMOTO evaluation. This suggests that
the additional hand-centric manipulation data provides useful supervision for learning detailed
multi-object interaction patterns. Qualitatively, Fig. 5(a) further shows improved hand and finger
coordination on a held-out GRAB sample.
Human-motion pretraining. We next evaluate the contribution of human-only motion pretraining.
Pretraining improves FID, text alignment, and Contact F1, while also increasing the diversity of the
generated motions. Interestingly, the resulting Diversity is higher than that of the held-out HUMOTO
distribution, suggesting that the human-motion prior broadens the motion space beyond the relatively
limited dynamics present in the HOI training data. As shown in Fig. 5(b) on a held-out HUMOTO
scene, this effect is particularly visible for prompts requiring larger and more dynamic full-body
motion.

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
We presented BUMP, a text-driven multi-object HOI framework that separates intentional motion
from physical consequences. BUMP generates the human and a small number of manipulated
driver objects, while responder object motion is obtained through physics simulation. We further
leverage heterogeneous supervision from human-only, full-body HOI, and lifted manipulation data,
and introduce outcome-conditioned editing that modifies the causal driver object trajectory from a
desired responder object behavior.
Limitations. Our current model supports at most two learned driver objects and assumes rigid-body
responder objects with prescribed human and driver object trajectories during simulation. The editing
process is local and contact-driven, and part of our training data relies on fitted full-body motion
from hand-centric observations. Extending the framework to more driver objects, richer physical
interactions, and long-horizon editing remains future work.
