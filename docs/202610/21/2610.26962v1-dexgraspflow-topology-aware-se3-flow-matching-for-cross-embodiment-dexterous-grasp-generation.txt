DexGraspFlow: Topology-Aware SE(3) Flow
Matching for Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Grasp
Generation
Bin Zhou1, Boyu Ma1,†, Xinyu Zhou1, Jiahua Dong1, Chuhao Zhou1, Yizhou Liu1, Hao Wan2, Jianfei Yang1,†
1MARS Lab, Nanyang Technological University, Singapore, 2ROKAE Robotics
†Corresponding authors
Differences in hand geometry, joint parameterization, and kinematic topology pose challenges for
cross-embodiment dexterous grasp generation. However, spatial relations among links alone are
insufficient to fully capture the hand’s kinematic topology. We propose DexGraspFlow, a conditional
link-level SE(3) flow-matching framework integrating geometric relations and kinematic topology. The
framework encodes local object geometry, link geometry, and current poses. A dual-stream DiT models
link–link coordination and link–object interaction, while attention biases derived from kinematic-tree
distances provide explicit structural information for modeling evolving geometric interactions. The
model jointly learns translational and rotational velocity fields, integrates them to generate link
poses, and recovers joint configurations through inverse kinematics. Additionally, inference-time
palm-orientation guidance enables controllable grasp generation without retraining. Across three
robotic hands and 30 object–hand pairs, DexGraspFlow achieves a 95.33% average simulation success
rate, with an average generation latency, including inverse kinematics, approximately 49% lower
than that of a state-of-the-art baseline. Further experiments demonstrate zero-shot adaptability to
seven finger-removal variants and palm-orientation control. A model trained on demonstrations for
the deployed hand achieves a 91% success rate over 100 real-robot grasping trials, supporting the
framework’s applicability to physical grasp execution.

Introduction
Dexterous hands are robotic end-effectors that inter-
act directly with the physical world, performing the
last centimeter of robotic manipulation. Through
coordinated finger motions, they enable versatile
grasping and precise manipulation, supporting the
practical deployment of embodied intelligence (Dong
et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026).
Reliable dexter-
ous grasping requires jointly accounting for object
geometry, hand–object interactions, and kinematic
constraints (Wang et al., 2023). Differences in link
geometry and kinematic topology across hands fur-
ther complicate cross-embodiment grasp generation.
Embodiment-specific joint representations differ in
dimensionality and physical meaning, making it dif-
ficult to learn a unified model directly from grasp
demonstrations collected across different hands (Li
et al., 2023). Training a separate model for each
hand also increases training and deployment costs.
These challenges motivate cross-embodiment dexter-
ous grasp generation methods that share grasping
knowledge while accommodating differences in hand
morphology and kinematics, facilitating the reuse of
grasping capabilities across robotic platforms.
Existing approaches address cross-embodiment grasp
synthesis through optimization or shared interac-
tion representations. Optimization-based methods
require computationally intensive iterative search for
each grasp instance (Liu et al., 2021), while contact-
and distance-based formulations necessitate auxiliary
matching or complex reconstruction to recover ex-
ecutable configurations (Li et al., 2023; Wei et al.,
2025). Recently, T (R, O) Grasp (TRO-Grasp) in-
troduced a transformation-graph diffusion model via
object-centric link poses and relative spatial relations
without requiring a shared joint parameterization (Fei
et al., 2026). However, relying solely on continuous
spatial relations ignores kinematic topology: spatially
adjacent links across diverse hands may belong to dis-
tant topological branches with fundamentally differ-
ent kinematic meanings. GraspGraphNet (Lee et al.,

message passing, but propagating information be-
tween distant links along the kinematic tree requires
multiple graph hops. Beyond unconditioned synthe-
sis, practical deployment also requires efficient control
over grasp approach orientations while maintaining
grasp stability across different hand embodiments.
To this end, we propose DexGraspFlow, a cross-
embodiment dexterous grasp synthesis framework
that unifies continuous SE(3) flow matching with
URDF-derived kinematic topology (Fig. 1). To re-
solve structural ambiguities across heterogeneous
hands, DexGraspFlow augments dynamic link states
and spatial relations with kinematic-tree shortest-
path distances, providing complementary geomet-
ric and structural information that explicitly sep-
arates spatial arrangement from kinematic connec-
tivity. Grasp synthesis is formulated as link-level
flow matching with separate translational reference
paths in R3 and geodesic rotational reference paths on
SO(3) (Lipman et al., 2023; Chen and Lipman, 2024).
This formulation preserves valid link orientations and
supports inference-time palm-orientation guidance
through tangent-space velocity corrections without
retraining. A geometry- and topology-aware dual-
stream DiT (Peebles and Xie, 2023) predicts these
coupled velocity fields, followed by batched inverse
kinematics (Kim et al., 2025) to recover executable
joint configurations.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section 2 reviews related work on cross-embodiment
grasp representations and geometric generative mod-
els. Section 3 presents the grasp representation and
geometry- and topology-aware dual-stream DiT ar-
chitecture of DexGraspFlow.
Section 4 evaluates
DexGraspFlow through grasp generation on unseen
objects, zero-shot generation on finger-removal vari-
ants, and palm-orientation-conditioned generation,
along with ablation studies and real-robot experi-
ments. Section 5 concludes the study and discusses
the limitations of the proposed framework and direc-
tions for future research.
The main contributions of this work are as follows:
• We address the core discrepancy that geometric
spatial relations do not reflect kinematic topo-
logical connectivity across diverse embodiments,
proposing a topology-aware link representation
that couples dynamic spatial interactions with
kinematic-tree distances to preserve structural
coordination under hand topology changes.

2.1
Cross-embodiment grasp representa-
tions
GraspIt!

supports
optimization-based grasp planning, while DFC (Liu
et al., 2021) and DexGraspNet (Wang et al., 2023)
employ iterative optimization for grasp synthesis
and dataset construction, incurring per-instance
computational costs. FFHNet (Mayer et al., 2022)
and UniDexGrasp (Xu et al., 2023) represent grasp
configurations through global hand poses and joint
coordinates, while UniDexGrasp++ (Wan et al.,
2023) learns grasping policies for a hand-specific
state and action space. However, differences in joint
dimensionality and semantics complicate sharing
these configurations across embodiments, motivating
hand–object interaction representations that do not
require a common joint parameterization.
GenDexGrasp (Li et al., 2023) uses hand-agnostic con-
tact maps and recovers hand configurations through
contact matching and optimization. D(R, O) Grasp
(DRO-Grasp) (Wei et al., 2025) predicts robot–
object point-to-point distances using a configuration-
invariant pretrained hand encoder, followed by link-
pose reconstruction and joint-configuration recovery.
Its dense pairwise representation incurs memory over-
head, while encoder pretraining adds a separate train-
ing stage. TRO-Grasp (Fei et al., 2026) models hands
via relative spatial transformations on fully connected
link–object and link–link edges. However, spatial
link proximity alone is insufficient across diverse em-
bodiments, as spatially adjacent links can belong
to disparate topological branches that convey funda-
mentally different kinematic semantics. Alternatively,
GraspGraphNet (Lee et al., 2026) encodes articulated
hand states using a kinematic-tree MPNN and incor-
porates object information through cross-attention
and dynamic interaction edges. Within its kinematic-
tree encoder, information exchange between distant
links relies on successive local message-passing steps.
DexGraspFlow instead uses link–link self-attention
to directly model interactions between all valid link
pairs, with kinematic-tree distance biases providing
structural information alongside state-dependent ge-
ometric relations. This design combines direct long-
range link interaction with explicit kinematic prox-
imity encoding.
2.2
Geometric generative models
Generative grasp synthesis has been explored through
adversarial and diffusion models (Feng et al., 2024;
Weng et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024), drawing on
broader denoising and score-based formulations (Ho
et al., 2020; Song et al., 2020). Flow-based approaches
learn continuous transport dynamics in Euclidean and
non-Euclidean spaces (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al.,
2023; Chen and Lipman, 2024).
Complementary
advances in equivariant geometric modeling (Fuchs
et al., 2020; Satorras et al., 2021), diffusion-based
grasp and motion optimization (Urain et al., 2023),
and Transformer generative architectures (Peebles
and Xie, 2023) provide foundations for geometry-
aware generation.
For cross-embodiment grasping, these formulations
differ in their conditioning requirements and the
computational path to executable grasps.
DRO-
Grasp (Wei et al., 2025) combines CVAE latent vari-
ables with features of an initial hand configuration
and the object to predict grasp distances. Although
latent sampling enables diversity, generation remains
conditioned on the initial hand state and requires
subsequent pose reconstruction.
TRO-Grasp (Fei
et al., 2026) instead samples link poses from noise
through graph diffusion in Lie-algebra coordinates.
Its denoiser processes node and edge features through
multiple graph layers, with repeated evaluations ac-
cumulating sampling costs. DexGraspFlow adopts
link-level SE(3) flow matching, jointly learning trans-
lational and rotational velocities from Euclidean and
geodesic reference paths, respectively.
Geometric
interactions and kinematic topology condition the
3


velocity field within a unified formulation support-
ing both unconditioned and condition-guided grasp
generation. Our experiments demonstrate lower gen-
eration latency than DRO-Grasp and TRO-Grasp
while maintaining competitive grasp success.
3
DexGraspFlow framework
DexGraspFlow generates cross-embodiment dexter-
ous grasps by conditioning continuous SE(3) flow
matching on object geometry, hand morphology, and
kinematic topology (Fig. 1). First, the object and
hand encoders extract object-patch tokens and static
link tokens from object and local link point clouds,
respectively (Sec. 3.1). Next, grasp generation is for-
mulated as link-level SE(3) flow matching (Sec. 3.2),
where a geometry- and topology-aware dual-stream
DiT jointly predicts translational and rotational ve-
locities via parallel link–link and link–object attention
(Sec. 3.3). Finally, flow integration can be steered via
training-free velocity corrections for palm-orientation
guidance, after which batched inverse kinematics re-
solves the generated link poses into executable joint
configurations (Sec. 3.4).

4.6
Real-robot experiments
Experimental setup and protocol. We conduct real-
robot experiments using the right arm of a ROKAE
Wheeled Dual-Arm Robot equipped with a Linker
Hand L20Lite, as shown in Fig. 9. An Intel RealSense
D455 captures RGB-D observations and object point
clouds, while FoundationPose (Wen et al., 2024) esti-
mates target-object poses. DexGraspFlow is trained
on 13,566 Linker Hand L20Lite grasp demonstrations
and evaluated on 10 physical objects unseen during
training.
Generated link poses are converted into joint con-
figurations through Pyroki IK and are mapped to
hand actuator commands. To ensure firm contact

5
Conclusion and limitation
5.1
Conclusion
We presented DexGraspFlow, a cross-embodiment
dexterous grasp generation framework that integrates
object and hand geometry, link-level poses, and kine-
matic topology into a shared representation. Grasp
generation is formulated through translational flow
matching in Euclidean space and rotational flow
matching on SO(3), with both velocity components
jointly predicted by a geometry- and topology-aware
dual-stream DiT. This design combines evolving spa-
tial interactions with the hand’s fixed kinematic
structure throughout generation. Batched inverse
kinematics converts the generated link poses into
joint configurations for execution. Crucially, closed-
form geodesic steering during flow integration en-
ables training-free, orientation-controllable dexterous
grasping, effectively bridging the gap between un-
constrained pose synthesis and task-oriented robotic
manipulation.
We evaluate DexGraspFlow in three settings: grasp
generation on unseen objects across different hand
embodiments, zero-shot grasp generation with unseen
finger-removal variants of these hands, and real-robot
execution using a model trained on grasp demon-
strations for the deployed hand. Across 30 held-out
object–hand pairs, it achieves a 95.33% average sim-
ulation success rate with an end-to-end latency of
0.126–0.136 s per grasp, approximately 49% lower on
average than that of TRO-Grasp. Under six-direction
palm-orientation conditioning, it achieves 86.69% suc-
cess with a mean palm-orientation error of 13.92 deg.
The framework further achieves 88.74% success on
seven finger-removal variants without retraining. In
real-robot experiments, a model trained on Linker
Hand L20Lite grasp demonstrations succeeds in 91
of 100 grasping trials on unseen physical objects. To-
gether, these results demonstrate the framework’s
effectiveness in grasp generation on unseen objects,
adaptation to finger-removal topology changes, orien-
tation guidance, and real-robot execution.
5.2
Limitation
Despite zero-shot generalization to finger-removal
variants, DexGraspFlow still faces challenges in gener-
alizing to completely novel hand embodiments whose
morphology differs fundamentally from the training
hands. In addition, the flow field generates link poses
in SE(3) without explicitly enforcing joint limits or
collision avoidance, leaving physical feasibility to the
subsequent IK optimization. Future work will in-
corporate differentiable kinematic and penetration
constraints directly into generative flow integration,
expand training hand diversity, and explore contact-
and task-oriented conditioning for versatile down-
stream manipulation.
