# AcrossVAM2.0: Conflict-Aware Point Supervision for Parameter-Efficient Video Diffusion Adaptation 

Nan Wu 1, Yee Hung Hong 11. Across Physcis (Beijing) Technology Co., Ltd 

Abstract 

Explicit point correspondence is attractive for adapting video generators: it describes how visible surfaces move, but can overwrite the appearance prior learned by a large pretrained model. We study this tension in a 5.01B-parameter text-to-video diffusion transformer. ACROSSVAM2.0 combines a confidence-weighted track-and-visibility head, zero-initialized residual feedback, and asymmetric PCGrad that removes only the auxiliary component opposing the primary flow-matching gradient on shared LoRA parameters. The design preserves the generator exactly at initialization and protects its primary update during adaptation. In a four-way ablation trained for 1,000 steps with three independent seeds, the full method lowers KLT forward–backward cycle error in all three seeds relative to a matched flow-only continuation (mean seed-level difference −0.00711 , SD 0.00505 ), while robust sharpness remains positive in all three. An expanded 24-prompt evaluation (216 videos) yields 9.7% lower aggregate CoTracker3 cycle error than the matched control and near-matched performance on six VBench dimensions; however, the seed-level tracking effect is heterogeneous and I3D-FVD regresses from 0.1824 to 0.2022. These results support a bounded claim: conflict-aware adaptation improves correspondence consistency without broad perceptual degradation, but does not uniformly improve video quality. We release equations, pseudocode, matched outputs, source tables, and artifact provenance. 

## 1 Introduction 

Text-to-video models have progressed from diffusion in pixel space to latent video diffusion and large diffusion transformers (Ho et al. 2020; Ho, Salimans, et al. 2022; Rombach et al. 2022; Peebles and Xie 2023; He et al. 2022; Blattmann et al. 2023). Open systems including ModelScope, Wan, and HunyuanVideo make high-capacity generators available for adaptation (J. Wang et al. 2023; WanTeam et al. 2025; Kong et al. 2024). Yet framewise realism does not guarantee that a surface point remains the same point after deformation, contact, camera motion, occlusion, or reappearance. This matters wherever generated video is edited, controlled by trajectories, or consumed as a model of physical change. Point trajectories provide a compact and interpretable description of such change. Tracking-any-point work has improved long-range correspondence and occlusion reasoning (Doersch et al. 2022, 2023, 2024; Harley et al. 2022; Karaev et al. 2024; Q. Wang et al. 2023), while controllable generation increasingly uses motion trajectories as an input interface (Geng et al. 2025). A tempting strategy is therefore to add track prediction as an auxiliary objective to a pretrained generator. The difficulty is optimization: the generative loss protects a broad appearance prior, whereas pseudo-tracks emphasize locally trackable edges. When both update shared parameters, a weighted sum can move the generator against its primary gradient. This paper isolates that problem rather than proposing an all-purpose motion architecture. We ask one question: can explicit correspondence supervision improve a technical temporal-consistency proxy without sacrificing a robust appearance safeguard during parameter-efficient adaptation? We answer it with the smallest falsifiable system, shown in Figure 1. A head predicts displacement and visibility from one transformer block. A zero-initialized residual can return these features to the generative path. On shared LoRA parameters (Hu et al. 2022), we measure the flow and structure gradients separately and project the latter only when their dot product is negative. Structure-only parameters receive the ordinary, unprojected gradient of the summed objective. Our contributions are: 1. a confidence-weighted track-and-visibility objective that attaches explicit correspondence to dense video latents without replacing them with a structural bottleneck; 2. a zero-initialized residual feedback path that exactly preserves the pretrained forward function at the start of adaptation; 3. an asymmetric PCGrad rule that protects the generative flow objective only where it shares parameters with the structural task; and 4. a matched four-way, three-seed study that separates prediction, feedback, and conflict handling, followed by a frozen 24-prompt evaluation with CoTracker3, six VBench dimensions, and I3D-FVD. AcrossVAM2.0 · ICLR 2027 preprint The evidence is deliberately bounded. KLT and CoTracker3 cycle errors are technical self-consistency diagnostics, not ground-truth semantic identity. The expanded evaluation has only three independent training seeds, and the prepared blinded human study remains unscored. We report these limits because they determine what the experiment can establish.

## 2 Related work 

Video diffusion and efficient adaptation. 

Video diffusion systems differ in data, latent representation, temporal modules, and scale (Ho, Salimans, et al. 2022; Singer et al. 2022; Ho, Chan, et al. 2022; Villegas et al. 2023; He et al. 2022; Guo et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2024; Blattmann et al. 2023). Diffusion transformers provide a scalable token backbone (Peebles and Xie 2023); DDIM and EDM systematize important sampling and design choices (Song et al. 2021; Karras et al. 2022); and flow matching or rectified-flow formulations offer a continuous transport view of generation (Lipman et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2023). Parameter-efficient methods insert small trainable modules (Houlsby et al. 2019) or low-rank updates (Hu et al. 2022); conditional branches such as ControlNet preserve a pretrained path while learning new controls (Zhang et al. 2023). Our setting is different from user-specified motion control: trajectories supervise adaptation, while inference remains text-to-video. 

Correspondence supervision. 

Classical Shi–Tomasi features and Lucas–Kanade tracking provide efficient short-baseline pseudo-labels (Shi and Tomasi 1994; Lucas and Kanade 1981); RAFT learns dense pairwise optical flow (Teed and Deng 2020). Modern point trackers reason over longer windows and visibility (Harley et al. 2022; Doersch et al. 2022, 2023, 2024; Karaev et al. 2024; Q. Wang et al. 2023). We do not claim a new tracker. We use conservative, confidence-masked pseudo-tracks to study how correspondence supervision interacts with a generator. 

Conflicting objectives. 

Weighted multi-task learning does not prevent conflicting gradients. GradNorm rescales tasks (Chen et al. 2018); multi-objective methods search for Pareto-aware updates (Sener and Koltun 2018); PCGrad removes pairwise opposing components (Yu et al. 2020); CAGrad and Nash-MTL derive alternative conflict-aware rules (Liu et al. 2021; Navon et al. 2022). We choose a one-sided PCGrad instance because the tasks are not symmetric: flow matching is the primary objective whose pretrained behavior should be protected, while tracking is auxiliary. ## 4 Experimental design 

4.1 Model, data, and matched variants 

The backbone is Wan2.2-TI2V-5B with 5,012,392,132 parameters. We train 12,604,420 parameters: rank-8 LoRA on attention 𝑞/𝑘/𝑣/𝑜 projections plus the structure adapter. All variants start from the same corrected 10k-step flow LoRA checkpoint. The cached index contains 1,080 clips (360 per domain); the valid-structure gate retains 241 digital-human, 296 entertainment, and 356 robot-video clips. 4.2 Evaluation and statistical unit 

For each of three independent training seeds, every variant generates the same fixed five prompts using matched sampling seeds, 17 frames at 480 × 832 , 12 sampling steps, and guidance scale 5. The prompts cover human gesture, articulated entertainment motion, robot contact, occlusion/reappearance, and fast camera motion. This is text-to-video: the input is the prompt ; there is no conditioning image. Figure 4 therefore shows prompt-to-output comparisons rather than inventing input frames. The primary ablation uses four diagnostics. (i) KLT forward–backward cycle error is the mean return error of tracks propagated forward and back; lower is better. (ii) final-track survival is the fraction remaining valid at the last frame; higher is better but can reward static or easy-to-track outputs. (iii) Laplacian variance is a no-reference sharpness proxy. (iv) temporal 𝐿W is mean adjacent-frame change and detects motion collapse or inflation. The confirmatory unit is the independently trained seed ( 𝑛 = 3 ); prompts are repeated measurements within a seed. We therefore report each seed, seed means, sample SDs, and ranges rather than treating videos as independent replicates. Before inspection, sharpness was required to exceed a median prompt-level log ratio of log(0.95) and temporal motion to remain within [log(0.9), log(1.1)] 

relative to control. We then freeze 24 prompts spanning six motion and appearance challenges and evaluate the Wan2.2 base model, matched flow-only control, and full method with the same three seeds, yielding 72 videos per variant. CoTracker3 (Karaev et al. 2024) provides a learned forward–backward correspondence diagnostic; VBench (Huang et al. 2024) measures subject consistency, background consistency, motion smoothness, dynamic degree, aesthetic quality, and imaging quality; and I3D-FVD (Unterthiner et al. 2018) compares each 72-video set with one fixed 72-clip held-out reference set. FVD is sample-size biased and the reference clips are not prompt-paired, so it is interpreted only as a descriptive, fixed-𝑁 distribution comparison. For paired method–control effects, we summarize seed-level differences, seed-𝑡 intervals, hierarchical seed/prompt bootstrap intervals, and exact sign tests; with 𝑛 = 3 , these analyses quantify uncertainty but are not used to claim significance. 5.2 Expanded evaluation confirms a correspondence–quality trade-off 

The frozen 24-prompt suite broadens the result beyond the five-prompt diagnostic (Figure 3). Averaged over 72 videos, the full method reduces CoTracker3 cycle error from 1.1009 to 0.9938 relative to the matched control (9.7%) and increases endpoint survival from 0.7466 to 0.7698. The seed-level cycle-error improvements are +0.0985 , −0.3305 , and +0.5533 

when oriented as control minus method, so this aggregate advantage is heterogeneous and its seed-𝑡 95% interval includes zero ( [−0.9908,1.2051] ). This learned-tracker result therefore supports, but does not replace, the directionally consistent KLT result. Across six VBench dimensions, full-method minus control differences range from −0.00207 to +0.00357 : subject consistency, background consistency, motion smoothness, and imaging quality increase slightly; dynamic degree is unchanged; and aesthetic quality decreases by 0.00207 (0.39%). Motion smoothness improves in all three seeds, but an exact one-sided sign test with 𝑛 = 3 is only 𝑝 = 0.125 . I3D-FVD improves substantially over the unadapted base (0.2022 versus 0.4929) but is worse than the matched control (0.1824). Together, these metrics identify the central operating point rather than an across-the-board win: correspondence improves while measured perceptual quality is largely retained, with a small FVD cost.

5.4 Conflict is not a rare transient event 

In the final 100 steps, the auxiliary gradient conflicts with the flow gradient in 41.25%, 57.5%, and 52.5% of updates across the three seeds (Figure 5). The corresponding unprojected cosine means are +0.00163 , −0.00386 , and −0.00305 . Near-zero average cosine therefore does not imply compatibility: frequent positive and negative interactions can cancel in the mean. This observation supports explicit per-step handling rather than selecting a smaller fixed auxiliary weight solely from average diagnostics.

## 6 Discussion 

What was learned. 

The central result is narrower and more useful than “tracks improve videos.” Explicit correspondence supervision reduces KLT cycle error consistently across seeds and lowers aggregate CoTracker3 cycle error, but zero initialization alone does not protect the generator after optimization begins. Separating primary and auxiliary gradients reveals recurrent conflict. Asymmetric projection preserves a correspondence gain while keeping six VBench dimensions close to the matched control. 

Why this matters. 

Large video models are expensive to retrain, so adaptation must preserve useful priors. A zero gate protects the function only at initialization; a conflict-aware update protects the direction of the primary objective throughout training. The distinction is general to pretrained generative models augmented with depth, pose, flow, segmentation, or physical-state heads. AcrossVAM2.0 · ICLR 2027 preprint 

What was not established. 

Neither KLT nor CoTracker3 cycle consistency is ground-truth identity through long occlusion, and Laplacian variance cannot replace preference judgments. The study uses three seeds, one backbone, and an eligible subset of 893 clips from a balanced 1,080-clip index. The expanded 24-prompt suite improves coverage but not the number of independent trained models. The Wan2.2 base is a pretrained-backbone reference, not an independent external architecture. Moreover, I3D-FVD is worse than the matched control, and the 72-pair blinded A/B package remains unscored. Annotated TAP-style correspondence, an external architecture under a comparable budget, and completed preregistered ratings are therefore still required for a state-of-the-art perceptual claim. The present evidence supports an optimization mechanism and a measured correspondence–quality operating point. 

## 7 Conclusion 

ACROSSVAM2.0 turns a broad motion-modeling ambition into a focused test: add explicit point correspondence to a large text-to-video generator without allowing the auxiliary task to dominate the pretrained generative objective. A confidence-masked structure head, zero-gated residual, and asymmetric PCGrad update reduce KLT cycle error in every training seed; a 24-prompt suite further shows lower aggregate CoTracker3 error and near-matched VBench quality. Seed heterogeneity, a temporal safeguard failure, a small FVD regression, and missing human ratings define the boundary. Preserving the initial function and preserving the primary optimization direction are distinct requirements, and reliable structural adaptation benefits from both.

## Appendix E. Selection-stage experiments are not confirmatory evidence 

Before the three-seed run, fourteen one-seed, 200-step screens examined auxiliary weights, tap blocks, gradient isolation, partial gradients, adapter-only updates, feedback energy, backbone anchoring, global PCGrad, and blockwise PCGrad. These screens were used for selection and are not pooled with the confirmatory estimates. Global PCGrad was advanced because its selection run reduced KLT cycle error while passing the robust sharpness floor; blockwise projection did not improve the selection trade-off. Disclosing this stage prevents the three-seed experiment from being mistaken for an unselected first test.
