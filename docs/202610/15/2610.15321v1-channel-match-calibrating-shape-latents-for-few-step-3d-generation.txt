Abstract
Recent native 3D generators achieve high-quality asset cre-
ation by modeling structured 3D latents with flow-based trans-
formers, but their high-resolution generation remains compu-
tationally expensive due to costly iterative sampling. In this
work, we introduce a few-step distillation framework for na-
tive 3D generation, enabled by continuous self-consistency
training with a custom JVP FlashAttention kernel and an an-
alytic velocity regression objective for sparse structured 3D
latents. To stabilize joint optimization, we further introduce
an adaptive loss weighting strategy that automatically bal-
ances them without manual tuning. Beyond acceleration, we
reveal a previously overlooked failure mode of few-step native
3D generation: sampling-induced latent distribution drift pre-
serves geometric correctness but disrupts downstream PBR
texture synthesis, causing texture artifacts on geometrically
valid assets. To address this issue, we propose Channel Match,
a training-free adaptive per-channel latent calibration method
that restores the latent distribution without modifying either
generator. We validate our approach on TRELLIS.2 at the
10243 resolution setting. Our two-step model achieves 4.5×
faster geometry sampling while maintaining geometry qual-
ity comparable to the many-step teacher, and Channel Match
further improves rendering quality by recovering the latent
interface between geometry and appearance generation.

Introduction
Recent advances in native 3D generators have enabled high-
quality asset creation by directly modeling structured 3D rep-
resentations. These models encode assets into sparse and
structured latent spaces and generate them with flow-based
transformers [Xiang et al., 2025, Liu et al., 2023, Lipman
et al., 2022]. Despite their impressive details, high-resolution
native 3D generators remain computationally expensive, as
each flow step requires processing a large number of 3D to-
kens in structured latent spaces, and generation typically re-
quires many iterative sampling steps. This computational cost
limits their deployment in interactive applications.
Few-step distillation provides a promising direction to re-
duce this cost. Consistency models and their continuous-
time formulations compress multi-step samplers into one or
two steps [Song et al., 2023, Lu and Song, 2025], while
distribution-matching and adversarial distillation methods
achieve near-teacher quality with even fewer evaluations [Yin
et al., 2024b, Lin et al., 2024, Sauer et al., 2024]. However,
directly applying these techniques to native 3D generation re-
mains challenging due to fundamental differences in latent
representations and generation pipelines.
First, native 3D generators rely on sparse and variable-
length structured latents, whereas existing continuous-time
distillation methods are primarily designed for dense fixed-
dimensional representations [Song et al., 2023, Lu and Song,
2025]. Such latent structures are essential for scaling 3D gen-
eration to high resolutions, but introduce challenges for com-
puting consistency objectives, particularly the velocity direc-
tional derivatives required by continuous-time formulations.
Second, native 3D generation pipelines contain intermediate
latent interfaces shared across multiple generation stages. Un-
like image generation where the latent is directly decoded into
pixels, a geometry latent in native 3D generation can serve as
the input to downstream appearance synthesis, such as PBR
texture generation. Therefore, reducing sampling steps may
preserve geometric structure while shifting latent statistics
away from the distribution expected by downstream genera-
tors, causing quality degradation that is invisible to geometry-
only evaluation.
In this work, we investigate few-step distillation for high-
resolution native 3D generation. We develop a distillation
framework tailored for sparse structured 3D latents, enabling
continuous self-consistency training through a custom JVP
FlashAttention kernel. To avoid the ambiguity of learning a
consistent but incorrect transport field, we incorporate an an-
alytic velocity regression objective that anchors the student
toward the true marginal transport. Furthermore, we intro-
duce an adaptive loss weighting strategy that automatically
balances self-consistency and velocity regression objectives
across timesteps without manual tuning of loss weights.
Beyond acceleration, we reveal a previously overlooked fail-
ure mode of few-step 3D generation: sampling-induced latent
distribution drift. The accelerated geometry sampler can pro-
duce structurally valid shape latents while deviating from the

Our contributions are summarized as follows.
• We reveal a previously overlooked failure mode in few-
step native 3D generation: aggressive sampling reduc-
tion can introduce distribution shifts in the geometry la-
tent that preserve geometric completeness but violate the
latent interface with downstream appearance synthesis,
causing texture artifacts that resemble structural defects.
• We propose Channel Match, a training-free adaptive per-
channel latent calibration method that restores the shifted
geometry latent toward the training distribution. With-
out retraining either the geometry or texture generator,
it eliminates texture artifacts, improves paired render-
ing quality toward the many-step teacher, and mitigates
similar degradation in naively few-step sampling.
• We develop a scalable few-step distillation framework for
high-resolution native 3D flow models. By enabling con-
tinuous self-consistency training on sparse structured 3D
latents with a custom JVP FlashAttention kernel and an
analytic velocity regression objective, our two-step stu-
dent achieves 4.5× faster geometry sampling with qual-
ity comparable to the many-step teacher and surpassing
SOTA baselines.

Related Work
Native 3D generation.
Structured-latent models generate
features on sparse active voxels via rectified-flow transform-
ers, decoding to meshes, Gaussians, or radiance fields [Xiang
et al., 2025, Ren et al., 2024, Kerbl et al., 2023]. TREL-
LIS.2 extends this line to 10243-grid latents with PBR mate-
rials and a separate texture stage [Xiang et al., 2026]. High-
resolution sparse representations similarly enable detailed ge-
ometry at large grid sizes [Li et al., 2026, He et al., 2025,
Zhang et al., 2024]. Other native generators diffuse compact
vector-set or primitive latents [Lai et al., 2025, Li et al., 2025,
Chen et al., 2025c, Zhao et al., 2025, Chen et al., 2025a], and
earlier feed-forward or latent-field methods trade fidelity for
speed [Tang et al., 2024, Lan et al., 2025]. Supported by large
datasets [Deitke et al., 2023b,a], we fix the shape and texture
stages of TRELLIS.2 and distill only the shape flow, so any
measured change stems from the shape latent alone.
Few-step distillation.
Progressive distillation halves the
step count repeatedly [Salimans and Ho, 2022]; consis-
tency models regress points on a trajectory to a shared end-
point [Song et al., 2023], and the continuous-time form re-
moves the discretization schedule once its training instabil-
ity is controlled through a simplified parameterization and
an adaptive weighting [Lu and Song, 2025, Karras et al.,
2022]. A second family replaces trajectory matching with a
distribution-level objective: distribution-matching, adversar-
ial, and unified trajectory-distribution distillations [Yin et al.,
2024b,a, Sauer et al., 2024, Lin et al., 2024, Luo et al., 2025],
2


which unifies the two, reach one-to-few-step image genera-
tion. These methods rely on 2D image discriminators, whose
signal is hard to obtain for a native 3D latent because quality
evaluation requires non-differentiable rendering. We therefore
use continuous-time self-consistency in velocity space, paired
with a ground-truth velocity loss to anchor the fixed point.
Forward-mode attention and 3D consistency.
The contin-
uous objective needs a forward-mode tangent through atten-
tion. FlashAttention [Dao et al., 2022] and its sparse exten-
sion SCFA [Pagliardini et al., 2023] keep attention memory-
efficient but expose only the primal output. The closest 3D
consistency model, ConTiCoM-3D [Eilermann et al., 2026],
avoids Jacobian-vector products entirely because they are ex-
pensive for unordered point clouds, and works teacher-free
in raw point space. Ours is the opposite: a teacher-based,
latent-space, sparse-voxel pipeline where a custom fused ker-
nel makes the Jacobian-vector product tractable at high reso-
lution.
Accelerating 3D generation.
Direct transfer of 2D distilla-
tion to native 3D is uncommon. FlashVDM accelerates both
the sampler and the decoder of a vector-set model but gener-
ates shape without appearance [Lai et al., 2025]. A parallel line
instead cuts attention cost with sparse attention [Chen et al.,
2025b, Wu et al., 2025]. The marginal-data-transport distilla-
tion of Zhou et al. [2026] converts the transport objective into
velocity matching and velocity distillation and reaches one-to-
two-step generation on an earlier structured-latent backbone; it
is the closest baseline, and we reimplement it on TRELLIS.2
for a controlled comparison. Neither line reports the latent
distribution-shift failure that Channel Match targets, which
appears once the shape stage is pushed to very few steps.
Statistics alignment.
Matching per-channel mean and vari-
ance to a reference is a long-standing idea, from batch normal-
ization [Ioffe and Szegedy, 2015] to adaptive instance normal-
ization for style transfer [Huang and Belongie, 2017]. Channel
Match uses the same primitive but contributes a diagnosis, not
a layer: a few-step shape latent drifts off the training marginals,
and realigning its per-channel statistics restores a frozen down-
stream stage.

JVP FlashAttention for Sparse Latents
Equation 4 is a Jacobian-vector product, so evaluating it re-
quires forward-mode differentiation through every layer of the
transformer, including attention. Stock FlashAttention [Dao
et al., 2022] returns the output but not its forward-mode

query-key-value tensor of the variable-length sparse latent, re-
turns the primal output and its tangent in a single fused pass,
and we patch the variable-length attention entry points used
by the backbone to call it during the tangent computation.

Experiments
5.1
Implementation Details
The student is initialized from the pretrained TRELLIS.2-4B
shape model, and we distill only the 1024-resolution shape
flow; the sparse-structure stage, the 512 shape flow, and the
texture stage are frozen. Training runs on eight A800-80GB
GPUs with distributed data parallelism. All experiments use
a fixed random seed of 42 for reproducibility. Because sparse
latents have a variable number of active voxels, we don’t use
a fixed batch size; instead we pack assets into each step until
a token budget of 40,000 active tokens is reached and accu-
mulate gradients, balancing per-step compute across differ-
ently sized assets. We use Adam with learning rate 2 × 10−6,
(𝛽1, 𝛽2) = (0.0, 0.999), and gradient clipping at 1.0; the
weighting network uses learning rate 10−2. The EMA de-
cay is 0.9999, 𝜎min = 10−5, the tangent clip is 𝜅= 4 times
the running median, the tangent warm-up is 1000 steps, and
the step scale is 𝜂= 1; the two losses are equally weighted
and training runs up to 80,000 steps in bfloat16. The JVP
FlashAttention kernel is a fused Triton kernel that patches the
backbone’s variable-length attention during the tangent pass.
The channel statistics ( ˆ𝜇, ˆ𝜎) for Channel Match are estimated
once on the training latents.
We compare our student at one, two, and four steps against
the pretrained teacher run at twelve steps (a many-step up-
per bound) and at two steps (a naive few-step baseline), the
marginal-data-transport distillation of Zhou et al. [2026] reim-
plemented on this backbone (MDT), and a self-consistency-
only ablation of our method that drops the ground-truth loss
Lgt (SCM). All configurations share the same frozen prefix
and texture stage.
5.2
Datasets
We distill on a 100K-asset subset of Objaverse and Objaverse-
XL [Deitke et al., 2023b,a], each asset providing a ground-
truth shape latent and a rendered conditioning image. For eval-
uation we use all 3,216 assets of the Toys4K object set [Sto-
janov et al., 2021], each conditioned on a single rendered
view.
5.3
Metrics
Geometry and appearance are scored under a fixed textured-
mesh protocol: four views at 30◦elevation and azimuths
{0, 90, 180, 270}. We report set-level Fréchet distance in In-
ception [Szegedy et al., 2016, Heusel et al., 2017] and DI-
NOv2 [Oquab et al., 2024, Stein et al., 2023] feature spaces
over the renders and ULIP-2 image–shape cosine similar-
ity [Xue et al., 2024] averaged over views. We render appear-
ance from the PBR-textured mesh that TRELLIS.2 produces.

Channel Match Improves the Texture
Table 3 reports the paired render comparison on the demon-

The full match optimizes LPIPS (0.0802 to 0.0747), SSIM

5.6
Ablations
Form of the calibration.
Table 4 isolates the calibration
form on the two-step student over the full Toys4K set. On
these geometrically simple assets, where the artifact is rare,
matching only the standard deviation is the safe choice: it
slightly improves the Inception Fréchet distance, whereas the
full match, which also moves the mean, is a touch worse
than doing nothing. This is the opposite of the demonstra-
tion assets in Table 3, where the artifact is common and the
full match helps most. The two observations agree: the full
match helps most where the collapse is severe (the artifact-
heavy demonstration assets), and the std-only match is the
safer choice where it is mild (the simple Toys4K assets). The
same std match lowers both Fréchet distances for the naively
two-step-sampled teacher (Table 4, lower block), so the shift
is distributional rather than specific to the distilled student.
Adaptive gate.
As an upper bound, an oracle that keeps,
per asset, whichever of the uncalibrated or calibrated latent
is closer to the ground truth lowers the two-step Inception
Fréchet distance of Table 1 from 14.09 to 13.53 while cali-
brating about half of the assets, so per-asset gating has head-
room. The deployable gate of Equation 10 approximates this
without ground truth, applying the calibration only when the
collapse score exceeds a threshold 𝜏𝑆. Table 5 sweeps 𝜏𝑆on
the demonstration assets: LPIPS, SSIM, and PSNR are essen-
tially flat once the match is applied to at least 75% of assets, so
the result is insensitive to 𝜏𝑆in this range. The default 𝜏𝑆=0.20
gives the best LPIPS and PSNR, and always-on calibration is
a safe fallback. The oracle bound above is on Toys4K FD, so
it is not directly comparable to this sweep; closing the gap to
it is left open.
Losses.
Removing the ground-truth velocity loss and keep-
ing only the self-consistency loss (the SCM row of Table 1)
raises the two-step Inception Fréchet distance from 14.09
to 14.76 and the DINOv2 Fréchet distance from 127.44 to
135.02, and lowers ULIP, confirming that the two losses
are complementary: self-consistency makes the field step-
agnostic while the velocity loss anchors its values. Removing
instead the self-consistency loss leaves a correct but not step-
agnostic field that degrades sharply at two steps, the standard
failure of few-step flow sampling without distillation.
5.7
Latent Drift and Sampling Steps
Table 6 quantifies the shift of Section 4.5 over Toys4K. The
latent is per-channel standardized, so ˆ𝜎𝑐= 1 and the drift is
one-sided: sampled channels fall below the training std. The
collapse score 𝑆(Eq. 10) sums exactly this deficit over chan-
nels, so it is a direct scalar measure of the shift. Both 𝑆and the
fraction of channels with sampled std below 0.8 grow mono-
tonically as steps shrink, and the four-step student matches the
teacher’s level, consistent with its being the strongest few-step
configuration in Table 1. The intervention (Table 3) shows the
shift causes the artifact: matching the statistics removes it, and
the monotonic trend here is consistent with the same account.
5.8
JVP Kernel Cost and Stability
Table 7 profiles the fused kernel on an A800-80GB GPU. The
forward-mode tangent adds training-time cost only: inference
remains unaffected. The essential property is memory rather
than latency. Against a naive torch.func.jvp baseline, our
kernel is 10.9× faster (41.7 vs 454.4,ms) and 𝑂(𝑁) rather than
𝑂(𝑁2) in memory, so where the naive path needs 67.6,GB and
runs out of memory at the 𝑁=30,000 production length, our
kernel still runs. This 𝑂(𝑁) memory is what lets the con-
tinuous objective fit on the high-resolution sparse latent. The
tangent is numerically stable: over 58,952 tokens we saw no
NaN or Inf, and the cap of Equation 5 at 𝜅= 4 roughly halves
the maximum tangent norm (175.0 to 82.3) while leaving the
median near 21, so it stabilizes the objective without distorting
typical tangents.
6
Limitations
We distill only the shape stage, so the end-to-end speedup
is bounded by the frozen texture sampler, and distilling it is
a direct next step. Channel Match corrects the per-channel
distribution shift and removes the resulting texture artifacts,
but it does not synthesize new geometric detail, so the four-step
student stays strongest on geometry-sensitive metrics; being
a per-channel affine map, it also ignores inter-channel and
spatial structure, which a full-covariance or grouped variant
could capture. The collapse-score gate already recovers most
of the available gain, and narrowing the remaining margin to
the ground-truth oracle is a promising direction. Extending the
same calibration to the texture stage is a natural next question
we leave open.
7
Conclusion
We distilled the high-resolution shape stage of TRELLIS.2
into a two-step student, using a continuous self-consistency
loss, made tractable on the sparse high-resolution latent by a
custom JVP FlashAttention kernel, together with an analytic
ground-truth velocity loss. At two steps the student reaches ge-
ometry comparable to the many-step teacher, matching it on

This appendix gives the full implementation of the fused
forward-mode (JVP) attention kernel used to train the con-
tinuous self-consistency objective, and its integration with
PyTorch forward-mode autodiff. We first restate why a cus-
tom kernel is needed, derive the forward-mode tangent
of softmax attention, describe the fused online-softmax
algorithm that evaluates it in 𝑂(𝑁) memory, and fi-
nally give the autograd bridge that exposes the kernel to
torch.autograd.forward_ad.
A.1
Why a Custom Kernel
The continuous self-consistency loss requires the total time
derivative of the student velocity along a teacher trajectory,

Integration with Forward-Mode Autodiff
The kernel is exposed to PyTorch through a custom
torch.autograd.Function whose forward calls the orig-
inal FlashAttention (primal only), whose backward keeps
standard reverse-mode available (re-running FlashAttention

the fused Triton kernel to return the output tangent. During
the tangent pass the backbone’s variable-length attention en-

A.5
Cost and Numerical Stability
The fused kernel keeps forward-mode differentiation at the
memory cost of standard attention: it is 𝑂(𝑁) in the token
count rather than 𝑂(𝑁2), so it runs at the 𝑁=30,000 produc-
tion length where the naive torch.func.jvp baseline needs
tens of gigabytes and runs out of memory (main paper, JVP-
cost table). The overhead is training-time only—inference
uses the plain student and never evaluates the tangent. The
per-token tangent 𝑔is stabilized by the smooth magnitude-
preserving cap of the main paper before it enters the self-
consistency target; over the training tokens we profiled we
observed no NaN or Inf, and the cap bounds the maximum
tangent norm while leaving the median essentially unchanged.
B
Additional Qualitative Results
In this section, we present additional qualitative comparisons
to further demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed dis-
tillation framework and Channel Match. Figure 3 provides
extended visual comparisons on the TRELLIS.2 demonstra-
tion assets. We compare our two-step student model against
the twelve-step teacher model, as well as the Self-Consistency
Match (SCM) and Marginal-Data-Transport (MDT) baselines.
Consistent with the main text, our approach recovers geomet-
ric details and texture quality that closely approximate the
many-step teacher. In contrast, the MDT baseline frequently
fractures the underlying geometry, while the SCM baseline
preserves geometric completeness but introduces noticeable
surface artifacts due to the uncalibrated shape latents.
C
Channel Match on TRELLIS.1
To evaluate the generalization of our latent calibration method,
we apply Channel Match to the earlier TRELLIS.1 architec-
ture. Unlike TRELLIS.2, TRELLIS.1 operates without a fixed
voxel resolution. As illustrated in Figure 4, while the distribu-
tion shift induced by few-step sampling in TRELLIS.1 does
not manifest as the severe dark or blotchy surface artifacts
observed in TRELLIS.2, it nonetheless causes a significant
overall degradation in texture quality. Applying our training-
free Channel Match accurately restores the per-channel statis-
tics of the few-step shape latents. This correction successfully
recovers the texture fidelity produced by the downstream ap-
pearance stage, confirming that sampling-induced latent shift
is a general phenomenon across different structured-latent
pipelines and can be mitigated by our calibration method.

We further validate the robustness of Channel Match at a
