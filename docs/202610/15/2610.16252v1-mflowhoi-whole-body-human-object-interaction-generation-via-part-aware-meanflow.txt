Abstract
Whole-body human–object interaction (HOI) generation re-
quires coordinating body and finger motion, object trajecto-
ries, and time-varying contacts. Existing methods typically
rely on iterative diffusion or test-time optimization, result-
ing in high inference costs. Direct one-step generation is
highly desired in downstream applications due to efficient
inference. However, it is challenging because these HOI com-
ponents differ substantially in structure and scale. We present
MFlowHOI, an efficient one-step framework for whole-body
HOI generation. A Contact-Aware Multipart RVQVAE or-
ganizes body, hand, object, and contact signals into a struc-
tured latent space, with a shared decoder preserving their
global coordination. An ST-DiT then models temporal and
cross-part dependencies in this space and generates a com-
plete interaction in a single MeanFlow evaluation. Predicted
object-local contact correspondences further enable geomet-
ric refinement and interaction-preserving transfer to photore-
alistic 3D Gaussian avatars with different body proportions.
Experiments demonstrate that MFlowHOI outperforms exist-
ing base generators in motion quality and text alignment while
substantially reducing inference time.
Introduction
Human–object interaction (HOI) generation supports appli-
cations in virtual reality, character animation, embodied sim-
ulation, and robotics. It requires the body, articulated hands,
and object to move coherently while maintaining plausible
spatial relations over time. This is particularly challenging for
dexterous interactions: small relative-pose errors can cause
unreachable grasps, floating contacts, or severe penetration
despite otherwise natural motion.
Existing methods capture complementary aspects of HOI.
Hand–object approaches model articulated hands and local
contact geometry
(Christen et al. 2022; Liu et al. 2023;
Cha et al. 2024; Liu and Yi 2024), but omit the full-body
motions to drive the hands. Whole-body methods coordi-
nate human and object trajectories (Ghosh et al. 2023; Li,
Wu, and Liu 2023; Li et al. 2024a; Peng et al. 2025), yet
often use simplified hand representations. Recent methods
jointly model body and finger motion (Zhang et al. 2025;
Ron et al. 2025; Pi et al. 2026; Pratikaki et al. 2026), but
typically rely on iterative diffusion, geometric guidance, or
test-time optimization to maintain contact (Li et al. 2024a;
Peng et al. 2025; Zhang et al. 2025; Karunratanakul et al.

2024; Ron et al. 2025; Pi et al. 2026). Although high-quality
results can be achieved, their high inference cost limits inter-
active use. Recently, MeanFlow (Geng et al. 2025a) enables
one-step generation by learning an interval-averaged veloc-
ity field and has shown promising results in human motion
synthesis (Yang et al. 2025; Geng et al. 2025b). However,
it is nontrivial to apply it to HOI because body motion,
finger articulation, object trajectories, and sparse contacts
between human and objects have different structures and
scales. Directly encoding them jointly can suppress subtle
hand and contact dynamics, whereas modeling them inde-
pendently weakens the quality of interaction coordination.
In our experiments, we find that directly applying one-step
MeanFlow to existing HOI representations leads to degraded
motion and unstable contact.
In this paper, we propose MFlowHOI, a one-step frame-

work for contact-aware whole-body HOI generation. Its
Contact-Aware Multipart RVQ-VAE decomposes an inter-
action into functional body parts, object motion, contact
probabilities, and object-local contact positions. Stream-
specific temporal encoders capture fine-grained dynamics,
while a shared decoder preserves global coordination, form-
ing a structured time–part latent space. In this latent space,
a conditional Spatio-Temporal Diffusion Transformer (ST-
DiT) learns the MeanFlow velocity field. It models temporal
dependencies within each stream and cross-part interactions
among the body, hands, object, and contacts. A single evalua-
tion generates a complete interaction latent, which is decoded
into whole-body and finger motion, object trajectories, and
contact cues. The resulting contact correspondences also en-
able geometric refinement and interaction-preserving trans-
fer to photorealistic 3D Gaussian avatars with different body
proportions. Experiments on GRAB demonstrate improved
distributional quality and text–motion alignment with sub-
stantially faster inference than existing HOI generators. Ab-
lations confirm the importance of the multipart latent space
and its temporal and cross-part modeling.
The main contribution of our method can be summarized
as follows. 1) We present a new one-step framework that
jointly generates coordinated whole-body and finger motion,
object trajectories, and time-varying contact cues with a sin-
gle network. 2) To enable stable conditional MeanFlow gen-
eration, we design a temporal multipart–based HOI latent
representation to model temporal dependencies between dif-
ferent body parts andobjects, andintroduce aspatio-temporal
diffusion transformer to balance heterogeneous HOI compo-
nents. 3) We predict contact correspondences between ob-
ject and body parts that support geometric refinement and
interaction-preserving transfer to photorealistic 3D Gaussian
avatars with different body proportions.
Related Work
Efficient and Controllable Motion Generation.
Human
motion generation has been extensively studied under various
conditions, including text, music, action labels, keyframes,
and joint trajectories. Diffusion-based methods have shown
strong generative performance: MDM (Tevet et al. 2023)
introduced a general framework for text- and action-
conditioned motion generation, while OmniControl (Xie
et al. 2024) and TLControl (Wan et al. 2024) extended dif-
fusion models with fine-grained spatial and trajectory con-
straints. DNO (Karunratanakul et al. 2024). Further treats a
pretrained diffusion model as a motion prior and optimizes
its initial noise to satisfy test-time objectives. Despite their
flexibility, these approaches require iterative denoising or
additional optimization during inference. Flow-based mod-
els offer a more efficient alternative: Motion Flow Match-
ing (Hu et al. 2023) generates motion through continuous-
time transport, and FlowMotion (Cuba, do Carmo Melí-
cio, and Gois 2025) improves temporal coherence using
target-predictive conditional flow matching. Recently, Flow-
erDance (Yang et al. 2025) and ARMFlow (Geng et al.
2025b) explored MeanFlow (Geng et al. 2025a) for one-
step dance and human-reaction generation. However, effi-
cient one-step generation remains underexplored for human–
object interactions, where human motion must be coordi-
nated with object motion and time-varying contact.
Hand–Object Interaction Generation.
Hand–object in-
teraction generation focuses on synthesizing plausible hand
poses or motion with respect to an object. Early studies com-
monly generate static grasps using object geometry, contact
maps, or interaction priors. Recent methods extend this set-
ting to dynamic interactions: ContactGen (Liu et al. 2023)
represents contacts through object-surface locations, hand
parts, and contact directions; D-Grasp (Christen et al. 2022)
uses physics-based control to synthesize dynamic grasping
behaviors; Text2HOI (Cha et al. 2024) predicts a contact map
before generating hand–object motion; and GeneOH (Liu
and Yi 2024) Diffusion adopts a contact-centric represen-
tation for interaction denoising. In addition, several recent
studies seek up better cross-modality consistency (Li et al.
2025; Huang et al. 2025; Christen et al. 2024) , geometric or
physical compliance (Zhang et al. 2021; Taheri et al. 2024;
Zhang et al. 2024; Zheng et al. 2023a) and interaction re-
covering (Zhou et al. 2022). These methods capture detailed
hand articulation and local contact geometry, but generally
do not model the complete human kinematic chain. Conse-
quently, a locally plausible hand pose or trajectory does not
necessarily correspond to natural and reachable motion of
the arms, torso, and root.
Human–Object Interaction Generation.
Human–object
interaction generation studies the coupled motion of a person
and a movable object. Recently, many works focus on full-
body generation (Taheri et al. 2022; Wu et al. 2022, 2024;
Li et al. 2024b; Kwon, Chen, and Joo 2026) and physically
plausible interactions (Braun et al. 2024; Di et al. 2026). Fur-
thermore, IMoS (Ghosh et al. 2023) generates intent-driven
full-body interactions with objects, while OMOMO (Li, Wu,
and Liu 2023) predicts hand locations from a given object
trajectory and uses them to condition body-motion synthesis.
CHOIS (Li et al. 2024a) jointly generates human and object
motion from language, initial states, and sparse object way-
points, using sampling-time guidance to improve contacts.
HOI-Diff (Peng et al. 2025) employs separate human and
object diffusion branches together with affordance modeling
and classifier guidance. These methods primarily represent
the body and object while omitting fully articulated finger
motion. More recent approaches model finer hand articula-
tion: DiffGrasp (Zhang et al. 2025) generates body and hand
motion conditioned on an object trajectory and applies recon-
struction guidance during diffusion sampling; HOIDiNi (Ron
et al. 2025) and CoDA (Pi et al. 2026) coordinate body, hand,
and object generation through staged diffusion or noise op-
timization; and Dex2HOI (Pratikaki et al. 2026) avoids ex-
ternal noise optimization but retains iterative diffusion sam-
pling. Therefore, simultaneously modeling body motion, ar-
ticulated hands, object motion, and temporal contact remains
challenging, particularly under efficient inference.
Method
Given the initial poses of a human and an object, the object
geometry, and a textual instruction, our goal is to generate
a complete 3D human–object interaction (HOI) sequence,

including whole-body human motion, articulated finger mo-

Data Representation
Human and Object Motion Representation.
Following
DiffGrasp (Zhang et al. 2025), we use SMPL-X to rep-
resent human motion. A human motion sequence is de-
noted by H ∈RT ×Dh, where each state is represented as
H(t) = {r(t), ∆d(t), θ(t)}. Here, r(t) ∈R6 is the global root
orientation, θ(t) ∈R52×6 contains the local rotations of 52
full-body joints, and ∆d(t) is the root translation relative to
the previous frame. The initial translation d(1) ∈R3 is repre-
sented separately. Similarly, an object motion sequence is de-
noted by O ∈RT ×Do, where O(t) = {R(t), ∆o(t)}. Here,
R(t) ∈R6 is the global rotation and ∆o(t) is the translation
relative to the previous frame. The initial object translation
o(1) ∈R3 is represented separately. The human and object
shapes are represented by the SMPL-X shape parameters β
and the BPS descriptor B ∈RK×3, respectively. Overall,
the human and object representations are (H, d(1), β) and
(O, o(1), B), respectively.

by a shared decoder.

Spatio-Temporal Attention Modeling.
ST-DiT operates
on the T ′×|S| time–part token grid. Temporal attention mod-
els the trajectory of each human, object, and contact stream,
while cross-part attention coordinates all streams at each
latent time step. Together, they jointly capture whole-body
dynamics, articulated hand motion, object motion, contact
probabilities, and object-local contact positions. Temporal,
part, and modality embeddings preserve the grid structure.
The condition c is injected through part-specific adaptive
normalization, allowing each stream to selectively access the
textual instruction, initial human–object state, and shape in-
formation. In particular, the contact streams provide explicit
interaction cues that connect human and object motion.

At inference time, we sam-

over the full interval: bz0 = ϵ −buθ(ϵ, 0, 1, c). We unpack
the generated time–part grid as {eZs}s∈S = Unstack(bz0)
and reconstruct the complete interaction using the frozen de-
coder: (H, O, bI) = D(L
s∈S eZs, cinit, cshape). Finally, we
restore the prescribed initial poses and integrate the human
and object translation increments from d(1) and o(1). The
base model therefore generates a complete HOI sequence
with one ST-DiT transport evaluation, without iterative inte-
gration or projection onto the residual codebooks.
Optional Contact-Guided Refinement
Our base model directly generates the final sequence with-
out post-processing. Nevertheless, its predicted interaction
representation establishes explicit correspondences between
human markers and object-local contact points, enabling two
optional refinement strategies. Their computational costs are
not included in the reported one-step generation latency.
Object-Only Refinement.
We fix the generated human
motion and object rotation, and optimize only the object


translation. Confident object-body part contacts are projected
onto the object surface and transformed into world coordi-
nates. We then estimate bounded, temporally smooth trans-
lation offsets that robustly reduce their discrepancies from
the corresponding human markers. The initial object state is
preserved, and MeanFlow is not rerun.
Upper-Body Noise Optimization.
Alternatively, we fix
the object trajectory and predicted contact targets, and adapt
Diffusion Noise Optimization (DNO) to our differentiable
one-step generator. The input noise is optimized using con-
tact, penetration, motion smoothness, and scene-consistency
objectives. An output projection preserves the human root
and lower body, as well as the object trajectory and con-
tact predictions, such that only the torso, arms, and articu-
lated hands can change. Each optimization iteration requires
only one ST-DiT evaluation and one frozen decoder pass.
Full objectives and implementation details for optimization
strategies are provided in the supplementary material.

Evaluation metrics.
Following CoDA (Pi et al. 2026), we
use its released text–motion evaluator to assess generation
quality. Fr’echet Inception Distance (FID) measures the dis-
tributional gap between generated and real motion features,
R-Precision (R@3) evaluates text–motion alignment via Top-
3 retrieval accuracy, and Diversity quantifies motion-feature
variation relative to the ground-truth (GT) value. For physi-
cal quality, foot skating (FS) measures horizontal foot-sliding
velocity in m/s, while interpenetration volume (IV) and depth
(ID) measure body–object overlap in cm3 and the maximum
penetration depth in mm, respectively. Contact ratio (CR)
denotes the proportion of body vertices within 5 mm of the
object surface. We also report the average warm inference
latency per sample (Time) in seconds.

ST-DiT improves semantic alignment, motion quality, and

We compare our 3DGS-based rendering pipeline with the 2D

tual scores across all evaluation criteria, with particularly
large gains in realism (4.35 vs. 1.46), object appearance con-
sistency (4.49 vs. 1.24), and object motion smoothness (4.53
vs. 1.25). The qualitative comparisons in Figure 6 further
show that our explicit 3D representation better preserves
object identity, temporal coherence, and hand–object align-

Conclusion and Limitations
We presented MFlowHOI, a one-step framework for whole-
body HOI generation. Its contact-aware multipart codec bal-
ances body, hand, object, and contact signals while preserv-
ing global coordination. Operating on the resulting time–part
latent grid, ST-DiT generates complete interactions with a
single MeanFlow evaluation. Experiments demonstrate im-
proved distributional quality and text alignment compared
with other generators, at substantially lower latency. Pre-
dicted object-local contacts further facilitate efficient geo-
metric correction and avatar deployment. Although promis-
ing results were achieved, our evaluation is limited to inter-
actions with a single rigid object. Extending the method to
articulated/deformable objects remains future work.
