HP-PnP-Flow: Hybrid Time Scheduling and Projection for
Plug-and-Play Image Restoration via Flow Matching†
Yong Li, Xiaojie Wang, Xiaoyan Zhang
School of Mathematics and Statistics, HNP-LAMA, Central South University, Changsha 410083, Hunan, China

Abstract
Image restoration is a fundamental inverse problem that seeks to recover high-quality
images from degraded observations. Plug-and-Play (PnP) methods provide a flexible frame-
work for integrating pretrained denoisers into iterative reconstruction, whereas flow matching
offers an efficient generative modeling approach based on continuous-time ODE dynamics.
Very recently, Martin et al. (ICLR, 2025) developed a PnP-Flow method, which integrates
the PnP framework with flow matching through a time-dependent denoiser constructed from
a pretrained flow matching model. By incorporating a different time schedule and an addi-
tional pixel-space projection operator, this paper proposes a novel efficient PnP-Flow algo-
rithm (HP-PnP-Flow) that enjoys rigorous convergence guarantees under mild assumptions,
without imposing restrictive conditions on iterates required by the aforementioned work. Nu-
merical experiments on CelebA and AFHQ-Cat demonstrate that the newly proposed HP-
PnP-Flow algorithm significantly outperforms PnP-Flow on several restoration tasks such
as denoising, deblurring, super-resolution, random inpainting, and box inpainting. Notably,
our algorithm achieves comparable or better reconstruction quality with substantially fewer
iterations, reducing the iteration count from 500 to 100 for deblurring and super-resolution
on AFHQ-Cat.

In recent years, the regularized minimization problem (1.3) has received considerable attention.
Early works addressed this problem by using proximal splitting methods, which handle the data-
fidelity and regularization terms separately. For example, forward-backward splitting (FBS) [4,10]

Plug-and-Play (PnP) method. Since designing an effective regularizer for complex priors is
challenging, the authors of [37] introduced the Plug-and-Play (PnP) methods, which replace the
proximal operator with an off-the-shelf denoiser such as DnCNN [43] or DRUNet [42]. Over the
last few years, much attention has been received on various PnP methods and their convergence
analysis (see, e.g., [21,28,29,32,35] and references therein). For example, the authors of [40] first
provide a new convergence theory for plug-and-play proximal gradient descent (PnP-PGD) under
prior mismatch where the denoiser is trained on a different data distribution to the inference task
at hand. By incorporating neural network denoisers, PnP methods have achieved promising results
across various image restoration tasks. However, classical PnP methods typically rely on generic
discriminative denoisers, for which an explicit regularization function may not be available. This
limits both the optimization interpretation of the method and its ability to represent complex
image distributions.

PnP-Flow method. Generative models provide a natural alternative by modeling the un-
derlying data distribution, and have achieved state-of-the-art performance in image generation,
inverse problems and a broad range of related applications [5, 9, 12, 19, 25, 26, 34]. In particular,
flow matching learns a time-dependent vector field whose ODE flow transports a base distribution
to the data distribution. Owing to simpler training objectives and more efficient sampling, flow
matching has recently emerged as a powerful framework for continuous-time generative modeling.
These developments have motivated the use of generative models as image priors within the PnP
framework. Very recently, Martin et al. [27] introduced PnP-Flow matching method by defining a
time-dependent denoiser using a pretrained flow matching model. Under regularity assumptions
and bounded support of the base distribution, they established convergence under the additional
assumptions that the iterates remain bounded and the time sequence satisfies P∞
k=0(1 −tk) < ∞.
Despite its good reconstruction performance, PnP-Flow still has several limitations. On the one
hand, the condition imposed on the time sequence is difficult to be satisfied in practice.
For
instance, the linear schedule tk = k/N used in [27] does not satisfy the required uniform bound-
edness with respect to the iteration budget N. On the other hand, the convergence guarantee is
obtained based on bounded iterates, a property that is not explicitly guaranteed by the original
algorithm. Motivated by this we ask the following question:
Can one design an improved PnP-Flow algorithm that enjoys rigorous convergence guarantees
under mild assumptions, while achieving improved reconstruction efficiency and quality?
An improved HP-PnP-Flow algorithm. In this work, we address this problem by proposing a
hybrid-projection PnP-Flow (HP-PnP-Flow) algorithm, based on a different time schedule and an
additional pixel-space projection operator. More precisely, the hybrid time schedule given by (3.1)
combines geometric and linear phases, while the projection operator defined by (3.2) explicitly
constrains the iterates to the prescribed pixel range. Under mild and reasonable assumptions,
we establish the convergence of HP-PnP-Flow (see Theorem 3.3), without requiring the a priori
boundedness assumptions on the iterates imposed in the original PnP-Flow framework in [27].
Also, the new hybrid time schedule ensures the resulting time sequence satisfies the uniform
boundedness assumption P∞
k=0(1 −tk) < ∞. Notably, numerical experiments demonstrate the
efficiency and high quality of the new algorithm. Ablation studies further validate the individual
effects of the initial time offset t0 and the hybrid time schedule, with their combination yielding
the best overall performance. In summary, our main contributions are as follows:
• We propose an improved PnP-Flow algorithm, called HP-PnP-Flow, by incorporating a novel
hybrid time schedule and an additional pixel-space projection operator.
• We establish the convergence of the newly proposed HP-PnP-Flow algorithm under mild
and reasonable assumptions.
• Extensive experiments on two datasets and five tasks demonstrate that our algorithm signif-
icantly outperforms PnP-Flow in terms of reconstruction quality and convergence efficiency.

4
Numerical experiments
In this section, we evaluate the performance of HP-PnP-Flow algorithm (3.3) on five canonical
image restoration tasks, including denoising, deblurring, super-resolution, random-pixel inpaint-
ing, and box inpainting, using two publicly available datasets. We compare our algorithm with
several baseline methods using PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, and perform ablation studies to assess
the effects of the hybrid time schedule and the initial time offset.
4.1
Experimental Setup
Datasets and preprocessing. We use two datasets with complementary characteristics.
• CelebA contains approximately 200, 000 celebrity face images (see [41]). All images are
center-cropped and resized to 128 × 128 pixels. The dataset provides well-structured facial
patterns and diverse texture details, making it suitable for evaluating image restoration
methods.
• AFHQ-Cat is the cat subset of the Animal Faces HQ dataset (see [8]), comprising roughly
5 000 images. Images are resized to 256 × 256 pixels. The higher resolution and fine fur
detail provide a more challenging test of generalization.
Both datasets use standard training, validation, and test splits. All quantitative evaluations are
performed on the test set. Pixel values are normalized to [−1, 1].
Degradation tasks. We consider five degradation tasks. In all cases, additive Gaussian noise is
included, with the task-dependent noise level specified below.
• Denoising: H = Id, with σ = 0.2.
• Deblurring: H is a Gaussian blurring operator with b = 1.0 on CelebA and b = 3.0 on
AFHQ-Cat, with σ = 0.05.
• Super-resolution: H is a bicubic downsampling operator with scale factor ×2 on CelebA
and ×4 on AFHQ-Cat, with σ = 0.05.
• Random inpainting: H is a random binary masking operator retaining 30% of the pixels,
with σ = 0.01.
• Box inpainting: H masks a central 40 × 40 region on CelebA and an 80 × 80 region on
AFHQ-Cat, with σ = 0.05.
Baseline methods. We compare our algorithm against several existing methods. PnP-Flow [27]
is used as our primary baseline. We additionally include three flow-matching-based restoration
methods, namely OT-ODE [30], D-Flow [16], and Flow-Priors [44], together with PnP-GS [20], a
PnP-based baseline. All baselines are evaluated using their optimal hyperparameters and officially
released weights under identical degradation settings and test splits for fair comparison.
12


Implementation details. All experiments are conducted on an NVIDIA Tesla V100S GPU using
PyTorch.
Random seeds are fixed for reproducibility.
For the flow-matching-based methods,
including our proposed method, we use the U-Net model weights and pretrained checkpoints
from [27]. The only exception is PnP-GS, which uses its own pretrained denoiser.
We note a difference in the evaluation protocol. Martin et al. [27] report results averaged over
25 batches of 4 images each, whereas we recompute all evaluation metrics using 100 independent
runs for each image. As a result, the values reported here may differ slightly from those in the
original publication. For each baseline method, we use the optimal hyperparameters reported
in [27], which were selected by grid search on the validation set to maximize PSNR. Detailed
hyperparameter settings for HP-PnP-Flow and all baseline methods are provided in Appendix A.

4.3
Comparison to PnP-Flow
In the following, we compare HP-PnP-Flow (3.3) with PnP-Flow (2.11) under the single-
sampling setting, and analyze their iteration-wise reconstruction trajectories across all five restora-
tion tasks and both datasets. Visual comparisons are provided in Appendix B.
One can observe that HP-PnP-Flow exhibits faster convergence during the early iterations. For
example, from Figures 6 and 7, we observe that, at iteration 20, HP-PnP-Flow improves upon PnP-
Flow by 7.58 and 7.96 dB in PSNR, and by 0.1611 and 0.219 in SSIM, on CelebA and AFHQ-Cat,
respectively. Similar early-stage advantages are also observed on the other four tasks. In terms
of equivalent PnP-Flow iterations, the reconstruction quality achieved by HP-PnP-Flow after 20
iterations is comparable to that of PnP-Flow after approximately 40-120 iterations, depending on
the task. The largest efficiency gap is observed for random inpainting (20 vs. 80-120 iterations),
whereas the smallest gap occurs for deblurring (20 vs. 40-60 iterations).
HP-PnP-Flow also achieves better final reconstruction quality. Across the five tasks and both
datasets, its converged PSNR exceeds that of PnP-Flow by 0.38-1.74 dB, with the largest im-
provements observed for super-resolution and random inpainting. On AFHQ-Cat, HP-PnP-Flow
requires only 100 iterations for deblurring and super-resolution, compared with 500 iterations for
PnP-Flow, and 60 iterations for box inpainting, compared with 100 for PnP-Flow. Taken together,
the faster early-stage convergence and reduced iteration budget enable HP-PnP-Flow to reach a
given PSNR level in substantially fewer iterations than PnP-Flow.

Conclusion
In this work, we propose an efficient hybrid-projection Plug-and-Play-Flow algorithm, an im-
proved Plug-and-Play algorithm for image inverse problems that integrates flow matching priors
with a hybrid time schedule and pixel-space projection operator. Furthermore, we establish the
convergence of HP-PnP-Flow without imposing the boundedness assumption on the iterates re-
quired in original PnP-Flow (see Proposition 4 in [27]), since the pixel-space projection keeps the
18


iterates within the prescribed pixel range. The hybrid schedule combines geometric and linear
growth phases, satisfies the convergence condition, and achieves faster early-stage reconstruction
and improved numerical performance over the linear schedule used in PnP-Flow. Experiments on
CelebA and AFHQ-Cat across five degradation tasks show that HP-PnP-Flow achieves the best
or second-best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS in most settings, while requiring fewer iterations than
PnP-Flow (e.g., 100 vs. 500 iterations for deblurring on AFHQ-Cat). Ablation studies further
demonstrate that the initial time offset t0 and the hybrid schedule contribute independently to
the performance gains, with their combination yielding the best overall performance.
Several directions remain for future investigation. First, we plan to establish quantitative error
bounds for HP-PnP-Flow under mild assumptions. Second, it would be interesting to explore
whether the proposed hybrid time schedule can be extended to other generative-model-based PnP
frameworks beyond flow matching.
