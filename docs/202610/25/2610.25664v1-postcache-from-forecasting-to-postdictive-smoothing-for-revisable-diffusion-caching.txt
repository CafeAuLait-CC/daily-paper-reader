ABSTRACT 

Diffusion and flow matching models achieve impressive visual generation quality but remain computationally expensive due to iterative sampling. Caching-based acceleration provides a practical training-free solution by reusing or predicting intermediate features. However, existing methods largely enforce a forward-only information flow: approximations produced for skipped model evaluations are im-mediately committed to the sampling trajectory and cannot benefit from feedback generated by later full-computation steps. We propose PoSTCache (Postdictive Smoothing Trajectory Cache ), a training-free and plug-and-play framework that reformulates diffusion caching as revisable trajectory inference. PoSTCache first uses an existing caching strategy to construct forward proposals for skipped steps. At a later full-computation step, the discrepancy between the refreshed predic-tion and its forward proposal yields a correction signal, which PoSTCache propa-gates backward to refine and reintegrate the preceding predictions. Viewing full-computation steps as sources of corrective feedback also changes how they should be selected. We therefore introduce the Posterior Variance Reduction (PVR) strat-egy, which estimates cross-timestep dependencies from offline denoising trajecto-ries and selects anchors according to their expected reduction in surrogate trajec-tory uncertainty rather than local approximation error alone. Experiments on rep-resentative image and video generation models show that PoSTCache consistently improves the quality–efficiency trade-off over representative caching methods. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Diffusion models (Song & Ermon, 2019; Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2020b) and flow matching models (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023) have become a dominant paradigm for modern visual generation. By progressively transporting noisy latent variables toward structured data samples, they enable impressive image (Rombach et al., 2022; Podell et al., 2024) and video synthesis qual-ity (Blattmann et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2024a). Despite this progress, sampling still requires tens to hundreds of steps, making user-facing applications latency-bound. Traditional acceleration techniques, such as distillation (Song et al., 2023) and post-training quan-tization (Li et al., 2023), often incur additional training costs, architectural modifications, or non-trivial deployment efforts. In contrast, caching provides a lightweight, training-free alternative by exploiting the temporal redundancy of intermediate representations (Liu et al., 2025a; Bu et al., 2025; Chung et al., 2026). Existing caching approaches have evolved from static feature reuse (Sel-varaju et al., 2024) to adaptive strategies that estimate inter-step redundancy online (Liu et al., 2025a; Ma et al., 2026; Haghighi & Alahi, 2026), extrapolate future feature trajectories with polynomial approximations (Liu et al., 2025b; Feng et al., 2026), or exploit shallow-layer dynamics to guide deep-layer feature reuse (Bu et al., 2025). More recently, caching has also been extended beyond the original feature space to spectral representations (Chung et al., 2026). This raises a broader question: does later sampling reveal new information that could help correct these earlier errors? In particular, periodic full-computation steps produce fresh predictions after the trajectory has evolved through preceding approximations, potentially exposing backward-looking information about accumulated errors. For example, the discrepancy between a refreshed prediction and its forward proposal can provide such corrective evidence. However, most existing methods use this newly revealed informa-tion only for subsequent sampling, rather than propagating it backward to revise earlier estimates. This motivates a postdictive smoothing view of diffusion caching, inspired by Bayesian smoothing, in which later evidence retrospectively revises uncertain intermediate estimates (Rauch et al., 1965; Rabiner, 1989; S¨ arkk¨ a & Svensson, 2023). Accordingly, we treat skipped predictions as provisional rather than final, allowing feedback from later full-computation steps to retrospectively refine these estimates and enable backward information flow , as illustrated in Figure 1(b). Motivated by this perspective, we propose PoSTCache, short for Postdictive Smoothing Trajectory Cache , a training-free and plug-and-play framework that reformulates cache-based acceleration as revisable trajectory inference. Building on an existing caching strategy, PoSTCache propagates feedback from subsequent full-computation anchors backward to refine skipped predictions and reintegrates the corrected trajectory. This shift also changes how anchors should be valued: from their local approximation error to their trajectory-level corrective value. We therefore introduce the Posterior Variance Reduction (PVR) strategy, which estimates cross-timestep dependencies from offline denoising trajectories and greedily selects anchors to reduce surrogate trajectory uncertainty under a fixed computation budget. 2 RELATED WORK 

Generative Model Acceleration. Acceleration methods for diffusion and flow-based models reduce either the cost of each model evaluation or the number of sampling steps. The former includes model compression techniques such as pruning (Fang et al., 2023), quantization (Li et al., 2023), and knowledge distillation (Meng et al., 2023), as well as computation-efficient designs such as FlashAttention (Dao et al., 2022) and token reduction (Bolya et al., 2022). The latter improves sampling efficiency through deterministic sampling with DDIM (Song et al., 2020a), high-order ODE solvers such as DPM-Solver (Lu et al., 2022), alternative generative dynamics including flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) and rectified flow (Liu et al., 2023), or few-step generation through progressive distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022), consistency models (Song et al., 2023), Shortcut Models (Frans et al., 2025), and MeanFlow (Geng et al., 2026). Although effective, these approaches often require additional training, architectural changes, or modifications to the sampling process. 

Cache-based Acceleration. Caching provides a lightweight and training-free alternative by exploit-ing the temporal redundancy of intermediate representations. Early methods reuse U-Net features with fixed schedules, including DeepCache (Ma et al., 2024) and FasterDiffusion (Li et al., 2024), while FORA (Selvaraju et al., 2024) and ∆-DiT (Chen et al., 2024b) extend feature or attention reuse to diffusion transformers. More recent approaches adaptively determine when to refresh cached features using different error proxies, such as TeaCache (Liu et al., 2025a), MagCache (Ma et al., 2026), and DiCache (Bu et al., 2025). TaylorSeer (Liu et al., 2025b) and HiCache (Feng et al., 2026) instead predict future features from historical states. Other methods incorporate trajectory priors, in-cluding spectral evolution in SeaCache (Chung et al., 2026), global path planning in DPCache (Cui et al., 2026), average-velocity stability in MeanCache (Gao et al., 2026), and offline residual rec-tification in ERTACache (Peng et al., 2026). Despite these advances, most methods remain online forward-only: skipped states are determined using historical information or offline priors and remain unrevised after future full-computation states become available. 

Bayesian Posterior Smoothing. Bayesian smoothing estimates an intermediate latent state using both past and future observations, whereas filtering conditions only on observations available up to the current time. For linear Gaussian systems, this principle underlies Kalman filtering (Kalman, 1960) and the Rauch–Tung–Striebel smoother (Rauch et al., 1965). A similar forward–backward in-ference procedure is used in hidden Markov models (Rabiner, 1989). Bayesian smoothing has been generalized to nonlinear and non-Gaussian state-space models (S¨ arkk¨ a & Svensson, 2023). These methods share the central idea that future evidence can retrospectively correct uncertain intermediate estimates, motivating our reformulation of diffusion caching as revisable trajectory inference.

3.1 PRELIMINARIES 

Diffusion sampling evolves a latent trajectory through a sequence of model predictions and state up-dates, resembling sequential Bayesian estimation (Kalman, 1960). At each denoising step, the model produces a prediction from the information available so far, which is then used by the numerical solver to advance the trajectory. When some predictions are replaced by inaccurate approximations, their errors can propagate through subsequent updates and accumulate along the trajectory. Bayesian smoothing provides a principled mechanism for revising such forward estimates once in-formative future evidence becomes available (Rauch et al., 1965; Rabiner, 1989; S¨ arkk¨ a & Svens-son, 2023). We investigate whether a later full-computation step provides useful evidence for retrospectively correcting these errors. Consider a cache interval bounded by two full-computation steps a < b . ek = ¯ uk − euk, rb = ¯ ub − eub, a < k < b, (3) where ek is the proposal error, and rb is the future-anchor residual. Following the Bayesian smooth-ing interpretation in Sec. 3.1, we hereafter refer to this residual as the anchor innovation .

These results indicate that the anchor innovation rb contains increasingly accurate directional information about the errors accumulated within the preceding interval. 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 SETTINGS 

Base Models and Compared Methods. We evaluate PoSTCache on three video generation models, Open-Sora 1.2 (Zheng et al., 2024), CogVideoX (Yang et al., 2025), and Wan 2.1 (Wan et al., 2025), and two image generation models, FLUX.1-dev (Labs, 2024) and Qwen-Image-2512 (Wu et al., 2025). We compare against representative diffusion caching methods, including TeaCache (Liu et al., 2025a), TaylorSeer (Liu et al., 2025b), MagCache (Ma et al., 2026), DiCache (Bu et al., 2025), HiCache (Feng et al., 2026), SeaCache (Chung et al., 2026), and SenCache (Haghighi & Alahi, 2026). We enable classifier-free guidance (CFG) (Ho & Salimans, 2022) for Qwen-Image-2512 and Wan 2.1, using identical guidance settings for full inference and all accelerated methods. 

Evaluation Metrics. We evaluate 200 DrawBench prompts (Saharia et al., 2022) with three ran-dom seeds for text-to-image generation and 944 VBench prompts (Huang et al., 2024) with five random seeds for text-to-video generation. Following prior work (Bu et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025a), we report latency, speedup, FLOPs, and peak allocated GPU memory. ImageReward and CLIP Score assess image quality, while VBench evaluates video quality. LPIPS (Zhang et al., 2018), SSIM (Wang et al., 2004), and PSNR measure reconstruction fidelity against full inference. All results are averaged over random seeds. 

Implementation Details. All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA H200 GPU. We use 10 offline calibration trajectories to estimate the cross-timestep covariance statistics. 5 CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 

In this work, we presented PoSTCache, a training-free and plug-and-play framework that refor-mulates diffusion caching as a revisable trajectory inference problem. By using feedback from subsequent full-computation steps, PoSTCache retrospectively refines skipped predictions and rein-tegrates the corrected sampling trajectory. Viewing anchors as sources of corrective feedback, we further introduced a posterior variance reduction criterion to select informative anchors according 9Preprint. Under review. to their expected reduction in surrogate trajectory uncertainty. Extensive experiments on represen-tative image and video generation models demonstrate that PoSTCache consistently improves the efficiency–fidelity trade-off and can be readily integrated with different forward caching strategies. Currently, PoSTCache estimates a shared anchor configuration from offline trajectories and applies it uniformly to all samples. Although this design is simple and effective, it may not fully capture sample-specific trajectory dynamics. A promising direction for future work is to combine the offline uncertainty structure with lightweight online observations, enabling the anchor schedule to adapt to each sampling trajectory without introducing substantial inference overhead.

A.1 SEQUENTIAL TRAJECTORY ESTIMATION VIEW OF DIFFUSION AND FLOW SAMPLING

ImageReward. ImageReward (Xu et al., 2023) is a learned human-preference metric for text-to-image generation. It evaluates the consistency and perceptual quality of a generated image with respect to its text prompt. A higher score indicates stronger alignment with human preference.

DiCache introduces substantial structural degradation in the lighthouse scene, while SenCache pro-duces more noticeable changes in object geometry and background appearance. PoSTCache there-fore provides a closer approximation to full inference while attaining the highest acceleration ratio among the compared methods in this setting. Overall, the qualitative results are consistent with the reconstruction metrics reported in the main paper. By revising skipped predictions using future-anchor evidence, PoSTCache reduces accumu-lated errors in local texture, object geometry, spatial composition, and temporal appearance across different image and video generation architectures. Target-specific reprofiling can improve the offline PVR objective or the held-out prediction-space error without improving the final reconstruction. This discrepancy reflects an offline–online gap: the offline profile is estimated on stored full-computation trajectories, whereas online correction changes the latent states and recursively affects subsequent model predictions. In addition, the current greedy selector is path dependent; small finite-sample perturbations of otherwise similar covariance profiles can alter early anchor choices and produce substantially different schedules.
