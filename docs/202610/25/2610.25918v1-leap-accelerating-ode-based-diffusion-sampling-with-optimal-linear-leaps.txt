Abstract
Diffusion models primarily rely on general-purpose ODE-based
numerical methods for training-free accelerated sampling, but per-
formance degrades with coarse time discretization. In this paper, we
propose optimal linear leaps (LEAP), a specialized method grounded
in theoretical and empirical investigations of sampling trajectory
geometry. Built upon low-dimensionality and cross-trajectory con-
sistency, LEAP formulates a supervised least-squares optimization
problem within the local trajectory subspace, achieving a precise ap-
proximation of future states without model evaluations. Extensive
experiments demonstrate that LEAP outperforms previous methods
across diverse modalities, including text-to-image (Stable Diffusion
3.5, FLUX.1-dev) and image-to-3D (TRELLIS.2) generation. Further-
more, LEAP can be seamlessly integrated into existing solver-based
methods, offering a plug-and-play enhancement for modern diffu-
sion models. Code is available at https://github.com/zju-pi/LEAP.

Despite their remarkable generation quality, the slow sampling pro-
cess imposes a critical bottleneck for latency-sensitive applications.
To address this issue, numerous acceleration techniques have
been proposed. Training-based approaches distill a pre-trained
multi-step model into an extremely few-step student model [12, 20,
28, 31]. However, distilling modern diffusion models with billions
of parameters (e.g., 8B Stable Diffusion 3.5 [8] and 12B FLUX [2]) is
challenging and inflexible to accommodate community-tuned vari-
ants and LoRA modules. In contrast, training-free methods require
minimal parameter updates, offering superior plug-and-play flexibil-
ity. Recent progress includes (i) solver-based method for calculating
ordinary differential equations (ODEs) [16, 18, 30, 46, 49], (ii) opti-
mized timestep scheduling [4, 26, 43], and (iii) feature caching [14,
21, 39, 44]. Existing high-order multi-step solvers [16, 18, 46] es-
timate future states via a linear combination of historical states
where the coefficients are derived from numerical analysis, assum-
ing sufficiently small and near-uniform step sizes. However, practi-
cal acceleration typically employs large and non-uniform step sizes,
making their assumptions and associated convergence guarantees
less reliable. Concurrently, emerging research on the geometric
regularity of diffusion trajectories [4, 5, 36, 38] has uncovered in-
triguing empirical patterns, such as cross-trajectory consistency
and low-dimensional concentration. While previous studies have
qualitatively observed these regularities, a rigorous mathematical
foundation remains largely absent. To move beyond heuristic de-
signs toward a principled approach, a deeper and more rigorous
investigation into the underlying mechanics is essential.
To bridge this gap, we conduct a comprehensive theoretical anal-
ysis of the geometric properties inherent in diffusion trajectories,
yielding the following two theoretical insights. Firstly, we establish
a theoretical bound on the alignment error between trajectories af-
ter orthogonal rotational alignment, revealing the cross-trajectory
consistency of diffusion models. Secondly, we rigorously character-
ize the intrinsic low-dimensionality of diffusion sampling trajecto-
ries, proving that individual paths are confined to a low-dimensional
subspace. Our analysis reveals that despite different noise initial-
izations, these generative paths share consistent low-dimensional
dynamics.
Leveraging these two mathematically grounded priors, we pro-
pose optimal linear leaps (LEAP), a training-free, solver-based ac-
celeration framework. In contrast to traditional numerical solvers

The intrinsic low-dimensionality of diffusion trajectories suggests
that local path segments can be accurately characterized by a linear
subspace. Leveraging this geometric regularity, we formulate the
prediction as a least-squares optimization problem, which iden-
tifies the optimal linear coefficients by minimizing the discrep-
ancy between linearly extrapolated historical states and multi-step
ground-truth trajectory samples. Motivated by the cross-trajectory
consistency, we demonstrate that the optimal linear extrapolation
coefficients are sample-agnostic. This property guarantees the trans-
ferability of coefficients derived from a limited set of reference tra-
jectories before inference, bypassing the overhead for per-sample
optimization. LEAP significantly enhances the sampling efficiency
while preserving high generation quality. Crucially, as it operates
entirely at the macroscopic trajectory level, our approach func-
tions as a model-agnostic and plug-and-play module. This allows
for seamless integration into diverse diffusion models without any
architectural modifications. Extensive experiments across diverse
modalities, including text-to-image generation and text-to-3D gen-
eration demonstrate the superior performance and versatility of
our method.
In summary, our main contributions are as follows:
• We prove the intrinsic low-dimensionality and geometric iso-
morphism of generative trajectories across diverse noise initial-
izations.
• Motivated by these theoretical insights, we propose LEAP, a
novel multi-step ODE solver that leverages least-squares align-
ment to learn sample-agnostic extrapolation coefficients, en-
abling highly accurate linear leaps with minimal computational
overhead.
• Extensive experiments across diverse generative tasks that span-
ning both text-to-tmage synthesis and complex image-to-3D
generation demonstrate the effectiveness of our method com-
pared to state-of-the-art training-free baselines.

Contemporary cutting-edge text-to-image models increasingly
favor the Flow Matching framework due to its conceptual simplicity
and flexible path design [2, 8]. Since they inherit the architecture

Trajectory Regularity. While diffusion models operate in an
immensely high-dimensional ambient space, recent empirical ob-
servations reveal that their sampling trajectories exhibit remark-
able geometric regularity [4, 5, 37]. Principal Component Analysis
(PCA) on individual sampling trajectory exhibits that the genera-
tive dynamics are inherently low-dimensional where the explained
variance is dominated by the first few principal components. Fur-
thermore, as illustrated in Fig. 2, when projecting multiple trajecto-
ries with distinct noise and prompt initializations into a shared 3D
space, they bundle into a smooth ‘tube’. This visual alignment high-
lights a striking cross-trajectory consistency, indicating that different
diffusion paths share nearly isomorphic macroscopic dynamics. To-
gether, this inter-trajectory low-dimensionality and inter-trajectory
consistency constitute the trajectory regularity, serving as the foun-
dational intuition for our subsequent theoretical analysis and the
design of our acceleration algorithm.

4
Experiments
We evaluate the proposed LEAP on three cutting-edge diffusion
models: SD3.5-Medium [8] and FLUX.1-Dev [2] for text-image task
and TRELLIS.2 [41] for image-3D task. The quality of generated
images is quantitatively evaluated using three metrics, including
ImageReward [42], CLIP Score [23], and HPSv2 [40]. The prompts
are drawn from the DrawBench [27] dataset. For image-3D task,
we evaluate 3D object on a test set of 100 randomly sampled images
from the Google Scanned Objects (GSO) dataset [7] by PSNR and
CLIP Score. By default, we fix a window size of 𝑟= 5 and set the
ridge regularization coefficient to 𝜆= 0.0001 across all experiments.
Unless otherwise specified, LEAP is compared with existing state-
of-the-art solvers under identical inference settings.
4.1
Main Results
Text-to-image Generation. We evaluate LEAP on text-to-image
generation, benchmarking it against representative solvers includ-
ing Euler method [30], DPM++ [19], UniPC [46], iPNDM [35], LTC-
Accel [50] and STORK-4 [34]. As shown in Tab. 1, LEAP consistently
achieves top-tier performance across diverse sampling budgets,
effectively preserving both text-image alignment and perceptual
quality for accelerated sampling. Moreover, LEAP consistently en-
hances the performance of both first-order and higher-order solvers,
demonstrating its versatile plug-and-play capability. By leverag-
ing a pre-computed, step-wise coefficient bank, LEAP seamlessly
adapts any solver without any solver-specific hyperparameter tun-
ing or model fine-tuning, making it a solver-agnostic enhancement
module. Fig. 4 provides a qualitative comparison of LEAP against
state-of-the-art solvers on SD3.5-Medium. Our LEAP exhibits su-
perior performance across four challenging scenarios including (1)
text rendering: LEAP accurately synthesizes legible text, whereas
baselines suffer from typographic errors; (2) physical lighting: LEAP
captures realistic transparency and intricate light refraction; (3) at-
tribute binding: LEAP precisely binds the localized attributes to
their respective partitions without semantic leakage.
Image-to-3D Generation. We further validate LEAP on image-
to-3D generation using the TRELLIS.2 [41] architecture. As pre-
sented in Tab. 2, our method achieves systematic improvements
in both PSNR and CLIP Score over the baseline on GSO dataset.
The qualitative results shown in Fig. 5 demonstrate LEAP’s supe-
rior capability in (1) topological correctness, maintaining precise
connectivity in complex jointed structures; (2) surface fidelity; (3)
geometric completeness, effectively generating intricate and high-
frequency geometries, yielding sharper textural details and superior
color accuracy.

high-order and multi-step solvers. PNDM [16] introduced multi-
step pseudo numerical methods, showing that leveraging historical
samples can significantly improve the quality-speed trade-off over
first-order samplers. Its improved implementation, iPNDM [35], is
frequently utilized in modern diffusion sampling. DEIS [45] further
exploited the semi-linear structure of diffusion dynamics through

and DPM++ [19] derived dedicated high-order solvers by analyt-

the predictor-corrector paradigm. Subsequent refinements such as
DPM-Solver-v3 [48] and DC-Solver [47], further improved solver
parameterization and correction stability. LTC-Accel [50] leverages
the local transition coherence of generative trajectories to dynam-
ically approximate adjacent network outputs, achieving training-
free acceleration. STORK [34] addressed numerical stiffness and
removed the reliance on semi-linear assumptions and proposed a
stabilized solver. Despite their differences, these methods still con-
struct update rules primarily from numerical analysis or step-wise
trend heuristics. In contrast, our method revisits multi-step solving
from a trajectory-geometric perspective and seeks to exploit the
intrinsic low-dimensional and cross-trajectory consistent structure
of generative trajectories more directly.
Diffusion sampling dynamics. Prior research on diffusion
sampling dynamics has mainly established trajectory regularity
through empirical observations, supported by preliminary theo-
retical insights under idealized settings. Notably, Wang et al. [37]
observed that individual reverse-time sampling trajectories are
effectively low-dimensional, often resembling two-dimensional ro-
tations. Chen et al. [5] studied deterministic PF-ODE trajectories,
finding that each trajectory closely follows the displacement be-
tween its endpoints, with residuals neatly captured by two or three
orthogonal directions, yielding a characteristic boomerang-like
shape. Their subsequent work [4] strengthened the cross-trajectory
consistency claim by aligning projected trajectories with orthog-
onal Procrustes, revealing a nearly canonical trajectory shape af-
ter sample-specific rotations and reflections. While these works
have empirically uncovered the trajectory regularity of diffusion
sampling dynamics, their theoretical grounding relies largely on
KDE-based surrogates of the empirical data distribution, analyti-
cally tractable Gaussian models, and supportive Gaussian-mixture
calculations. To the best of our knowledge, prior literature lacks
a unified theorem within a Mixture-of-Gaussian framework that
jointly formalizes both trajectory low-dimensionality and cross-
trajectory isomorphism. In contrast, our work provides a principled
derivation that simultaneously establishes these two properties.
6
Conclusion
In this paper, we introduced LEAP, a novel, training-free multi-step
ODE solver that effectively addresses the performance degradation
of diffusion models in low-NFE regimes. Our approach is built upon
a rigorous investigation of the geometric properties of diffusion
sampling trajectories. By proving the intrinsic low-dimensionality
and cross-trajectory consistency of generative paths, we formulated
a supervised least-squares optimization within the local trajectory
subspace. This allows LEAP to derive optimal, sample-agnostic ex-
trapolation coefficients, achieving accurate future state approxima-
tions with minimal computational overhead. Extensive evaluations
demonstrate that LEAP serves as a versatile, plug-and-play enhance-
ment for modern large-scale diffusion models. LEAP consistently
outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across diverse modalities,
including text-to-image generation using Stable Diffusion 3.5 and
FLUX.1-Dev and image-to-3D generation using TRELLIS.2. Future
work will delve deeper into the geometric properties of diffusion
sampling trajectories under practical settings to further align our
theoretical framework with realistic, finite-step sampling scenarios.
