## SUPER MOTION : SOURCE -P RESERVING DENOISING FOR TEXT -D RIVEN HUMAN MOTION EDITING 

Fa-Ting Hong, Peter Wonka 

King Abdullah University of Science and Technology 

{fating.hong, peter.wonka }@kaust.edu.sa 

ABSTRACT 

Text-driven human motion editing aims to realize a requested change while pre-serving compatible source content. Existing diffusion editors rely largely on learned conditioning for preservation of the unedited part, yet their outputs can lose temporal detail as denoising proceeds. We propose the Source-Preserving Denoising framework (SuperMotion) , which explicitly reuses the source at each reverse step for source preservation. We first align the source motion to the output timeline and predict a preservation gate that controls reuse across frames and fea-ture dimensions. A clean-space source anchor then utilizes the learned preserva-tion gate to blend the predicted clean motion with the aligned source and passes the corrected estimate directly to the sampling posterior. Because the aligned source is a realized motion rather than a regression output, the anchor injects sample-level temporal detail that a reconstruction-trained denoiser tends to smooth away. To learn effective source reuse, we supervise the anchored estimate against the editing target and match its second temporal differences through a temporal high-frequency loss. These objectives require no explicit edit masks. Extensive ex-periments show that SuperMotion improves editing accuracy, reaching 33.20% full-pool R@1 on MotionFix, while reducing temporal-detail attenuation and pre-serving motion dynamics as it realizes the requested changes. Ablations confirm that the learned preservation gate is responsible for the gain and that it reuses the source to retain the unedited content properly.

Concretely, we define a detail ra-tio : the magnitude of second-order temporal differences of the running clean-motion estimate ˆx0 at a given reverse step, normalized by the same statistic computed on the ground-truth target. A well-calibrated re-verse process should drive this ratio toward 1× and hold it there. Fig-ure 2 shows that prior diffusion edi-tors behave otherwise: the median ra-tio decreases along the reverse trajec-tory, crosses 1×, and keeps falling, reaching 0.33 × for TMED (Athana-siou et al., 2024), 0.66 × for Omn-iME (Shi et al., 2026), and 0.71 ×

for SimMotionEdit (Li et al., 2025). The decline concentrates in the final low-noise steps, where the estimate already matches the target detail level and further denoising suppresses lo-cal temporal variation instead of re-fining it. A denoiser trained with a reconstruction objective approximates a posterior mean (Efron, 2011), and such distortion-optimal estimates are necessarily smoother than individual samples (Blau & Michaeli, 2018). The effect is especially costly in motion editing. The content that should be preserved already exists in the source, but regenerating the sequence from noise forces the model to resynthesize it, after which it is pulled toward the mean. These observations motivate explicit control over source reuse during denoising. In frameworks that reconstruct the target from noise, source conditioning provides information about the input mo-tion, but retaining its content still depends on the denoiser’s predictions (Athanasiou et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025). The source could also provide a clean signal against which those predictions are adjusted at each reverse step. Related ideas appear in diffusion-based image editing and inpainting, where spatial masks or known regions guide the preservation of existing content (Avrahami et al., 2022; Lugmayr et al., 2022; Couairon et al., 2022). Applying this principle to motion editing re-quires accounting for differences between the source and target sequences. Because no preservation mask is supplied, the model must learn how strongly to reuse the aligned source while allowing the instruction to change the movement. We introduce the Source-Pres er ving Denoising framework (SuperMotion) to learn this balance within the reverse process. Its central component, a Clean-Space Source Anchor , blends the cur-rent clean-motion estimate with a temporally aligned source through a learned Source Preservation Gate . The gate assigns a reuse weight to each frame and feature dimension at each denoising step. Applied after classifier-free guidance and clean-motion reconstruction, the anchor produces the es-timate used directly by the sampling posterior. This placement gives the gate a consistent interpre-tation as a convex blending weight and avoids the inverse noise-scale factor required to express the same clean-motion correction in velocity space. Supervision against the editing target trains the gate to adjust source reuse without an explicit edit mask. We train SuperMotion with a source-anchor loss that learns source reuse from the editing target and a temporal high-frequency loss that matches second temporal differences. We conduct extensive experiments to evaluate our method. The results show that SuperMotion improves instruction compliance while reducing temporal-detail attenuation. Ablation studies show 2Under review that supervising the anchored prediction against the editing target is critical to learning effective source reuse. Our contributions are threefold. 1. We identify temporal-detail attenuation as a systematic failure of diffusion motion editors. Their clean-motion estimates lose second-order temporal variation during late denoising, and several published editors score below a copy-source baseline. 2. We propose SuperMotion, which reuses a temporally aligned source at each reverse step through a learned per-frame, per-dimension gate on the clean-motion estimate, trained from the editing target without edit masks. 3. SuperMotion reaches 79.17% batch R@1 and 33.20% full-pool R@1 on MotionFix and 46.88% batch R@1 on STANCE Adjustment, surpassing the strongest prior editor by 1.88, 1.18, and 3.13 points. It restores the detail ratio to 1.035, against 0.66 to 0.71 for prior editors, confirming that the learned preservation gate recovers high-frequency detail from the source motion. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Human Motion Generation. Text-driven human motion generation has progressed through con-tinuous latent modeling, diffusion-based synthesis, and discrete motion modeling. Guo et al. (Guo et al., 2022) investigate diverse text-conditioned motion synthesis and introduce HumanML3D, sup-porting subsequent advances in language-driven animation. TEMOS (Petrovich et al., 2022) learns compatible text and motion distributions through variational training, while MotionCLIP (Tevet et al., 2022a) aligns motion representations with the CLIP embedding space. Diffusion-based meth-ods, including MDM (Tevet et al., 2022b), MotionDiffuse (Zhang et al., 2024), and FLAME (Kim et al., 2023), generate motions through text-conditioned iterative denoising. MLD (Chen et al., 2023) instead performs diffusion in a compact latent space to reduce computational cost. Discrete mo-tion representations provide an alternative to continuous-space generation. T2M-GPT (Zhang et al., 2023a) combines vector quantization with autoregressive token prediction, while MotionGPT (Jiang et al., 2023) models motion and language within a unified token-based framework. MoMask (Guo et al., 2024) and MMM (Pinyoanuntapong et al., 2024) adopt masked motion modeling, en-abling iterative token reconstruction and flexible generation. Beyond global text conditioning, FineMoGen (Zhang et al., 2023b) introduces fine-grained spatio-temporal control, and MaskCon-trol (Pinyoanuntapong et al., 2025) incorporates spatial constraints into masked motion generation. Together, these approaches improve the diversity, efficiency, and controllability of human motion synthesis. Our focus is source-conditioned editing rather than synthesizing a new realization from text alone. SuperMotion retains a diffusion backbone and introduces a learned source-reuse operator to preserve compatible motion content during denoising. 

Human Motion Editing. Human motion editing modifies an existing sequence while retaining con-tent compatible with the requested change. Early generative approaches enable editing through se-mantic latent manipulation or constrained reconstruction. MotionCLIP (Tevet et al., 2022a) supports editing in a language-aligned latent space, while MDM (Tevet et al., 2022b) and FLAME (Kim et al., 2023) support motion completion and editing through constrained diffusion. MMM (Pinyoanun-tapong et al., 2024) reconstructs masked motion tokens, and FineMoGen (Zhang et al., 2023b) sup-ports fine-grained editing through spatio-temporal descriptions. These approaches establish useful editing capabilities, although their control interfaces and supervision differ from paired instruction-based editing. MotionFix (Athanasiou et al., 2024) establishes a benchmark of source–instruction– target triplets and introduces a source-conditioned diffusion editor. Subsequent work improves in-struction alignment, motion preservation, and editing versatility. SimMotionEdit (Li et al., 2025) jointly trains motion editing and motion-similarity prediction, while PartMotionEdit (Yang et al., 2025) combines part-aware modulation with part-level similarity supervision and bidirectional text– motion interaction. MotionReFit (Jiang et al., 2025) expands editing supervision through Motion-CutMix and uses an autoregressive diffusion model with a motion coordinator. MotionLab (Guo et al., 2025) unifies generation and editing through a motion–condition–motion formulation based on rectified flows. OmniME (Shi et al., 2026) combines intermediate-feature, preservation, and positive–negative semantic supervision. 3 METHOD 

Given a source motion and an editing instruction, our goal is to generate a motion that realizes the requested change while retaining compatible source content. Our Source-Preserving Denoising (SuperMotion) makes source reuse an explicit operation inside the reverse process, applied to the clean-motion estimate at every step and controlled by a gate learned from the editing target alone. 3.1 PROBLEM FORMULATION 

Text-based 3D motion editing takes a source sequence s ∈ RTs×D and a natural-language instruction 

c, and produces a motion ˆx0 ∈ RT ×D that carries out c. We use the MotionFix feature of D=207 

z-normalized dimensions per frame (pelvis translation increments, 6D orientation, z-orientation in-crements, 21-joint body pose, and 22 local joint coordinates) at 30 fps. The instruction typically changes only part of the source. Over the remaining frames and coordinates the target coincides with the source, so the underlying edit is close to an identity mapping. Diffusion editors nevertheless regenerate the full sequence from Gaussian noise with s and c used only as conditions, so every reverse step re-synthesizes coordinates that could have been copied. A denoiser trained with a reconstruction loss approximates the posterior mean E[x0 | xt], and such distortion-optimal estimates are smoother than any individual sample (Blau & Michaeli, 2018). The identity part of the edit is therefore re-estimated from noise at every step and pulled toward a mean it does not belong to. Let x0 be the clean target motion and

5 CONCLUSION 

In this work, we analyze the denoising process of existing methods and identify the temporal-detail attenuation as a systematic failure of diffusion motion editors. We propose Source-Preserving De-noising, which makes source reuse an explicit operation at every reverse step by blending the clean-motion estimate toward the aligned source through a learned per-frame, per-dimension gate. Ex-tensive experiments demonstrate that SuperMotion consistently outperforms state-of-the-art editors on both MotionFix and STANCE Adjustment. Ablations attribute the improvement to the learned preservation gate, which enables selective source reuse and preserves high-frequency temporal detail that competing methods attenuate. 9Under review
