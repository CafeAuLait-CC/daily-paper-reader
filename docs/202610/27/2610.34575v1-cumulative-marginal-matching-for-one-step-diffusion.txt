Abstract 

The sampling procedures of diffusion and flow-matching mod-els involve hundreds of denoising steps incurring substantial inference time, which has motivated a growing body of work on training schemes for few-step and one-step generation. Ex-isting approaches primarily seek to align the generation tra-jectory of few-step and base models, yet their training objec-tives only involve discrepancies at a single point in the tra-jectory, encouraging only local point-level alignment. While recent methods such as Shortcut-CSL introduce cumulative objective that enforce alignment over the entire trajectory, this is suboptimal as they do not directly enforce alignment between the distributions induced by the two models through-out the generation process. We propose Cumulative Marginal Matching (CMM), a principled objective for few-step genera-tion: by viewing flow-matching as a sequence of intermediate marginal distributions connecting the prior and data distri-butions, CMM directly optimizes the cumulative discrepancy between the base and few-step marginal distributions over the entire sequence. Specifically, we formulate this discrepancy using the Wasserstein-2 distance. By exploiting the pushfor-ward representation of flow-matching, we transform an in-tractable cumulative Wasserstein objective into a tractable surrogate that can be optimized using samples from the in-termediate marginal, which are readily generated through the conditional interpolation process of flow-matching. Further-more, we show that the existing Shortcut-CSL objective arises as a first-order approximation of that of the CMM, establish-ing a theoretical connection between the two objectives. Ex-periments on standard benchmarks demonstrate improvement in generation quality over Shortcut-CSL and other state-of-the-art one- and few-step baselines under identical training budgets. 

# Introduction 

Modern generative models have largely adopted diffusion (Sohl-Dickstein et al. 2015; Ho, Jain, and Abbeel 2020; Song et al. 2021; Rombach et al. 2022) and flow-matching processes (Lipman et al. 2023; Albergo, Boffi, and Vanden-Eijnden 2023) as their underlying generation paradigm, transforming a simple prior distribution, e.g., Gaussian noise, into a target data distribution. These methods demonstrate remarkable capability in generating structured data such as

Both diffusion and flow-matching models require simulating an integral to generate samples, which in practice requires an iterative procedure that gradually transforms Gaussian noise into data samples. As a result, high-quality generation typically requires hun-dreds of denoising steps. This limitation has motivated a growing body of research on developing methods capable of producing high-quality samples in just one or a few denois-ing steps (Salimans and Ho 2022; Yin et al. 2024; Song et al. 2023; Frans et al. 2025; Geng et al. 2025). Methods for one- and few-step generation can be broadly categorized into two classes: two-phase pretrain-and-distill approaches and single-phase approaches that jointly train the base diffusion/flow-matching models and few-step models. arXiv:2610.34575v1 [cs.LG] 25 Oct 2026 In two-phase methods, a high-quality base diffusion or flow-matching model is first trained to learn the flow map from the prior to the data distribution. The knowledge encoded in this transformation is then distilled into a student (few-step) model capable of generating samples directly in one or a few steps (Salimans and Ho 2022; Yin et al. 2024; Liu, Gong, and qiang liu 2023; Kim et al. 2025; Lee, Lin, and Fanti 2024). In contrast, recent single-phase approaches parameterize both the base and few-step generators using a shared backbone network and combine their training by jointly optimizing flow-matching and distillation objectives. Representative ex-amples include Consistency Training (Song et al. 2023; Song and Dhariwal 2024; Lu and Song 2025), which enforces map-ping consistency across different points of the generation pro-cess; Inductive Moment Matching (Zhou, Ermon, and Song 2025), which aligns the base and few-step models through moment-matching objectives; Shortcut Models (Frans et al. 2025; Nguyen et al. 2025; Lin et al. 2026), which learn multi-step transitions to enable generation with a variable number of denoising steps; and MeanFlow (Geng et al. 2025), which distills the base model’s dynamics by matching the average flow in the generation trajectory. All of these methods formu-late distillation as alignment between the teacher and student trajectories, differing primarily in the extent of supervision. Existing methods’ distillation objectives are defined through discrepancies evaluated at individual time points, resulting in what we refer to as point-level objectives . More recently, Shortcut-CSL (Regmi, Ghimire, and Li 2026) extends this formulation by accumulating discrepancies over the entire trajectory, giving rise to a trajectory-level objective , encour-aging each generation step to account for its impact on future steps. However, we find that such a cumulative objective is suboptimal as it does not precisely target distribution-level discrepancies between the base and few-step models. From a flow-matching perspective, both the base and few-step generators can be viewed as transporting a common prior distribution through a sequence of intermediate marginal distributions toward the data distribution. This perspective naturally shifts the focus of distillation to matching the dis-tributional evolution itself. In this work, we propose Cumula-tive Marginal Matching (CMM), a principled objective that encourages alignment between the intermediate marginals induced by the base and few-step models throughout the gen-eration process. Specifically, we formulate the objective as the cumulative Wasserstein-2 discrepancy between the corre-sponding marginals, and show that the pushforward structure of flow matching naturally yields a tractable upper bound that is efficiently optimized using only samples from the in-termediate marginal, which are readily generated through the conditional interpolation process of flow-matching. Fur-thermore, we establish a theoretical connection between the existing Shortcut-CSL objective and CMM, showing that the former can be interpreted as a first-order approximation of the latter. This reveals that, while Shortcut-CSL introduces a long-horizon trajectory-level objective, it is not a precise target for matching the distributional evolution of the models. We make the following three main contributions: 

• We propose Cumulative Marginal Matching (CMM), a principled objective for few-step generation. CMM mea-sures the cumulative discrepancy between the sequence of intermediate marginals induced by the base and few-step models, and we derive a tractable upper bound that enables efficient optimization. 

• We show that the existing Shortcut-CSL objective arises as the first-order approximation of CMM, providing a theoretical explanation of its relationship to the proposed objective. 

• We demonstrate the effectiveness of CMM on standard benchmarks. We improve few-step generation quality over state-of-the-art one- and few-step baselines under identi-cal training budgets. 

# Related Works 

Early approaches on accelerating diffusion-based generation primarily follow a two-stage paradigm, where a high-quality teacher model is first trained and subsequently distilled into a student model capable of generating samples in significantly fewer steps. Representative examples include Progressive Distillation (Salimans and Ho 2022), which recursively re-duces the number of denoising steps through teacher-student distillation, ReFlow (Liu, Gong, and qiang liu 2023), which improves generation efficiency by learning straighter trans-port trajectories, and Distribution Matching Distillation (Yin et al. 2024), which directly distills a pretrained diffusion model into a one-step generator with distribution-matching objective. More recent approaches adopt a single-stage train-ing paradigm that jointly optimizes the base model and few-step models within a unified framework. Examples include Consistency Models (Song et al. 2023; Song and Dhariwal 2024), which enforce consistency across different points in the generation process; Inductive Moment Matching (Zhou, Ermon, and Song 2025), which aligns teacher and student dynamics through moment-matching objectives; MeanFlow (Geng et al. 2025), which distills generation dynamics by matching the average teacher flow; and Shortcut Models (Frans et al. 2025), which train a single network to predict larger-step transitions and enable flexible one- and few-step generation, and flow-map formulation for few-step genera-tion (Boffi, Albergo, and Vanden-Eijnden 2025, 2024), which generalizes MeanFlow and Consistency Models by flow-map formulation. These methods largely rely on objectives that enforce local consistencies, i.e. consistency along a single point in the trajectory, which are independent of one another. Such objectives encourage few-step models to take steps that are only locally aligned with the base model, ignoring how current step affects the subsequent steps along the trajectory. Recent work has proposed training few-step models with a trajectory level objective that encourages few-step models to account for the downstream effects as well. Shortcut-CSL 

(Regmi, Ghimire, and Li 2026) put forward a perspective of viewing few-step models through the lens of optimal con-trol and propose a novel objective that cumulatively penal-izes misalignment between the base and the few-step models along the entire trajectory. This leads to improved one- and few-step generation performance compared to point-level distillation objectives. However, we find that the Shortcut-CSL objective is suboptimal as it does not directly enforce agreement between the distributions induced by the base and few-step models over time. To this end, our work consid-ers the evolution of the intermediate marginal distributions themselves and proposes a cumulative marginal matching ob-jective that encourages distributional alignment throughout the entire generation process.

# Empirical Analysis 

In this section, we present an empirical study of the per-formance, scalability, and efficiency of the proposed CMM, comparing it against baseline few-step generation methods. We train our method on CelebA-256 (Liu et al. 2015) and ImageNet-256 (Deng et al. 2009) benchmarks. In the fol-lowing section, we evaluate the sample quality of both the baselines and our method on these benchmarks. We further examine the scalability of our approach when applied to larger backbone networks. Next, we report the training time of our method and compare it against the baselines. All mod-els are trained in the latent space of a stable VAE (Rombach et al. 2022), and generative performance is measured using the FID-50K metric. The backbone network parameterizing the velocity field vθ is a medium scale diffusion transformer DiT-B-2 (Peebles and Xie 2023) unless stated otherwise. Model training for all experiments are done on Nvidia A100 GPU. Other experimental details and hyperparameters are provided in the Appendix.

For fewer generation steps, such as two-step generators, this constraint may exceed the representational capacity of the model, resulting in opti-mization difficulties and unstable performance. In contrast, four-step models possess a larger Lipschitz budget and are therefore better able to satisfy the CMM constraint, leading to improved performance. This observation motivates our hybrid training strategy. We apply CMM only to models with sufficient capacity (up to four-step generation), where the stronger supervision is beneficial, while training more aggressively distilled mod-els (two-steps) using CSL alone. In this regime, the two-step model is no longer forced to reproduce behaviors that may lie beyond its representational capacity. Since CMM improves the quality of the four-step model, the two-step model ulti-mately learns from a stronger teacher than would be avail-able under CSL-only training. This combination yields better performance than either applying CMM uniformly across all shortcut levels or training all models exclusively with CSL.

# Conclusion 

In this work, we propose a cumulative objective that opti-mizes the discrepancy between the sequence of marginal dis-tributions induced by the base and few-step models. We fur-ther establish theoretical connections between our framework and existing approaches, showing that existing methods can be viewed as approximations of our objective. Experiments on standard generative modeling benchmarks demonstrate that our method consistently improves generation quality, as measured by FID scores. An interesting direction for future work is to empirically investigate the implications of The-orems 0.5 and 0.6, which suggest a fundamental limitation on few-step generation and may explain the performance gap between few-step models and their continuous-time counter-parts. # Experimental Details 

For experiments in Tables 1 & 2 in the main paper, we follow the basic hyperparameter setup from the Shortcut-CSL paper, which are detailed in Table 3 below. Due to computational limitations, we follow the setting of Shortcut-CSL and sample total of ∼40K image samples from 30 classes for class-conditional training on ImageNet-256. B is the number of data points in a batch used for flow-matching loss, K denotes the number of data points used to compute the CMM loss ( LCM M ), and R is the number of terms used to approximate LCM M . For fair comparison with Shortcut and Shortcut-CSL , we keep the values of B and K the same in these baselines and our method. For our method and Shortcut-CSL , we have used a value of R = 2 . For Meanflow baseline, we report its best performance from Shortcut-CSL paper, corresponding to the optimal ratio of flow-matching to bootstrap targets. The evaluation metric is FID-50k, which is essentially the Wasserstein-2 distance between the features of the True and generated images, obtained from the Inception-v2 network. It is a popular metric and widely used to evaluate generative models’ quality. The mean and standard deviation of the FID scores from three independent runs are reported. The number of denoising steps in the base flow-matching model is set to 128. The 3*256*256 images of the CelebA and ImageNet are compressed to the latent space of VAE sd-vae-ft-ema of size 3*256*256 . The flow-matching model is trained in this latent space, and during inference, the generate latent codes are fed to the VAE decoder to obtain images.
