# Rethinking Fixed Temporal Grids: Frequency-Disentangled Motion Generation 

## Yunjiao Zhou, Junlang Qian, Gen Li, Xinying Guo, Lihua Xie, Jianfei Yang 

Nanyang Technological University, Singapore 

Abstract 

Most human motion generation methods encode motion as tokens on a uniform temporal grid, where every token spans the same fixed time window. Human motion, however, is tem-porally heterogeneous: slowly evolving global trajectories co-exist with rapid transient events such as foot contacts and joint impulses. Forcing such multi-scale dynamics onto tokens of identical temporal resolution entangles motion frequencies, leaving slow regions redundant while smoothing out the rapid details that distinguish realistic motion. We propose FreqMo ,a scale-adaptive motion representation that decomposes mo-tion into wavelet frequency bands, separating dynamics across temporal scales while preserving temporal localization and exact reconstruction. Unified Frequency Residual Quantiza-tion (UFRQ) then encodes all bands within a single shared codebook, compressing the token sequence threefold and en-abling stable single-stage generation. Experiments show Fre-qMo attains SOTA fidelity with substantially improved high-frequency preservation, and the same decomposition transfers to continuous diffusion backbones. 

## Introduction 

Realistic human motion generation (Khani et al. 2025; Xiao et al. 2025) underpins virtual reality (Burdea and Coiffet 2003), embodied intelligence (Duan et al. 2022), and digital human synthesis (Varol et al. 2017). Following language and vision (Achiam et al. 2023; Peebles and Xie 2023), prevailing methods encode motion on a fixed temporal grid where ev-ery token spans identical temporal support (Cho et al. 2025; Dai et al. 2024; Lu et al. 2025). This mirrors words in text or patches in images, where semantics reside within individ-ual units and uniform discretization is well justified. Human motion, by contrast, is a continuous, non-stationary process whose semantics arise not from isolated poses but from tem-porally extended interactions: the evolution of a stride, the impulse of a landing, the deceleration into rest. Despite this distinction, few works question whether a fixed grid inher-ited from other modalities suits the dynamic nature of human motion (Figure 1). This oversight has concrete representational conse-quences. The inter-frame dynamics that define motion se-mantics are temporally heterogeneous, spanning orders of magnitude from slowly evolving global trajectories to near-instantaneous events such as foot contacts and joint impulses. A common practice augments each token with temporal

Right: FreqMo resolves it by adapting window sizes to intrinsic frequency scales. derivatives (velocity, acceleration) (Guo et al. 2022a), yet this captures higher-order statistics of the same entangled mixture rather than disentangling its frequency components. Since dynamics at distinct temporal scales correspond to dis-tinct spectral frequencies, projecting them onto identically supported tokens forces these components to mix insepara-bly, leading to frequency entanglement (Figure 7) (Yuan et al. 2024; Fu 2024). Tokens spanning slow dynamics carry redundant information, while those spanning rapid transients smooth out the details they should preserve. This distor-tion originates at the representation level: a structural con-sequence of single-resolution indexing that no per-token en-richment can resolve, not a capacity deficit. Disentangling these frequencies is not as simple as trans-forming motion into the frequency domain. First, decompos-ing motion by frequency alone, as a global spectral transform does (Fourier 1822), records which frequencies occur but not when, untenable for motion, whose semantics are defined by precisely when events happen. A natural remedy reintroduces time through local windows (Allen and Rabiner 1977), but at a fixed scale: one window resolves all frequencies alike, too coarse for fast transients and too fine for slow trends. What motion needs is thus resolution that adapts to frequency, to-gether with temporal localization. On this basis, we adopt the Wavelet Transform (Mallat 1999), whose variable-resolution filtering gives broad context to low-frequency trajectories and fine precision to high-frequency transients while preserving arXiv:2610.03012v1 [cs.CV] 2 Oct 2026 temporal resolution. This makes frequency disentanglement a property of the representation rather than the architecture. However, directly quantizing multi-scale wavelet features inevitably introduces quantization error. A residual scheme mitigates this, encoding each band against the reconstruction of the earlier ones. The order of this refinement, however, is decisive: the wavelet hierarchy runs from high-frequency detail to coarse global structure, so refining in that order would fix transient details before the low-frequency structure that anchors the motion. We instead encode coarse to fine, locking the global structure first so that residual error is confined to the high-frequency detail where it least matters. Building upon this, we present FreqMo , a scale-adaptive motion generation framework. FreqMo decomposes mo-tion latents into hierarchical frequency bands via multi-level wavelet transform, aligning the representation with the in-trinsic temporal scale of each dynamic component. To in-stantiate this in discrete generative models, we propose Uni-fied Frequency Residual Quantization (UFRQ), which en-codes all frequency bands into a single shared codebook through coarse-to-fine residual refinement, replacing frag-mented multi-codebook pipelines and cutting the token se-quence to one third of comparable designs. A scale-aware masked transformer (Chang et al. 2022) then generates all bands simultaneously for stable single-stage generation. The decomposition is moreover agnostic to the generator: trans-planting it into a continuous diffusion pipeline improves that model as well, showing the gain lies in the representation rather than any particular quantization scheme. The main contributions are summarized as follows: 

• We identify single-scale temporal rigidity as a structural limitation in existing motion representations and charac-terize the resulting frequency entanglement. 

• We propose Unified Frequency Residual Quantization, which encodes wavelet-disentangled bands into a single shared codebook while preserving temporal localization and exact reconstruction, replacing multi-stage residual pipelines with stable single-stage generation. 

• We show that the same representation improves gen-eration quality under both discrete and continuous paradigms, confirming frequency disentanglement acts at the representation level and transfers across architectures. 

## Related Work 

Motion Latent Representation 

Learning an effective motion representation is a foundational challenge (Liu et al. 2022; Siarohin et al. 2021; Zhu et al. 2023). One major paradigm utilizes continuous latent spaces, typically via Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) (Kingma and Welling 2013; Chen et al. 2023; Guo et al. 2022a), with re-cent methods operating directly on deterministic or causal latents (Meng et al. 2025; Xiao et al. 2025; Yu, Watanabe, and Fujiwara 2026). However, the high precision of the con-tinuous space can be detrimental, forcing the model to expend capacity encoding irrelevant, high-frequency noise and mi-nor signal variations. This hinders generalization and compli-cates the subsequent generative task. An alternative paradigm learns a discrete representation using Vector Quantization (VQ-VAE) (Van Den Oord, Vinyals, and Kavukcuoglu 2017). By mapping poses to a finite codebook, VQ acts as a power-ful regularizer, forcing the model to discard noise and learn a robust, semantic pose vocabulary (Zhang et al. 2023a; Jiang et al. 2023; Lu et al. 2025). Given this robustness, our work builds upon the discrete quantization paradigm. 

Motion Generative Models 

Given a latent representation, a generative model is required. Diffusion Models (Ho, Jain, and Abbeel 2020; Meng et al. 2025; Weng et al. 2026) achieve strong quality by iteratively denoising a sequence, but suffer from computational over-head and slow inference. Autoregressive models (Vaswani et al. 2017; Lu et al. 2025; Yu, Watanabe, and Fujiwara 2026; Bian et al. 2025) generate tokens sequentially; while strong at capturing temporal dependencies, they are also slow and can accumulate errors. Multimodal and whole-body vari-ants further extend this paradigm (Xu et al. 2026; Zhang et al. 2026). To balance these trade-offs, non-autoregressive masked transformers (Chang et al. 2022; Pinyoanuntapong et al. 2024; Zeng et al. 2025), inspired by BERT (Devlin et al. 2019), have emerged as a powerful alternative. By predicting masked tokens in parallel, these models enable fast inference while maintaining high quality via iterative refinement. Therefore, we build our generator upon the bidi-rectional masked transformer framework. 

## Method 

Given a text description c, our goal is to generate a motion se-quence X = {xt}Tt=1 , where each xt ∈ RJ denotes the pose at frame t. Conventional representations discretize motion on a uniform temporal grid, forcing dynamics at different temporal scales to share identical support and entangling them before generation begins. To resolve this, we introduce 

FreqMo , separating motion across temporal scales through two components: a frequency-disentangled representation that decomposes latent motion into multi-scale bands via wavelet transform and encodes them through UFRQ; and a text-conditioned masked transformer that generates all fre-quency tokens jointly while modeling their cross-scale de-pendencies. The wavelet decomposition is the core of Fre-qMo, and because it is independent of the generator, it applies to both continuous and discrete latent spaces. 

Frequency-Disentangled Motion Representation 

A motion representation compresses raw motion into a com-pact, structured form that facilitates generation. Given a mo-tion sequence X ∈ RT ×J , a motion encoder EM (·) projects it into continuous latent embeddings h ∈ RT ′×d, where T ′

is the downsampled temporal length and d the embedding dimension. However, since EM operates on a uniform tem-poral grid, h conflates slowly evolving global trajectories and rapid local transients within the same fixed-resolution space. To resolve this, we decompose h into a hierarchy of frequency-disentangled bands prior to any sequence model-ing or quantization, ensuring that multi-scale dynamics are structurally separated at the representation level. This hierarchy is the frequency-disentangled representation: each band captures dynamics at a specific temporal scale, and exact invertibility ensures no kinematic information is discarded during decomposition. 

Unified Frequency Residual Quantization 

Quantizing each band {AL, Dl} independently would frag-ment the latent space across scales and let quantization er-rors at coarse levels irreversibly corrupt finer details. UFRQ avoids both by encoding bands from coarse to fine within a single shared codebook, progressively capturing reconstruc-tion residuals. Starting from the coarsest band AL (Algo-rithm 1), we iteratively quantize Al, reconstruct a partial sig-nal via IDWT (with high-frequency components zeroed), and compute the residual against the current signal. This residual is then decomposed to the next finer approximation Al−1 and quantized in the same way. All frequency bands share a sin-gle codebook C, as wavelet decomposition naturally aligns coefficient magnitudes across levels. During decoding (Al-gorithm 2), quantized approximations are reconstructed in reverse order and accumulated, with the final residual added to recover the full signal. This coarse-to-fine encoding or-der ensures low-frequency structure is captured with high fidelity first, while quantization errors are relegated to less critical high-frequency bands through progressive residual refinement.

Inference 

At inference time (Figure 3), motion is generated from text in two stages. Given c, the model first generates 

z = [ zL, . . . , z 0] via iterative refinement: starting from all 

[MASK] tokens, it performs M steps, at each step predict-ing confidence scores, unmasking the top km positions, and re-predicting remaining tokens. The completed sequence is decoded into ˆh via Algorithm 2 and passed to the motion decoder DM to produce the final motion ˆX.

## Conclusion 

This work introduces FreqMo, which addresses an over-looked limitation in motion generation: frequency entan-glement from uniform temporal tokenization. Decompos-ing motion into hierarchical frequency bands and encoding them in a unified codebook via coarse-to-fine residual quan-tization, FreqMo achieves SOTA fidelity with faithful high-frequency preservation. Its consistent gains across discrete and continuous paradigms confirm the improvement stems from the multi-scale representation, pointing to frequency-disentangled modeling as a direction beyond human motion.
