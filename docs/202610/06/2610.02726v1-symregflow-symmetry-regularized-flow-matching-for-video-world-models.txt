ABSTRACT
Flow-matching-based multi-view world models generate realistic videos, but are
commonly restricted to fixed camera rigs. Extending them to continuously vary-
ing camera poses requires paired pose–video observations with dense pose cov-
erage, which are costly to acquire.
We introduce SymRegFlow, a symmetry-
regularized flow-matching framework for multi-view-consistent video genera-
tion across continuous viewpoints without ground-truth novel-view RGB super-
vision. For each target pose, SymRegFlow geometrically warps source views into
noisy anchors and combines masked dual-anchor supervision with cross-anchor
denoising-output consistency to mitigate anchor-specific errors. Under an affine
Gaussian surrogate, we prove that suitable consistency regularization recovers the
clean-reference optimum at fixed noise levels, strictly outperforming single- and
merged-anchor baselines. Experiments on Cosmos-Drive-Dreams and nuScenes
demonstrate high-quality, multi-view-consistent autonomous-driving video gener-
ation: on nuScenes, SymRegFlow achieves the lowest FVD and FVMD among the
evaluated baselines, reducing FVD by over 31% relative to the best baseline, and
source-conditioned inference also attains the best FID and instance preservation.

INTRODUCTION
Video world models have become powerful generative simulators of dynamic environments. In
autonomous driving, recent systems jointly synthesize the RGB streams of a surround-view camera
rig, conditioned on maps, object layouts, and text (Gao et al., 2024a;b; Agarwal et al., 2025). Joint
multi-view learning can implicitly capture spatial consistency across views. We refer to the desired
property as video SE(3) symmetry: under a rigid camera-pose change g ∈SE(3), the scene content
and dynamics remain unchanged, while the generated RGB video transforms consistently with the
induced reprojection on co-visible regions (Dupont et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2022). Because SE(3) is
continuous, this consistency should extend across camera poses; current models, however, support
only the finite viewpoints represented by their training rigs.
Continuous-view generation supports sensor simulation, closed-loop autonomy evaluation (Yang
et al., 2023), training-data augmentation (Tonderski et al., 2024), and cinematographic camera con-
trol (He et al., 2025; Bai et al., 2025a). However, nuScenes and Waymo Open (Caesar et al., 2020;
Sun et al., 2020) provide synchronized videos only from fixed calibrated cameras, leaving interme-
diate viewpoints and novel camera heights or baselines without paired targets. ReCamMaster and
SynCamMaster obtain synchronized multi-camera training videos from Unreal Engine (Bai et al.,
2025a;b), incurring high generation costs, a sim-to-real gap, and unmodeled simulator artifacts.
To avoid simulator-generated supervision, we use off-the-shelf 3D perception models to warp ob-
served source views to sampled novel poses, producing noisy anchor videos affected by geometry er-
rors, occlusions, and resampling. We introduce SymRegFlow, which combines masked dual-anchor
supervision with cross-anchor denoising consistency under shared diffusion noise, while retaining

observed-view supervision to preserve pretrained generative capabilities. This symmetry inductive
bias reduces sensitivity to anchor-specific errors without requiring ground-truth novel-view videos.
SymRegFlow is a fine-tuning objective that keeps the flow-matching training and inference frame-
work.
Our implementation adds continuous-view conditioning, directed cross-view attention
(DCVA), and source-latent replacement for source-conditioned inference. Our contributions are:
• We propose SymRegFlow for cross-view-consistent video generation across continuous view-
points without novel-view RGB supervision, using masked dual-anchor learning and cross-
anchor denoising consistency.
• We prove that, under an affine Gaussian surrogate, suitable consistency regularization counter-
acts anchor-induced variance inflation and recovers the clean-reference optimum at each fixed
noise level, strictly improving upon single- and merged-anchor training.
• We show that on the nuScenes midpoint-view benchmark, SymRegFlow achieves the lowest
FVD and FVMD among the evaluated baselines (over 31% lower FVD than the best baseline),
while source-conditioned inference also attains the best FID and instance preservation.

2.1
PRELIMINARIES
Symmetry and equivariance.
A symmetry is a group transformation that leaves an object or dis-
tribution unchanged. Let X and Y denote the input and output spaces, and let ρX (g) and ρY(g)
denote how a group element g acts on each space. A map F : X →Y is equivariant if it commutes
with these actions: F(ρX (g)x) = ρY(g)F(x). Invariance is the special case in which ρY(g) is the
identity (Bronstein et al., 2021). Thus, symmetry specifies the structure to preserve, while equivari-
ance specifies how a model preserves it. For the video SE(3) symmetry defined in the introduction,
ρX (g) changes the requested camera pose and ρY(g) is the induced RGB reprojection. The scene
content and dynamics remain invariant, but its observation changes; the generator should therefore
be camera-pose equivariant on co-visible regions (Dupont et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2022).

Directed Cross-View Attention.
To protect observed-view fidelity and multi-view consistency
from noisy anchors, Directed Cross-View Attention (DCVA) restricts observed-view queries to
observed-view tokens while allowing novel-view queries to attend to all tokens. Parameter-free
masks enforce this observed-to-novel information flow in every full-attention layer. Sec. 3.3 evalu-
ates its trade-off between view fidelity, cross-view agreement, and computational cost.
Appendix A gives the two-pass, three-view training procedure in Algorithm 1, including the video
backbone’s prediction parameterization. Algorithms 2 and 3 describe source-cond. inference (novel-
view synthesis conditioned on observed source RGB) and joint-generated inference (joint three-view
generation from noise), respectively.

SE(2) TOY EXAMPLE
We illustrate SymRegFlow in an SE(2) MNIST setting with observed views x1 and x2 at −90◦
and 0◦, respectively. A pose α ∈(0, 1) specifies the novel-view angle ϑα = −90◦(1 −α); the

A1 and A2 increases with their respective source–target angular distances, simulating viewpoint-

the observed views and fine-tune it with these anchors under Eq. (6). We compare against single-
anchor baselines (A1-only and A2-only) and Merged anchor, which uses their mask-weighted fusion
AM. Appendix D details anchor construction and training.

EXTENSION TO LATENT FLOW MATCHING
The preceding formulation uses RGB anchors, whereas latent flow-matching models operate in au-
toencoder latent space. Let E and D be the frozen encoder and decoder, with Xs = E(xs). We
first introduce the induced latent-warp operator Ws→n := E ◦Πs→n ◦D. In practice, assuming
near-perfect autoencoder reconstruction, we directly warp the source RGB before encoding:
As := E(Πs→n(xs)) ≈Ws→n(Xs).

Here, Πs→n is the pixel-space warping operation defined above, and As is the practical latent anchor
used for fine-tuning. Downsampling the corresponding visibility mask to latent resolution yields
M s, and the latent anchor satisfies
M sAs = M s(Xn + Es),

where Es denotes the latent-space warping error. During fine-tuning, these latent anchors replace

video implementation retains the backbone’s supervised velocity losses, equivalent to t−2-weighted
clean-output losses, while evaluating consistency on clean predictions (Algorithm 1).

EXPERIMENTS
Our video models are adapted from the Cosmos-Transfer2.5 multi-view flow-matching backbone
(Ali et al., 2025), which we fine-tune on Cosmos-Drive-Dreams (Ren et al., 2025a) and nuScenes
(Caesar et al., 2020) using 29-frame clips at 1280 × 720 resolution per view, following prior work
(Guo et al., 2025). We report quantitative evaluation on nuScenes. Following the depth-based
warping of GEN3C (Ren et al., 2025b), we unproject source frames into 3D using estimated depth
and render them at target camera poses to obtain partial anchors and visibility masks.

Effectiveness of dual-anchor fine-tuning.
Dual-anchor fine-tuning improves novel-view gener-
ation even without consistency regularization: C0 outperforms all three anchor baselines in novel-
view FVD and both VBench consistency scores. For example, C0 lowers novel-view FVD from
562.23 for the strongest single-anchor baseline AL to 545.14, while C0.05 lowers it from 704.32 for
merged-anchor AM to 498.94. Although AM uses the same two anchors as SymRegFlow for train-
ing, it inherits artifacts from mismatched RGB merging, including misaligned overlaps and residual

RELATED WORK
4.1
SYMMETRY AND EQUIVARIANCE IN WORLD MODELS
Generative models commonly preserve symmetric target distributions through equivariant score or
velocity fields (K¨ohler et al., 2020). Equivariant diffusion uses an E(3)-equivariant denoiser, while
equivariant flow-matching methods constrain continuous normalizing-flow vector fields or probabil-
ity paths (Hoogeboom et al., 2022; Klein et al., 2023; Song et al., 2023). Flow Equivariant World
Models instead build motion-aligned equivariance into a structured latent memory (Lillemark et al.,
2026). More generally, these methods require symmetry-specific denoisers, vector fields, or latent-
memory architectures. Architecture-agnostic alternatives avoid such redesign but rely, respectively,
on group-averaged training targets or symmetry-structured noise during training and sampling (Tong
et al., 2025; Liu & Vahdat, 2025). SymRegFlow introduces a symmetry inductive bias through its
fine-tuning objective, aligning denoising outputs induced by independently warped, noisy anchors
over the union of valid anchor supports while retaining the underlying flow-matching formulation.
4.2
CAMERA-CONTROLLED VIDEO WORLD MODELS
Training-free camera control.
Training-free methods keep a pretrained video generator frozen
and enforce a target trajectory at inference through latent replacement, sampling-time guidance, or
test-time optimization (Hou & Chen, 2025; Park et al., 2026; Zhou et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026).
They avoid paired training data but typically require modified sampling or trajectory-specific refine-
ment. SymRegFlow instead learns camera control by fine-tuning and retains the standard sampler
for arbitrary target poses; source-cond. inference additionally uses source-latent replacement.
Training-based camera control.
MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl, 4DiM, and ReCamMaster amortize
camera-controlled generation using costly pose-annotated or synchronized pose–video pairs, gener-
ally producing one target stream (Wang et al., 2024b; He et al., 2025; Watson et al., 2025; Bai et al.,
2025a). ReCapture (Zhang et al., 2025) fits video-specific LoRAs to valid pixels of a noisy warped
anchor for each requested trajectory, masking missing regions and relying on the pretrained prior to
repair artifacts. It neither couples source-camera anchors nor enforces cross-view symmetry. Sym-
RegFlow instead uses dataset-level fine-tuning and agreement among independently warped anchors
to reduce view-specific errors.
Joint-view world models.
Joint-view generators couple synchronized spatio-temporal camera to-
kens through self- or cross-view attention, conditioned on rig identities, maps, boxes, text, or ac-
tions.
Examples include MagicDrive, Drive-WM, and DriveDreamer-2 for driving (Gao et al.,
2024a; Wang et al., 2024a; Zhao et al., 2025), and Cosmos-Predict/Transfer2.5 for driving and multi-
camera robotics (Ali et al., 2025). Their cross-camera consistency remains tied to training-rig slots
and poses. SymRegFlow introduces a continuously pose-conditioned target slot while preserving
the pretrained multi-view prior, without novel-view RGB supervision.
5
CONCLUSION AND DISCUSSION
SymRegFlow extends pretrained multi-view world models to cross-view-consistent video generation
across continuous viewpoints without novel-view RGB supervision, combining masked dual-anchor
9


supervision with cross-anchor denoising consistency to mitigate source-dependent biases. Under an
affine Gaussian surrogate, suitable regularization recovers the clean-reference optimum at each noise
level. On nuScenes, SymRegFlow achieves the lowest FVD and FVMD among evaluated baselines,
with source-conditioned inference also attaining the best FID and instance preservation; ablations
confirm improved novel-view quality and reduced source-dependent attention bias.
Limitations.
SymRegFlow depends on anchor quality: depth, projection, and visibility errors can
corrupt supervision, especially near occlusions or under large viewpoint changes. Dual-anchor con-
sistency cannot correct shared systematic errors, and jointly unseen regions lack direct supervision.
Better anchors and geometric-uncertainty modeling remain open directions.

Interpretation and scope.
SymRegFlow combines supervision from both noisy anchors with
shared-noise consistency. Supervision alone fits the inflated anchor variance q + ν; consistency
penalizes sensitivity to anchor disagreement and, at the calibrated strength, recovers the clean-
reference optimum. Stronger regularization further shrinks the denoiser’s slope below the clean
optimum, increasing excess risk. Zero excess risk means attaining the minimum total risk q/D(t),
not zero reconstruction error. The comparison concerns clean-reference evaluation, not the numeri-
cal values of different training objectives. It holds for the stated affine, independent-error surrogate
with unit masks and fixed source predictions; it does not assert the same guarantee for the full neural
network or one constant λc across all diffusion times.

IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS AND ADDITIONAL ABLATION RESULTS
We first describe the shared implementation and study-specific evaluation protocols for the main
video experiments and ablations, then present additional quantitative and attention-bias analyses.
C.1
IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS
Backbone and adaptation.
All video experiments use Cosmos-Transfer2.5 (Ali et al., 2025) with
29-frame clips at 1280×720 resolution per view. Fine-tuning uses rank-96 LoRA with LoRA alpha
12, gray fill for invalid warp regions, and the continuous-view conditioning described below. Dual-
anchor variants regularize clean predictions; the default SymRegFlow configuration uses DCVA and
λc = 0.05. For the quantitative nuScenes experiments and ablations, training uses 16 GPUs and a
global batch size of 32. LoRA and view-embedding learning rates are 5 × 10−4 and 5 × 10−3,
respectively, with cosine decay, 25 warm-up steps, and weight decay 0.01. Reported quantitative
checkpoints are evaluated at step 2,048.
Novel-view pose conditioning.
Cosmos-Transfer2.5 (Ali et al., 2025) conditions each view on
a world-scenario video composed of projected lane/map polylines and 3D object bounding boxes,
rather than captured RGB footage, to specify scene layout and viewpoint. During training, we render
this representation at the novel camera poses and feed its encoded latents into SymRegFlow through
the backbone’s control branch. Both Cosmos-Drive-Dreams (Ren et al., 2025a) and nuScenes (Cae-
sar et al., 2020) provide the map annotations, object geometry, and camera calibration required for
this rendering.
To minimize modifications to the pretrained backbone, we retain its integer-indexed embeddings
for existing rig cameras. Our autonomous-driving data synthesis setting requires novel views in-
terpolated along the six-camera ring. We therefore parameterize the continuous view embedder by
the endpoint camera identities and a scalar ring coordinate, with additional geometric condition-
ing supplied by the world-scenario renderings. The pretrained camera embeddings remain frozen;
Fourier features of the global ring coordinate drive a zero-initialized, endpoint-vanishing MLP resid-
ual added to the linearly interpolated embeddings (α = 0.5 at the midpoint; Sec. C.5).
This design specializes the general pose-conditioned formulation to the interpolation setting evalu-
ated here. The formulation is compatible with arbitrary target-pose inputs: eϕ can instead encode
camera extrinsic and intrinsic matrices, Pl¨ucker rays, or other pose representations and be trained
jointly during fine-tuning.
Sampling.
The nuScenes results in Tables 1 and 2 use 35 denoising steps, guidance scale 3, and
seed 42. Table 1 reports both joint-generated inference, without observed-RGB source-slot re-
placement, and source-cond. inference, with replacement during the first 30 steps. Table 2 uses
joint-generated inference only.
Distributional metrics.
Novel-view FID (Heusel et al., 2017) and FVD (Unterthiner et al., 2018)
compare generated clips with unique captured source-view clips as unpaired distributions; captured
midpoint RGB is unavailable, and world-scenario renderings are never used as RGB ground truth.
FID uses 2,048-dimensional Inception features, and FVD uses 400-dimensional I3D features from
29-frame clips, pooling all scenes in each study.
Cross-view consistency.
MEt3R (Asim et al., 2025) projects FeatUp-upsampled DINO features
through MASt3R geometry and averages cosine distances over usable overlap and valid synchro-
nized frame pairs. Images preserve aspect ratio with a maximum long edge of 512 pixels. Valid
pairs require at least 5% raw overlap and 1% usable overlap after excluding relative depth disagree-
ments above 0.10. The reference views and temporal inputs differ between studies, as specified
below.

Anchor corruption.
The angular distances from x1 and x2 to the target at ϑα = −90◦(1 −α) are
90◦α and 90◦(1−α), respectively. Normalizing by 90◦gives d1(α) = α and d2(α) = 1−α. Let Eb
denote the base distortion level. For s ∈S = {1, 2}, we set the scale of Es to Eb+ds(α), confidence
to ws = 1 −ds(α), and loss mask to ms = ws ¯ms, where ¯ms is the binary sampling-validity
mask. The warp perturbs morphology, anisotropic scale and shear, angle, translation, a smooth
deformation grid, and pixels with additive noise. Their magnitudes scale with Eb + ds(α), using
respective coefficients 3◦, 0.035, 0.055, and 0.040 for the last four perturbations. This construction is
deliberately heterogeneous: the two anchors contain errors that are neither identical nor independent
in every pixel, while their expected severity increases with source–target pose distance.
Evaluation protocol.
We evaluate the nine interior poses α ∈{0.1, . . . , 0.9} at base distortion
levels Eb ∈{0.2, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.8}. Each pose–distortion cell contains 256 samples generated with
a 50-step Heun solver. The same base Gaussian samples (seed 2027) are reused across methods and
poses. PSNR and SSIM compare source-cond. predictions with exact rotations of held-out MNIST
validation images. Pixel-space Fr´echet distance (FD) compares joint-generated target-slot samples
with the corresponding validation distribution using flattened, bilinearly downsampled 16×16 pixel
features; no Inception features are used. Each method–distortion setting currently has one fine-
tuning seed; variation across base distortion levels therefore measures robustness to anchor quality
rather than optimization uncertainty.
