ABSTRACT
This work studies prediction parameterization for stochastic generative dynam-
ics in diffusion models. Existing velocity-based generative models provide the
simplicity of learning a single transport field, but their standard formulation is
deterministic, whereas stochastic extensions generally require additional score in-
formation or an intermediate velocity-to-score reconstruction. To retain single-
field prediction while directly supporting stochastic reverse dynamics, this paper
introduces Mean Velocity Matching (MVM). MVM constructs a Gaussian per-
turbation process for which the conditional expectation of a restoration-oriented
velocity, (x0 −xt)/t, directly forms the reverse-SDE drift. Consequently, a sin-
gle learned field is sufficient to parameterize the stochastic reverse process without
separately estimating or reconstructing the score. Because direct regression of this
velocity becomes unbounded near t = 0, MVM further introduces a
√
t-scaled
parameterization that preserves the reverse dynamics while yielding a bounded
training target. The same learned field also induces a deterministic probability-
flow ODE, enabling stochastic and deterministic sampling to be studied within
a unified formulation. Experiments with Transformer-based generative models
achieve an FID of 1.93 at 65 NFE on ImageNet 32 × 32 and 2.07 at 90 NFE on
ImageNet 256 × 256. Controlled SDE–ODE comparisons further show that the
ODE performs better under very low NFE, whereas the stochastic reverse pro-
cess achieves lower FID when sufficient function evaluations are available. These
results demonstrate that MVM provides a direct single-field parameterization of
stochastic reverse dynamics while maintaining competitive generation quality.

INTRODUCTION
Diffusion models have achieved strong performance in image synthesis (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021;
Ho et al., 2020), video generation (Ho et al., 2022), three-dimensional content generation (Poole
et al., 2023), and large-scale text-to-image synthesis (Rombach et al., 2022; Saharia et al., 2022;
Nichol et al., 2022). They combine a gradual perturbation process with a learned reverse process
that transports tractable noise back to the data distribution, and this view extends naturally from
discrete-time chains to continuous-time stochastic differential equations (SDEs) (Song et al., 2021b).
Transformer backbones such as DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023) further show that diffusion models scale
effectively with model capacity and computation.
A central design choice in diffusion models is the variable predicted by the neural network. DDPM
(Ho et al., 2020) adopts perturbation-noise prediction and reported that direct x0-prediction pro-
duced worse sample quality in its early experiments. Later work showed, however, that the pre-
ferred parameterization can depend strongly on the sampling regime. In progressive distillation, for
example, the implied x0 estimate from ϵ-prediction becomes ill-conditioned as the signal-to-noise
ratio approaches zero, motivating direct data prediction and diffusion v-prediction (Salimans & Ho,

with, or reconstructing it through, score information.

RELATED WORK
2.1
NOISE PREDICTION PARADIGMS
Noise prediction is a standard diffusion parameterization and is closely related to score matching
(Hyv¨arinen, 2005), which estimates log-density gradients without evaluating normalized densities.
Score-based generative models extend this idea to progressively perturbed distributions (Song &
Ermon, 2019; 2020).
DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) uses a simple and effective noise parameterization. For a Gaussian pertur-
bation process
xt = αtx0 + σtϵ,
ϵ ∼N(0, I),
(8)
the network predicts ϵ, which is analytically related to the score and therefore provides a convenient
representation of reverse denoising dynamics.
Continuous-time formulations generalize diffusion chains to SDEs (Song et al., 2021b) and associate
the reverse SDE with a probability-flow ODE sharing the same time-dependent marginals.
Subsequent work improves training and sampling while retaining noise/score targets. Improved
DDPM (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021) and Variational Diffusion Models (Kingma et al., 2021) refine
objectives and variances, while Karras et al. (Karras et al., 2022) highlight the importance of pre-
conditioning, noise-level sampling, loss weighting, and numerical solvers.
For sampling, DDIM (Song et al., 2021a) introduces deterministic implicit generation and DPM-
Solver (Lu et al., 2022) reduces network evaluations with high-order solvers. Classifier-free guid-
ance (Ho & Salimans, 2021) controls the fidelity–diversity trade-off and is widely used in large-scale
image-generation systems (Rombach et al., 2022; Saharia et al., 2022; Nichol et al., 2022).
Because equivalent parameterizations can induce different loss weightings and conditioning, clean-
data and mixed signal–noise targets remain important alternatives.

2.2
DATA PREDICTION PARADIGMS
Another class of methods predicts the clean sample. Although x0 and ϵ are algebraically related
under Gaussian perturbations, their regression errors are weighted differently across noise levels.
Clean-data prediction is natural for generalized corruption. Cold Diffusion (Bansal et al., 2023) uses
deterministic degradations such as blur, masking, and pixelation, while Soft Diffusion (Daras et al.,
2023) extends to linear corruption operators and predicts a clean image whose corrupted version
matches the observation.
Related ideas appear in discrete diffusion: Structured Denoising Diffusion Models (Austin et al.,
2021) predict clean discrete variables, and Vector Quantized Diffusion (Gu et al., 2022) operates in
a discrete latent space for text-to-image generation.
Consistency Models (Song et al., 2023) likewise map states on the same probability-flow trajectory
toward a common endpoint, enabling generation with few network evaluations. The relative effec-
tiveness of data and noise prediction depends on the perturbation process and training weighting;
neither target is universally superior, and each induces a different optimization geometry.

v-prediction for progressive distillation. Under a variance-preserving parameterization

A conceptually different family models probability transport through neural vector fields. Continu-
ous normalizing flows and neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018; Grathwohl et al., 2019) use deterministic
dynamics, while Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023) learns marginal vector fields along prescribed
conditional probability paths.
Rectified Flow (Liu et al., 2023) progressively straightens transport paths to reduce discretization
error and permit coarser integration. Optimal-transport extensions further improve path geome-
try (Tong et al., 2023; Pooladian et al., 2023), and rectified-flow Transformers scale well to high-
resolution image synthesis (Esser et al., 2024).
Deterministic flows and stochastic diffusions can describe the same density evolution. Score-based
SDEs (Song et al., 2021b) connect reverse-time SDEs with probability-flow ODEs, while Stochas-
tic Interpolants (Albergo et al., 2025) relate transport equations, forward/backward Fokker–Planck
equations, velocity fields, scores, and stochastic drifts in one framework.

EXPERIMENTS
The experiments are conducted on class-conditional ImageNet at resolutions of 32×32 and 256×256
(Deng et al., 2009). ImageNet-32 is used to evaluate the basic generation performance of MVM,
while ImageNet-256 examines its ability to generate higher-resolution images. For ImageNet-32, the
reverse process is parameterized by a pixel-space DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023), with REPA introduced
to accelerate training (Yu et al., 2025). For ImageNet-256, an RAE with a frozen DINOv3 encoder
reduces the spatial resolution before a DDT-style Transformer performs latent generation (Zheng
et al., 2025; Sim´eoni et al., 2025); REPA is also employed. SA-Solver is used for sampling (Xue
et al., 2023). Classifier-free guidance is used in the ImageNet-32 SDE–ODE comparison, while
REPA internal guidance is employed for the reported ImageNet-256 result. The first experiment
evaluates generation quality using FID (Heusel et al., 2017). The second compares the FID–NFE
curves of the reverse SDE and its ODE counterpart on ImageNet-32 (Song et al., 2021b).
4.1
MAIN GENERATION RESULTS
This paper first evaluates the generation quality of Mean Velocity Matching (MVM) on ImageNet
at two representative resolutions, namely 32 × 32 and 256 × 256. Following common practice
in generative modeling, Fr´echet Inception Distance (FID) is adopted as the primary metric, where
a lower value indicates that the generated distribution is closer to the real data distribution. The
number of function evaluations (NFE) is additionally reported to characterize the computational
cost of sampling. All FID values of MVM are computed from 50,000 generated samples.
The compared methods cover representative diffusion-, score-, flow-, GAN-, latent-diffusion-, and
Transformer-based generative models. The results are summarized in Tables 1 and 2.
On ImageNet 32 × 32, MVM achieves an FID of 1.93 with 65 function evaluations using reverse-
SDE sampling. As shown in Table 1, the proposed method substantially improves upon the reported
diffusion-, score-, and flow-based baselines while requiring fewer function evaluations.
The present study further evaluates MVM on the more challenging class-conditional ImageNet
256 × 256 benchmark. As shown in Table 2, MVM obtains an FID of 2.07 using 90 function
evaluations. This result demonstrates that the proposed mean-velocity formulation remains effective
when scaling from low-resolution generation to higher-resolution class-conditional image synthesis.

1.935, respectively, whereas the ODE obtains 2.088, 2.025, and 2.034. As shown in Figure 2, the
ODE is preferable under a strict low-NFE budget, while the stochastic reverse process yields lower
FID from NFE = 39 onward. The best observed SDE result is 1.9349 at NFE = 65, whereas the
best ODE result is 2.0253 at NFE = 52. Therefore, sufficient numerical resolution is particularly
important for the stochastic reverse dynamics, which ultimately attain better generation quality than
the corresponding deterministic flow.
5
CONCLUSION
This paper introduces Mean Velocity Matching (MVM), a generative framework that constructs a
tractable diffusion path through Fourier transform and learns the reverse dynamics using a single
mean-velocity field. The resulting formulation supports both stochastic reverse-SDE and determin-
istic probability-flow ODE sampling while preserving the same forward process. Experiments on
ImageNet 32 × 32 and 256 × 256 demonstrate strong generation quality, achieving FID values of
1.93 and 2.07, respectively. The SDE–ODE comparison further shows that the ODE is advantageous
at very low NFE, whereas the SDE attains lower FID when more function evaluations are available.
These results establish MVM as an effective and scalable framework for image generation.
