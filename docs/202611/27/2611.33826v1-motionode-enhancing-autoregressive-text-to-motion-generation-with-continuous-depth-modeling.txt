Abstract
Autoregressive text-to-motion generation requires local mo-
tion predictions to reconcile cross-temporal textual require-
ments with the available motion context.
While existing
methods primarily improve motion representations and gen-
eration order, the internal formation of conditioned motion
representations remains less explored. We introduce Mo-
tionODE, a continuous-depth post-training framework that
refines hidden-state evolution in pretrained autoregressive
generators. MotionODE reinterprets selected Transformer
residual branches as local vector fields and replaces their
original updates with midpoint Runge–Kutta (RK2) integra-
tion, while preserving the motion representation and outer
generation mechanism. To address the mismatch between
pretrained update paths and newly introduced intermedi-
ate states, we propose mixed one-step/two-step RK2 post-
training under motion-token supervision, with optional dis-
tillation and update-map constraints to preserve pretrained
behavior. This minimally invasive framework supports both
next-token and next-scale generation without training the
generator from scratch.
Extensive experiments on Hu-
manML3D demonstrate consistent improvements in text–
motion alignment across MoScale, BAMM and LMR, high-
lighting internal representation updates as a complemen-
tary direction for improving autoregressive motion genera-
tion.

1. Introduction
Text-to-motion generation aims to synthesize natural, se-
mantically consistent human motion from language descrip-
tions, with applications in character animation [25], hu-
man–computer interaction [21], and embodied intelligence
[30]. It offers an intuitive way to control digital characters
while reducing motion-authoring costs. The task requires
translating action semantics and event ordering into coor-
dinated movements, demanding both textual alignment and
temporal coherence [14].
Recent advances have established autoregressive gener-
ation over discrete motion representations as an effective
approach to text-to-motion synthesis [9, 34, 38].
These
methods encode continuous human motion into discrete to-
kens, predict motion tokens or token groups conditioned on
text, and reconstruct the resulting sequence using a motion
decoder. Existing approaches explore different generation
mechanisms, including next-token [11], grouped-token [6],
and next-scale prediction [36], while improving motion rep-
resentations, conditioning mechanisms, and generation or-
der. Nevertheless, the local prediction objective does not
directly capture all requirements of the complete motion:
an individual token or token group must contribute to se-
mantic relationships that span multiple temporal segments.
Accurate local predictions alone do not guarantee that the
generated sequence respects the action order specified by
the text or maintains coherent transitions between motion
stages [27]. Coordinating global textual requirements with
the evolving motion context thus remains an important chal-
lenge for autoregressive text-to-motion generation.
This challenge also depends on how conditioned motion
representations are formed before each prediction: local to-
ken choices must reconcile cross-temporal textual require-
ments with the available motion context. While existing
Transformers build these representations through discrete
residual updates, their internal update trajectories offer an
optimization dimension complementary to tokenization and
generation order [15].
We therefore ask: can redesign-
ing hidden-state evolution improve text-conditioned motion
generation while preserving the pretrained representation
and autoregressive mechanism?
To investigate this question, as shown in Fig. 1, we intro-
duce MotionODE, a continuous-depth post-training frame-
work for pretrained autoregressive text-to-motion genera-
tors, with two key components.
First, MotionODE en-
ables minimally invasive architectural adaptation. We reuse
the pretrained weights of selected Transformer residual
branches to define local vector fields, interpret their original
residual updates as unit-step Euler-style updates, and intro-
duce midpoint Runge–Kutta integration (RK2) to evolve the
hidden states. This adaptation preserves the base model’s
motion representation, text-conditioning interface, outer
generation order, and motion decoder, without requiring a
redesign or training the entire system from scratch. Be-
cause it operates on internal residual computation rather
than a particular generation granularity, the framework is
applicable to different autoregressive formulations, includ-
ing next-token and next-scale generation. Second, we pro-
pose mixed one-step/two-step RK2 post-training. Simply
replacing the integration scheme does not guarantee better
generation: pretrained weights are optimized along the orig-
inal discrete update path, whereas a new integrator queries
different intermediate states and changes the inputs to sub-
sequent branches. We therefore alternate between one-step
and two-step RK2 over the same integration interval, train-
ing a shared set of parameters under ground-truth motion-
token cross-entropy supervision.
We further investigate
prediction-distribution distillation and local update-map an-
choring to balance adaptation to new integration paths with
preservation of pretrained behavior. At inference, the gener-
ator uses a selected integration step count without requiring
the original teacher model.
We evaluate MotionODE on HumanML3D using autore-
gressive generators with different generation mechanisms.
Extensive experiments demonstrate that MotionODE con-
sistently improves text–motion alignment across existing
autoregressive generators. Our main contributions are sum-
marized as follows:
• We propose MotionODE, a continuous-depth post-
training framework that improves pretrained autoregres-
sive text-to-motion generators by reformulating selected
residual updates as ODE integration, without changing
their motion representations or generation mechanisms.
• We introduce mixed one-step/two-step RK2 post-training
to adapt pretrained generators to new integration paths.
Our minimally invasive design supports both next-token
and next-scale generation.
• Extensive experiments on HumanML3D demonstrate
consistent improvements in text–motion alignment across
MoScale, BAMM and LMR.
2. Related Work
Text-to-motion generation.
Existing methods primar-
ily follow diffusion-based and autoregressive paradigms.
Diffusion-based approaches progressively denoise motion
under text conditioning: MotionDiffuse [34] and MDM [28]
explore fine-grained conditioning and geometric supervi-
sion, while MLD [2] improves efficiency through latent-
space diffusion.
Subsequent studies investigate acceler-
ated sampling [3], energy-based composition [32], struc-
tured cross-modal interactions [10], hybrid diffusion–
autoregressive modeling [20], and hierarchical flow match-
ing [16]. Autoregressive methods instead formulate mo-
tion synthesis as discrete token prediction. TM2T [8] intro-
duces text–motion token translation, T2M-GPT [31] com-
bines vector-quantized motion representations with a GPT-


style generator, and MotionGPT [11] unifies language and
motion modeling. Later methods improve tokenization and
generation through residual quantization and masked pre-
diction [9, 23], model scaling [18], hierarchical streaming
generation [6], and next-scale autoregression [36]. Com-
plementary approaches enrich generation with retrieved
motion exemplars [33], body-part-aware retrieval and fu-
sion [13], persistent temporal context [35], and retrieval-
augmented masked modeling [17]. Despite these advances
in representations, generation mechanisms, and contextual
information, the internal updates that form conditioned rep-
resentations before autoregressive prediction remain less
explored; MotionODE addresses this gap through mini-
mally invasive continuous-depth adaptation and mixed RK2
post-training, while preserving the pretrained motion repre-
sentation and generation mechanism.
Neural ODE methods. Neural Ordinary Differential Equa-
tions [1] model hidden-state evolution through continuous-
depth dynamics parameterized by neural networks. Sub-
sequent work improves their expressiveness through aug-
mented state spaces [4], gradient accuracy through check-
pointed differentiation [7], and efficiency through dynam-
ics regularization [5]. ODE Transformer [15] extends this
perspective to sequence modeling by introducing Runge–
Kutta-inspired Transformer blocks.
The relationship be-
tween discrete residual networks and continuous dynam-
ics has also been studied through approximation analy-
sis [26] and implicit regularization during training [19].
More recently, Neural ODE Transformers [29] parameter-
ize attention and feed-forward weights as functions of con-
tinuous depth, enabling dynamical analysis and adaptive
fine-tuning, while layer-parallel training [12] exploits an
ODE formulation to parallelize Transformer training across
depth. MotionODE shares their numerical-integration per-
spective on representation evolution, but reuses selected
residual branches of pretrained autoregressive motion gen-
erators as local vector fields and adapts them through mixed
one-step/two-step RK2 post-training, without introducing
continuous-depth weight parameterization or changing the
outer generation mechanism.

4. Experiment
4.1. Experimental Details
We evaluate MotionODE on HumanML3D, which repre-
sents human motion using 22 joints at 20 FPS. We study
MoScale, BAMM, LMR, following the standard training,
validation, and test splits distributed with the baselines.
Each baseline and its adapted counterpart share the same
motion representations, normalization statistics, evaluators,
and data-loading compatibility fixes. Starting from the cor-
responding official pretrained checkpoints, we update the
motion-generation Transformers while freezing the motion
tokenizers and text encoders; BAMM’s residual refiner is
also frozen. Selected self-attention and feed-forward resid-
ual branches are reformulated using midpoint RK2 over a
unit depth interval, with one and two integration substeps
alternated across microbatches.
Post-training uses AdamW with an effective batch size
of 64, a peak learning rate of 10−5, β = (0.9, 0.99), weight
decay of 10−5, and gradient clipping at 2.0. We train for
six epochs, comprising 2,232 updates, with a half-epoch
warmup followed by cosine decay to 10−6. The training
seed is fixed to 10711, and checkpoints are saved at epochs
2 and 6 for validation-based selection.
The full config-
uration combines the native cross-entropy objective with
frozen-teacher KL distillation and normalized Euler-map
anchoring, each weighted by 1, and uses a field-correction
coefficient of 0.5 with a finite-difference step of 0.01; these
additional components are examined separately through ab-
lations. The teacher is removed at inference, and no infer-
ence parameters are added. The selected MoScale, BAMM

4.2. Main Result
Quantitative results.
Table 1 evaluates MotionODE on
three pretrained backbones:
LMR (Discrete), MoScale,
and BAMM, with the latter two representing next-scale
and next-token motion generation, respectively. Across all
three backbones, MotionODE consistently improves text-
to-motion retrieval performance and reduces motion–text
matching distance, indicating better semantic alignment
without changing the original generation mechanism. For
LMR, R-Precision improves from 0.497 to 0.504, from
0.687 to 0.692, and from 0.782 to 0.788 for Top-1, Top-2,
and Top-3, respectively, while matching distance decreases
from 3.098 to 3.066. These improvements extend the ob-
served alignment benefits to an additional pretrained gen-
erator. The adapted MoScale and BAMM models also ex-
hibit increased multimodality, suggesting that their align-
ment gains do not come at the expense of conditional mo-
tion diversity; this metric is not yet available for the adapted
LMR model.
These results are consistent with the motivation for
combining continuous-depth adaptation with integration-
path-aware post-training: midpoint RK2 evaluates resid-
ual branches at intermediate hidden states, while mixed
one-step/two-step training adapts pretrained parameters to
the resulting integration paths. Rather than relying on a
direct solver replacement, MotionODE jointly adapts the
hidden-state updates and their learned dynamics. Impor-
tantly, the consistent alignment gains across the evaluated
backbones, including next-token and next-scale generation,
suggest that this approach is not tied to a particular autore-
gressive factorization. Nevertheless, distributional quality
does not improve uniformly: BAMM achieves a slightly
lower FID, whereas both MoScale and LMR (Discrete) ex-
hibit an alignment–fidelity trade-off.
For LMR, FID in-
creases from 0.039 to 0.098, and Diversity moves farther
from the real-motion reference, increasing from 9.599 to
9.748. Thus, improved semantic alignment does not nec-
essarily imply better motion-distribution fidelity. Overall,
MotionODE provides a transferable strategy for improving
the semantic accuracy of pretrained autoregressive motion
generators, while highlighting the need to balance align-
ment and distributional quality.

4.3. Ablation Study
We conduct ablation studies on MoScale to examine auxil-
iary losses, field correction, and integration-step configura-
tions. Table 2 reports results averaged over 20 evaluation
replications with CFG 3 and starting seed 2. We use one-
step RK2 inference unless otherwise specified.
Auxiliary losses. KL distillation is the most consistently
beneficial auxiliary constraint. Across four pairs with the
field-correction and map-anchoring settings held fixed, re-
taining KL improves Top-1 retrieval and reduces FID. In the
full configuration, removing KL noticeably degrades FID
and also reduces multimodality. These results are consis-
tent with KL providing an output-level constraint that limits
distributional drift during integration-path adaptation. Map
anchoring has a smaller and more configuration-dependent
effect. With field correction enabled, retaining it improves
Top-1 and Top-3, while FID and matching distance remain
nearly unchanged. Without KL, map anchoring also recov-
ers part of the Top-1 degradation, although it does not im-
prove every metric. Thus, the two constraints play different
roles: KL provides the more consistent alignment–fidelity
benefit, whereas map anchoring offers a local regularization
effect whose utility depends on the remaining components.
Field correction and interactions. The field-correction ab-
lations reveal a trade-off rather than a uniform gain. Rel-
ative to removing field correction alone, the full configu-
ration achieves better Top-2 and Top-3 retrieval and a di-
versity value closer to the real-motion reference, whereas
the uncorrected variant obtains slightly better Top-1, FID,
matching distance, and multimodality. The interaction with
map anchoring is particularly important: removing both
components improves retrieval and FID relative to the full
configuration. This indicates that field correction and lo-
cal update anchoring should not be interpreted as indepen-
dently additive improvements.
Meanwhile, KL remains
beneficial with or without these components, confirming
that its contribution is more robust to the choice of hidden-
state adaptation mechanism.
Matched field-evaluation budget.
Under the matched
student field-evaluation budget, the full configuration with
one-step RK2 obtains better Top-1 and Top-3 retrieval and
higher multimodality than the uncorrected two-step RK2
control. The two-step control instead achieves lower FID
and matching distance. Consequently, spending the avail-
able branch evaluations on additional integration substeps
is not uniformly preferable to the corrected one-step con-
figuration. The comparison exposes two distinct uses of
the evaluation budget: the selected configuration favors re-
trieval accuracy and conditional variation, while the two-
step alternative favors distributional fidelity.
Here, the
matched budget refers to student field evaluations rather
than measured end-to-end runtime.
Training-step configurations. Compared with the histor-
ical fixed-step checkpoints, mixed-step training followed
by one-step RK2 inference yields lower FID, higher multi-
modality, and diversity closer to the real-motion reference.
The fixed-step configurations achieve somewhat stronger
retrieval and lower matching distance, but these gains are
accompanied by reduced conditional variation, particularly
for the two-step checkpoint.
The results therefore favor
the mixed-step configuration when distributional fidelity
and conditional diversity are considered alongside seman-
tic alignment. Since the historical checkpoints use different
training recipes, this comparison characterizes the resulting
configurations rather than isolating the training-step sched-
ule alone.
Final configuration. We adopt mixed one-step/two-step
RK2 training and one-step RK2 inference as our final oper-
ating configuration. Among the evaluated training-step con-
figurations, it provides a favorable balance between text–
motion alignment, distributional fidelity, and conditional
diversity, without requiring two integration steps at infer-
ence. The budget-matched comparison further shows that
its retrieval and multimodality advantages are not obtained
simply by assigning more student field evaluations. Never-
theless, the component ablations demonstrate that the full
configuration is not uniformly optimal across all quality
metrics.
We therefore present it as a selected quality–
computation trade-off, rather than claiming dominance over
every tested variant.
5. Conclusion
We have presented MotionODE, a continuous-depth post-
training framework for pretrained autoregressive text-to-
motion generators. Inspired by ODE Transformer, Motion-
ODE reinterprets selected residual branches as local vec-
tor fields and adapts their hidden-state evolution through
mixed one-step/two-step midpoint RK2 training, while pre-
serving the original tokenization, conditioning, and gen-
eration mechanisms.
Experiments on LMR (Discrete),
MoScale, and BAMM show improved text–motion align-
ment across multiple backbones, including next-scale and
next-token generation, supporting the applicability of this
framework to different autoregressive factorizations. Quali-
tative comparisons further illustrate improved adherence to
fine-grained pose constraints and the progression of multi-
stage actions. Together, these findings highlight integration-
path-aware post-training as a practical approach to improv-
ing the semantic accuracy of existing motion generators
without redesigning their generation pipelines.


Limitations.
Our evaluation focuses on HumanML3D,
leaving generalization to broader motion domains and more
diverse textual descriptions for future study.
Moreover,
alignment improvements do not uniformly translate into
lower FID, motivating further investigation of the balance
between semantic accuracy and motion-distribution fidelity.
