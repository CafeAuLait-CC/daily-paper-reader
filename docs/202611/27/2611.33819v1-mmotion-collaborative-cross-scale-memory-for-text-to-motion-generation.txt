Abstract
Text-to-motion generation aims to synthesize realistic hu-
man motions from natural-language descriptions, enabling
applications in animation, virtual avatars, and embodied
agents. Despite recent progress, existing methods either re-
trieve motion exemplars or retain long-range context, but
rarely exploit memory to jointly reason about semantics, dy-
namics, and generation history. In this paper, we propose
MMotion, a collaborative multi-scale memory framework
that unifies scale-conditioned semantic retrieval, latent dy-
namics modeling, and causal working memory. Specifically,
semantic memory provides motion priors at different gener-
ation scales, dynamics memory captures latent state tran-
sitions, and working memory preserves causal context from
previously generated motions. A collaborative fusion mech-
anism integrates these complementary memories to deter-
mine what motion to generate, how it should evolve, and
how it should remain consistent with its history. MMotion
is architecture-agnostic and can be readily integrated into
both diffusion-based and next scale autoregressive models.
Extensive experiments across standard benchmarks and di-
verse backbones demonstrate consistent improvements over
strong baselines.
1. Introduction
Text-to-motion generation aims to synthesize realistic hu-
man motion from natural-language descriptions. As a fun-
damental interface between language and human behavior,
it has broad applications in character animation [26], virtual
reality [27], human–computer interaction [22], and embod-
ied intelligence[17]. Beyond generating visually plausible
poses, a successful model must accurately interpret com-
positional textual instructions, preserve fine-grained motion
details [12]. Achieving these objectives simultaneously re-
mains challenging because human motion exhibits com-
plex dependencies across semantic, temporal, and skeletal
scales.
Recent text-to-motion methods have progressed from de-
terministic sequence prediction to discrete motion modeling
and probabilistic generation [4, 13, 38]. Autoregressive ap-
proaches [8, 10, 37] typically tokenize motion sequences
and predict motion tokens conditioned on text, benefit-
ing from the expressive sequence-modeling capabilities of
Transformers. Diffusion-based approaches [28, 34, 35] in-
stead learn to progressively denoise motion representations,
achieving strong generation quality and diversity.
Other
methods employ hierarchical representations, masked mod-
eling, or retrieval augmentation to improve semantic align-
ment and motion fidelity [1, 18, 29]. Despite this progress,
most methods encode the input and intermediate states us-
ing transient features, without explicitly preserving reusable
knowledge throughout generation. A few recent approaches
[11, 16, 36] introduce memory through exemplar retrieval
or long-context retention; however, they typically model
only a single aspect of motion and fail to coordinate seman-
tic intent, dynamic evolution, and generation history across
scales.
We argue that effective memory for text-to-motion gen-
eration should jointly determine what motion to generate,
how it should evolve, and how it remains consistent with the
generated history. To this end, we organize semantic knowl-
edge, motion dynamics, and causal context into a collabo-
rative multi-scale memory system, enabling complementary
guidance throughout generation.
Specifically,
we introduce MMotion,
a collabora-
tive multi-scale memory framework that unifies scale-
conditioned semantic retrieval, latent dynamics modeling,
and causal working memory. Its cross-scale semantic mem-
ory retrieves scale- and position-conditioned motion pri-
ors, providing global structural guidance at coarse scales
and fine-grained details at finer scales. Its latent dynam-
ics memory models first- and second-order state changes
to capture how motion evolves and to encourage plausi-
ble transitions. Its working memory summarizes previously

Moreover, to effectively inject memory without disrupt-

lightweight adaptation. This design makes MMotion read-

2. Related Work
Diffusion-based text-to-motion generation.
Diffusion
models have emerged as a prominent paradigm for text-
conditioned motion synthesis. MotionDiffuse [35] intro-
duces fine-grained text conditioning into the diffusion pro-
cess, while MDM [28] incorporates geometric objectives to
improve motion quality. MLD [2] performs diffusion in
a compact latent space, and ReMoDiffuse [34] augments
denoising with retrieved text–motion exemplars.
Recent
studies have further explored efficient sampling, compo-
sitional generation, and structured motion representations.
MotionLCM [3] accelerates synthesis through latent con-
sistency modeling, EnergyMoGen [33] introduces energy-
based composition in latent diffusion, and SALAD [9] ex-
plicitly models interactions among skeletal joints, temporal
frames, and textual tokens. Rethinking Diffusion [21] revis-
its continuous motion representations and combines diffu-
sion with masked autoregressive generation, while Motion-
HiFlow [14] adopts hierarchical flow matching for coarse-
to-fine synthesis. These approaches primarily advance the
generative process, representation, or conditioning mecha-
nism. In contrast, MMotion provides complementary mem-
ory guidance and can be directly integrated into diffusion-
based backbones.
Autoregressive text-to-motion generation.
Autoregres-
sive methods typically discretize motion sequences and for-
mulate text-to-motion synthesis as conditional token predic-
tion. TM2T [7] employs vector quantization and sequence-
to-sequence modeling to translate between text and motion
tokens. T2M-GPT [32] combines a motion VQ-VAE with a
GPT-style Transformer, while MotionGPT [10] unifies lan-
guage and motion within a shared token space. Subsequent
methods improve motion tokenization and sequence mod-
eling through residual quantization, masked prediction, and
hierarchical decoding [8, 24]. More recently, ScaMo [20]
investigates scaling laws for autoregressive motion gener-
ation using a finite-scalar-quantized tokenizer and a text-
prefix Transformer.
MOGO [5] introduces hierarchical
residual motion tokens for efficient streaming generation,
and MoScale [37] replaces conventional next-token predic-
tion with coarse-to-fine next-scale autoregression. Unlike
these methods, which encode generation context implic-
itly in token prefixes and hidden states, MMotion explic-
itly maintains semantic, dynamic, and causal memories. It
therefore complements next scale autoregressive modeling
without altering its underlying token-prediction formula-
tion.
Memory for text-to-motion generation. Memory mech-
anisms have been introduced to provide motion genera-
tors with information beyond their instantaneous represen-
tations. ReMoDiffuse [34] retrieves relevant text–motion
pairs from an external database and incorporates their mo-
tion features into diffusion-based denoising. MoRAG [11]
extends retrieval-augmented generation paradigm through
body-part-aware retrieval and multi-source motion fusion.
InfiniMotion [36] retains temporal information to support
arbitrarily long motion generation. More recently, ReMo-
Mask [16] combines cross-modal retrieval with masked mo-
tion modeling and semantic spatio-temporal fusion.
Ex-
isting approaches mainly use memory to retrieve motion
exemplars or retain long-range context. MMotion instead
unifies scale-conditioned semantic retrieval, latent dynam-
ics modeling, and causal working memory, jointly optimiz-
ing their interactions to coordinate what motion to generate,
how it evolves, and how it remains consistent with its his-
tory.
2.1. Overview
Given a text description y, text-to-motion generation aims
to produce a motion sequence x = {xt}T
t=1 that matches
the description and remains coherent over time. Most exist-


ing methods generate motion using only the text condition
and the hidden states of the current sample. They cannot
explicitly reuse motion patterns learned from other samples,
reason about how the current motion should evolve, or re-
tain a structured record of what has already been generated.
Consequently, they may omit important action details, pro-
duce unnatural transitions, or gradually deviate from the in-
tended motion.
We propose MMotion, a unified memory-augmented
framework for next-scale autoregressive and diffusion-
based text-to-motion models. As shown in Fig. 1, MMo-
tion contains three complementary modules. First, a cross-
scale semantic memory retrieves motion patterns that match
the text, generation scale, and position within the genera-
tion process. Second, a latent dynamics memory estimates
how the current motion state should evolve. Third, a causal
working memory summarizes the motion generated thus far.
Together, these modules answer three questions: what mo-
tion should be generated, how it should evolve, and what
has already been generated.
The three memory outputs are fused to produce a small
residual correction to the backbone feature. This design al-
lows MMotion to improve the current prediction while pre-
serving the knowledge and generation behavior of the pre-
trained model.
Let e denote the text embedding, hs,n the backbone hid-
den state, zs,n the motion latent, and ps,n the normalized
position of generation step n within scale s.
For next-
scale autoregressive models, s follows the model’s native
coarse-to-fine scale hierarchy, and ps,n indicates the posi-
tion within the corresponding within-scale denoising pro-
cess.
For diffusion models, we partition the N reverse-
denoising steps chronologically into coarse-to-fine stages.
In our implementation, three scales are defined by the nor-
malized stage endpoints s ∈{0.3, 0.6, 1.0}, with ps,n de-
noting the relative position of step n within each stage.
In both cases, the refined feature is passed to the original
prediction head, leaving the native sampling procedure un-
changed.
We next introduce the three memory modules, their col-
laborative fusion, and the training strategy.

Progressive training.
Directly training all newly intro-
duced modules together may disrupt the pretrained genera-
tor before the memories become reliable. We therefore use
a three-phase training strategy.
In Phase A, the backbone is frozen and the residual cor-
rection is disabled. We train semantic retrieval, dynamics
prediction, and working-memory encoding using their cor-
responding objectives. This phase allows each memory to
learn its basic function without affecting the generator.
In Phase B, we gradually activate the residual correction
and introduce teacher supervision and the trust-region loss.
The three memories and the collaborative fusion layers are
then optimized jointly, while the backbone remains frozen.
In Phase C, we activate low-rank adapters in the final
layers of the generator and train them with a smaller learn-
ing rate.
This allows the output layers to adapt to the
memory-enhanced features after the memory modules have
stabilized.
We also enable retrieval-weight recalibration
and margin convergence in this phase to improve late-stage
training stability.
At inference, the teacher branch is removed.

indicate better performance, whereas Diversity is assessed
by its proximity to the corresponding real-motion value.
3.2. Main Results
Results on HumanML3D. As shown in Table 1, MMo-
tion consistently improves both backbones, demonstrating
that the proposed memory framework generalizes across
discrete
autoregressive
and
continuous
flow-matching
paradigms. When integrated with MoScale, MMotion im-
proves all R-Precision metrics while preserving competi-
tive distributional fidelity, indicating that memory retrieval
strengthens text–motion correspondence without substan-
tially disrupting the pretrained motion prior. Its integra-
tion with MotionHiFlow yields the strongest overall perfor-
mance, establishing new best results in semantic alignment,
FID, and MM-Dist while maintaining diversity close to that
of real motions. These complementary gains suggest that
the three memory components address distinct limitations
of parametric generation: semantic memory resolves textual
ambiguity, dynamics memory supplies reusable motion pat-
terns, and working memory preserves temporal consistency
throughout generation. The simultaneous improvements in
retrieval accuracy and distributional quality further indicate
that MMotion does not merely bias generation toward easily
recognizable actions, but instead produces motions that are
both semantically faithful and realistic. We provide a qual-
itative analysis in Fig. 2, with additional visualizations in-
cluded in the supplementary material. Results on KIT-ML.
Table 2 further demonstrates the effectiveness of MMotion
under the smaller and more heterogeneous KIT-ML bench-
mark. MotionHiFlow equipped with MMotion achieves the
best R-Precision and MM-Dist, substantially strengthening
text–motion alignment while retaining competitive FID and
realistic diversity. This result is particularly informative be-
cause the memory modules are trained without modifying
the pretrained backbone, showing that retrieved semantic
and dynamics knowledge can compensate for limited super-
vision without sacrificing the underlying generative prior.
The improvements are less uniform for MoScale: MMo-
tion enhances Top-1 retrieval while Top-2/3 accuracy and
MM-Dist do not consistently improve. This contrast sug-
gests that memory cues are more naturally assimilated by
the continuous, hierarchical refinement process of Motion-
HiFlow than by MoScale’s discrete multi-scale decoding,
where retrieved information may compete with early token
decisions. Nevertheless, the strong and consistent gains ob-
tained with MotionHiFlow across both benchmarks estab-
lish MMotion as an effective plug-in framework rather than
an improvement tied to a particular dataset.

3.3. Ablation Study
In this paper, we conduct controlled ablations on Hu-
manML3D to examine how the key design choices of MMo-
tion affect its performance.
All variants share the same
checkpoint and random seed and differ only in the speci-
fied inference-time intervention.
Where should memory be injected?
We conduct con-
trolled ablations on HumanML3D to examine how the key
design choices of MMotion affect its performance. All vari-
ants share the same checkpoint and random seed and differ
only in the specified inference-time intervention. MMotion
is designed to guide the entire generation process rather than
a single stage. We therefore restrict memory injection to ei-
ther coarse or fine scales in Table 3(a). Both variants remain
effective, but neither provides the balanced performance of
all-scale injection, which yields the best FID, MM-Dist,
and Top-2/3 R-Precision. This comparison supports apply-
ing memory throughout the generation hierarchy, such that
retrieved knowledge can shape global motion structure at
coarse scales and refine local dynamics at fine scales.
How much information should be retrieved? Having es-
tablished the value of all-scale injection, we next vary the
number of retrieved entries in Table 3(b). Retrieving too
few entries may omit useful motion patterns, whereas re-
trieving too many may introduce less relevant information.
Although different values favor individual metrics, K = 8
achieves the best Top-1 R-Precision and FID, offering the
most favorable balance between retrieval coverage and rel-
evance. We therefore use K = 8 as the default setting for
MMotion.
Does the internal memory design matter? We next de-
termine whether MMotion benefits from meaningful mem-
ory operations rather than merely from additional residual
features. As shown in Table 3(c), shuffling semantic asso-
ciations degrades R-Precision, FID, and MM-Dist, demon-
strating the importance of retrieving correctly matched se-
mantic knowledge. Disabling dynamics-memory retrieval
likewise weakens alignment and distributional fidelity, con-
firming the contribution of reusable dynamics priors. Al-
though simplified working-memory variants improve iso-
lated metrics, the complete design provides a stronger over-
all trade-off, supporting the complementary roles of its in-
ternal mechanisms.
How do the three memories work together? Finally, Ta-
ble 3(d) progressively activates the semantic, dynamics, and
working memories to reveal how their contributions accu-
mulate. Semantic memory first improves text–motion align-
ment, while adding dynamics memory further strengthens
Top-1/2 R-Precision and MM-Dist. Incorporating working
memory completes MMotion and yields the best Top-1/3
R-Precision, FID, and MM-Dist, suggesting that tracking
the evolving generation helps reconcile retrieved knowl-


edge with the motion already synthesized. Although not
every metric improves monotonically, the complete frame-
work achieves the most balanced semantic alignment and
distributional fidelity.
4. Conclusion
This paper proposes MMotion, a model-agnostic three-
memory framework that enhances text-driven motion gen-
eration through semantic, dynamics, and working mem-
ories.
MMotion can be integrated into pretrained gen-
erators while keeping their backbones frozen.
Exper-
iments on HumanML3D and KIT-ML show consistent
improvements across next scale autoregressive and flow-
matching paradigms, particularly in text–motion align-
ment and motion quality.
These results demonstrate the
potential of explicit memory to improve motion genera-
tion efficiently without retraining the underlying generative
model.
