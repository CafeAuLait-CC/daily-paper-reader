# Abstract 

In this paper, we move beyond the conventional setting of motion in-betweening that relies on structured pose inputs, and instead propose a new paradigm that is conditioned on egocentric (first-person) visual observations, thereby reducing the requirements on both users and upstream systems; we term this task Egocentric Human Motion Bridging. This is motivated by realistic consumer scenarios in VR/AR and robotics, where users typically have access only to raw egocentric imagery (e.g., from wearable devices) without reliable human pose annotations or motion capture signals. Unlike conventional motion interpolation in human pose-conditioned settings or motion generation conditioned on a complete temporal language or image context, the egocentric perspective introduces large viewpoint variations, partial body visibility, and severe temporal ill-posedness due to cross-modal endpoint constraints. To this end, we propose Ego-HMB, a unified diffusion-based model that internalizes both endpoint human pose estimation capability and temporally consistent 3D motion infilling ability within a single model through two training stages. Specifically, we first introduce Egocentric-to-Canonical Pretraining (pretraining stage), where we design a hierarchical curriculum with progressively increasing temporal spans. Then, we present a novel Motion Bridge Diffusion Training with endpoint and physics awareness as a fine-tuning stage, where our bridge diffusion diffuse from neighboring poses to enhance temporal continuity while preserving the global constraints between the two endpoints of a 3D motion sequence. Extensive experiments and comparisons with one-stage baselines (a generative model conditioned directly on two egocentric images) and two-stage baselines (estimation models followed by interpolation models) demonstrate that our approach consistently outperforms these baselines. Code is available at https: //github.com/gaogehan/Ego-HMB .

# 1 Introduction 

Human motion generation based on deep learning and diffusion models has achieved substantial success in VR/AR and robotics, significantly reducing the cost of motion creation for both pro-fessionals and general users while enabling diverse forms of interactive entertainment. Existing approaches can be broadly categorized into several classes: (1) multimodal-to-motion generation (typically conditioned on text or audio); (2) motion estimation (usually from video or monocular images); and (3) motion in-betweening/interpolation (given starting and ending poses). These tasks generally require dense user inputs or fully specified 3D pose sequences.

However, in common VR/AR and robotic application scenarios, users who intend to obtain motion sequences often have access only to sparse image representations, rather than complete pose tra-jectories. Generating full motion sequences from such sparse image inputs is therefore practically significant, as it reduces both user expression cost and storage overhead (by relying on sparse images instead of dense motion data). To address this realistic requirement, we propose a new task termed egocentric human motion bridging task. In essence, the objective is to generate a coherent motion segment from two endpoint egocentric images. This task differs from existing paradigms in several aspects: 1) Unlike other cross-modal generation tasks, such as text- or scene-to-motion generation, where the conditioning signal usually describes a holistic description of the entire motion to be generated, our task is constrained by implicit start and end conditions. 2) It is not standard motion in-betweening/interpolation, since users are not required to provide complete 3D starting and ending poses. The notion of bridging refers to leveraging image modalities distinct from motion representations to connect human motion endpoints and synthesize the intermediate motion segment. Therefore, how to effectively address the motion bridging task remains insufficiently and unsystemat-ically explored in the existing literature. Based on current technical paradigms, two intuitive solution paths can be derived, both relying on the adaptation or composition of existing model frameworks, and thus serving as strong baselines for comparison with our method, as shown in Fig. 1. The first approach treats sparse ego images as conditioning signals and trains a conditional generative model directly. For example, diffusion-based text/scene-to-motion models such as [30, 25] can be adapted by replacing text or audio inputs with ego-image features. However, this strategy requires large-scale paired data, is vulnerable to temporal inconsistency in high-dimensional sequence generation, and lacks explicit structural constraints to ensure accurate alignment with the desired start and end states. The second approach follows a two-stage pipeline: first estimates 3D endpoint poses from input images [34], and then uses a motion in-betweening model [28, 25] to generate 2intermediate motion. While modular and intuitive, this design suffers from error accumulation, as inaccuracies in endpoint estimation can propagate and cause trajectory drift. It also introduces higher structural complexity, model size, and inference cost than an end-to-end formulation. Our core motivation for this task is twofold. First, sparse endpoint ego observations make the problem highly ill-posed, while self-occlusion and limited body visibility further increase endpoint pose uncertainty. Thus, strong pose priors are needed to constrain the solution space beyond explicit conditioning. Second, the generated intermediate motion must jointly satisfy endpoint consistency, temporal continuity and plausibility, avoiding discontinuities or unstable body structures. To address these challenges, we propose a unified bridge diffusion-based generative model trained in two stages, enabling a single framework to internalize both endpoint human pose estimation and physically consistent 3D motion generation, as presented in Fig. 1. Our method has two key features: 1) we propose egocentric-to-canonical endpoint pretraining with a time-span scheduling strategy, which strengthens the model’s endpoint pose priors and enables more accurate endpoint fitting and alignment; and 2) we introduce the first motion bridge diffusion model that diffuses from neighboring motion frames. This design preserves end-to-end structural consistency while promoting physically continuous and plausible motion generation. Overall, our method avoids the strong coupling difficulty and error accumulation inherent in prior approaches. To rigorously validate the effectiveness of our approach, we implement the two aforementioned strategies as strong baselines and conduct systematic ablation studies on all major components of our framework. Experiments are performed on the EE4D-Motion dataset [25], a large-scale motion capture benchmark comprising 208 hours of high-quality mocap data. Extensive quantitative and qualitative evaluations consistently demonstrate that our method achieves significant improvements over competing approaches across multiple metrics. Our contributions can be summarized as follows: • We introduce a new task, egocentric human motion bridging task, which aims to generate a coherent 3D motion segment from sparse egocentric image observations, addressing a practically relevant yet underexplored problem setting. • We propose a unified bridge diffusion-based framework that jointly internalizes endpoint pose estimation and temporally continuous 3D motion completion through a two-stage train-ing paradigm, enabling structurally consistent and physically plausible motion generation within a single model. • We conduct comprehensive comparisons against strong baselines, including both direct conditional generation (one-stage) and estimation–interpolation pipelines (two-stage), and perform systematic ablation studies. Extensive quantitative and qualitative results demon-strate the clear improvements of our approach. 

# 2 Related Work 

Human Motion Task: Human Motion Modeling has gradually become a prominent research direction in computer vision and computer graphics. On the one hand, human motion exhibits strong nonlinearity, complex temporal dependencies, and multimodal characteristics, which pose significant challenges for modeling. On the other hand, it has broad application value in animation synthesis, virtual reality, human–computer interaction, robotic control, and behavior understanding. Human motion tasks typically focus on generating or predicting plausible sequences of human joint movements under given conditions. The core challenge lies in modeling high-dimensional, structured, and strongly constrained spatiotemporal data. Depending on the task formulation, existing research can be broadly categorized into motion prediction [20, 2, 33, 29], motion generation [11, 10, 21, 35, 31, 27, 16], motion interpolation [12, 28, 6, 15]. Besides, motion generation tasks can be categorized according to the input modality, with common settings including text-to-motion [10, 11, 37, 31], audio-to-motion [5, 4, 3], and scene-to-motion [32, 25]. In this work, we focus on the growing interest in leveraging first-person perspectives to enhance perception for embodied intelligence and VR/AR applications, enabling the generation of human motion. To this end, we introduce a new task termed Egocentric Human Motion Bridging. Specifi-3cally, the goal is to directly generate intermediate motion sequences from sparse egocentric image observations. 

Bridge Diffusion Models: The Bridge Diffusion Models (BDMs) [17, 36, 23, 22] are a recently proposed variant of diffusion models that incorporates endpoint constraints into the generative process by leveraging the Brownian bridge stochastic process. Unlike standard denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPM) [14], which learn to reverse a diffusion trajectory from pure Gaussian noise to the data distribution, BDM constructs a conditional stochastic path between two sources. This formulation explicitly enforces boundary consistency during generation. So far, Bridge Diffusion has primarily been explored in image transformation and interpolation tasks. Brownian Bridge Diffusion Model (BBDM) [17] introduces a brownian bridge diffusion framework for image-to-image translation. By treating the source and target images as the two endpoints of a stochastic bridge, the model learns a conditional diffusion trajectory that smoothly transforms one image domain into another. Denoising Diffusion Bridge Models (DDBMs) [36] model distribution-to-distribution translation via diffusion bridges. By learning the bridge score and solving the associated stochastic differential equation, they offer a unified framework related to score-based diffusion and OT/flow matching, with improved results on image translation. TLB-VFI [22] applies the Brownian bridge formulation in latent space for video frame interpolation. In this setting, the first and last frames serve as fixed endpoints, and intermediate frames are generated along a time-aware stochastic bridge. In this work, we are the first to extend Bridge Diffusion to human motion modeling. We resolve the incompatibility of Bridge Diffusion with multi-frame sequence data. We formulate human motion bridging as a cross-modal stochastic bridge that connects sparse egocentric image observations to a complete motion sequence. Unlike image translation or completion, our task requires inferring full-body intermediate motion trajectories from sparse visual inputs under strong structural and temporal constraints. To this end, we design a tailored adaptation of the bridge diffusion framework to generate temporally coherent, kinematically plausible human motion while respecting observational guidance. 

# 3 Method 

In this section, we first formulate our new egocentric human motion bridging task and then detail our technological innovations: 1) a short-to-long curriculum endpoint pretraining with progressively increasing temporal spans, which facilitates scaling the pose estimation capability from image-frame pairs by leveraging a larger amount of paired data; and 2) the first motion bridging diffusion model, which generates continuous motion by diffusing from neighboring frames rather than from pure noise, thereby enabling the recovery of intermediate motion from endpoint poses and improving the modeling of smooth temporal transitions. 

3.1 Problem Formulation 

Given an egocentric image sequence I1: N = ( I1, I 2, . . . , I N ) acquired by a head-mounted camera, each frame satisfies Ii ∈ RH×W ×3. The corresponding 3D human motion is denoted by X1: N =(X1, X 2, . . . , X N ), where each pose Xi is represented with SMPL-X [26] parameters as Xi =(Rri , t ri , θ i, β i). Here, Rri ∈ R3 and tri ∈ R3 denote the global root rotation and translation, θi ∈

R21 ×3 contains the local joint rotations, and βi ∈ R10 captures the body shape. Following standard practice, we treat body shape as time-invariant, so βi = βj for all i, j .

Egocentric Human Motion Bridging Task aims to generate a plausible motion segment that connects two temporally sparse egocentric observations. Formally, the task is to model p(X1: N | Is, I e), where 

Is and Ie denote the egocentric images captured at the start and end of the target interval. The output is a motion sequence X1: N that bridges the two observations, such that the generated motion is consistent with both endpoint images. The term bridging highlights precisely this property: the model must rely on egocentric visual cues, instead of explicit motion parameters, to bridge two motion endpoints and synthesize the missing motion segment. This task is particularly challenging because the input is highly ill-posed. Egocentric images only provide partial first-person observations of the surrounding scene, while the wearer’s body is largely invisible. As a result, the model must infer the 4latent human pose and dynamics from limited visual evidence, while ensuring temporal consistency with both endpoint observations. 

3.2 Egocentric-to-Canonical Endpoint Pretraining 

In the pretraining stage, we learn a reliable mapping from egocentric observations at the first and last frames to canonical 3D human poses, treating the task as egocentric motion estimation. By decomposing each motion sequence into multiple endpoint pairs with different temporal gaps, we increase the paired training data and reduce the ambiguity caused by limited visibility, self-occlusion, and depth uncertainty. We pretrain the model with a conditional bridge diffusion objective on endpoint pose estimation. Specifically, given a motion window X1: N , egocentric image features (Os, O e), we construct a pretraining sample by selecting a pair of subframes (Xs, X e) with temporal gap g = e − s, where 

g is progressively scheduled from short to long values. The selected endpoint frames are placed at the two ends of a fixed-length window, yielding ( ˜Xs, ˜Xe). Only the corresponding conditioning inputs ( ˜Os, ˜Oe) remain visible at the two endpoint positions, while all intermediate frames are masked out. The model is then trained to learn pθ ( ˜X(s,e ) | ˜O(s,e )) under the same bridge diffusion framework. In our implementation, the temporal gap follows a curriculum g ∈ { 8, 16 , 32 , 64 } and progressively enlarge it until reaching the full-length setting, so the model first learns short-range endpoint consistency and then gradually handles larger pose variation across time. Importantly, this pretraining formulation allows each original motion window to be converted into multiple endpoint-pair supervision samples with different temporal spans, effectively increasing the amount of usable training data by several times. As a result, the model sees much richer ego-to-pose correspondences during training, which helps alleviate the ill-posed nature of egocentric 3D human pose and motion estimation. This provides a stronger initialization for the subsequent full-sequence modeling stage.

We first briefly review standard diffusion models and conventional bridge diffusion, and then introduce our motion bridging diffusion model for human motion generation from heterogeneous conditions.

Motivation. Inspired by bridge diffusion, we note that its bridging idea is particularly natural for human motion. A motion sequence is inherently transitional, with each frame evolving smoothly from nearby states, so modeling motion as a stochastic bridge provides a suitable inductive bias for temporal continuity and coherent kinematics. 

Why Standard Bridge Diffusion is Insufficient for Motion Sequences? Although this formulation is natural for image-to-image translation, it is not directly suitable for human motion generation in 5our setting. First, the original bridge objective depends explicitly on a same-modality reference y in the output space. In our task, however, the available conditions are heterogeneous, such as egocentric image observations and camera trajectory. Second, human motion is a structured temporal sequence rather than a single static sample, so the bridge should respect frame-wise temporal relations instead of treating the whole sequence as an unstructured paired object. Solutions : To make bridge diffusion suitable for motion sequences, we introduce two key modifications. First, instead of defining a global bridge between two fixed endpoints, we construct a local frame-wise bridge: splitting the sequence around its temporal center, each frame is paired with a nearby temporal neighbor, which is more natural for motion due to its local smoothness and short-range temporal dependency. Second, inspired by MDM, we predict the clean motion x0 rather than the bridge noise, while still designing the forward process so that the terminal state approaches a standard Gaussian distribution. This allows direct supervision in motion space, which is more compatible with geometric losses, and also enables a unified generation process conditioned on heterogeneous modalities, without requiring a same-modality output reference as in standard bridge diffusion.

In this section, we present in detail the strong baselines constructed for the new egocentric human motion bridging task, as well as the technical implementation, evaluation metrics, and dataset details. These experimental designs are intended to demonstrate the unified formulation and effectiveness of our method on this task. 

4.1 Implementation 

We adopt a conditional bridge diffusion model to model the entire human motion sequence. The denoising network is based on a Transformer decoder architecture with 12 layers, 12 attention heads, and a latent dimension of 768. The input to the network consists of noisy motion tokens, while the conditioning information includes egocentric image features. The image features, together with the diffusion timestep embedding, form context tokens that are injected into the denoising process through cross-attention. For the egocentric human motion bridging task, we retain only the conditioning information from the first and last frames, while masking out all trajectory and image conditions for the intermediate frames. This forces the model to recover the complete intermediate motion based solely on the endpoint constraints. The training objective is to predict the clean motion representation itself, and an MSE loss is computed over the valid frames. We adopt a head-centric motion representation, rather than the pelvis-centric representation commonly used in motion synthesis literature, which makes it better suited to egocentric devices. We use DINOv2 [24] fine-grained features to extract representations from egocentric images, since they have been shown to be more effective than CLIP in [25]. 

4.2 Dataset 

We use EE4D-Motion [25], a version of the large-scale EgoExo4D [8] dataset annotated with SMPL-X [1]. It is a 3D human motion dataset comprising 208 hours of time-synchronized 3D motion data, egocentric videos, and associated annotations. The dataset captures real-world human activities performed by participants wearing Project Aria glasses, covering diverse scenarios such as cooking, dance, sports, music, healthcare, and bike repair. To construct the training set, we extract 8-second video clips sampled at 10 fps ( N = 80 ) with a sliding window of 2 seconds, resulting in 143K training samples. For validation, we use the same clip length and frame rate but sample clips every 20 seconds, yielding 4,400 validation samples.

4.5 Results Quantitative Results: Tables 2 and 3 compare our method with one-stage and two-stage baselines on endpoint estimation and motion generation, respectively. MDM, Genmo, and UEM directly generate motion in a one-stage manner, while EgoAllo+UEM adopts a two-stage pipeline that first estimates endpoint poses and then performs motion generation. Our conditional unified motion bridge diffusion model achieves the best endpoint accuracy and motion quality scores while maintaining comparable Diversity (9.403). In particular, the lower endpoint MPJPE-based errors indicate more accurate endpoint pose estimation, while the improved Foot Slide, Foot Contact, Semantic Similarity, FID, and Jitter scores demonstrate more plausible, temporally stable motion generation. Besides, Table 1 demonstrates that our unified bridge diffusion design achieves better model inference performance compared with other models. We further evaluate cross-dataset generalization by directly applying models trained on EE4D-Motion to Nymeria, without training or fine-tuning on the target dataset (Table 4). Our method achieves the lowest endpoint MPJPE (0.369), FID (0.059), and Jitter (0.153), together with the best foot-related metrics and Semantic Similarity. Diversity remains comparable to the baselines at 9.533. These zero-shot results support the transfer of our endpoint estimation and motion generation capabilities beyond the training dataset. 

Qualitative Results: Figure 2 compares motion sequences generated from the same start and end egocentric images. The one-stage baseline exhibits weak endpoint alignment and implausible hand configurations, while the two-stage baseline improves the global structure but still produces physical artifacts such as floating feet. Our method better aligns the endpoints with the reference poses and preserves more plausible body configurations and contact patterns throughout the sequence. These observations agree with the quantitative results in Tables 2 and 3, supporting the benefit of jointly modeling endpoint estimation and motion bridging. Additional comparisons of temporal continuity are provided in the supplementary video.

# 5 Conclusion and Limitation 

In this paper, we introduced Egocentric Human Motion Bridging, a new task that generates co-herent 3D human motion segments from sparse egocentric endpoint observations. To address the challenges of partial body visibility, endpoint ambiguity, and temporal ill-posedness, we proposed Ego-HMB, a unified bridge diffusion framework that jointly internalizes endpoint pose estimation and temporally consistent motion completion through a two-stage training strategy. Extensive experi-ments demonstrate that our method outperforms both direct conditional generation and two-stage estimation–interpolation baselines, producing more accurate, continuous, and plausible motions. A current limitation of this work is that we focus on human motion bridging from egocentric images, which are commonly available in VR/AR and robotics scenarios, while other modalities such as text and audio are not yet explored. Nevertheless, our framework can be naturally extended to incorporate these additional modalities as conditioning signals. Another promising direction is to further adapt the generated motion representations for direct humanoid robot motion control, enabling broader embodied applications. 10 Acknowledgments and Disclosure of Funding
